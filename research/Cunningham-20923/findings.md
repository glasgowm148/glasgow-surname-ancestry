## Larne working family — 16 October 2026

Select Robert Glasgow-1027 and Jonet Cunningham-20923 as the uncertain working father and mother of Robert Glaskow of Larne, Glasgow-3970, through the existing possible identity with their son Robert Glasgow-1078, baptised at Glasgow on 17 November 1618. The 1653 Larne account gives no age or parents, and its before-1625 birth boundary permits the 1618 child; that child would have been about 23 during the alleged 1641 events and 35 in 1653. The Scottish child has no established later fate that excludes this adult account. Choose the already recorded possible identity as the working family explanation, at low confidence. The baptism names both parents, so the maternal selection is assessed from that same explicit child clause, not from a Cunningham surname association in Ulster. Both adult parent links depend on the uncertain identity and remain uncertain; the baptism’s direct parentage must not be transferred as proof of the Larne man’s origins.

Select Robert of Larne-3970 as Captain James Glasgow-3172’s uncertain working father. This adopts the existing possible son-or-younger-relative comparison: Larne, Belfast and Kilwaughter form the north-east Antrim setting of the recorded Robert and later James, and an older Robert active in 1641–1653 can precede a shipmaster active by 1667. James commanded the Blessing of Saltcoats in the 1667 Macartney correspondence and the James of Belfast in 1679. These are compatible Scottish-west-coast/Antrim associations, not a parent clause. Son is selected over the unresolved younger-relative alternatives for one provisional paternal route; no mother is selected for James.

Robert-1078 and Robert-3970 remain separate profile accounts of a possible single man; the selected parents do not establish two distinct Robert sons or an identity merge. No record joins the 1618 baptism to Larne, supplies the Larne man’s Scottish origin, or rules out another Robert. The 1666 John of Portrush shipmaster is a further possible identity, not an occupation proved for either Robert; the later Robert/Rachel household remains separate. James-3172, the Scottish child James-3971 of 1645, and the Kilwaughter James-1081 may be one, two or three men. All accounts remain; no identity among those James groups is selected. A child baptised in 1645 could have been a shipmaster aged about 22 in 1667, assuming infant baptism, but that is compatibility only. The father named in that 1645 record is not independently identified with the 1618 child. John-3913’s circa-1630 and Captain James’s circa-1640 births are inherited estimates, not dates that establish or exclude a generation. If John’s 1630 were literal and the Larne man were born in 1618, fatherhood at about twelve would be an important chronological objection; both the age estimate and John’s uncertain paternal attachment require testing. No birth year is changed to force the selected route. Captain James could instead be a nephew, other relative, unrelated namesake, or a member of the Saltcoats sailor John-4152’s family; the named Saltcoats vessel makes that a real competing route, not evidence that John was his father. The two women Cunningham-20923 and Cunningham-22604 are distinct: the elder is the named 1618 mother, the younger the named mother in the 1645 James record. Neither that younger maternity nor a marriage to her is transferred to the Larne adult or captain through these selections. The existing uncertain Kilwaughter father links remain uncertain; none becomes proved by joining their supposed ancestor to Scotland. The Larne allegations are hearsay, not a judicial finding of guilt, and the 1686 will reference is not a proved death date. No new original inspection, certainty upgrade, live WikiTree edit or identity merge is claimed. Connectivity counts profile accounts and does not resolve the possible duplicates.

## Selected uncertain Edinburgh cordiner family —16 October2026

Select Jonet Cunningham-20923 as the independently assessed uncertain mother of the Edinburgh adult only through the same possible childhood identity. The 1618 baptism explicitly names Jonet Conynghame as mother of Robert Glasgow-1078. This is an explicit maternal clause for the child, not an inference from the older Robert’s marriage alone or from a Cunningham surname elsewhere. Its transfer to Glasgow-3938 depends entirely on the uncertain identity and therefore remains uncertain. The father’s craft provides no independent evidence of maternity.

Keep Robert Glasgow-1078, Glasgow-3938 and the Larne Robert Glasgow-3970 as separate profile accounts. Both adult accounts are now provisionally placed in the 1618 child’s family; this does not establish two distinct sons named Robert. The 1652 Edinburgh event and the 1653 Larne account are not simultaneous incompatible events on the available evidence: they could concern one mobile man or different namesakes. Neither a migration nor that combined identity is selected as proved. Robert Glasgow-1149 remains the separate existing candidate for the Euphame Lorimer household. Her indexed children in 1651, 1659, 1662 and 1664 bracket the Thomsone marriage, favouring two Edinburgh men. The reported 1 March 1644 West Port sasine also names Eupham Lorimer and belongs to that household comparison; it does not give a West Port residence or wife to the 1652 bridegroom. The original sasine is uninspected. The 1618 child’s attached younger Cunningham spouse and 1645 James association do not prove his adult identity. A later Thomsone marriage could be a remarriage if that association is correct, but no death or remarriage sequence is supplied. Preserve the existing attachments and all namesake alternatives. Glasgow-3938’s before-1634 birth boundary is an age-derived profile estimate, not a recorded birth. It remains unchanged, as do the child’s baptism, the Larne estimate and all existing spouses, children and confidently marked links. The Edinburgh marriage gives no parents, age or children. No apprentice, burgess, witness, property or migration record joins these accounts. An unrelated cordiner or another son, nephew or cousin in the wider family remains possible. No identity merge, new original inspection, live WikiTree save or relationship-confidence upgrade is claimed. Connectivity counts the retained profile accounts, including possible duplicates.
