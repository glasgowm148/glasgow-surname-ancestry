# Findings: [Thomas Glasco (Glasco-143)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasco-143)

Last updated: 2026-09-08

## Current conclusion

This profile is not proved to be the Dublin watchmaker recorded at
Christchurch Lane in 1779 and 20 Ormond Quay in 1783. Its uncertain birth about
1766 is an estimate based on a reported 1785 marriage to Mary King; neither the
marriage nor Mary's dates has a substantive citation beyond a generic record
index link. The estimate conflicts with adult status in 1779 but is too weak to
exclude identity.

The profile's attached parents, [[Glasco-144]] and [[Duffy-6026]], are likewise
not established by its cited evidence. Do not add Miss Hynes, the watchmaker
events, or another marriage, and do not use the 1779–1783 cluster to validate
the attached parent-child chain.

## Identity comparison and relationship caution

- [[Glasco-144]] is directly supported as Margaret Duffy's 1773 groom, but no
  cited record proves he was this profile's father.
- [[Glasco-173]] is directly supported as Sarah's father with wife Catherine in
  1760, but no cited record proves he was [[Glasco-144]]'s father.
- No inspected source proves that any of these three Thomases had serial
  marriages to Catherine, Margaret Duffy, Miss Hynes and Mary King.

Keep the watchmaker as a free-space documentary cluster unless an
address-bearing marriage, baptism, burial, probate or trade record links him to
this profile.

## Identity assessment — 4 October 2026 newspaper review

The [5 October 1779 original advertisement](https://www.newspapers.com/image/1050086381/) and [4 April 1780 repeat](https://www.newspapers.com/image/1050049040/) name only Glasco, watch and clock maker, moving from Christ-Church-lane to 20 Ormond Quay near the market. The address supports the existing 1779–1783 Thomas trade cluster, but the newspaper gives no first name, spouse, parents or children and does not assign the event to this profile. Keep Glasco-143, Glasco-144 and Glasco-173 compatible under their current limits. See the [consolidated sourced draft](/research/subjects/newspapers-early-glasgow/#dublin-glasco-watchmaker). No relationship change is proposed.

## Current conclusion — newspaper review, 5 October 2026

This is a chronologically compatible Thomas Glasco namesake for the 1799 Tinicum Island subscriber; no profile identification is proved.

## Source findings — newspaper review

The [Aurora, 9 April 1799, p.3](https://www.newspapers.com/image/586542475/) and [11 April, p.1](https://www.newspapers.com/image/586542512/) print one STOP THIEF notice signed Thomas Glascoe: $115 reportedly stolen from his Tinicum Island house on 31 March. The reward is twenty dollars for recovery of the money, or fifteen for the thief alone.

## Identity and relationship assessments — newspaper review

The notice supplies residence but no age, relatives or migration link to this Dublin profile. Thomas is the reported victim; William Steel is the alleged thief. Check Tinicum tax, land, church and probate records before assignment. No family amendment is supported.
