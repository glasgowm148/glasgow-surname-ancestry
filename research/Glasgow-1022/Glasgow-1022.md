== Biography ==

'''John Glasgow''' was born about 1751 in County Antrim, Ireland. His age was
reported as ninety-nine in the detailed 1851 return and rounded to one hundred
in another transcription.<ref name="Glenleslie1851Detailed">[https://durrushistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/1851irishcensusreturnscommadelimitedtextfilejul24_071.pdf ''1851 Irish Census Returns: detailed comma-delimited transcription''], Glenleslie household 21.</ref><ref name="Glenleslie1851Macafee">[https://www.billmacafee.com/census/1851censuskilconway.pdf Bill MacAfee, ''1851 Census, Barony of Kilconway''], Glenleslie household 21.</ref>

John married [[Alexander-16967|Rose Alexander]] about 1791. The detailed return
records 1791 as the year of first marriage for both John and Rose. The year 1839
belongs to the different John Glasgow, aged forty, who was described as John's
nephew in the same household.<ref name="Glenleslie1851Detailed" />

In 1851 John was a farmer at Glenleslie in Dunaghy parish. Rose, aged eighty,
was with him. The return directly identifies three of their children:

* [[Glasgow-1369|Nancy Glasgow]], aged thirty-six when she died of dysentery in 1847;
* [[Glasgow-1050|Andrew Glasgow]], a linen weaver aged thirty when he died of consumption in 1848; and
* [[Glasgow-1368|Ellen Glasgow]], a twenty-six-year-old linen weaver, recorded as married since 1844.<ref name="Glenleslie1851Macafee" />

The same household contained granddaughters Ann Glasgow, eighteen, and Mary Ann
or Maryan Glasgow, seventeen; grandchildren Jane Grimes, five, and James Grimes,
nine months; and a married nephew John Glasgow, about forty. Their intervening
parents were not named. William Alexander, described as John's brother-in-law,
had died in 1846, and Sarah Alexander was visiting the household.<ref name="Glenleslie1851Macafee" />

John probably died after the 1851 census, perhaps about 1852. No original death
or burial record has been found.

== Research Notes ==

=== Other possible children ===

[[Glasgow-867|John Glasgow]] and [[Glasgow-2908|Samuel Glasgow]] of Lisnagaver
were brothers. The younger John's 1846 marriage names his father as '''John
Glasgow, farmer''', and the 1851 census explicitly calls Samuel his
brother.<ref name="JohnMarriage1846">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view/?record_id=cima-3156063 IrishGenealogy.ie], civil marriage of John Glasgow and Margaret Garvin, 16 November 1846.</ref><ref name="Lisnagaver1851">[https://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Rasharkin/Lisnagaver/34/ 1851 Census of Ireland], Lisnagaver household 34.</ref>

The younger John's family gravestone says that he died in 1895 aged seventy-
eight, implying birth about 1816 or 1817, although the 1851 return reported him
as fifty.<ref name="RasharkinStone">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/RasharkinP/graveyard.html Pauline O'Keeffe and Helen Evans, ''Rasharkin Second Presbyterian Church headstones''], transcription made August 2004, Glasgo memorial erected by James Garvin Glasgo.</ref> If the gravestone age is correct, this John would have been about sixty-five when the younger John was born. He remains a possible identity for the named father, but the shared name and occupation do not identify him uniquely. Both relationships remain uncertain.

[[Glasgow-1400|George Glasgow]], whose wife Sarah was also an Alexander, headed
the immediately preceding Glenleslie household. His location, age and Alexander
association make him a plausible son, but no record calls him John's son.<ref name="Glenleslie1851Macafee" />

[[Glasgow-2407|John Glasgow]], a farmer at Cross in Ballyclug, married Rachel
Grey in 1865. The original certificate calls him a bachelor and names his father
as '''John Glasgow, farmer'''.<ref name="CrossMarriage1865">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1865/11578/8260226.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil marriage register], John Glasgow and Rachel Grey, 11 September 1865.</ref> His 1874 death age implies birth about 1809.<ref name="CrossDeath1874">[https://civilrecords.irishgenealogy.ie/churchrecords/images/deaths_returns/deaths_1874/020673/7253774.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil death register], John Glasgow, Cross, 27 January 1874.</ref> He is another possible son of a local John farmer. He is not the 1851 nephew, who was recorded as married since 1839. [[Glasgow-1657|John Glasgow of Cross]], who died in 1836, is a competing and geographically closer father candidate, so no attachment to this John is justified yet.

=== Unresolved grandchildren and nephew ===

Ann and Mary Ann Glasgow retained the Glasgow surname, but their father or
mother has not been identified. Jane and James Grimes imply that another child
of John had formed a Grimes family. No compatible civil marriage was found for
Jane Grimes in the Ballymena district after 1860.

The forty-year-old nephew is especially important because his father should
identify a brother or brother-equivalent of John. [[Glasgow-2056|John Glasgow]]
was considered, but his 1850 marriage names his father as Henry Glasgow, farmer,
and no record makes that Henry John's brother.<ref name="John2056Marriage">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1850/09386/5399841.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil marriage register], John Glasgow and Matilda Little, 1850.</ref>

=== Parentage and local cluster ===

No inspected record identifies John's parents. [[Glasgow-1143|Robert Glasgow]]
was previously attached as his father, but the reviewed Kilwaughter church and
petition material never names John as Robert's son. The relationship is a tree
reconstruction rather than a documented fact.

[[Glasgow-1505|William Glasgow of Larne]] is currently retained as John's
uncertain father. Their ages are compatible, but no baptism, will, deed or
church entry names John as William's son, and no record bridges William's Larne
property to John's later Glenleslie household.

[[Glasgow-1367|Robert Glasgow]] headed Glenleslie household 23. He was born
about 1779, twelve years before John and Rose's reported marriage, and the census
does not call him John's son. He was therefore removed from this family and his
parents were left blank.<ref name="Robert1851">[https://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Dunaghy/Glenleslie/23/ 1851 Census of Ireland], Glenleslie household 23.</ref>

The older Ballyboggy records provide a competing local ancestry. The 1740
Protestant Householders' Return records '''Joh Glask''' in Dunaghy, and a
three-life lease identifies [[Glasgow-3959|John Glasgow]] and James Wier as
joint Ballyboggy tenants.<ref name="Householders1740">[https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/1740religiousreturnsulstername.pdf Bill MacAfee, ''Protestant Householders' Returns, 1740''], record 2107, Joh Glask, Dunaghy.</ref><ref name="BallyboggyLease1740">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D2223/15/56, Ballyboggy title-deed bundle, lease of 1740; [https://apps.proni.gov.uk/eCatNI_IE/SearchPage.aspx PRONI eCatalogue].</ref> He was an adult before this John was born and is therefore a plausible father, grandfather or collateral relative. The lease description does not name his children.

James Glasgow and James Glasgow junior were among the Ballyboggy tithe
occupiers in 1825. Ballyboggy adjoins Glenleslie, and the same records repeatedly
contain Alexander and Wier households.<ref name="Tithe1825">[https://www.billmacafee.com/182030stithe/tithenorthantrimrevisionorder.pdf Bill MacAfee, ''North Antrim Tithe Applotment Books arranged in document order''], Dunaghy parish, Ballyboggy, 1825.</ref> This is evidence for a long-running local tenant network, not a parent-child chain.

=== Y-DNA ===

A direct paternal descendant of [[Glasgow-951|Alexander Glasgow of Lisnagaver]]
and the tested line of [[Glasgow-2738|James J Glasgow]] share the confirmed
R-FT20271 branch and are genetic distance three at Y-111. The result supports a
shared male Glasgow ancestor, probably several generations earlier, but it does
not identify John as that ancestor or distinguish whether the Glenleslie and
Lisnagaver household heads were brothers, cousins or more distant relatives.<ref name="FTDNA">[https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview FamilyTreeDNA Glasgow Surname Project, public Y-DNA results], kits B580327 and 1002232.</ref>
