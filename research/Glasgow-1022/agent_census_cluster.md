# 1851 Glasgow cluster reconstruction

Updated: 2026-07-17

## Direct evidence

### Glenleslie, Dunaghy

The surviving 1851 return places the households in this enumeration order:

| House | Glasgow household | Material facts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | George Glasgow, 36 | Antrim-born linen weaver; married Sarah in 1837. |
| 21 | John Glasgow, 99, and Rose, 80 | Antrim-born farmer; the return says they married in **1791**. Their household includes children/deceased children and a **nephew John Glasgow, 40**, married in 1839. |
| 22 | Patrick Hamill | Intervening non-Glasgow household. |
| 23 | Robert Glasgow, 72, and Catherine, 68 | Antrim-born farmer; married in 1803. Daughter Margaret, niece Mary and grandchildren were present; sons John, 40, and Samuel, 30, were absent in America. |

Source: [1851 census transcription, Glenleslie households 20-23](https://durrushistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/1851irishcensusreturnscommadelimitedtextfilejul24_071.pdf), PDF pp. 102-103; Robert's [saved original page 1](../Glasgow-1367/sources/1851_census_glenleslie_house_23_page_1.pdf) and [page 2](../Glasgow-1367/sources/1851_census_glenleslie_house_23_page_2.pdf).

The nephew in John's house is **not** John `Glasgow-867`: the nephew was 40,
married in 1839 and physically enumerated at Glenleslie, while `Glasgow-867`
was 50, married Margaret in 1846 and was simultaneously head of house 34 at
Lisnagaver.

### Lisnagaver, Rasharkin

| House | Household | Material facts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 34 | John Glasgow, 50 | Antrim-born farmer; wife Margaret; **brother Samuel**, 60, Derry-born. |
| 35 | Alexander Glasgow, 40 | Antrim-born farmer; wife Mary and three children. |
| 36 | John Watters | Intervening non-Glasgow household. |
| 37 | Thomas Glasgow, 35 | Antrim-born farmer; wife Mary Ann and five daughters. |

Source: [1851 census transcription, Lisnagaver households 34-37](https://durrushistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/1851irishcensusreturnscommadelimitedtextfilejul24_071.pdf), PDF p. 183. John's [1846 original marriage](../Glasgow-867/sources/john_glasgow_margaret_garvin_marriage_1846.pdf) names his father as **John Glasgow, farmer**. Daniel Glasgow's original 1871 will independently proves that Thomas was Daniel's brother.

## What adjacency supports

- John and Samuel are proved brothers by the household relationship, not by
  adjacency.
- Alexander immediately follows their household; Thomas is two household
  numbers later. This makes a local collateral relationship plausible,
  especially given the rare surname, similar farming status and later land
  continuity.
- It does **not** prove they were cousins "at a minimum." Enumeration numbers
  are sequence identifiers, not statements of kinship or necessarily exact
  property adjacency. Unrelated same-surname households remain possible.
- Glenleslie is a separate townland and parish cluster. Robert's house 23 was
  separated from John senior's house 21 by the Hamill household. This is a
  useful association, not a father-son statement.

## Gortereghy caution

The c.1861-62 Griffith's Valuation index lists **Daniel Glasgow** and **Robert
Glasgow** at Gortereghy, and Alexander, John and Thomas at Lisnagaver. This is
strong evidence for two localized Glasgow clusters, but the index supplies no
ages, spouses or relationships. It has not yet proved that the Gortereghy
Robert is `Glasgow-1367`, last securely identified at Glenleslie in 1851.
Daniel also held Kilrea/Claragh valuation entries, so a land entry need not be
his sole residence.

Sources: [Rasharkin Griffith's index](https://www.failteromhat.com/griffiths/antrim/rasharkin.htm) and [Macafee North/Mid Antrim transcription](https://www.billmacafee.com/1860griffiths/northmidantrimgriffiths.pdf).

## Evidence-based tentative structure

The least-conflicted provisional reconstruction is:

1. **Unknown John Glasgow, farmer** is the proved/strongly inferred father of
   John `Glasgow-867` and Samuel `Glasgow-2908`.
2. John `Glasgow-1022` is a **candidate** for that father because he was an
   older Antrim farmer named John and his reported 1791 marriage fits Samuel's
   approximate birth. No record connects him to Lisnagaver, however, and the
   different reported birth counties require explanation.
3. Daniel `Glasgow-3896` and Thomas `Glasgow-987` form a proved sibling pair.
   Alexander `Glasgow-951`, their near neighbour and near contemporary, is a
   reasonable **possible brother**, not proved. The late Alexander/Rosanna
   parent tradition remains only a hypothesis.
4. Robert `Glasgow-1367` should remain **parentless** rather than be attached
   to `Glasgow-1022` as a son. Robert's census-implied birth about 1779 precedes
   John's explicitly reported first marriage in 1791 by twelve years and
   cannot make Rose his mother. A paternal link would require an undocumented
   nonmarital birth, a major age error, or an error in the marriage-year field.
5. Robert may be recorded in notes as a **probable member of the wider
   Glenleslie-Gortereghy Glasgow network**, with possible relationships ranging
   from son/half-son of John senior to brother, uncle or cousin of Daniel. The
   current evidence cannot rank those relationships reliably.

## Recommended WikiTree handling

- Keep John `Glasgow-867` and Samuel `Glasgow-2908` attached to
  `Glasgow-1022` only if the relationship is marked **uncertain** and the
  explicit caveat is retained that the marriage proves merely a father named
  John Glasgow, farmer.
- Do **not** attach Robert `Glasgow-1367` to `Glasgow-1022` yet. Record the
  cluster hypothesis in research notes instead.
- Preserve Daniel-Thomas as siblings. If a tentative connection is needed,
  group younger Alexander with Daniel and Thomas as a possible sibling, rather
  than forcing Robert into their parent generation.
