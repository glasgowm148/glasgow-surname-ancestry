# Gortereghy and Lisnagaver Glasgow cluster audit

Last updated: 2026-07-17

## Answer

The Robert Glasgow listed with Daniel Glasgow at Gortereghy in Griffith's
Valuation should **not presently be identified as Robert `Glasgow-1367` of
Glenleslie**. A younger Robert, `Glasgow-938`, is the materially better
candidate. He was Alexander `Glasgow-951`'s ten-year-old son at Lisnagaver in
1851 and his original 1866 marriage records him as a 25-year-old farmer of
Lisnagaver, son of Alexander Glasgow, farmer. His bride Jane Graham was of
Gortereghy. This gives that younger man a direct, independently identified link
to both adjoining townlands at the right date.

Daniel at Gortereghy is probably Daniel `Glasgow-3896`, but the printed
valuation names no age, occupation or relative, so this is a strong identity
inference rather than a direct proof.

## Relationship assessment

### Gortereghy/Lisnagaver

The most coherent tentative reconstruction is:

- Daniel `Glasgow-3896` and Thomas `Glasgow-987`: **proved brothers**.
- Alexander `Glasgow-951` and Thomas: **probable brothers**, based on the
  same small farming cluster, similar ages, movements between Gortereghy and
  Lisnagaver, and later family testimony; not directly proved.
- Robert `Glasgow-938`: **proved son of Alexander** and the best candidate for
  the Griffith Gortereghy Robert.
- Therefore the Griffith pairing is most likely Daniel with his probable
  nephew Robert, not Daniel with Robert `Glasgow-1367`.
- John `Glasgow-867` and Samuel `Glasgow-2908`: proved brothers within the
  Lisnagaver cluster. They were probably relatives of Alexander and Thomas,
  but even `cousin` is not a proved minimum; surname and townland clustering
  cannot distinguish first cousins from more distant paternal kin.

### Robert `Glasgow-1367` and John `Glasgow-1022`

Robert belongs in the **Glenleslie** problem, not in the Gortereghy proof
chain. If WikiTree requires a tentative placement, an uncertain son of John
`Glasgow-1022` by an unidentified pre-1791 partner is chronologically possible:
John was about 27-28 when Robert was born, and the men later farmed in the same
small townland. It is not demonstrated. Robert was born about twelve years
before John's recorded 1791 marriage to Rose, so Rose cannot safely be attached
as Robert's mother. A much younger brother, nephew, or cousin of John remains
plausible.

**Recommendation:** do not use the Griffith Gortereghy entry to attach
`Glasgow-1367` to John. If the user wants a working tree, attach Robert to John
only as an explicitly uncertain/hypothesised father relationship, leave the
mother blank, and state that co-residence plus generational spacing are the
entire basis.

## Remaining decisive record

The Gortereghy and Lisnagaver valuation-revision books should show the precise
plot occupier succession. A transfer from Daniel to Thomas/Daniel Neely, or from
Alexander to the younger Robert, could upgrade these inferences. Until that
book is inspected, neither Griffith Robert nor Griffith Daniel is identified
by a direct relationship statement.
