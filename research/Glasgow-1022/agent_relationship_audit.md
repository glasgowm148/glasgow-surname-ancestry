# Relationship audit: Glasgow-1022 and the Rasharkin cluster

> **Superseded where inconsistent (5 September 2026):** Alexander-951's
> parents remain unknown and his relationship to Thomas/Daniel is possible
> brother or cousin, not probable brotherhood.

Date: 2026-07-17

## Recommended tentative reconstruction

The most coherent working tree is **two collateral branches**, not one large
undifferentiated sibling group:

```text
unknown earlier Glasgow ancestor(s)
|
+-- John Glasgow (Glasgow-1022), farmer, born about 1752
|   +-- Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1367), born about 1779 [uncertain son]
|   +-- Samuel Glasgow (Glasgow-2908), born about 1790 [uncertain son]
|   +-- John Glasgow (Glasgow-867), born about 1801 [uncertain son]
|
+-- older Alexander Glasgow (Glasgow-3904) [relationship to John unknown]
    +-- Daniel Glasgow (Glasgow-3896), born about 1796 [uncertain son]
    +-- Alexander Glasgow (Glasgow-951), born about 1811 [probable son]
    +-- Thomas Glasgow (Glasgow-987), born about 1815 [uncertain son]
```

John `Glasgow-1022` and the older Alexander should be treated as probable close
paternal relatives, possibly brothers or first cousins. There is not enough
evidence to choose the degree. This arrangement makes younger John, Alexander
and Thomas cousins or close cousins, matching their repeated Lisnagaver cluster,
and makes Robert and Daniel cousins or close cousins at Gortereghy if the
Griffith entries are correctly identified.

## Robert's best placement

**Attach Robert `Glasgow-1367` to John `Glasgow-1022` as an uncertain father
relationship, but do not attach Rose Alexander as Robert's mother.**

Evidence in favour:

- In 1851 John was a 99-year-old Antrim-born farmer in Glenleslie household 21;
  Robert was a 72-year-old Antrim-born farmer in Glenleslie household 23. Only
  the Patrick Hamill household 22 separated them in enumeration order.
- Their 27-year age gap is normal for father and son.
- Robert has no contradictory record naming another father.
- Robert's household included a niece, showing that at least one of his siblings
  or Catherine's siblings also belonged to the local network.

Evidence against / qualification:

- The 1851 return says John and Rose married in **1791**, whereas Robert was born
  about **1779** and Robert and Catherine married in 1803. Robert therefore
  cannot be treated as a son of that marriage. If John was his father, Robert
  came from an earlier unidentified relationship or was born outside marriage.
- The census never calls Robert John's son; enumeration order is cluster
  evidence, not a relationship statement.
- Robert's proposed Coltrim (1827/1831) and Lisnagaver (1834) identities are not
  directly linked to his certain Glenleslie household.

Assessment: **best available placement, moderate confidence as father, no
identified mother**.

## John and Samuel

- [John's original 1846 marriage](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view/?record_id=cima-3156063)
  names his father as **John Glasgow, farmer**.
- The [1851 Lisnagaver census](https://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Rasharkin/Lisnagaver/34/)
  directly calls Samuel John's brother.
- `Glasgow-1022` was named John and was a farmer; he married Rose in 1791. John's
  circa-1801 birth fits this marriage. Samuel's civil death age implies birth
  about 1789/90, one or two years before it, but an elderly age can readily be
  approximate.
- No record links the Lisnagaver brothers to Glenleslie or explicitly identifies
  their father with `Glasgow-1022`.

Assessment: keeping both as **uncertain sons of `Glasgow-1022` is reasonable**.
Rose may be left as uncertain mother for John; Samuel's mother is less secure
because of the birth/marriage chronology.

## Daniel, Thomas and Alexander

- Daniel's original 1871 will directly calls Thomas his late brother. This is
  the strongest relationship evidence in this cluster.
- Daniel Neely Glasgow's 1888 marriage names his father as Thomas Glasgow,
  farmer, and places the family at Lisnagaver, securely identifying Daniel's
  will-proved brother with `Glasgow-987`.
- In 1851 John/Samuel headed Lisnagaver household 34, younger Alexander headed
  35, an unrelated Watters household was 36, and Thomas headed 37. They were a
  tight townland cluster, but not literally three adjoining houses.
- Griffith's Valuation (1861-62) records Alexander, John and Thomas at
  Lisnagaver, and Daniel and Robert at adjoining Gortereghy. The name-only
  transcription does not itself identify the two latter occupiers with
  `Glasgow-3896` and `Glasgow-1367`.
- Younger Alexander is independently recorded at Gortereagh in 1837-38 and at
  Lisnagaver by 1843, demonstrating movement between the two townlands within
  this Glasgow cluster.
- A 2007 descendant account names Alexander and Rosanna as Thomas's parents.
  This is late evidence, but combined with Daniel's direct brother statement it
  makes Daniel a candidate son of the same older Alexander, not a son of John.

Chronology correction: if `Glasgow-3904` fathered Daniel about 1796, his current
estimated birth about 1790 is impossible and must be changed to **before about
1775**. Rosanna, presently estimated born about 1790, cannot be Daniel's mother;
Daniel and Thomas may have been paternal half-brothers, or one of the estimated
dates/traditional parent claims is wrong.

Assessment: keep Daniel and Thomas together as proved brothers. Tentatively put
both under older Alexander only if the father links are marked uncertain; do not
name Daniel's mother. Younger Alexander is a probable third member of this
sibship, but no record calls him their brother.

## Sources and identity cautions

- [Detailed 1851 census transcription](https://durrushistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/1851irishcensusreturnscommadelimitedtextfilejul24_071.pdf): John/Rose marriage 1791; John household 21; Robert household 23; Lisnagaver households 34-37.
- [Griffith's Valuation, Rasharkin](https://www.failteromhat.com/griffiths/antrim/rasharkin.htm): name-only entries for Daniel and Robert at Gortereghy and Alexander, John and Thomas at Lisnagaver.
- PRONI revision-book catalogue: Gortereghy is covered by `VAL/12/B/4/17A-E`, beginning in 1864. The revisions remain the best way to distinguish the older and younger Robert namesakes.
- Do not use the Gortereghy Robert entry as proof of `Glasgow-1367`: Robert
  `Glasgow-938`, born about 1840/41, is another viable occupier and is later
  associated with Gortereghy.
- Do not call the Lisnagaver men a minimum of first cousins as a proved fact.
  The records support **probable close kin**; the proposed cousin structure is
  the best current reconstruction.
