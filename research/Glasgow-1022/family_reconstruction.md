## Where each Robert goes

### Robert `Glasgow-1367`, born about 1779

This is the Glenleslie Robert. He may be retained as an **uncertain son** of
John `Glasgow-1022`, with no mother attached, if a connected working tree is
preferred. The entire case is the same small townland in 1851, matching Antrim
birthplaces and farming status, and a normal 27-year generational gap.

The objections are substantial: Robert was born about twelve years before John
and Rose's reported 1791 marriage, the census does not call him a son, and an
unrelated household intervenes in the enumeration. Parentless is the more
conservative evidence tree; uncertain son is the best connected hypothesis.

The 1834 tithe transcription places an Alexander Glasgow and a Robert Glasgow
at Lisnagaver. That makes the older Robert a possible earlier member of the
same wider cluster, but the entry has no age, spouse, plot succession, or
relationship and cannot identify him as either John's son or Alexander's kin.

### Robert `Glasgow-938`, born 1840

This is the best Gortereghy Robert. The 1851 census proves him a son of younger
Alexander at Lisnagaver. His 1866 marriage independently calls him a Lisnagaver
farmer and son of Alexander, while his bride lived at Gortereghy. This is a much
stronger identity chain than moving the elderly Glenleslie Robert there.

If the Griffith Daniel is Daniel `Glasgow-3896`, the two Gortereghy occupiers
were probably **uncle Daniel and nephew Robert**, because Daniel was Thomas's
proved brother and younger Alexander was Thomas's probable brother.

PRONI revision books `VAL/12/B/4/17A` and `17B` materially strengthen this
identification. The 1864 book records Robert Glasgow in the separate house at
map reference `18c`, and the 1865-84 book carries him forward there. A
25-year-old farmer whose 1866 marriage links Lisnagaver and Gortereghy fits this
sequence far better than an 86-year-old Glenleslie man. The adjoining `18b`
house belonged to Daniel McCochrane, not Daniel Glasgow. Griffith's name index
places Daniel Glasgow somewhere in Gortereghy in 1862, but his map reference has
not been verified; neither source states a family relationship.

## What the 1851 order means

- Glenleslie: George Glasgow 20, John `1022` 21, Hamill 22, Robert `1367` 23.
- Lisnagaver: John/Samuel 34, Alexander 35, Watters 36, Thomas 37.

These are enumeration sequences, not proof that each numbered household shared
a property boundary. They strongly support local Glasgow clusters, particularly
when combined with land and church records, but they do not prove that the
Lisnagaver men were "cousins at a minimum." They could have been brothers,
uncle and nephew, cousins of several degrees, or related through an earlier
common ancestor.

## Chronology corrections

- John and Rose's first-marriage year is 1791, not 1839. The latter belongs to
  the nephew John in their household.
- If older Alexander `Glasgow-3904` fathered Daniel about 1796, Alexander must
  have been born by about 1775, not about 1790.
- Rosanna, currently estimated born about 1790, cannot be Daniel's mother unless
  that date is substantially wrong. Leave Daniel's mother blank.

## Direct answer

Keep older Robert `Glasgow-1367` attached to John `Glasgow-1022` only as an
**uncertain working son**, with no mother. Do not use Gortereghy as support for
that link. The Gortereghy Robert belongs provisionally with younger Alexander
`Glasgow-951` as his proved son `Glasgow-938`; he was probably Daniel's nephew.

Keep John `Glasgow-867` and Samuel `Glasgow-2908` as uncertain sons of
`Glasgow-1022`. Keep Daniel and Thomas as proved brothers, younger Alexander as
their probable brother, and the older Alexander as an uncertain father of that
three-man branch. Do not structurally connect the John and older-Alexander
branches until a relational source identifies their shared ancestor.
