### Collateral update, 13 August 2026

Two new original civil records extend the family without solving John's own
parentage.

- The 1851 mortality schedule directly identifies Andrew `Glasgow-1050` as
  John's son. Samuel John `Glasgow-1419`'s 1866 marriage names his father as
  **Andrew Glasgow, labourer**. The age and County Antrim context make Andrew
  `Glasgow-1050` the strongest match, but the marriage omits the father's
  residence, so the father-son identity remains probable rather than certain.
- John `Glasgow-2407`, later farmer of Cross, named his father as **John
  Glasgow, farmer** in his original 1865 marriage. He is not the 1851 nephew:
  the nephew was married from 1839, whereas the 1865 groom was explicitly a
  bachelor. John-1022 is a possible father, but John `Glasgow-1657` of Cross is
  a geographically stronger competing candidate. Leave John-2407 parentless.

These results distinguish the married nephew, Robert's son John in America, the
Lisnagaver John/Samuel brother pair, and the Cross farmer as separate research
lines.

The newly reviewed Kilwaughter material supplies no evidence that this John was
a son of Robert Glasgow-1143. The 17 May 1747 discipline entry names no woman or
child. The 1 April 1772 petition places John under Larne, while Robert Glasgow,
James Glasgow and Hugh Shannon occur consecutively under Kilwaughter. Remove any
claim that the discipline case establishes John's father; the structured link
to Robert-1143 remains unsupported.

The records support two related-looking but not yet connected branches.

- John `Glasgow-867` and Samuel `Glasgow-2908` are proved brothers. John's
  original 1846 marriage names their likely father as **John Glasgow, farmer**.
  A family gravestone places the younger John's birth about 1816/17, although
  his 1851 age implies about 1801. John `Glasgow-1022` is a possible identity
  for the father, but the later date would make him about sixty-five at the
  birth and removes the basis for calling him the clear leading candidate. Keep
  both relationships uncertain and leave their mother blank.
- Alexander `Glasgow-951` should not be attached as John's son from the Mary
  evidence. Mary Glasgow was 28 in 1871 and therefore cannot be John's
  granddaughter Maryan, who was already 17 in 1851.
- Robert `Glasgow-1367` is not the Robert at Gortereghy. Household adjacency at
  Glenleslie is insufficient to make him John's son; leave Robert parentless.
- Daniel `Glasgow-3896` and Thomas `Glasgow-987` are proved brothers. Younger
  Alexander `Glasgow-951` is a possible brother or cousin. The Gortereghy Robert
  was most likely Alexander's proved son Robert `Glasgow-938`; without the
  Alexander–Thomas brother link, an uncle-nephew relationship is not proved.
- The exact relationship between the John and older-Alexander branches remains
  unknown. They may have been brothers, making the Lisnagaver heads cousins, but
  father-son and more distant collateral relationships remain possible.

The fuller 1851 Glenleslie return also exposes three unresolved branches which
should be pursued rather than forced into existing children. Ann Glasgow, 18,
and Mary Ann/`Maryan` Glasgow, 17, were explicitly John's granddaughters. Jane
Grimes, 5, and James Grimes, nine months, were also his grandchildren, implying
an unidentified child in a Grimes household. A married John Glasgow aged about
40 was explicitly John's **nephew**, providing direct evidence that John-1022
had at least one sibling or sibling-equivalent relationship still to identify.

John `Glasgow-2056` is only a candidate for that nephew, not an identification.
His original 1850 marriage names his father as **Henry Glasgow, farmer**. He can
be the census nephew only if Henry is independently shown to be John-1022's
brother or equivalent relative.

The adjacent George household contains George's wife Sarah Alexander, while
John's household contains his wife Rose Alexander, an unrelated-status visitor
also named Sarah Alexander and John's brother-in-law William Alexander. This is
a Glasgow-Alexander local network, but the record does not state how either
additional Alexander was related to Rose. The earlier Ballyboggy records extend that
network geographically and chronologically, but do not yet prove John-1022's
parentage: John Glasgow co-leased Ballyboggy with James Wier in 1740, and James
Glasgow plus James Glasgow junior occur there among Alexander and Wier occupiers
in 1825. Ballyboggy directly adjoins Glenleslie.

The current attachment to [[Glasgow-1505|William Glasgow of Larne]] remains a
valid uncertain working hypothesis, but the 1740 [[Glasgow-3959|John Glasgow of
Ballyboggy]] is the geographically stronger father-or-grandfather candidate.
The two models should remain open: neither record names John-1022 as a child.

## Parentage and Y-DNA synthesis, 13 August 2026

No inspected record names John's father. The Y-DNA does, however, narrow the
kind of relationship worth pursuing. Alexander `Glasgow-951` (kit B580327) and
the James J Glasgow `Glasgow-2738` line (kit 1002232) are both confirmed
R-FT20271 Big Y lines. They are genetic distance 3 at Y-111, differ at four of
662 compared Big Y STRs, and share the derived DYS714=27 state. The local SAPP
model places their paternal branching node at about 1750, with a displayed 95%
interval of 1700-1750. That is a search window, not a proved date: the same
uncalibrated model places a known control relationship about a century too
recently.

This result proves that Alexander's direct paternal line and James J's tested
line share a male Glasgow ancestor. It does **not** identify John as that
ancestor. The kit reported for Robert `Glasgow-1143` has only marker testing,
is genetic distance 10 from Alexander at Y-111, and has no confirmed downstream
Big Y placement. Using the present WikiTree pedigree to make John a descendant
of Robert, then using that assumed pedigree to interpret the marker match, would
be circular.

The best documentary placement remains the Dunaghy-Kilconway tenant network.
John could only have fathered Robert `Glasgow-1367` or older Alexander
`Glasgow-3904` through an unidentified relationship predating his reported 1791
marriage to Rose. A brother/cousin relationship among the older household heads
requires fewer assumptions than making both men John's sons. Leave John's own
parents unknown and keep the broader paternal relationship as a research
cluster rather than a structured edge.

A new candidate occurrence was found in the 1803 North Antrim agricultural
census: **John Glesco** at Carnbuck, Loughguile parish. He is the only entry in
the covered parishes that the compiler standardised as Glasgow rather than
Glass. John `Glasgow-1022`, then about 52, is age-compatible, but the census
gives no age, spouse, occupation or relation and Carnbuck is not Glenleslie.
The name should remain a candidate occurrence, not be folded into his biography
as fact.

Exact-name, spelling-variant, residence and townland searches of the volunteer
Registry of Deeds index found no deed naming John or a relative at Glenleslie,
Lisnagaver or Carnbuck. The negative is bounded by the incomplete index and
does not establish that no sublease or unindexed deed existed.

The complete published North Antrim portion of the 1796 Flaxgrowers' List has
no Glasgow, Glasgo or Glask entry. This is a bounded negative only: it records
premium recipients rather than all householders and cannot show that the family
had left Dunaghy.<ref>[https://www.billmacafee.com/1796flaxgrowerslist/1796flaxgrowersantrim.pdf Bill MacAfee, ''Flaxgrowers' List for the Parishes in North Antrim, 1796''].</ref>

## Identity of the resident nephew John Glasgow

The 1851 census of John Glasgow's Glenleslie household includes a married nephew, John Glasgow, aged 40, born about 1810–11, married in 1839 and working as a weaver. Robert Glasgow's nearby household separately records a forty-year-old son John as absent in '''America'''. The census therefore distinguishes the resident nephew from Robert's son. He also remains distinct from John Glasgow of Lisnagaver ([[Glasgow-867]]), who headed his own household in 1851 and died in 1895.

| Glasgow-3474 | **Strongest current candidate** | The original civil register identifies him as a married farmer of **Lisnagaver**, aged 78, who died on 24 June 1885; his son James Glasgow of Lisnagaver reported the death. The age is reasonably compatible with the 1851 census and the immediate locality fits. He was not [[Glasgow-867]], whose family gravestone records death in 1895. No record yet directly calls the 1885 man Robert's son, so this remains probable rather than proved. |
| Glasgow-3140 | **Not the nephew; probably conflated** | Its parent attachment to Robert and Catherine corresponds to the son recorded in America in 1851, not the resident nephew. Its Elizabeth Fee and Samuel family belongs to an Armagh reconstruction: Samuel's published biography places his origin in County Armagh. A widowed farmer John Glasgow died at Killyreavy, County Armagh, on 27 December 1882, aged 66; his son-in-law James Campbell reported the death. Campbell had married Martha Jane Glasgow, daughter of John Glasgow, farmer of Killyreavy, in 1873. The Armagh family may therefore represent another John improperly combined with Robert's emigrant son. |
| Glasgow-2407 | **Ruled out** | His original 1865 marriage to Rachel Grey describes him as a bachelor. He therefore cannot be the John who reported that he had married in 1839. |

The resident nephew remains unidentified. Glasgow-3474 is the strongest of the
three proposed candidates because he was a married, age-compatible Lisnagaver
farmer, but no record yet explicitly connects him to John-1022 or Glenleslie.
Glasgow-2407 is ruled out, and Glasgow-3140 cannot be the nephew if its attachment
to Robert reflects the man explicitly recorded in America in 1851.
