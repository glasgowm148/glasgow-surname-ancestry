The working conclusion is that `Glasgow-1024` is a composite of a late father tradition, a 1995 pedigree, early Augusta/Cecil records, an 1804 Pennsylvania naturalisation applicant and the unrelated 1831 Cookstown burial.

No located record rules out Glasgow-2988 and Glasgow-1024 being the same person. The case is indirect: the estimated generation fits, Corvanaghan is roughly nine miles from Moneymore, and no post-1766 Irish record has been identified for the Corvanaghan householder. The 1826 Kildress tithe list names David and John Glasgow at Muntober, John at Whitehill and William at Oritor, but no Glasgow occupier at Corvanaghan. This is consistent with departure or extinction of that holding, but sixty years is too long an interval for it to prove migration.

The obstacle is that Glasgow-2988 itself is not identified by the Corvanaghan return. That occurrence is already represented by Glasgow-3137, while two other men called James Glasgo were householders at Drumearn in adjacent Derryloran in 1766. The 1770 Turk compilation and 1804 Pennsylvania naturalisation index do not name an Irish townland, relatives or associates.

**Current conclusion:** Glasgow-2988 is a credible possible duplicate of Glasgow-1024, and Glasgow-3137 is the strongest record-based candidate for both. None of the three identities is proved to be the same man.

## Tyrone record reassessment - 20 August 2026

The 1759 will-index place is **Drumcarn**, not Drumearn. It therefore does not
uniquely identify the linked father `Glasgow-1025`. It could chronologically
belong to this James after the births of Robert and Arthur, which would fit the
later tradition that Arthur was a ward by 1766, but the index gives no age,
wife, children or executors. This remains a hypothesis, not a death assignment.

`Glasgow-2988` and `Glasgow-3137` duplicate the same documented person: the
1766 Corvanaghan householder. Merging those profiles removes a catalogue
duplicate but does not prove that the resulting person was James of Moneymore.

## Sibling and Mitchell-family reassessment — 20 August 2026

Faris's catalogue summary says Robert emigrated with three brothers and one sister. Since Robert, Arthur and Joseph are the three independently established brothers in the present reconstruction, one further brother is expected.

A 2024 Adams County family-history article identifies John Glasgow of Peters Township, Pennsylvania, who died in 1779, as that brother and states that the surviving brothers Joseph, Robert and Arthur moved to Virginia after John's death. Joseph was a witness to John's 1779 will. The article is secondary and the witness relationship alone does not prove kinship, but together they provide a substantially better explanation of Faris's fourth brother than the unsupported Glasgow-3305 profile.

- [Faris catalogue summary](https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567)
- [Adams County family-history account](https://www.peoplesdefender.com/2024/03/29/our-ancestors-of-adams-county-3/)
- [Cumberland County will index](https://www.sampubco.com/wills/pa/pacumberland01.htm)

The Cookstown Mitchell family represented by Glasgow-1970 is a serious same-generation comparison. The 1879 *History of Hillsdale County* says James Glasgow and his Mitchell wife moved from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750, settled near Cookstown, and had a son John whose descendants later went to Michigan. The narrative is accurately reflected by Glasgow-1970 -> Glasgow-1968 -> Glasgow-1969 and Glasgow-1967.

[History of Hillsdale County, page 263](https://archive.org/details/bad0928.0001.001.umich.edu/page/263/mode/1up)

Glasgow-1970 cannot be ruled out by geography or name. A merger is not presently justified, however. The account places his son John and John's family near Cookstown into the nineteenth century, while the emigrant-family traditions place Arthur's mother and elder brothers in America by 1766 or Robert there by 1769. Identifying the two Jameses would require additional migration, remarriage or a significant error in one of those traditions.

Glasgow-2988 also remains unresolved. His only concrete local occurrence is the 1766 Corvanaghan householder, and no post-1766 record has yet been identified. That leaves him chronologically compatible with migration, but there were several contemporary James Glasgows in Derryloran and the census entry supplies no relatives.

### Revised candidate ranking

1. The father described by Faris as James Glasgow of Moneymore remains an unidentified traditional person.
2. Glasgow-2988 cannot be ruled out and is the strongest existing-profile candidate for a man who disappears from the located Irish record after 1766.
3. Glasgow-1970 is a documented Cookstown-family progenitor but is probably distinct unless the conflicting migration chronology can be resolved.
4. The early Augusta/Cecil County James is a separate candidate whose American presence before Robert's Irish birth requires explanation.
5. Glasgow-3305 is not supported as a sibling; John of Peters Township now better fits the expected fourth brother.

No merge among Glasgow-1024, Glasgow-2988 and Glasgow-1970 is presently evidence-safe.
