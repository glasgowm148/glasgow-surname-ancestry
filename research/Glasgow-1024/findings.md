# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1024)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1024)

> **Draft only.** Make no live WikiTree changes while research on [Glasgow-3305](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3305) is active.

## Current-profile material preserved

The replacement biography now retains the complete earlier Immigration and Naturalization section rather than reducing it to candidate-table entries. It includes the full Ancestry/Turk 1770 quotation, FamilySearch naturalization identifier KXX1-HYS, the Diane Scholten descent statement, identity links to Glasgow-2988 and Glasgow-2989, and the full Big Y kit 959947 pedigree audit.

These items are preserved with corrected evidence language. The Turk entry is a compiled index whose own annotation permits arrival, marriage or first mention; the 1804 record establishes a same-name naturalization applicant but does not identify him as Robert's father. The Y-DNA kit most plausibly descends through Robert and is not an independent test of Robert's father.

## Provenance and locality update — 20 August 2026

The death about 1831 is not merely uncertain: it is unsupported and should be removed. The located pedigree trail originally supplied no death date.

| Date | Pedigree development |
|---|---|
| 1990 | Faris calls Robert a son of “James Glasgow of Moneymore”; the accessible summary supplies no father vital dates. |
| 1995 | World Family Tree 6075 supplies James c.1722, Mary c.1727, marriage c.1748 and three sons, but no death for either parent. |
| 2010 | Earliest recovered WeRelate copy still has no death event. |
| Later | Unsourced 1803 and then c.1831 deaths appear in online trees. |

The strongest specific source of 1831 contamination is [Glasgow-4024](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4024), the Cookstown man buried on 13 March 1831 aged 40. This cannot prove how the error arose, but it proves that the nearby exact-name burial belongs to another generation.

The 1766 return for Moneymore and Ballygrooby contains no Glasgow or Harkness household head. This is qualified negative evidence, because the return does not name every resident, but it conflicts with the asserted established household of James, Mary and several children. Contemporary surname geography is stronger around Derryloran, Drumearn and Cookstown and does not identify which local James, if any, was Robert's father.

The working conclusion is that `Glasgow-1024` is a composite of a late father tradition, a 1995 pedigree, early Augusta/Cecil records, an 1804 Pennsylvania naturalisation applicant and the unrelated 1831 Cookstown burial.

## Conclusion

This profile conflates an unproved **James Glasgow of Moneymore** with several same-name men and records. The defensible core is David Faris's secondary statement that [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-12)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-12) was a son of “James Glasgow of Moneymore.” Robert's pension proves Robert's County Derry birth and 1769 migration but does not name his father.

The 1831 death should be removed. St Luran's register records a different [James Glasgow of Cookstown (Glasgow-4024)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4024), buried 13 March 1831 aged 40. He was created as an unconnected profile on 20 August 2026.

## Candidate comparison: James of Moneymore

| [Glasgow-2989](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2989) | Estimated c.1740 Derryloran profile, attached to Glasgow-2988 | Tree relationship only; could instead represent either 1766 Drumearn householder or another man |
| [Glasgow-3444](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3444) and [Glasgow-3986](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3986) | Born 1776 Cookstown and 1773 Larne respectively | Later generation; excluded as Robert's father |
| Early Augusta/Cecil James | American deed references from 1747–1753 | Cannot be Robert's father without evidence of return travel because Robert said he was born in Ireland in 1749 |

No located record rules out Glasgow-2988 and Glasgow-1024 being the same person. The case is indirect: the estimated generation fits, Corvanaghan is roughly nine miles from Moneymore, and no post-1766 Irish record has been identified for the Corvanaghan householder. The 1826 Kildress tithe list names David and John Glasgow at Muntober, John at Whitehill and William at Oritor, but no Glasgow occupier at Corvanaghan. This is consistent with departure or extinction of that holding, but sixty years is too long an interval for it to prove migration.

The obstacle is that Glasgow-2988 itself is not identified by the Corvanaghan return. That occurrence is already represented by Glasgow-3137, while two other men called James Glasgo were householders at Drumearn in adjacent Derryloran in 1766. The 1770 Turk compilation and 1804 Pennsylvania naturalisation index do not name an Irish townland, relatives or associates.

**Current conclusion:** Glasgow-2988 is a credible possible duplicate of Glasgow-1024, and Glasgow-3137 is the strongest record-based candidate for both. None of the three identities is proved to be the same man.

## Tyrone record reassessment - 20 August 2026

The 1759 will-index place is **Drumcarn**, not Drumearn. It therefore does not
uniquely identify the linked father `Glasgow-1025`. It could chronologically
belong to this James after the births of Robert and Arthur, which would fit the
later tradition that Arthur was a ward by 1766, but the index gives no age,
wife, children or executors. This remains a hypothesis, not a death assignment.

`Glasgow-2988` and `Glasgow-3137` duplicate the same documented person: the
1766 Corvanaghan householder. Merging those profiles removes a catalogue
duplicate but does not prove that the resulting person was James of Moneymore.

A genuine Harkness-Glasgow marriage occurs in the same regional network. The
1892 biography of [[Harkness-2201|William Glasgow Harkness]] names his parents as
[[Harkness-2200|James Harkness of Oritor]] and [[Glasgow-4026|Margaret Glasgow of
Moneymore]]. This is positive FAN-network
evidence that the two families intermarried and modestly increases the
plausibility of another Glasgow-Harkness marriage. It does not identify Mary
Harkness or directly prove that she was the wife of this James.

The most promising proof records are PRONI `T1959/1-8`, `D1413/3a-b` and
`T1762`, followed by the Drapers' estate papers `D3632`. See the [candidate
matrix](../../surname-research/james-glasgow-tyrone-moneymore-candidate-matrix.md).

## Sibling and Mitchell-family reassessment — 20 August 2026

Faris's catalogue summary says Robert emigrated with three brothers and one sister. Since Robert, Arthur and Joseph are the three independently established brothers in the present reconstruction, one further brother is expected.

A 2024 Adams County family-history article identifies John Glasgow of Peters Township, Pennsylvania, who died in 1779, as that brother and states that the surviving brothers Joseph, Robert and Arthur moved to Virginia after John's death. Joseph was a witness to John's 1779 will. The article is secondary and the witness relationship alone does not prove kinship, but together they provide a substantially better explanation of Faris's fourth brother than the unsupported Glasgow-3305 profile.

- [Faris catalogue summary](https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567)
- [Adams County family-history account](https://www.peoplesdefender.com/2024/03/29/our-ancestors-of-adams-county-3/)
- [Cumberland County will index](https://www.sampubco.com/wills/pa/pacumberland01.htm)

The Cookstown Mitchell family represented by Glasgow-1970 is a serious same-generation comparison. The 1879 *History of Hillsdale County* says James Glasgow and his Mitchell wife moved from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750, settled near Cookstown, and had a son John whose descendants later went to Michigan. The narrative is accurately reflected by Glasgow-1970 -> Glasgow-1968 -> Glasgow-1969 and Glasgow-1967.

[History of Hillsdale County, page 263](https://archive.org/details/bad0928.0001.001.umich.edu/page/263/mode/1up)

Glasgow-1970 cannot be ruled out by geography or name. A merger is not presently justified, however. The account places his son John and John's family near Cookstown into the nineteenth century, while the emigrant-family traditions place Arthur's mother and elder brothers in America by 1766 or Robert there by 1769. Identifying the two Jameses would require additional migration, remarriage or a significant error in one of those traditions.

Glasgow-2988 also remains unresolved. His only concrete local occurrence is the 1766 Corvanaghan householder, and no post-1766 record has yet been identified. That leaves him chronologically compatible with migration, but there were several contemporary James Glasgows in Derryloran and the census entry supplies no relatives.

### Revised candidate ranking

1. The father described by Faris as James Glasgow of Moneymore remains an unidentified traditional person.
2. Glasgow-2988 cannot be ruled out and is the strongest existing-profile candidate for a man who disappears from the located Irish record after 1766.
3. Glasgow-1970 is a documented Cookstown-family progenitor but is probably distinct unless the conflicting migration chronology can be resolved.
4. The early Augusta/Cecil County James is a separate candidate whose American presence before Robert's Irish birth requires explanation.
5. Glasgow-3305 is not supported as a sibling; John of Peters Township now better fits the expected fourth brother.

No merge among Glasgow-1024, Glasgow-2988 and Glasgow-1970 is presently evidence-safe.

## FamilySearch full-text deed pass - 21 August 2026

Exact-name searches covered James Glasgow, James Glasgo, James Glascow, Mary
Harkness and Mary Glasgow in eighteenth-century Moneymore, Derryloran,
Cookstown and Kilrea. No record naming Mary Harkness or Mary Glasgow was found
in those localities.

The James results were inspected rather than assigned by name alone. They
belong to already-separated men in Larne and the Aghadonvane/Leitrim-Donegal
network, including the Larne ropemaker and farmer and James of Aghadonvane.
Examples include the [1766 Larne ropemaker deed](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-J9XP-J), the [1776 Aghadonvane mortgage](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-N9YN-4), the [1780 Aghadonvane transaction](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-P9LW-G), and the [1778 Larne farmer affidavit](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-9WVP-C). None names Moneymore, Derryloran, Cookstown, Kilrea, Mary Harkness, or the emigrant sibling group. No result can therefore be assigned to Glasgow-1024.
