# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1024), born about 1721, Moneymore, County Londonderry, Ireland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1024)

## Current conclusion

The profile's structured 1831 Moneymore death conflicts with its own biography,
which says James and Mary emigrated to North America about 1770 and that James
was naturalised there in 1804. The local-continuity claim and attached father
and children require source-level reconstruction.

David Faris's catalogue summary independently describes Robert Glasgow
(1749-1839) as a son of **James Glasgow of Moneymore**, but supplies no dates,
wife or migration for the father. Ellen Glasgow's later account says Arthur was
a ward who emigrated with his mother and elder brothers in 1766, implying that
Arthur's father was already dead or legally absent. Those statements do not
establish that the father was this live profile. They instead increase the risk
that Glasgow-1024 combines the father of the emigrant siblings with a different
James who emigrated and was naturalised in 1804.

Newly recovered kinship evidence proves that
[Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-12)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-12),
[Arthur Glasgow (Glasgow-649)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-649) and
[Joseph Glasgow (Glasgow-1543)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1543)
were brothers. It does **not** prove that this profile was their father. A
published transcription of a 6 November 1806 family letter supplies the
Robert–Arthur–Joseph kin terms, and Joseph's 1831 will independently calls
Arthur his deceased brother. Neither document names their parents.

### Y-DNA pedigree audit

Big Y kit `959947`, previously treated as a possible independent line from the
James generation, most plausibly returns to Robert `Glasgow-12` through Robert's
son William `Glasgow-563` and the chain `Glasgow-1829` → `Glasgow-556` →
`Glasgow-1851` → `Glasgow-1877`. The final link to the private tester is not
publicly exposed. The kit therefore tests a probable **descendant of Robert**;
it does not independently prove Robert's father.

Its reported earliest-ancestor label “James Glasgow” is consistent with Faris's
later statement that Robert's father was James of Moneymore, but it remains a
copied pedigree label. It cannot resolve whether that man is the conflicted
WikiTree profile `Glasgow-1024`.

## Parentage-candidate assessment — Unknown Glasgow

[Unknown Glasgow (Glasgow-3905)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3905)
is a structural placeholder for [Adam Glasgow's (Glasgow-3902)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3902)
unidentified father, not a documented individual. The prior suggestion that
James-1024 fathered this placeholder is unsupported and should not be attached.

The apparent bridge through [Arthur Glasgow (Glasgow-649)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-649)
was circular: Arthur was only recorded in the project register as a *possible*
brother of the placeholder, while Arthur's own attachment to James-1024 is
itself derivative. Robert Glasgow's pension declaration proves County Derry
birth and 1769 migration but names no father or siblings. Faris's later summary
supports a reported father named James of Moneymore for Robert, but supplies no
evidence that this was James-1024 and no child who can be identified as Adam's
father.

James-1024's current dates and family are also internally unstable: a 1721
birth is incompatible with attached son Joseph's estimated 1735 birth, and the
biography's Pennsylvania migration/naturalisation narrative conflicts with the
structured Moneymore death. These conflicts do not disprove the existence of a
different, older James of Moneymore; they prevent this profile from serving as
a proved grandfather of Adam-3902.
