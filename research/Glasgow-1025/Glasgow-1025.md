## Suggested corrections

Any claim that James's attached descendants are genetically proved should be
narrowed. Kit `200475` is publicly R-FT20271, but its earliest reported ancestor
is Robert Glasgow (1749–1836, Scotland), and the father-son chain from that man
to James-1025 has not been proved from original records.

## Paste-ready research note

```text
== Research Notes: Y-DNA and branch placement ==

A public Big Y result places a tested line currently connected to James on
WikiTree at haplogroup R-FT20271. A separately tested direct male-line
descendant of [[Glasgow-951|Alexander Glasgow]] is also R-FT20271.<ref>
[https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview
FamilyTreeDNA Glasgow Project, public Y-DNA results].</ref>

This proves that the two tested paternal lines share a direct male-line
ancestor. It does not prove that James was that ancestor or that the tested
Robert Glasgow line descends from him. The intervening WikiTree parent-child
relationships remain uncertain.

The most useful next genetic test is a Big Y-700 upgrade for a documented male
descendant of the Kilwaughter household, followed by comparison of private
variants with the Alexander and Robert test kits. The result could show whether
the tested Drumearn/Moneymore candidate line is closer to Kilwaughter, closer to
Alexander's Killoquin line, or joined to both only through an older common
ancestor.

The 1759 Drumearn probate entry belonged to the Consistory Court of Armagh,
whose jurisdiction included Derryloran. Its will and administration indexes
should be searched for an executor, administrator, bondsman or surviving
abstract naming relatives.<ref>[https://www.familysearch.org/da/search/catalog/696653
FamilySearch catalogue, Armagh will and administration-bond indexes].</ref>

A 1767 survey of James Stewart's estates near Cookstown survives as National
Library of Ireland MS 9627. It should be inspected for Drumearn tenants and
historical variants of Glasgow; a successor to James's holding may identify a
son, brother or nephew.<ref>[https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/MS_UR_020697
National Library of Ireland, MS 9627].</ref>
```

## Do not add as fact

* That James was R-FT20271: no proved direct male-line pedigree currently joins

* That James belonged inside the Kilwaughter nuclear family. The present DNA
  supports a wider paternal cluster only.
