# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1025), about 1700–1759, Drumearn](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1025)

Last updated: 2026-08-12

## Current conclusion

The 1759 probate entry for **James Glasgow of Drumearn, County Tyrone** is a
strong match to this profile: both the year and the unusually specific
residence agree. It materially strengthens Drumearn as this man's documented
residence and 1759 as his terminal year.

It does not prove that he was born in County Tyrone, lived at Moneymore, headed
one of the two James Glasgow households in Derryloran in 1740, or fathered any
particular later Glasgow. The two Jameses and John at Drumearn in the 1766
religious return must be later namesakes or a younger generation if this
identification is correct.

James remains a possible member of the broad R-FT20271 paternal cluster, but
the DNA does not identify him. A pedigree audit materially weakens the earlier
attempt to map kit `959947` to this profile. Public locality records and the
saved WikiTree descendant tree instead place that private tester most plausibly
in Robert `Glasgow-12`'s family through son William `Glasgow-563`, followed by
`Glasgow-1829`, `Glasgow-556`, `Glasgow-1851` and `Glasgow-1877`. The final
parent-child link to the private tester is not publicly exposed, so that mapping
remains probable rather than proved.

The reported earliest-ancestor label “James Glasgow” is more naturally read as
the James of Moneymore whom later family history calls Robert's father, currently
represented—also uncertainly—by `Glasgow-1024`. It is not evidence for the
different James of Drumearn represented here. Kit `333002` is an older Y-37
result associated with the same reported family tradition.

The fuller private match extract places `959947` in a provisional STR pattern
with `200475`, `999763` and `478604`: all display `DYS19=15`, `DYS456=16` and
`DYS712=19`; `959947` is distinguished by `DYS650=20`. Relative to Alexander
`Glasgow-951` kit `B580327`, it has five differing Big Y STRs among 660 compared
markers and twelve non-matching variants. This refines the genetic neighbourhood
but does not identify any ancestor as James-1025.

The most efficient placement test is a Big Y-700 upgrade for a documented
Kilwaughter male line—preferably kit `254947`, representing Robert
`Glasgow-1143`, or `B835762`, representing James William Glasgow born 1731—and
a private-variant comparison with `B580327` and `200475`. Until then, describe
James-1025 as a possible R-FT20271 collateral, not securely inside the
Kilwaughter pedigree.
