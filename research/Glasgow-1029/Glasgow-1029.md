== Biography ==
* '''Parents:''' John Glasgow and Jonet Spreull.
** Jonet Spreull came from the Spreulls of Cowden in Dumbartonshire. Tradition gives descent from Walter Spreul, Seneschal of the Lennox.<ref>''The Heraldry of the Spreulls of Scotland'', pp. 70–73. (Internet Archive item formerly cited; current online copy unavailable)</ref><ref>Renfrewshire Local History Forum, “Cowden Hall.” https://rlhf.info/wp-content/uploads/3.4-Cowden-Hall-Hughson-McCrae.pdf</ref>
** A protocol in 1575 names her as Stephen’s mother and notes that she still lived at that date: “Stephen Glasgow in 1575, son of late John Glasgow, with consent of Jonet Spreull, his mother.”<ref>Protocol abstract cited in University of Glasgow thesis and in Town Clerks abstracts listed below.</ref>
* '''Place:''' The burgh of Glasgow. Stephen held land near St Kentigern (St Mungo).
* '''Office:''' Water bailie. The council chose the water bailie from a leet of two. The statutes removed any officer for negligence.

== Marriage and How Acre ==

On 25 June 1582 a property description placed How Acre in Craignestock beside lands occupied by '''Stephen Glasgow and [[Graham-38221|Margaret Grahame]], spouses'''. This directly proves the couple and their Glasgow property association, but does not supply a marriage date or Margaret's parents.<ref>Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. 8, protocol 2431, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsofproto08glas/page/56/mode/2up pp. 56–58], 25 June 1582.</ref>

== Offices and civic context ==

* From 1574 to 1586 the water bailie sequence ran Mr William Logan, then John Clerk, then '''Stephen Glasgow''' in 1585. Logan and Stephen did not sit on the council. Clerk may have sat.
* In 1586 the burgh named '''Stephen Glasgow''' curator to [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]] with Sir William Herbesoun.

== Property, land, and legal acts  chronology ==

* '''1 June 1574.''' Court perambulation of the burgh bounds noted a missing “marche stane” on the march between the lands of '''Stewin Glasgw''' and Archibald Steven.<ref>[https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-burgh-records/vol1/pp1-34 ''Records of the Burgh of Glasgow'', Vol. I, 1 June 1574]</ref>
* '''12 May 1574.''' Stephen stood as a witness at a sasine for two barns near Craigmak with William Hegate, Paul Reid, and James Andersoun.<ref>12 May 1574. James Flemyng, bailie, gave sasine to Sir Mark Jamesoun for Agnes Heriot, Joanna Heriot, and Helen Heriot for two barns near Craigmak. Witnesses included William Hegate, '''Stephen Glasgow''', Paul Reid, and James Andersoun. Text: https://archive.org/stream/abstractsprotoc00robegoog/abstractsprotoc00robegoog_djvu.txt</ref>
* '''17 June 1575.''' Stephen sold a fore and back tenement with yard and pertinents on the west side of the street from the market cross to the South Nether Port to John Scott, baker and citizen. Mr Adam Walles, bailie, gave sasine. Witnesses: John Tempill, George Burell, David Landes, Robert Young, citizens, and John Watsoun, officer.<ref>University of Glasgow thesis (see Sources) cites the protocol for the sale by '''Stephen Glasgow''' to John Scott, 17 June 1575.</ref>
* '''2 April 1586, 9 a.m.''' John Grahame, bailie, transferred sasine of a great fore and back tenement with yard on the west side of the street from the metropolitan church to the market cross to '''Stephen Glasgow'''. Robert Rowet, bailie, gave sasine. Witnesses: Archibald Wilsoun, merchant; Archibald Wilsoun, armourer; and '''William Spreull''', citizens.<ref>“Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow,” vol. 6, entry 2 April 1586.</ref>
* '''15 July 1586, 10 a.m.''' John Grahame, bailie, transferred sasine to '''George Glasgow''' as heir to the late '''William Glasgow''' his father. The property was a dilapidated back tenement on the west side of the same street. Bounds: lands formerly of Robert Pirrhie, now of John Clerk, tailor, to the east; lands of David Lyndsay to the west; lands of the late George Herbesoun to the south; lands of Sir Bartholomew Symsoun to the north. Witnesses: Hugh Blair, servant to the Laird of Blair; John Maxucll in Bogtoun; '''Stephen Glasgow''', citizen; Andrew Farie, sergeant.<ref>“Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow,” vol. 6, entry 15 July 1586.</ref>
* '''1 September 1586, 9 a.m.''' '''George Glasgow''', with consent of his mother Margaret Herbesoun, liferenter, and of his curators Sir William Herbesoun and '''Stephen Glasgow''', sold a ruinous back tenement with pertinents on the west side of the same street to John Angus, merchant, and Helen Darroch, spouses. John Grahame, bailie, gave sasine. Witnesses: John Clerk, tailor, citizen; John Pirrie, writer.<ref>“Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow,” vol. 6, entry 1 September 1586.</ref>
* '''2 March 1598–9, 2 p.m.''' John Anderson, sailor and procurator for '''Stephen Glasgow''', resigned a substantial fore and back tenement with yard and pertinents on the west side of the same street to Archibald Hegate, notary and citizen.<ref>“Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow,” vol. 6, entry 2 March 1598–9.</ref>

== Research Notes ==
=== Surviving identity alternative — 16 October 2026 ===

James [[Glasgow-3332]] remains a surviving cross-profile identity alternative for the 1624 Quothquan weaver. Its circa-1634 birth is an unsupported estimate and cannot by itself exclude that earlier man. Identification with the distinct weaver indexed at Back of Lindsaylands in 1680 would exclude a death before 1624 if established, but that later identity is itself only possible. Retain [[Glasgow-3910]] as the record-defined 1624 account for the selected uncertain family; do not merge the profiles or transfer [[Glasgow-3332]]’s unproved parent and child attachments. Those existing family fields remain unchanged, and the proposed Stephen placement is an alternative to that account’s current pedigree. === Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

Select Stephen [[Glasgow-1029]] as the uncertain working father of James [[Glasgow-3910]]. James’s weaver occupation and Quothquan locality favour using the 1612 court defender and the 1624 testator as one working adult account. Stephen is documented as an adult in Glasgow in 1575 and with wife Margaret Grahame in 1582; his approximate 1550 birth is an estimate, not a birth record. The existing tree already places John [[Glasgow-3296]], the 1650 Quothquan testator, under Stephen, uncertain. The preferred model treats the two early Quothquan men as paternal brothers rather than making the later John James’s father. A Stephen born around the mid-sixteenth century could have fathered James in the 1570s–1590s and John in a similar generation. This uses a named family already connected to Quothquan, at very low confidence, rather than inventing a missing parent. No record names Stephen as James’s father or James and John as brothers.

The route depends both on the proposed James–John brotherhood and on John’s already uncertain Stephen parentage. The Glasgow–Montfoid–Mid Calder–Quothquan identities are not proved migrations. John’s 1650 testament names his sister Bessie and no child or parent; omission of James, already dead by 1624, does not disprove brotherhood. John himself could instead be James’s father if older than his inherited estimate, or the men could be uncle/nephew, cousins or unrelated local namesakes. The older John and Thomas tenants in the 1520s–1530s remain possible ancestral relatives with unproved intervening generations; no direct father edge to them is selected. Margaret Grahame is Stephen’s documented spouse in 1582, not a documented mother of James, so no mother is attached. John’s existing mother field and all earlier parent and identity alternatives remain unchanged.

Stephen’s already documented parents and 1582 spouse remain as previously assessed; this adds only the uncertain son James.
