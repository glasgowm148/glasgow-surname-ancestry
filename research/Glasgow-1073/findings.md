## Antigua minister exclusion — 1707/08

The exact source context confirms that the Antigua occurrence belongs to a
different John Glasgow. Fothergill's *A List of Emigrant Ministers to America*
places “Glasgow, John, Antegoa, March 10, 1707-1708” among Church of England
ministers and schoolmasters who received a £20 royal passage bounty after the
Bishop of London appointed them to a colonial cure. The introduction also says
some recipients did not proceed. This establishes an Anglican appointment, not
arrival in Antigua.

`Glasgow-1073` was a Church of Scotland Presbyterian minister continuously at
Kilbirnie from 1688 until death in 1721, and his son Samuel was baptised there
on 9 March 1707, one day before the bounty date. No Anglican ordination or
colonial link exists. The Antigua event must remain excluded from this profile.

Source: Gerald Fothergill, [*A List of Emigrant Ministers to America,
1690–1811*](https://archive.org/stream/listofemigrantmi00fothuoft/listofemigrantmi00fothuoft_djvu.txt),
entry citing Money Book 19-200; Findmypast `US/EMIGRANTMINISTERS/0394`.

## Relationship assessment — son James Glasgow, minister of Edinburgh Castle (Glasgow-1140), 6 October 2026

The Kilbirnie entry in *Fasti Ecclesiae Scoticanae*, new ed., [vol. 3](https://archive.org/details/fastiecclesiaesc03scot), lists "James, min. of Edinburgh Castle" first among Rev. John's children by his second wife, Margaret Scott. The Edinburgh Castle entry ([vol. 1](https://archive.org/details/fastiecclesiaesc01scot)) gives James M.A. Glasgow 10 March 1718, commission 7 July 1737, ordination 14 February 1738 and death 9 February 1746, aged about 48. *The Daily Gazetteer* reports his royal commission as chaplain to the Castle garrison ([3 November 1737, p. 1](https://www.newspapers.com/image/1142270617/)) and his ordination as minister of the Castle ([23 February 1738, p. 1](https://www.newspapers.com/image/1142271259/)). A Kilbirnie baptism index entry of 17 March 1700 for James, son of John Glasgow and Margaret Scot (OPR 596/10/30), agrees. On the Fasti, a compiled source, [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1140)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1140) is John's son; that he is also the 'brother James Glasgow, student of divinity at Glasgow' of the 1722 letter noted above is a consistent inference.

## Source findings — a son at the University of Glasgow, 1730–1740

The owner's early notes (a Checkvist export of 10 March 2025) copy this entry from the University of Glasgow's online biography [WH10905](https://www.universitystory.gla.ac.uk/biography/?id=WH10905): "Addison 1727-1897 reads: 'F.Johannis Glasgow A.M.V.D.M Pastoris Eclesiae de Kil Burny' [Matriculation album 1730]; held Dundonald Bursary; died April, 1740 [Univ. Minute, 26th June, 1740]." The Latin describes the student as son (*filius*) of John Glasgow, M.A., minister of the word of God and pastor of the church of Kilbirnie. The work cited is evidently W. Innes Addison's *Roll of the Graduates of the University of Glasgow, 1727–1897* (1898), quoting the 1730 matriculation album. Seen only as a quotation at second hand (notes, online biography, Addison, album); neither Addison nor the album has been checked here.

By inference the father is this John, minister of Kilbirnie from 1688 until his death in 1721; the album describes him after his death. The notes head the entry "John Glasgow M.A (1700-1740)", but the quoted text gives neither the student's forename nor his birth year, so both are the owner's reconstruction. A student matriculating in 1730 was probably a son of the second marriage, to Margaret Scott in 1699 (inference). Grade: possible child, from a derivative quotation. Confirm the forename, class and bursary in Addison and in the matriculation album before any child profile is created or linked.

## Source findings — Kilbirnie barony decree naming the minister's widow and children, 12 June 1723

The owner's undated notes link the National Records of Scotland catalogue entry [GD20/1/512](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD20%2f1%2f512), among the titles to the barony of Kilbirnie in the Papers of the Earls of Glasgow (Crawford Priory), and summarise it in their own words as "a decree from 12th June 1723. It states that Margaret, Janet, and Jean Glasgow (children of the late Mr. John Glasgow, minister of Kilbirnie), along with Margaret Scott and Mr. James Glasgow (widow and son of Mr. John Glasgow), were adjudicated against Patrick, Viscount of Garnock, regarding the new barony of Kilbirnie". The summary adds an extract renunciation dated 21 July 1731 and registered 14 February 1733.

Seen only as this second-hand summary of a catalogue description: neither the catalogue's own wording nor the decree has been read here, and the phrase "were adjudicated against" does not make clear who obtained adjudication against whom. If the catalogue bears the summary out, the entry would show that the minister was dead by June 1723 (consistent with the Fasti's December 1721), that Margaret Scott was his widow, and that a son Mr James and daughters Margaret, Janet and Jean were then living. It would not show which daughters were Jean Cunningham's and which Margaret Scott's. Mr James fits James Glasgow, minister of Edinburgh Castle (Glasgow-1140), recorded above as a son from the Fasti (inference). Treat as a lead, not yet graded.

Next records: first, read the GD20/1/512 catalogue entry itself and copy its exact wording into this file; then the decree and the 1731 renunciation, for the daughters' status and any husbands.
