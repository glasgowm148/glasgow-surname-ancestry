== Biography ==

[[Category:Glasgow Name Study]]

'''Robert Glasgow''' was baptised at '''Glasgow High Kirk, Lanarkshire, Scotland''', on '''17 November 1618'''. He was recorded as the lawful son of '''[[Glasgow-1027|Robert Glasgow]]''' and '''[[Cunningham-20923|Jonet Conynghame]]'''.<ref name="Robert1618">National Records of Scotland, Glasgow High Kirk Old Parish Register 644/1, reported reference 10/272, baptism of Robert Glasgow, 17 November 1618; reported transcription: "Robert lawful sone to Robert Glasgow & Jonet Conynghame - Godf: John & George Glasgowis."</ref>

The register transcription also names '''John and George Glasgow''', apparently as godfathers, witnesses or sponsors. Their precise relationships to Robert have not yet been established.

No record presently proves Robert's later residence, occupation, marriage, children or date of death.

=== Parents ===

Robert's parents are directly named in the 1618 baptismal entry:

* '''[[Glasgow-1027|Robert Glasgow]]'''.
* '''[[Cunningham-20923|Jonet Conynghame]]'''.

The elder Robert was associated with Glasgow and was recorded as a cordiner.

Robert baptised in 1618 should not be confused or merged with his father.

=== Possible marriage to Jonet Cunningham ===

The original Glasgow parish register records [[Glasgow-3971|James Glasgow]] on 25 November 1645 as the son of a '''Robert Glasgow''' and '''Jonet Cunynghame''', with James Cunynghame and Johne Muir named as godfathers.<ref name="James1645">Scotland's People, Church of Scotland Old Parish Registers, Glasgow parish 644/1, reference 40/56, James Glasgow, male, 25 November 1645, parents indexed as “Rot. Glasgow/Jonet Cunnyngham”. Original register image inspected 31 August 2026; it names James Cunynghame and Johne Muir as godfathers but gives no occupation, residence, age, birthplace or parentage for Robert. See also [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VQHK-VMK FamilySearch record VQHK-VMK], a derivative index.</ref>

It is chronologically possible that the father in that record was Robert baptised in 1618. He would have been about twenty-seven years old in 1645.

The record does '''not''', however, give sufficient identifying information to prove that the two Roberts were the same man.

'''[[Cunningham-22604|Jonet Cunningham]]''' should therefore be treated as an '''uncertain wife''' of this Robert unless the original parish registers or another record establish continuity.

This younger Jonet Cunningham should not be confused with Robert's mother, '''[[Cunningham-20923|Jonet Conynghame]]'''.

=== Possible son James Glasgow ===

[[Glasgow-3971|James Glasgow]], recorded at Glasgow on 25 November 1645, was the son of a '''Robert Glasgow''' and '''Jonet Cunnyngham'''.<ref name="James1645" />

If the father in that record was Robert baptised in 1618, James was his son.

The chronology is entirely compatible, but the identification of the father has not yet been independently proved.

The relationship should therefore remain marked '''uncertain'''.

The Scottish James recorded in 1645 has separately been proposed as identical with '''[[Glasgow-1081|James Glasgow of Kilwaughter]]''', County Antrim. That Scottish-to-Irish identification is itself unproved and should not be used as evidence that this Robert migrated to Ireland.

=== Later life ===

No record has yet been identified that certainly concerns Robert after his baptism.

Several later records for men named Robert Glasgow are chronologically possible matches, but none presently provides enough identifying information to connect them securely with this profile.

A '''Robert Glasgow''' was employed as one of Glasgow's town officers in 1675 and received cloth for a uniform in an entry dated 23 October 1675.<ref name="Burgh1675">''Extracts from the Records of the Burgh of Glasgow, A.D. 1663-1690'' (Scottish Burgh Records Society, 1905), p. 204, entry dated 23 October 1675.</ref>

Robert baptised in 1618 would have been about fifty-six or fifty-seven at that date, so the identification is chronologically possible.

No evidence presently connects the town officer with the child baptised in 1618. The 1675 occurrence should therefore remain an unconnected same-name record unless further evidence is found.

=== Death ===

Robert's date and proved place of death are unknown. The earlier Kilwaughter death-place tradition is retained in the structured field only as uncertain; no inspected record places this Scottish Robert there.

The 1653 Larne occurrence concerns the separate profile [[Glasgow-3970|Robert Glaskow of Larne]] and should not be used to infer this Robert's death.

=== Scottish and Irish connections ===

Although no record presently proves that this Robert migrated to Ireland, movement between '''Glasgow and the Larne district''' was entirely practicable during his lifetime.

The '''Agnew family of Lochnaw''' held and administered the Kilwaughter estate near Larne. Their surviving papers include an Irish estate series covering the period from '''1636 to 1712'''.<ref name="AgnewEstate">National Records of Scotland, Papers of the Agnew Family of Lochnaw, GD154/505–534, Irish estate at Kilwaughter, County Antrim, 1636–1712.</ref>

This provides a direct Scottish administrative connection into the district where members of the Glasgow family were later recorded. No tenancy, lease or estate record naming this Robert Glasgow has yet been identified in the Agnew papers.

Glasgow Town Council records from '''1655''' concern burgesses who had left Glasgow and settled elsewhere. Twelve of the group were reportedly living in Ireland with their wives and families.<ref name="AbsentBurgesses">Glasgow Town Council minutes, 30 June and 18 August 1655, Glasgow City Archives C1/1/13; ''Extracts from the Records of the Burgh of Glasgow, 1630–1662'', printed p. 317 ([https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/burgh_records_1630_to_1662.pdf#page=399 scan]); James R. Anderson, ed., ''Burgesses & Guild Brethren of Glasgow, 1573–1750'' (1925), cross-referenced entries.</ref>

The printed council extract names Cuthbert Robisoun and suppresses thirty other names. Anderson’s burgess roll identifies ten of the twelve Ireland residents: Cuthbert Robisoun, John King, Allan Cuthbert, Samuel Wallace, Hew Brown, William Gulliland, William Rodger, John Sinkler, Archibald Craig and Robert Hamilton. None was named Glasgow; two remain unidentified in print. This is a modest negative finding, not proof that the 1618 Robert remained in Scotland.

Contemporary testimony from the Larne area documents wartime movement by boat between County Antrim and Scotland by settlers travelling with their families.<ref name="LarneTravel">1641 Depositions, examination describing William Willson's movement by boat with his family from the Larne district to Scotland and subsequent return, [https://1641dep.abdn.ac.uk/items/show/42875.html public deposition item].</ref>

These records demonstrate a credible migration and travel mechanism between western Scotland and the Larne district during Robert's lifetime.

They provide useful historical context for the former hypothesis identifying him with Robert Glaskow of Larne, but they do '''not''' document Robert's personal movements or establish that the two men were identical.

=== Separation from Robert Glaskow of Larne ===

This Robert should presently be maintained as a '''separate individual''' from [[Glasgow-3970|Robert Glaskow of Larne]], the man recorded as living at Larne, County Antrim, in 1653. The 1653 occurrence belongs to Glasgow-3970 and does not establish any later event for this Scottish Robert.

An examination taken on '''21 April 1653''' contains the description:

:''one Robert Glaskow liuing in Larne''

The record does not identify the Larne man's age, birthplace, parents, wife or children.<ref name="RobertLarne">Aidan Clarke et al., editors, ''1641 Depositions'', vol. III: ''Antrim, Derry, Donegal, Down & Tyrone'' (Dublin: Irish Manuscripts Commission, 2014), TCD MS 838, examination dated 21 April 1653; [https://1641dep.abdn.ac.uk/items/show/42821.html public deposition item].</ref>

Earlier research proposed that the Larne resident was the Robert baptised at Glasgow in 1618.

That hypothesis was based upon several circumstantial factors:

* Compatible chronology.
* The shared given name and surname.
* The relative rarity of the surname in surviving County Antrim records.
* Known movement between western Scotland and County Antrim.
* Glasgow burgesses known to have settled in Ireland.
* The Scottish administration of the nearby Kilwaughter estate.
* The later appearance of Glasgow households at Kilwaughter.

These factors make the proposed identity possible, but none establishes it.

The 1653 proposed-transplantation material adds only network context. The surviving printed declaration names Captain James McCullogh in the relevant east-Antrim quarter but not James Browne; Young's Pinkerton-derived version names Browne, and a contemporary commissioners' letter says the printed names contained a serious mistake.<ref name="Transplantation1653">Ireland, Commissioners for the Settling and Securing the Province of Ulster, ''By the Commissioners for the settling and securing the province of Vlster'' (Dublin: William Bladen, 1653), British Library 816.m.17[74], Wing I341, [https://catalog.folger.edu/record/538557 catalogue record]; Robert M. Young, ''Historical Notices of Old Belfast and its Vicinity'' (Belfast, 1896), pp. 80–86, [https://archive.org/details/historicalnotice00youn/page/80/mode/2up Internet Archive].</ref> Robert Glaskow appears in neither list state. The discrepancy concerns the Browne–McCullogh circle around the Larne man; it does not link him to the child baptised in Glasgow or to either Jonet Cunningham.

No record has yet been found stating that Robert of Larne:

* Was born at Glasgow.
* Was the son of [[Glasgow-1027|Robert Glasgow]].
* Was the son of [[Cunningham-20923|Jonet Conynghame]].
* Had previously lived in Glasgow.
* Was married to [[Cunningham-22604|Jonet Cunningham]].
* Was the father of the James Glasgow recorded at Glasgow in November 1645.

The Scottish and Larne Roberts should therefore remain separate profiles unless a documentary bridge is found.

=== Why the Scottish and Larne profiles were separated ===

The former reconstruction effectively required several separate propositions to be true:

:Robert Glasgow baptised at Glasgow in 1618
:↓
:Robert Glasgow, father of James at Glasgow in 1645
:↓
:Robert Glaskow living at Larne in 1653
:↓
:ancestor of the later Kilwaughter Glasgows

Each step is chronologically and historically plausible, but none is presently established by direct evidence.

Combining those propositions on one profile gave the overall reconstruction more apparent certainty than the surviving records justify.

The more conservative approach is therefore to maintain separate research identities:

* '''Robert Glasgow of Glasgow''' — baptised in 1618; parents proved.
* '''Robert Glasgow, father of James in 1645''' — possibly the 1618 Robert, but not proved.
* '''Robert Glaskow of Larne''' — documented at Larne in 1653; origins unknown.

The profiles should only be merged if new evidence establishes continuity.

=== Other men named Robert Glasgow ===

'''[[Glasgow-1149|Robert Glasgow]]''', associated with Inveresk and Musselburgh in Midlothian, appears in later Scottish records and should remain separate unless evidence establishes otherwise.

On 4 July 1666 a '''Robert Glasgow, cordiner''', was admitted an Edinburgh burgess by right of his wife Euphame, daughter of the deceased Andrew Lorimer, beltmaker and burgess.<ref name="EdinburghBurgess1666">Marguerite Wood, ed., ''Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses and Guild-Brethren, 1406–1700'' (Edinburgh, 1929), p. 208, entry dated 4 July 1666; [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=218 digital scan].</ref> The shared cordiner trade with this Robert's father is notable, but the abstract supplies no age, father or birthplace. It is an alternative Scottish same-name lead, not proof of this Robert's later life.


A further adult candidate appears in the Argyll Estates Archives catalogue. In 1666 a '''Robert Glasgow, master of the ''John of Portrush''''', gave a receipt for transporting barrels of coal for the earl of Argyll.<ref name="Portrush1666">Argyll Estates Archives, Campbell family, dukes of Argyll papers, “Various accounts and receipts,” 1666–1678, GB 3452 ARG-04-03-67-78, former reference PFV 67, catalogue p. 149, [https://www.argyll-papers.com/downloads/campbell-family-dukes-of-argyll-papers.pdf catalogue description]. The original receipt has not yet been inspected.</ref> The 1618 Robert would have been about 48, and the cross-channel setting makes this a relevant lead. The catalogue gives no age, birthplace, residence, kin or Larne connection, and a vessel called the ''John of Portrush'' does not prove that its master lived at Portrush. This man is a same-name candidate, not a documentary bridge to [[Glasgow-3970|Robert Glaskow of Larne]].

The obvious customs follow-up is limited by record survival. The E72 port-book series begins in 1668, no Ayr or Irvine customs book for 1666 has been located in the fragmentary E71 material, and the published surviving Ulster port books relate to 1612–1615. The original Argyll receipt is therefore the best immediate source for identifying the shipmaster.

An NRS catalogue search found another possible west-coast occurrence. An instrument of resignation and sasine at Ayr dated 6 August 1688 was written by '''Robert Glasgow, servitor to the notary''' Alexander McDermeit, depute clerk of the burgh.<ref name="Ayr1688">National Records of Scotland, GD86/741, instrument of resignation and sasine, 6 August 1688, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browseDetails.aspx?reference=GD86%2F741 catalogue record].</ref> A literate former shipmaster might later have worked for a notary, but the records are twenty-two years apart and neither gives age, kin or birthplace. Their handwriting and signatures must be compared before treating them as one man; neither is presently identified with the child baptised in 1618.

The original North Leith poll-tax page records another adult namesake on 3 October 1694. '''Robert Glasgow''' was one of four servants in the household of Alexander Ainslie, who was explicitly styled clerk to the glass manufactory.<ref name="Leith1694">National Records of Scotland, Scottish Exchequer poll tax, North Leith parish, E70/4/10, image 11, 3 October 1694; [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/virtual-volumes/volume-images/volume_data-E70-4-10/REX01666?image_number=11 original image], Scotland's People Virtual Volumes.</ref> A modern study describes Robert as a glassmaker and dates the tax to 1695, but the original page itself calls him only a servant and is dated 1694.<ref>Jill Turnbull, “The Scottish Glass Industry, 1610–1750” (PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh, 1999), pp. 171, 202, 324 and 350, [https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/fe29343e-a341-412e-85bd-96afca6214e5/content PDF].</ref> A second contemporary record almost certainly concerns the same man: on 16 July 1694 Robert Glasgow gave a receipt in Ainslie's name for bottles supplied to the Earl of Breadalbane.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Breadalbane Muniments, GD112/15/59, item 24, 16 July 1694, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD112%2F15%2F59 catalogue description].</ref> This proves a functional and probably trusted role in the glassworks business, though not the skilled occupation of glassmaker. The 1618 Robert would have been 76. Chronology permits comparison, but there is no identifier connecting him to Ainslie, Leith or the glassworks.


A stronger younger chronological alternative is [[Glasgow-3011|Robert Glasgow]], baptised at Glencorse in 1676. He was seventeen in 1694 and is the sole exact-name Robert Glasgow baptism returned for that parish from 1640 through 1690; a same-name man married at Glencorse in 1704. No record connects him to Ainslie or the glassworks, but his existence materially weakens any argument that the 1694 servant continued this 1618 life.

Exact Scotland's People OPR searches found no Glasgow, Glasgo, Glascow, Glaskow or Glassgow baptism in the Leith parishes from 1694 to 1730, and no exact Robert Glasgow marriage there from 1694 to 1710 or burial from 1694 to 1730. Register survival and spelling limit those negative results.

A Robert Glasgow was employed as one of Glasgow's town officers in 1675.<ref name="Burgh1675" />

It is chronologically possible that the town officer was Robert baptised in 1618, but no evidence links the two occurrences.

Researchers should also distinguish this profile from '''[[Glasgow-1027|Robert Glasgow]]''', his father.
