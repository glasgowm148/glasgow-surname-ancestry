# Findings: James Glascho (Glasgow-1081), Kilwaughter

Last updated: 2026-09-21

## Current conclusion

This profile represents the Irish James `Glascho`, a Kilwaughter householder in
1669. His identification with the same-name congregational officer from 1700,
apparently deceased by 1726, is a working continuity hypothesis, not an explicit
recorded identity. A same-name successor remains possible. The 1727 Connor wills
index entry for James Glasgow of Kilwater is a strong match to the later officer.

The Scottish James recorded in 1645 is now correctly represented separately as
[Glasgow-3971](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3971). Their identity is an
open, low-to-moderate hypothesis, not a proved merge. No source names the Irish
James's parents or children.

The repeated association with [John Glasgow (Glasgow-3913)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3913)
is stronger than a one-record coincidence. Their entries are consecutive in the
1669 roll (record numbers 5347-5348), place both men at Kilwaughter Demesne and
use the same uncommon `Glascho` spelling. About thirty years later men named John and James
represented Kilwaughter and served as its overseer respectively. This supports
a persistent local family cluster, but neither man is explicitly identified as
the earlier householder. **Close kinship remains probable as a working cluster
assessment**, conditional on the identity continuity; neither source names a
relationship or establishes brothers, father and son, cousins or another degree.

The 1669 transcription distinguishes the Kilwaughter `Glascho` entries,
standardised as Glasgow, from numerous `Glass` entries including an established
Drummaul family. Do not use western County Antrim Glass households as children
or descendants of this James without a bridging record.

## Relationship assessment

- John Glasgow-3913 was probably a close relative. That assessment rests on
  the same-name cluster in two independent record groups, not merely the adjacent
  surname entries; continuity of the individuals and the exact relationship remain
  unproved.
- Robert Glasgow-3970 at Larne in 1653 is a possible father or older relative only.
- Robert-1502, Hugh-1503, James-1504, William-1505 and Robert-1143 belong to the
  later district cluster; none is proved as James's child.
- [Hugh Glasgow (Glasgow-1499)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1499)
  is not supported as a separate son. Porter's session-list transcription assigns
  `deceased 1726` to James himself and gives 8 July 1767 for the documented Hugh
  Glasgow. Hugh-1499's only defining date is therefore a misattribution, and the
  profile is a probable duplicate of Hugh-1503 rather than evidence for another
  child of James.

## Identity audit — 21 September 2026

The earlier summary treated 1669–1726 as one continuous biography. The sources
remain valid occurrences, but their linkage is now explicitly qualified: if the
1669 householder was at least 21, survival to 1726 would make him at least about
78. That is possible and does not disprove continuity, but a son or other
same-name successor must also be tested. Porter's `deceased 1726` belongs to the
later James, not to Hugh; this correction leaves that reading intact.

The Belfast shipmaster [James Glasgow-3172](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3172)
has an unexamined 1686 will extract, PRONI T/828/15, indexed through
[NLI MS_UR_012684](https://sources.nli.ie/Record/MS_UR_012684). A will date alone
cannot exclude his survival into the later Kilwaughter period. Confirmed probate
or death in 1686 would exclude identity with the later officer; it would not by
itself distinguish him from the 1669 householder. No merge or new kinship is
supported. See the [bounded audit](../larne-proof-2026-09-21/early-james.md) for
search scope and access limits.
