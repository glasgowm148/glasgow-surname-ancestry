=== Inherited profile background; identity unresolved ===

The following complete inherited account is retained as the earlier assessment. Its mixed generations and unnamed Lynch “Title of the Book/Article” citation cannot establish identity, population, generations or parentage. The selected Saltmarket model above remains uncertain; the profile may instead represent Stirling John or conflate several men.

This page is listed with an approximate date of 1500, but it primarily serves as a placeholder for [https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Space:John_Glasgow-1 John Glasgow (1475–1536)], who was likely born around 1450. Various records mention a John Glasgow at different times between 1475 and 1575. These entries most likely refer to at least two or three generations of John Glasgows. Given the time span and naming patterns, there could be more individuals, but it is clear that the same John could not have appeared in all these records.

This marks one of the earliest known appearances of the Glasgow surname outside of the "de Glasgu" lineage, which is documented between 1259 and 1419. A significant portion of the "de Glasgu" individuals are named John, which complicates efforts to distinguish their individual timelines. Based on common medieval generational spans—typically 25 to 30 years—there would have been roughly 11 to 12 generations between 1259 and 1575. Considering the prominence of the name "John" as a firstborn male in this period, it is likely that at least 6 distinct individuals named John Glasgow appeared in records during this time, though there could have been more.

Although no direct relationship has been definitively proven, it is highly probable that the three individuals named Glasgow, who are mentioned in the '''Protocol Books of the Town Clerks of Glasgow''', are related to the John mentioned in 1475, given the patterns of naming and the family’s prominence in local records:

* '''[[Glasgow-1093|John Glasgow (b. abt. 1510)]]''' – Active between 1536 and 1575.
* '''[[Glasgow-1098|William Glasgow (b. abt. 1510)]]''' – Active between 1526 and 1536.
* '''[[Glasgow-1096|Robert Glasgow (b. abt. 1520)]]''' – Active between 1554 and 1568.

Considering the relatively small population of Glasgow in the early 1500s—estimated at between 2,500 and 3,000<ref>Lynch, Michael (2001). *Title of the Book/Article*, p. 267.</ref>—the probability of these individuals being closely related is high. The surname "Glasgow" was less common at the time, and the repeated appearance of multiple members of the Glasgow family in the same documents further suggests a shared familial connection. It was common in smaller communities for surnames to reflect familial ties, especially when the population was limited and families played prominent roles in local affairs.

The Glasgow family’s frequent mention in these historical documents points to their significance in the area during the period, and it’s possible that additional archival research could uncover further details about their relationships and activities during the 15th and 16th centuries.
