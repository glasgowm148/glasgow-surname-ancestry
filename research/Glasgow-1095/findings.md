## Relationship assessment — Eufame Craig as possible widow, 30 August 2026

On 16 November 1564 `Eufame Craige, the relict of wmquhill {blank} Glesgw`, holding conjunct fee, franktenement and liferent of the whole back tenement between the late John Rankin south and Robert Young north, sold her liferent to John Glasgow elder for £40 (Renwick, *Abstracts of Protocols*, vol. III, protocol 809, [printed pp. 58–59](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc06scogoog/page/n71/mode/2up); manuscript GCA B/10/1/3, fol. 54; printed abstract inspected, not the manuscript). This is the rear part of the parcel John held in 1527.

The leading hypothesis is that Eufame was this older John's widow: a liferent reserved while John held the whole property could survive the transfer of the fee to William by February 1528/29 and still burden the rear parcel in 1564. Eufame as William's later widow remains possible but less likely, because it requires Esbell Brade's death and William's remarriage between 31 December 1534 and 16 March 1535/36. Grade: **leading hypothesis**, a property-based reconstruction, not a recorded marriage. The printed abstract leaves the husband's forename blank; an image of the manuscript is the decisive check.

Recorded in `research/medieval-glasgow-bridge-1447-1534/report.md` (30 August 2026) and `surname-research/pre-1700/evidence-register.md`; carried into this file on 7 October 2026.

## Record findings and identity limits — 17 October 2026

### Possible Quothquan tenant, 1525–1532

The following Lanarkshire tenant is a possible identity for the older John represented provisionally in this account. The court entries are consolidated as one local documentary career, without treating their byname or tenancy as proof of the Saltmarket identity.

14 March 1524/25: Jhon of Glesquho is among men fined for failure to appear at the head court. On 15 March [1525/26], Jhon Glesquho is explicitly included among all the tenants of Quodquen, again for nonappearance. The latter heading omits the year; the bracketed year follows Appendix B. (William Croft Dickinson (ed.), Court Book of the Barony of Carnwath 1523–1542 (1937), printed pp.31–32, 35–36 and Appendix B p.cxix; PDF 160–161; https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1271/7974/127179749.23.pdf#page=160. Printed edition inspected 15 October 2026; original manuscript uninspected.)

6 February 1531/32: Costin Thomsone stands surety for Jhone Glasgw’s goods. The same court names Jhone of Glasgw among Upper Quothquan tenants who forcibly recovered seized property from the barony officer after he reached Libberton. After their nonappearance and witness evidence, the court decreed forfeiture of their tenancies, holdings and movable goods on the lord’s lands, with imprisonment for a year at his will. This records a decree, not proof that imprisonment was carried out. (William Croft Dickinson (ed.), Court Book of the Barony of Carnwath 1523–1542 (1937), printed pp.136–139; Appendix B p.cxxii; PDF 265–268; https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1271/7974/127179749.23.pdf#page=265. Printed edition inspected 15 October 2026; original manuscript uninspected.)

8 October 1532: Jhone Glasgw appears with the Upper Quothquan group in a judgment for breaking an arrestment on malt belonging to James Atzesone, held in Robert Cowan’s barn at Libberton. (William Croft Dickinson (ed.), Court Book of the Barony of Carnwath 1523–1542 (1937), printed pp.142–144; Appendix B p.cxxii; PDF 271–273; https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1271/7974/127179749.23.pdf#page=271. Printed edition inspected 15 October 2026; original manuscript uninspected.)

The remaining indexed John passages include tenant nonappearance on May 1526 (day unresolved) and 22 January 1526/27, followed by a claim against Jhone Kello on 13 October 1530, deferred to the next court. The last record gives an adversary, not a relative. The recurring tenant names support the consolidated local identity but do not prove an inherited tenancy. The May header prints 15 May while Appendix B lists 26 May; no exact day is adopted. (Dickinson (1937), printed pp.40–41,51–52,115–117; Appendix B pp.cxix,cxxi; PDF170,181,244–246; https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1271/7974/127179749.23.pdf#page=246. Printed edition inspected 15 October 2026; original manuscript uninspected.)

The recurrence of the name, Upper Quothquan locality and Kello, Justice, Robeson and Thomson associates supports one John across these meetings. No entry names his parent, wife or child. The provisional Saltmarket John’s estimated chronology is compatible, while [John Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1093), adult and married by 1534, is a genuine alternative whose approximate birth does not exclude the earlier tenant. [John of Hirmanscheillis](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3181) and [John, son of James elder of Corsankell](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3193), remain weaker long-career comparisons. [James Glasgow, the seventeenth-century Quothquan weaver](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3910), is a possible later family connection with missing generations. Thomas of Quothquan may have been a relative; no fatherhood, brotherhood or inherited tenancy is proved.
