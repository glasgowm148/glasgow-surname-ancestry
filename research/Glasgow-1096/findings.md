## Current conclusion

Father assessment: [William Glasgow (1500)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1098) is **probable on strong circumstantial evidence**, not proved by an explicit kinship formula.

This is stronger than a bare chronology-and-surname placement. William's proved
son [[Glasgow-1093|John Glasgow]] and Robert repeatedly occupied the same
compact south-burgh property network in 1557-73. Their lands bounded the same
tenements in 1557 and 1560, and a John Glasgow, fisher, held the property next
to Robert and witnessed Robert and Margaret Rowand's transaction in 1572/73.
Robert and John were then described together as fishers in 1573. Robert had
already witnessed [[Glasgow-1097|Archibald Glasgow]]'s 1551 purchase, whose
exact east-side parcel was in John's hands by 1554/55. The combined pattern is
that of one close surname, craft and property group, not three isolated men.

The 1573 fisher entry also names **Archibald Herbertsone** between Robert and
John. Archibald is the record-proved husband of [[Glasgow-3916|Isobella
Glasgow]], who is independently a probable daughter of William because her
husband witnessed John's disposal of William's inherited parcel in 1564. This
creates a repeated Robert–John–Isobella/Archibald cluster rather than a merely
bilateral Robert–John association, materially strengthening the proposed
sibling group while still supplying no explicit kin term.

Male primogeniture also explains why only John was later served heir to
William's inherited tenement: that inheritance does not exclude younger sons
who established neighbouring holdings. The competing explanations remain that
the fisher John was Robert's own son, or that Robert was a cousin or unrelated
namesake. Because two generations of adult Johns overlapped, the evidence does
not justify a `certain` WikiTree status. It does justify retaining William as
Robert's uncertain father while describing the relationship as **probable**.

Robert is now proved to have had **at least two sons**. The 1596 burgess
admission calls [Ninian Glasgow (Glasgow-3188)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3188)
Robert's **second son**. Ninian's 1613 testament independently leaves a gown to
"Johne Glasgw his brother." Together these records establish another son of
Robert named **John Glasgow**, alive on 10 January 1613 and probably the elder
or first son. No existing John profile can yet be identified uniquely with him.

[Margaret Rolland (Rolland-239), born about 1535, Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Rolland-239)
is Robert's **confirmed spouse**. Protocols 1903, 1904 and 2125 call the couple
spouses. Protocol 2002 identifies Robert among the fishers, matching the 1596
occupation and resolving the property-holder to this profile. Robert was alive
on 7 February 1574/75, so the live `after 1572` death boundary is obsolete.

[Elizabeth Glasgow, widow of Alexander Kaine](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3915)
is a new **possible daughter**, not a proved child. Ninian's sons
[Robert](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1027) and
[William](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3075) each have a daughter whose
name is recorded as Elizabeth/Elizabethe or Bessie. This independent repetition
makes Elizabeth worth testing as Ninian's missing older sister. It remains only
a naming-pattern hypothesis: Elizabeth's current `before 1530` birth is no more
than ten years after Robert's approximate 1520 birth, and no source links the
Easter Corswood widow to Robert's Glasgow household.

## Kilbride identity candidate - 31 August 2026

[Robert Glasgow of Kilbride (Glasgow-3919)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3919)
is now the leading later identity for this Robert. The five-year interval from
Robert and Margaret Rowand's 1575 city act to the 1580 Kilbride charter is
compatible. Robert's 1554 co-witness David Mathy belongs to a documented route
into the Glencairn estate landscape, while Kilbride co-occupants include
Dunlop, Wilson and Young surnames also recurring in Robert's burgh/property
network.

Assessment: **probable same man, moderate-to-strong circumstantial**. The 1580
record supplies no occupation, spouse or city designation, so retain separate
profiles and an identity link rather than merge.
