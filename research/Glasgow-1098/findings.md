## Current conclusion

William is directly documented in Glasgow burgh with wife **Esbell Brade** and
son **John Glasgow** in 1534. A newly recovered boundary in protocol 1233 calls
him `q. Wylzem Glasgw` on **16 March 1535/36**, proving that he had died by then.
John was later served heir of his father William in 1564. These records do not
prove William's own birth, death place or parents.

The current `1500, Stirlingshire` birth and `1543, High Street/Merchant City`
death are not established by the inspected evidence; the 1543 death is
incompatible with the 1535/36 `q.` record. A Glasgow property record does not
prove either vital-event location. The claimed marriage before 1514
is also unsupported; the direct evidence proves only that William and Esbell
were spouses by 31 December 1534.

The wider audit found one actionable status omission on a pre-1600 descendant.
[[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]], born about 1569, already has William
[[Glasgow-3522]] as a confident father, but the status of attached mother
[[Herbison-439|Margaret Herbesoun]] is unset. Protocol 2903 calls George heir of
the deceased William, his father; protocol 2916 calls Margaret his mother and
liferenter. These companion acts directly establish both parents and securely
identify the same minor heir and property. Margaret's relationship should be
set to **confident**, WikiTree's strongest non-DNA parent status. This meets the
project threshold of a pre-1600 descendant explicitly confident to both
parents without upgrading any circumstantial edge.

[John Glasgow (Glasgow-1095)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1095)
remains a possible father, not a proved one. Of William's attached children,
only [John-1093](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1093) is named as his son
in the inspected protocols. [Isobella-3916](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3916)
is now a **probable daughter on strong circumstantial evidence**: her proved
husband Archibald Herbisoune witnessed John dispose of the exact parcel John had
just inherited as William's heir, and she repeats Esbell/Isobel's forename.
The record does not state kinship, so both parent edges remain uncertain.
[Elizabeth-3915](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3915) is a separate
possible daughter or close female kinswoman: her husband Alexander Kaine is
probably the Alexander Kano listed immediately after William in the 1526
Sandilands remission, and she later occupied a Sandilands Calder holding.

The maintained account of William's probable father is the canonical
[John Glasgow of Saltmarket free-space biography](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Space:John_de_Glasgow_alias_Smith_/_John_Glasgow_of_Saltmarket%27),
estimated born about 1480 and dead by 21 October 1553. Use that page as the
research destination rather than the stale, unsourced pre-1500 profile
Glasgow-1095, which is outside the requesting manager's control.

The principal new clue is property continuity. On 2 May 1527 a tenement of an
older John Glasgow lay on the west side of the Cross-to-South-Port street
between John Rankin south and James Young north and owed a four-shilling annual
to James Houston's foundation. A newly recovered instrument, source-dated 20
February 1528/29 (explicitly dual-dated in the edition's contents), already places
William Glasgow north of the Henryson/Houston parcel, with Andrew Craig south.
That parcel is later Rankin's. Houston's St Andrew prebend rental, confirmed in
1542 and transumed in 1548/49, then charges the same four-shilling annual upon
the tenement of the late William Glasgow between the late John Crownar north
and John Rankin south. William's son John inherited the fore tenement in 1564,
still between late John Rankin and Robert Young. This is strong same-title
succession within at most about twenty-two months, but it does not state that
the older John was William's father.

Continued title reconstruction strongly identifies the 1534 back tenement as
the rear component of this block. In 1563 William's son John and Jonet Spreull
conveyed that rear fee to their own son John, bounded by Robert Young, late John
Rankin and St Mary's land. In 1564 Eufame Craig, widow of an unnamed Glasgow,
sold her outstanding liferent in it to John elder. The leading reconstruction
is that Eufame was the older John's widow and that her reserved liferent
survived the passage of the fee to William. The same-day Craig transactions and
Craig witnesses fit a coordinated family settlement. Making her William's
widow is less likely because it requires Esbell's death and a remarriage within
about fourteen months. Original-book targets are [NRS
NP1/5](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=NP1%2f5)
and Glasgow City Archives
[B/10/1/3](https://cityarchives.glasgowlife.org.uk/Record.aspx?src=CalmView.Catalog&id=B%2f10%2f1%2f3).

## Recommended WikiTree changes

1. Replace the 1526 citation number `3433` with **3412** and explain that this
   William is not individually identified as the later burgess.
2. Add protocol 1196 as direct proof of wife
   [Brade-232](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Brade-232) and son John-1093.
3. Change marriage to **before 31 December 1534, Glasgow, Lanarkshire,
   uncertain**; remove the unsupported `before 1514` claim.
4. Change death to **between 17 February 1534/35 and 16 March 1535/36, place
   unknown**. Remove the incompatible 1543 date and death location.
5. Change birth to **before 1526, place unknown, uncertain**, or leave it blank.
   Do not present 1500 or Stirlingshire as recorded facts.
6. Keep John-1093 as a confirmed son. Treat Isobella-3916 as probable but retain
   both parent edges as uncertain. Mark the other attached children
   [James-1168](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1168),
   [Robert-1096](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1096),
   [Archibald-1097](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1097) uncertain unless
   each profile supplies independent parent evidence.
7. Keep Glasgow-1095 as an uncertain father and state that no inspected record
   names William's parents. Add the narrowed 2 May 1527–20 February 1528/29
   search interval for the missing transmission. Present Eufame as the likely
   wife of the older John, clearly labelled as the leading property-based
   reconstruction rather than a recorded marriage.

## Do not infer

- Birth or death at the site of the Glasgow tenement.
- That every early-sixteenth-century William Glasgow occurrence is this man.
- That protocol 1196 proves any child other than John.
- That succession to the older John's exact tenement proves a father-son
  relationship without the missing transmission instrument.
