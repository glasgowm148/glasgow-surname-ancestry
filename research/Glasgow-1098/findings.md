Glasgow-1095, which is outside the requesting manager's control.

The principal new clue is property continuity. On 2 May 1527 a tenement of an
older John Glasgow lay on the west side of the Cross-to-South-Port street
between John Rankin south and James Young north and owed a four-shilling annual
to James Houston's foundation. A newly recovered instrument, source-dated 20
February 1528/29 (explicitly dual-dated in the edition's contents), already places
William Glasgow north of the Henryson/Houston parcel, with Andrew Craig south.
That parcel is later Rankin's. Houston's St Andrew prebend rental, confirmed in
1542 and transumed in 1548/49, then charges the same four-shilling annual upon
the tenement of the late William Glasgow between the late John Crownar north
and John Rankin south. William's son John inherited the fore tenement in 1564,
still between late John Rankin and Robert Young. This is strong same-title
succession within at most about twenty-two months, but it does not state that
the older John was William's father.

Continued title reconstruction strongly identifies the 1534 back tenement as
the rear component of this block. In 1563 William's son John and Jonet Spreull
conveyed that rear fee to their own son John, bounded by Robert Young, late John
Rankin and St Mary's land. In 1564 Eufame Craig, widow of an unnamed Glasgow,
sold her outstanding liferent in it to John elder. The leading reconstruction
is that Eufame was the older John's widow and that her reserved liferent
survived the passage of the fee to William. The same-day Craig transactions and
Craig witnesses fit a coordinated family settlement. Making her William's
widow is less likely because it requires Esbell's death and a remarriage within
about fourteen months. Original-book targets are [NRS
NP1/5](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=NP1%2f5)
and Glasgow City Archives
[B/10/1/3](https://cityarchives.glasgowlife.org.uk/Record.aspx?src=CalmView.Catalog&id=B%2f10%2f1%2f3).

## Source findings

### Combined succession review — 28 September 2026

The older John-to-William succession of the same Rankin–Young property is the strongest earlier-generation lead. Protocol 809, 16 November 1564, expressly describes Eufame Craig's **conjunct fee, franktenement and liferent** in the back tenement, but leaves her deceased Glasgow husband's forename blank. Its property boundaries connect it to the same family block. These are existing record details, reconsidered alongside the 1527, 1528/29, 1542 and 1564 sequence; they are not a newly discovered parent statement. The sequence supports succession and probable kinship, while the older John's profile identity, Eufame's husband and the exact degree remain unresolved. A father inference does not require a literal son clause, but must resolve those identity and degree questions. No birth estimate is treated as an exact age or used alone to exclude fatherhood.

The original 1527–1529 transfer behind the sequence remains the priority, but its precise archival item has not been identified. NP1/5 and B/10/1/3 remain separately listed targets for checking the later title reconstruction; they are not verified repositories of that missing early transfer. No publicly accessible original transfer image was located in this batch.

## Saltmarket father assessment — 16 October 2026

The older John Glasgow who held the Saltmarket Rankin–Young tenement in 1527 is assessed as the probable father of William Glasgow-1098. William occupies the matching parcel by 20 February 1528/29; the later St Andrew prebend rental retains its four-shilling burden, west-side street position and Rankin boundary. The 1534 family settlement and 1564 service as heir then directly identify the younger John Glasgow-1093 as William’s son. Read together, these describe a sustained family title rather than merely two men sharing a surname. Lineal succession from the earlier John to William is the preferred reconstruction, while the last William-to-John step is independently documented. The grade is now probable, below documented or strongly supported. This brings the formal local father assessment into line with the stronger property argument already developed in the research.

The missing 1527–1529 conveyance has not been found. No inspected instrument calls William the earlier John’s son, and no recorded ages distinguish father from brother. A brother, collateral heir or purchase from a namesake remains possible. Indeed, the neighbouring Henryson parcel in the 1528/29 instrument passes between brothers; title succession alone cannot specify kinship. The combined parcel descriptions are correlated, and the later rental is not an independent record of the original transfer. Glasgow-1095 remains a provisional representation of the Saltmarket owner: its identification with the Stirling witness or the 1506 alias-Smith purchaser is not proved. The probable grade applies to the property-defined Saltmarket father hypothesis, not to every imported biographical claim. No mother is inferred: Eufame Craig may have been the earlier John’s widow, but this does not establish her as William’s mother. No Stirling parent, birth estimate, death location or identity merge is upgraded. The live WikiTree father flag remains uncertain; probable is a local research grade.

This is a re-evaluation of an existing father attachment, not a new parent or a newly discovered record. The earlier generic uncertain assessment and its 28 September basis are retained in the structured history. All working family membership and confidently marked paternal connectivity remain unchanged. The printed 1528/29 boundary on p.90 and four-shilling rental on p.36 were freshly inspected; the other title steps retain their earlier maintained source reviews. The original manuscript transfer remains uninspected. ## Record findings and identity limits — 17 October 2026

### Possible Carnwath witness, 1524

The William recorded as ville Glesquho in the Carnwath court is a possible earlier identity for this man, whose birth date is unknown and who had an adult married son by 1534.

7 June 1524: the court continued a violent-blood case against Roben Ake and James Scot, ordering witnesses including ville Glesquho to be called. The next court header, 21 June 1524, comes below this passage and is not its date. (William Croft Dickinson (ed.), Court Book of the Barony of Carnwath 1523–1542 (1937), printed pp.14–17; Appendix B p.cxviii; PDF 143–146; https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1271/7974/127179749.23.pdf#page=143. Printed transcription inspected 15 October 2026; original manuscript not inspected.)

The witness is named with John Vyld and Robert Chanslar in the case involving Roben Ake and James Scot, but no residence, trade or relative is stated. The chronology fits this William before his death bound in March 1535/36. The possible 1526 remission involving William Glasgow and James Somervile is a further comparison, not an identity bridge. [William Glasgow, son of John](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3522), is a weaker rival: his later sixteenth-century career would require a considerably earlier birth and long life to fit 1524, but his estimated circa 1540 birth alone is not an exclusion. No Quothquan residence or relationship to the court’s other Glasgows is inferred.
