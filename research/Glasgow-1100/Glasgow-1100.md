== Biography ==

'''Margaret Glasgow''' was baptised at Glasgow High Kirk on 6 May 1610 as the
lawful daughter of [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]] and Margaret Steuart. John
Bornes and David Scheirar witnessed the baptism.<ref name="MargaretBaptism">Scotland's
People, Glasgow High Kirk Baptisms 1609-1777, baptism of Margaret Glasgow, 6
May 1610, lawful daughter of George Glasgow and Margaret Steuart, reference
1/233.</ref><ref name="MargaretBaptismFMP">Findmypast,
[https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=R_693716335&tab=this “Scotland,
Parish Births & Baptisms 1564-1929,” Margaret Glasgow], baptised 6 May 1610 at
Glasgow High Church, daughter of George Glasgow and Margrat Steuart, record
R_693716335 (accessed 3 September 2026).</ref>

She married [[Stewart-62608|James Stewart]]. On 18 July 1633 James, a merchant,
was admitted burgess and guild brother of Glasgow by right of his marriage to
Margaret, who was explicitly described as the lawful daughter of George
Glasgow, merchant, burgess and guild brother.<ref name="StewartBurgess">James
R. Anderson, ed., ''Burgesses & Guild Brethren of Glasgow, 1573-1750''
(Edinburgh: Scottish Record Society, 1925), printed p. 86,
[https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso43scotuoft/page/n100/mode/2up
James Stewart, 18 July 1633].</ref>

== Research Notes ==

The structured mother attached to this profile is
[[Stewart-65226|Margaret Stewart]], George Glasgow's recorded wife. A former
biography link used `Steuart-563`. The baptism proves the mother's name but
does not by itself prove that those two WikiTree IDs represent the same woman;
use the attached profile in the narrative and do not merge the profiles
without a separate identity audit.

The profile material gives two transposed dates for Margaret's marriage: 7
April 1632 in the structured field and 4 July 1632 in the cited biography. The
associated source is Glasgow OPR 644/1, reference 230/109. Neither date should
be presented as settled until the original entry is checked; the 1633 burgess
record independently establishes the marriage itself.

Because the record documents baptism rather than birth, it does not prove an
exact birth date.
