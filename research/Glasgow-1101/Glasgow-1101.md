'''Archibald Glasgow''' was baptised on '''19 June 1614''' at Glasgow High Kirk. He was recorded as the lawful son of [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]] and [[Stewart-65226|Margaret Steuart]]. He graduated '''Master of Arts''' from the University of Glasgow in '''1635'''.<ref>University of Glasgow, University Story, “Archibaldus Glasgua,” graduate, Master of Arts, 1635. https://universitystory.gla.ac.uk/people?search=&surname=Glas&forename=</ref>

== Probable Identification in County Donegal ==

A series of Irish records concerns a clergyman named '''Archibald Glasgow''' who was active in County Donegal between approximately 1641 and 1677.

The records form a geographically and chronologically coherent clerical career and probably concern one man. The uncommon name, chronology and clerical background also create a strong circumstantial case that this was the Archibald Glasgow baptised in Glasgow in 1614 and educated at the University of Glasgow.

No inspected Irish record, however, explicitly identifies the Donegal clergyman as:

* the son of George Glasgow and Margaret Steuart;
* a native of Glasgow;
* the man baptised at Glasgow High Kirk in 1614; or
* the University of Glasgow graduate of 1635.

The identification should therefore be treated as probable rather than conclusively proved.

Records concerning the Irish Archibald include:

* An '''Archibald Glasgow''' serving as rector of '''Killea, County Donegal''', in or before 1641, with later restoration in 1661.<ref>Ulster Plantation and Migration project, person record for Archibald Glasgow. https://ulster-settlers.maynoothuniversity.ie/people/32084</ref>
* A clergyman of that name associated with '''Faughanvale''' in 1654 and '''Taboyne''' in 1660.<ref>Ulster Plantation and Migration project, person record for Archibald Glasgow. https://ulster-settlers.maynoothuniversity.ie/people/32084</ref>
* An Archibald Glasgow possibly mentioned in a 1657 disciplinary record concerning drunkenness.<ref>Virtual Record Treasury of Ireland. https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/IMC-AH-15-1944-MacLysaght</ref>
* A cleric named '''Archibald Glasgow''' appointed rector and vicar of '''Clondevadock, Tullyfern and Aughnish''' in County Donegal in April 1661.<ref>James Seaton Reid, ''A History of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland'', 1853, p. 278. https://en.geneanet.org/archives/ouvrages?action=detail&book_type=livre&livre_id=6279357&page=278</ref>
* An '''Archibald Glasgow''' appearing in a County Donegal Hearth Money Roll dated 1665.<ref>Virtual Record Treasury of Ireland, TCD MS 6232. https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/TCD-MS-6232</ref>
* '''Archibald Glasgow of Ramelton, County Donegal''', named as obligee in a bond from William Dutton of Greenforte dated 1675/76.<ref>Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1825/A/2, bond from William Dutton of Greenforte to Archibald Glasgow of Ramelton, 1675/76.</ref>
* '''Archibald Glasgow of Rathmalton, County Donegal, clerk''', whose prerogative will was proved in '''1677'''.<ref>Sir Arthur Vicars, ''Index to the Prerogative Wills of Ireland, 1536–1810'', entry for Archibald Glasgow, clerk, Rathmalton, County Donegal, 1677. https://archive.org/details/indextoprerogati00vica</ref>
* A separate probate-index transcription records '''Archibald Glascoe of Rathmatton, County Donegal''', in '''1679'''.<ref>Irish probate index transcription supplied July 2026, entry for Archibald Glascoe, Rathmatton, County Donegal, 1679.</ref>

“Rathmalton” was an historical spelling associated with Rathmelton or Ramelton. “Rathmatton” in the 1679 transcription is probably a spelling or transcription variant of the same place. These names should not be confused with Rathmullan.

The Archibald named in the 1675/76 Ramelton bond and the Rathmalton clergyman whose will was proved in 1677 were almost certainly the same person. The uncommon name, occupation, place and close dates strongly support that conclusion.

The 1679 entry probably represents a duplicate index entry, a later probate action or a transcription of the same estate rather than a second local Archibald Glasgow. The two-year discrepancy has not been resolved and should not be silently merged into a single probate date.

If the Donegal clergyman was the man baptised in Glasgow in 1614, he died before probate was granted in 1677, aged approximately 62 or 63. Ramelton was his recorded residence, but it has not been established as his place of death.

== Wife and Children ==

No wife or child has yet been identified from an inspected primary record.

The existence of Archibald’s 1677 prerogative will is potentially important. The original will was probably among the records destroyed in Dublin in 1922, but a pre-destruction genealogical abstract may survive in the notebooks compiled by Sir William Betham.

Such an abstract may identify:

* Archibald’s wife;
* sons or daughters;
* married daughters or sons-in-law;
* siblings;
* executors;
* other legatees; or
* property inherited by his heirs.

The separate 1679 entry should also be investigated to determine whether it represents a duplicate, a later grant, an administration or another action concerning the same estate.

Until a will abstract or another direct record is examined, no spouse or child should be attached to this profile.

== Possible Connection to Thomas Glasgow ==

No record has been found proving that Archibald was the father, brother or other close relative of Thomas Glasgow of Ballindrait, Shannon or Lifford.

Thomas Glasgow was described as being of '''Lifford, County Donegal''', and as an esquire, in connection with the 1689 attainder proceedings. His transactions also place him within the same broad Donegal landholding network as Abraham and James Creighton.

The chronology allows Thomas to have been Archibald’s son, nephew or younger brother, but this is speculation. Archibald’s 1675/76 bond names only Archibald Glasgow and William Dutton and gives no family relationship.

Neither the 1677 nor the 1679 probate-index entry supplies the contents of Archibald’s will or names any beneficiary.

The PRONI catalogue descriptions for Thomas Glasgow’s known transactions likewise name Thomas, Abraham Creighton and James Creighton, but do not identify Thomas’s parents, wife, children, heirs or siblings:

* D1939/18/11/16

The full deeds may contain witnesses, recitals, endorsements or inheritance language omitted from the catalogue descriptions.

== Other Proposed Relationships ==

A possible relationship with [[Glasgow-992|William Glasgow (1614–)]], who married Bessie Howie, has previously been suggested because the surname Howie appears in Archibald’s baptismal entry.

The presence of the same surname is insufficient to establish that Archibald and William were brothers or cousins. William Howie was named in Archibald’s baptismal entry, but the record does not state that he was a relative.

A connection with [[Glasgow-1097|Archibald Glasgow (c.1533–)]], a burgess and property owner in Glasgow, has also been proposed. The suggested great-uncle and grand-nephew relationship depends upon several reconstructed parent-child links that have not been established from contemporary records.

These relationships should remain research hypotheses.

== Research Priorities ==

The most important unresolved record is the genealogical abstract of Archibald’s 1677 will, if it survives among the Betham notebooks.

Other useful records would include:

* the underlying source for the 1679 Archibald Glascoe probate-index entry;
* records explaining whether the 1679 entry was a duplicate, later grant or administration;
* the complete document for PRONI D1825/A/2;
* complete copies of Thomas Glasgow’s five D1939 transactions;
* Raphoe diocesan probate and administration records;
* marriage bonds and licence indexes;
* parish registers for Killea, Ramelton and neighbouring areas;
* property records naming Archibald’s heirs after 1677; and
* original Glasgow baptismal records for other possible children of George Glasgow and Margaret Steuart.

== Research Conclusion ==

The Donegal records from 1641 to 1677 probably document the career of one clergyman, culminating in the 1675/76 Ramelton bond and the probate of the will of Archibald Glasgow of Rathmalton in 1677.

The 1679 entry for Archibald Glascoe of Rathmatton is probably connected with the same estate, but its precise nature remains unresolved.

Identity with the Archibald Glasgow baptised in Glasgow in 1614 and awarded an M.A. in 1635 is a strong chronological and occupational fit. It remains circumstantial because no inspected Irish record names his Scottish parents, birthplace or university.

No spouse, child or proved line of descent to Thomas Glasgow or Nathaniel Glasgow has been identified.

=== Scottish–Donegal family review — 17 October 2026 ===

Prefer the Glasgow child baptised on 19 June 1614 and the Archibaldus Glasgua who graduated M.A. in 1635 as the earlier identity of the Donegal cleric. The uncommon full name, compatible age at graduation, clerical career and 1639 Irish clerk occurrence form a coherent Scottish-to-Irish career. A baptism in 1614 places him at about 21 in 1635 and 25 in 1639. These are conditional ages, not separately recorded ages. No incompatible parentage or simultaneous Scottish household is identified for this proposed cleric. The other known same-generation Scottish Archibald, the Leith man [[Glasgow-3078]], has a separately traced craft career: apprentice to cordiner Thomas Park in October 1639 and shoemaker/heir in May 1647. That positive career sequence makes him a weaker fit to the 1635 graduate and Irish clerk than the 1614 Glasgow child. It is a ranking argument, not an absolute exclusion or an assertion that a man could never change occupation. The Irish testator is securely the Ramelton/Rathmalton cleric by exact name, residence and profession. Assess his existing [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]] and [[Stewart-65226|Margaret Steuart]] parent links as probable, reflecting the probable Scottish childhood identification. The baptism explicitly names both parents, so maternity has its own record basis rather than being inferred from George’s spouse field.

No Irish record reviewed names Archibald’s Scottish parents or university. The graduation and 1639 clerk entry do not themselves state the 1614 baptism date. Another Archibald remains possible. This is a preferred combined identity, not documented migration or proof that every early Scottish and Irish occurrence is one man. The quoted baptism witnesses are reused from the maintained reading, not newly checked against an image. Margaret’s own Stewart ancestry, George’s wider family, and the 1647–1661 ministry chronology retain their existing grades.

Betham’s inspected abstract of the April 1673 will of [[Glasgow-1101|Archibald Glasgow]], clerk of Rathmalton, explicitly identifies [[Glasgow-3556|Thomas Glasgow]] as his eldest son. Prefer the existing Thomas of Lifford/Raphoe account for that son: the exact uncommon name, the adjacent Donegal setting and the next adult generation fit the independently documented Raphoe-linked court case of 1684, Lifford occurrence of 1689 and Ballindrait/Shannon deeds of 1692–1695. The 1692 Creighton court association and later estate material strengthen the continuity of that adult Thomas account; they are not independent statements of his parentage. The full local and generational fit warrants a probable father link to Archibald. A further unique estate or household identifier would strengthen it, but its absence does not require leaving this coherent family at merely uncertain.

Betham gives the son no age, residence, spouse or estate. He could be another Thomas, so the Lifford identification remains inferred rather than documented. The profile’s before-1671/circa-1670 birth estimates are not ages supplied by the abstract or court records and are not used as proof of either identity or exclusion. No maternity is assigned to Thomas from Elizabeth’s status as Archibald’s wife. Nathaniel and George remain Thomas’s separately assessed children; the later Killybegs James and Belfast Captain James are not made relatives by this review.

Betham’s inspected pre-destruction abstract explicitly identifies Elizabeth as wife of [[Glasgow-1101|Archibald Glasgow]], clerk of Rathmalton. The existing Elizabeth account represents that record-defined wife, while Archibald’s Irish identity is secured by exact name, clerical designation and residence. The spouse relationship is documented independently of the probable Scottish baptism and Thomas-to-Lifford identifications. The provisionally read M’Auley surname is retained as a reading, not installed as a certain surname.

The abstract is not the complete will; April 1673 is the will date and 1677 the separate probate index date. It does not establish a wedding date, Elizabeth’s birth or parents, or her maternity of every named child. Her existing profile name and estimates are preserved.

Four existing assessments change: two probable Scottish parent links for Archibald, a probable Archibald–Thomas father link, and a documented Archibald–Elizabeth marriage. This is a confidence review of existing attachments, not four new relatives. No live WikiTree save, identity merge, new mother for Thomas or new manuscript inspection is claimed.
