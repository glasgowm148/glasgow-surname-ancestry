== Biography ==

James Glasgow was christened on 21 May 1738 in Covington and Thankerton, Lanarkshire, Scotland. He was the son of [[Glasgow-1070|George Glasgow]] and [[Williamson-12853|Agnes Williamson]].<ref name="Baptism1738">"Scotland Births and Baptisms, 1564-1950," database, FamilySearch ([https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XTLX-GQN record XTLX-GQN], accessed 11 February 2020), James Glasgow, christened 21 May 1738, Covington and Thankerton, Lanarkshire; son of George Glasgow and Agnes Williamson.</ref>

No record has yet been found that proves what happened to James after his christening.

=== Possible Adult Records ===

A James Glasgow was recorded as the father of Robert Glasgow, christened on 17 February 1760 in Wandell and Lamington. Robert's mother was Marion Saidler.<ref name="Robert1760">"Scotland Births and Baptisms, 1564-1950," database, FamilySearch ([https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XTFT-KJ9 record XTFT-KJ9], accessed 16 September 2021), Robert Glasgow, christened 17 February 1760, Wandell and Lamington, Lanarkshire; son of James Glasgow and Marion Saidler.</ref>

A James Glasgow married [[Scott-41785|Mary Scott]] on 20 June 1766 in Wandell and Lamington.<ref name="Marriage1766">"Scotland Marriages, 1561-1910," database, FamilySearch ([https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XT1J-9QW record XT1J-9QW], accessed 11 February 2020), marriage of James Glasgow and Mary Scott, 20 June 1766, Wandell and Lamington, Lanarkshire.</ref>

James and Mary had a son:

* George Glasgow, christened on 7 May 1769 in Wandell and Lamington.<ref name="George1769">"Scotland Births and Baptisms, 1564-1950," database, FamilySearch ([https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XTFT-2J1 record XTFT-2J1], accessed 16 September 2021), George Glasgow, christened 7 May 1769, Wandell and Lamington, Lanarkshire; son of James Glasgow and Mary Scot.</ref>

== Research Notes ==

The 1766 marriage and the 1769 christening appear to concern the same James Glasgow. Both records are from Wandell and Lamington, and both name his wife as Mary Scott or Mary Scot.

The 1760 record may also concern the same man. This would suggest that Marion Saidler died, or that the earlier relationship ended, before James married Mary Scott in 1766. No supporting burial or marriage record has yet been found.

The James christened in 1738 is a reasonable candidate for the man recorded at Wandell and Lamington because:

* Covington and Thankerton is close to Wandell and Lamington.
* He would have been about 22 in 1760 and 28 in 1766.
* His father was named George, and James and Mary named their son George.

Other possible men named James Glasgow were reviewed, but currently appear less likely: 

* [[Glasgow-1469|James Glasgow (1736-)]], christened in 1736, was associated with Kilwinning, Ayrshire, and is said to have moved to Cookstown, County Tyrone, Ireland, around 1750. This conflicts with the later records in Wandell and Lamington. 
* [[Glasgow-3486|James Glasgow (1730-)]], born around 1730 in Kirknewton and East Calder, was associated with Katharine Rule and their daughter Marion, born in 1751. No record currently connects him to Wandell and Lamington, Marion Saidler or Mary Scott.

None of these points proves that the records concern the same man. The parish records do not give James's age, parents, occupation or exact residence.

The original register images should be checked for further details, particularly James's residence, occupation, marital status and witnesses.

=== Robert and Margaret Brash ===

[[Glasgow-1538|Robert Glasgow, Margaret Brash’s husband]], may be the same adult as [[Glasgow-1530|Robert Glasgow]]. The older widower sequence and age-79 burial favour the 1760 Wandell and Lamington child. James Glasgow and Marion Saidler are selected as probable parents of Robert-1538, 62% each, dependent on that unresolved identity chain. This does not prove two distinct sons named Robert, reconcile Robert’s competing births, or establish James’s own childhood.
