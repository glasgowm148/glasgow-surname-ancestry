## Current conclusion — newspaper identity review, 6 October 2026

- [Maryland Gazette, 1777-10-23, p.2](https://www.newspapers.com/image/41048937/): Samuel Glasgow (absent soldier, First Maryland Regiment, ordered to return) — **possible** match. Soldier identity (not a WikiTree profile) is strong: the MSA Biographical Series entry for Samuel Glasgow, First Maryland Regiment (private 1776, corporal 1776, sergeant 1777), records his desertion from camp at Morristown on 12 July 1777 and a deserter advertisement (Maryland Journal, December 1777) giving his birth in Cecil County 1753/54 - the same man as this notice's absent Samuel of the First Maryland. The MSA biography says he 'may have been' the Samuel son of Ann and James Glasgow Sr. (d. 1784) of Cecil County, with siblings Ezekiel (d. 1780), Adam (d. 1782-83), James, Elizabeth Mary, William and Hannah - which is the WikiTree family of Glasgow-2868 and Hartshorne-320 (children 1134 Samuel, 3289 Hannah, 3290 James, 3291 Elizabeth Mary, 3292 Adam d. 1782, 3293 Ezekiel d. 1780). So the soldier -> Glasgow-1134 link rests on name + Cecil birth + a family MSA itself calls uncertain. ## Identity assessment — newspaper candidates withdrawn, 6 October 2026

- [Aurora General Advertiser, 1791-09-21, p.2](https://www.newspapers.com/image/584905489/): no longer a candidate for this profile. Samuel Glasgow is listed under Washington County among Pennsylvania justices of the peace in commission on 1 September 1791. County records identify him as Samuel Glasgow, Esquire, of Hanover Township: commissioned 7 May 1788, previously a Peters Township wheelwright, and still a JP in the 1800 Hanover assessment. Glasgow-3175 (died 1801, Hanover Township) is the better candidate, but Glasgow-1134 (later of Hanover, Beaver County) has not been excluded, so the match stays possible.
