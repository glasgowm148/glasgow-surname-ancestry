# Findings: [Nathaniel Glasgow M.A. (Glasgow-1136), Presbyterian minister, died 1743](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1136)

Last updated: 2026-09-08

## Current conclusion

The Findmypast/Dobson record `US/GPC/02394306` is a high-confidence match to
this profile. Its underlying entry is Nathaniel's 1707 M.A. graduation at the
University of Glasgow and describes him as `Scoto-Hibernus`. “Scots-Irish” is
the source title, not evidence of United States residence. The live biography
already records the graduation; use this as confirming evidence only.

The profile's present 1732 death is wrong. Contemporary Synod minutes report
that Nathaniel Glasgow died in **April 1743**. Later Synod assistance to the
widow `Glasgow` and to the plural `children of Mr Glasgow, deceased` shows that
he left a widow and at least two children, but names none of them.

The 1746 Clogher probate entry for **Nathaniel Glasgow, resident at Cavan,
Donaghcavey** is a strong match. Donaghcavey contains Fintona, the later name
used for his Golen congregation. The rare forename, matching locality and
three-year interval make delayed probate much more likely than a second man.

No inspected record proves that Nathaniel was a son or grandson of Archibald
Glasgow of Ramelton or Thomas Glasgow of Lifford. His `Scoto-Hibernus`
university description also does not prove birth in Glasgow city.

The original Synod sequence materially corrects his early ministry. Monaghan
Presbytery had entered him on first trials by 1715; by 1718 he was on second
trials under **Augher Presbytery**, intended for America. Augher ordained him on
or about **3 February 1718/19** for that purpose. A June 1722 minute says he had
never yet held charge of a congregation, directly weakening the later claim
that he was installed at Ballyjamesduff in 1721. He supplied Newtown in October
1723 and was again associated with Monaghan Presbytery in 1724 before his proved
installation at Golan in 1732.
