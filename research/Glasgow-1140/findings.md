# Findings: James Glasgow (Glasgow-1140), baptised 17 March 1700

## Current conclusion

The Irish settlement claim on the profile is disproved. It results from both a
name/grammar error and reliance on documents exposed as forged in the Crawfurd
peerage litigation.

The recipient of the genuine part of the 23 March 1720 letter was Robert
Glasgow, factor at Kilbirnie. However, the supposed Irish settler was **James
Crawfurd**, brother of Viscount Garnock—not a brother of Robert Glasgow and not
James Glasgow `Glasgow-1140`.

## Source findings

| Source | Finding | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| James Dobie, [*Examination of the Claim of John Lindsay Crawfurd* (1831), pp. 102–106](https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/9537/95375734.23.pdf) | Describes the genuine letter to Robert Glasgow and identifies the four-line reference to Garnock's brother James settling in Ireland as a later insertion by William Bradley. | Directly disproves the profile's use of the 1720 letter. |
| Same, pp. 105–106 | Names the person in the supposed 1725 remittance as **James Crawfurd** and states that both the account entry and corresponding receipt were forged. | Directly disproves the profile's second Irish claim. |
| Kilbirnie Old Parish Register, reference 596/10/30 | Records the 17 March 1700 baptism of James, son of John Glasgow and Margaret Scott. | Direct for the Scottish child and parents; silent on Ireland. |

The profile statement that the financial transaction showed James Glasgow was
well established in Ireland should therefore be removed, not merely marked
uncertain.
