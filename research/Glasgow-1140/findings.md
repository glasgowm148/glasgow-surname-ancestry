# Findings: James Glasgow (Glasgow-1140), baptised 17 March 1700

## Current conclusion

The Irish settlement claim on the profile is disproved. It results from both a
name/grammar error and reliance on documents exposed as forged in the Crawfurd
peerage litigation.

The recipient of the genuine part of the 23 March 1720 letter was Robert
Glasgow, factor at Kilbirnie. However, the supposed Irish settler was **James
Crawfurd**, brother of Viscount Garnock—not a brother of Robert Glasgow and not
James Glasgow `Glasgow-1140`.

A separate genuine document now supplies James's adult status. NRS
`RH15/120/178` is a letter dated 22 November 1722 from Robert Glasgow at
Paddockholm to **James Glasgow, student of divinity at Glasgow, his brother**.
Robert-1075 was baptised at Kilbirnie in 1693 and is the established
Paddockholm man. The document therefore identifies an adult James in this
family and places him at Glasgow as a divinity student. It does not state his
age or parents, and it supplies no evidence that he settled in Ireland or
became a captain.
