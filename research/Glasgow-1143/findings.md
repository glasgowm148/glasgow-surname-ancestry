## Current conclusion

The Findmypast/Tepper entry `US/GPC/00345374` is a high-confidence match to
this profile. The image reads “Glasgow, Robt., Ballykeel, Co. Ant. Lease. Hill
Docts. 1738. (O.D.).” It is a lease index, not evidence of United States
migration. Ballykeel is in Kilwaughter parish, and the date and property
context coincide with Robert's already documented 1738 Agnew lease.

Robert married Rachel Wilson in November 1729 according to a derivative
congregation-register chronology. The same chronology directly records him and
Rachel as parents of James (24 May 1731), Ann (15 November 1733) and Rachel
(8 November 1738). Deed memorial 74015 and the 1817 equity case independently
corroborate the wife and the two surviving older children.

The equity evidence is unusually valuable because it comes from Robert's
grandson Robert Glasgow-1983. In sworn answers he calls Rachel his **paternal
grandmother**, Ann Glasgow his **father's sister**, and himself Rachel's eldest
male heir through her son James. It therefore supplies the collateral language
normally missing from the congregation register.

The same litigation reconstructs Rachel's Wilson family. Rachel was one of six
sisters of Archibald Wilson: Jane, Margaret, Rachel, Anne, Elizabeth and
Susanna. Ann Glasgow married Hugh Shannon. James Glasgow married Mary, daughter
of another Wilson sister, making James and Mary first cousins if the pleaded
relationships are accurate. Neither the pleadings nor James's will calls Mary a
Crozier or Crosier.

Original-image review of memorial 159661 (20 December 1764) now independently
confirms `Jas Glasgow son of the sd Robt` in Robert's Ballykeel lease to Hugh
Shannon. See the dated proof section below.

No inspected record identifies Robert's father. His current placement under
Glasgow-1502 is therefore unproved.

## McCracken alliance in Robert's descendant line

Robert's proved son James `Glasgow-1144` carried the family to Newberry, South
Carolina. James's will and the later equity case identify two of his children
in McCracken marriages: Rachel Glasgow married Robert McCrackin, and her
brother Wilson Crossier `Glasgow-1982` married Mary McCracken. Wilson and Mary's
daughter Mariah then married William McCracken. This is therefore a compact
pattern within Robert's own descendant line: two siblings and then a child of
one sibling married McCrackens across two generations.

No inspected source states how Robert, Mary and William McCracken were related.
The key comparison with the nineteenth-century Carnfinton–Lisnagaver cluster is
not merely the surname repetition but whether both sets represent repeated
marriage with one extended McCracken family. The Newberry marriages do not yet
prove a pre-migration Antrim association.

The public Glasgow Y-DNA project places kit 254947, submitted for this Robert
c.1710 line, in a working FT25406 subgroup from its STR signature. It is not yet
SNP-confirmed and the project explicitly requests a Big-Y upgrade. At 37
published markers it differs by two single steps from the James-1731 kit and six
from the FT20271 William-attributed kit. This supports broader paternal kinship,
not a particular father, grandfather or paper pedigree.

estimated at thirty-one acres and a half, with half the office houses and
associated appurtenances, held under William Agnew of Kilwaughter.

The continuation expressly includes `Jas Glasgow son of the sd Robt` among the
lease lives, alongside Robert Glasgow the grantor and Elinor [surname not
legible on the saved screenshot; the FamilySearch machine text reads Jones] otherwise
Agnew, daughter of William Agnew. **This is direct contemporary father–son
wording**, rather than an inference from the `James Glasgow junior` life in
memorial 74015 (13 February 1738/9). Read in the same Ballykeel tenure and independently
documented household, it corroborates Robert-1143 as father of James-1144.

residence: those identity details still depend on the register and other records.

Hugh Shannon's participation is proved, but this lease does **not** call him

The deed and memorial were witnessed by James Shutter and Hugh Kennedy; the

## Identity assessment — the Robert of the session list and of 1747, 6 October 2026

The list Robert and the 1747 Robert are one man: the list covers every session member from 1721 to 1769, names one Robert, and Porter links its note to the 1747 minute. From his place on the list he was probably made an elder between mid-1721 and 1747 (inference from list order).

He is **probably Robert Glasgow-1143** (inference; no record states it). Robert Glasgow-1502, an adult bondsman in May 1700, would have been in his late sixties or older in 1747, while Robert-1143, married in 1729, was about forty and is documented at Kilwaughter in 1738/9, 1741 and 1764. A charge of fornication implies that the offender was unmarried when the offence was committed, or that it preceded a marriage. For Robert-1143 that fits only if his wife Rachel had died, which is known only from a derivative transcription of an 1817 pleading ("died in 1742"); it is consistent with the identification, not evidence for it. A third, otherwise unrecorded Robert cannot be excluded, because the session minutes for 1701-1721 are lost.

No record securely attributable to Robert-1502 is known after 27 February 1700/01.

## Identity assessment — "James Glasgow junior" in the 1738/9 lease, 6 October 2026

"Jas Glasgow Junr" is one of the three lives in William Agnew's lease of Ballykeel to Robert Glasgow, dated 13 February 1738 (1738/9 by modern reckoning; Registry of Deeds memorial 74015, re-read on the saved register images). "Junior" most naturally distinguishes him from another James Glasgow known to the parties, usually a living one, but it does not prove one and it states no relationship. The memorial names no other James.

The officer James Glasgow of Kilwaughter was dead by then ("deceased 1726"; will indexed 1727). The attested candidates for an older James alive in 1738-44 are James Glasgow-1504 (married 1727, children 1728-1740 in the derivative register chronology, residence unrecorded) and James Glasgow of Larne, who took a townparks lease on 30 August 1744 (PRONI D2977/3A/4/81/116, known from a catalogue line only); they may be one man.
