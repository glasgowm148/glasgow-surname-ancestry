## Current conclusion

James's 1775 will names his wife Mary and six children: Robert, John, Archibald,
Wilson, Margaret and Rachel. The 1817 equity complaint repeats the children,
identifies Margaret as wife of John Kinard and Rachel as wife of Robert
McCrackin, and identifies James as a son of Rachel Wilson. Robert Glasgow-1983's
sworn answer independently calls James his father, Mary his mother and Rachel
his paternal grandmother. The amended answer makes Archibald Wilson Rachel's
brother, so it also identifies James's maternal uncle and Robert's paternal
great-uncle.

Rachel's McCrackin marriage was part of a repeated alliance within this exact
family. Her brother Wilson Crossier `Glasgow-1982` married Mary McCracken, and
their daughter Mariah married William McCracken. The three unions span two
successive generations of Robert `Glasgow-1143`'s descendant line. Their force
depends on identifying the relationships among Robert, Mary and William
McCracken; no inspected record yet supplies those relationships or an Irish
origin for the Newberry McCrackens.

The congregation-register chronology additionally records James born 24 May 1731
to Robert and Rachel Glasgow. The conclusions are strongly supported by concordant
transcriptions of distinct original records. Original images remain the final check.

The equity pleadings say James's wife Mary was a daughter of one of Rachel
Wilson's five sisters. On the pleaded relationships James and Mary were first
cousins. Neither the pleadings nor James's will gives Mary the surname
Crozier/Crosier or identifies which sister was her mother. The structured
`Crosier-512` identity therefore exceeds the documentary evidence.

Public Y-DNA kit B835762, submitted with James William Glasgow (1731-1775) as
earliest paternal ancestor, is closest to marker-only Henry-root kit 325862 at
one allele step across Y-37; the GroupingApp independently places both in Tight
Subgroup 1. Neither kit has a terminal SNP result. This is a provisional
FT25406-side lead—not proof of FT25406 membership—and needs a Big-Y upgrade. It
corroborates a paternal neighbourhood but does not replace the documentary
parentage above.

## McCrackin probate and descendant update — 13 August 2026

King’s Creek cemetery evidence now uniquely identifies Rachel's husband as the
Robert McCrackin who died 10 August 1817 aged 46. Ruth and Pulauski are
explicitly called children of R. and R. McCrackin. Cyrus McCrackin, who
administered Robert's 1817 estate and is buried in the same family plot, is a
strong but still unproved son candidate. The 1821 Robert estate belongs to a
different man.

The 1790 Newberry transcript has five McCrackin households, not two: two James
households plus Arthur, John and Thomas. If Robert, then about 19, was already
in Newberry, Thomas's one-adult-male household cannot contain him; a James or
Arthur household could. Arrival after 1790 remains a competing explanation.
Volume II of Bundrick and Suber's probate abstracts covers boxes 11–25, exactly
including Thomas 1804, William 1805 and Arthur 1812/1817. William's estate,
jointly administered by Thomas and Robert, remains the best non-PRONI kinship
target; no free online copy of volume II was located.

The same public collection's links for South Carolina implied marriages,
volumes VI and VII, were checked as a further-marriage route. Each download is
only a two-page cover/sample file and contains no searchable Glasgow or
McCracken entry; it is not the advertised full volume.

The grandchild generation has also expanded. Hiram `Glasgow-3995`, a son of
Wilson and Mary, married Mary Ann Brandon Kennedy. Their daughter Cleora
Glasgow married Leland McNary Speers on 17 October 1867; their son Leonidas is
independently identified in a biographical account. John Leland Glasgow,
Absolom's son, married Sarah Owens and later Susannah Cromer. These marriages
extend the Newberry descendant map but do not identify Robert McCrackin's
father.

## Correction: 1768 Larne assignment — 20 August 2026

The 28 March 1768 assignment should be removed from this profile. Inspection of the [original memorial image](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-DDXW) shows that the assignor was **James Glasgow, ropemaker**, not soapmaker. He was assigning the plot, workhouse and remainder of the same ropewalk lease granted in 1760 to James Glasgow of Belfast, ropemaker. The occupational and property continuity, followed by a Larne “Roper” reference in Alexander Holmes's 1799 will, identifies the record with the local ropemaker represented by `Glasgow-1509`, not with the South Carolina settler.

The same 1768 memorial separately names another James Glasgow, a tanner, as witness. That tanner sequence is treated under `Glasgow-3991`. The deed therefore documents two contemporary Larne men and cannot be used as evidence that this profile disposed of property before emigrating.
