== Biography ==
'''James Glasgow''' was born before 16 June 1641, probably in Scotland. His precise date and place of birth have not been identified.

He became a weaver and burgess of Edinburgh.

=== Apprenticeship and burgess status ===

On 16 June 1641 James, explicitly described as a son of '''Robert Glasgow, servant to William Crichtoun in Wester Duddingston''', was apprenticed to William Sclaitter, weaver.<ref name="Apprentice1641">Francis J. Grant, ed., ''Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh, 1583–1666'' (Scottish Record Society, 1906), printed p. 71, [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso28scotuoft/page/71/mode/2up scan]: “Glasgow, James, son to Robert G., servitor to William Crichtoun, in Wester Duddingston, with William Sclaitter, weaver 16 June 1641.”</ref>

James was admitted as a burgess of Edinburgh on 20 June 1649. The entry identifies him as a weaver, states that he had been apprenticed to the deceased William Sklatter, and repeats the apprenticeship date of 16 June 1641.<ref name="Burgess1649">Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=218 ''Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses and Guild-Brethren, 1406–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1929), printed p. 208: “Glasgow, James, B., weiver, as p. to umq. Wm. Sklaitter, weiver, B. (16 June 1641) 20 June 1649.”</ref>

The matching master and exact apprenticeship date establish that the 1641 apprentice was the later Edinburgh weaver and burgess.

=== Marriages ===

James married first '''[[Whyte-2772|Bessie Whyte]]''' at Edinburgh on 6 July 1649.<ref name="EdinburghMarriages">Henry Paton, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_marriages_for_the_parish_of_edinburgh_1595-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=277 ''Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1905), printed p. 267: “Glasgow, James, weaver; Bessie Whyte 6 July 1649” and “James, weaver; Agnes Duncan 11 Nov. 1653.”</ref>

He married second '''[[Duncan-16106|Agnes Duncan]]''' at Edinburgh on 11 November 1653.<ref name="EdinburghMarriages" />

The matching name, occupation and parish, together with the sequential dates, identify the bridegroom in both entries as the same James Glasgow.

=== Children ===

James's known children were:

* '''[[Glasgow-3097|Helen Glasgow]]''', daughter of Bessie Whyte;
* '''[[Glasgow-3099|Jonet Glasgow]]''', daughter of Bessie Whyte;
* '''[[Glasgow-1374|Robert Glasgow]]''', son of Agnes Duncan;<ref name="RobertBaptism">“Scotland, Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950,” database, ''FamilySearch'', [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VRSV-T1T Robert Glasgow, baptised 5 May 1655, Edinburgh], son of James Glasgow and Agnes Duncan.</ref>
* '''[[Glasgow-3143|Marion Glasgow]]''', daughter of Agnes Duncan;
* '''[[Glasgow-1373|Bethia Glasgow]]''', daughter of Agnes Duncan; and
* '''[[Glasgow-1057|John Glasgow]]''', son of Agnes Duncan.

The individual baptismal records should be consulted for the evidence identifying each child's mother.

Marion Glasgow married William Calder at Edinburgh on 29 June 1686.<ref name="MarionMarriage">Henry Paton, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_marriages_for_the_parish_of_edinburgh_1595-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=278 ''Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1905), printed p. 268: “Glasgow, Marion; William Calder 29 June 1686.”</ref>

On the following day William Calder was admitted as a burgess through his wife Marion, who was explicitly described as a daughter of James Glasgow, weaver and burgess.<ref name="CalderBurgess">Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=101 ''Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses and Guild-Brethren, 1406–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1929), printed p. 91: “Calder, Wm., B., weaver, be r. of w. Marion, dr. to James Glasgow, weaver, B. 30 June 1686.”</ref>

This directly establishes Marion as James's daughter.

James's date and place of death have not been identified. The maintained master-apprentice records place him alive on 18 October 1693; their full citations are retained below.

=== Duddingston father assessment — 17 October 2026 ===

[[Glasgow-1156|Robert Glasgow of Duddingston]] is now assessed as the probable father of [[Glasgow-1150|James Glasgow, Edinburgh weaver]], the Edinburgh weaver. Grant’s 16 June 1641 apprenticeship names James’s father Robert and places the entry at Wester Duddingston with William Crichtoun and master William Sclaitter. The 20 June 1649 burgess admission repeats Sclaitter’s name and the exact apprenticeship date, securely connecting the recorded son to the adult weaver. The Robert buried at Duddingston on 17 January 1647 fits the father’s name, parish and preceding generation, six years after the apprenticeship and before James’s independent household began in 1649. Taken together, the specific local father and the Duddingston burial favour one Robert over an otherwise unattested second contemporary Robert in that parish. The previous assessment treated the lack of a unique identifier as a reason to stop at plausible; the combined argument warrants probable, while remaining an inference.

The apprenticeship proves James’s father was Robert; identifying that father with the burial account [[Glasgow-1156|Robert Glasgow of Duddingston]] is probable rather than documented. The burial index gives no age, son, wife or occupation, and burial in the parish does not itself prove residence at Wester Duddingston. A second local Robert remains possible. The two craft entries are a linked identity chain, not two independent statements naming the father. The exact scope of “servitor” in the printed apprenticeship wording need not decide the inference: the entry supplies the father’s name and Duddingston association. No mother is identified, so Deborah Ivey is not upgraded. Robert’s unsupported Archibald-1097 ancestry and his compatible identification with Andrew-3163’s recorded son Robert-4006 remain separate questions. Neither the later Fisherrow sasine identity nor the Edinburgh weaver’s possible Lindsaylands continuation is needed for this father conclusion.

=== Parentage ===

The apprenticeship record directly identifies James's father as '''Robert Glasgow, servant to William Crichtoun in Wester Duddingston'''.<ref name="Apprentice1641" />

'''[[Glasgow-1156|Robert Glasgow]]''' is now the probable father-profile match on the combined Duddingston burial, apprenticeship and burgess evidence. The named father is direct; identification with the burial account remains inferred. The live WikiTree father flag remains uncertain.

The record does not name James's mother.

'''[[Ivey-2264|Deborah Ivey]]''' is not identified as his mother in any inspected record. Any attachment should remain uncertain and be treated as a working hypothesis rather than an established relationship.

=== Distinct from the Inveresk James ===

This James was not '''[[Glasgow-3923|James Glasgow]]''', who was baptised at Inveresk and Musselburgh on 19 March 1626 as a son of [[Glasgow-1148|Alexander Glasgow]] and [[Clerk-296|Jannet Clerk]].<ref name="InvereskBaptism">“Scotland, Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950,” database, ''FamilySearch'', James Glasgow, baptised 19 March 1626, Inveresk and Musselburgh, son of Alexander Glasgow, [https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VQ4V-GQW record VQ4V-GQW] (accessed 2025).</ref>

The 1641 apprenticeship names Robert Glasgow, not Alexander Glasgow, as the father of the Edinburgh weaver. The two men should remain on separate profiles.

=== Incorrect child profile ===

'''[[Glasgow-1376|James Glasgow]]''' is not a supported child of this family and should not be attached as a son born on 29 January 1657.

The cited record for that date is the baptism of Marion Glasgow and names James Glasgow as her father. It is not a birth or baptism record for a son named James.

=== Identity caution ===

This James should not be confused with '''[[Glasgow-3077|James Glasgow]]''', who married [[Clerk-460|Helen Clerk]] and was associated with [[Glasgow-3093|John Glasgow]].

The Edinburgh weaver is identified by:

* the 1641 apprenticeship naming him as a son of Robert Glasgow;
* his apprenticeship to William Sclaitter;
* his 1649 admission as an Edinburgh burgess;
* his occupation as a weaver;
* his marriages to Bessie Whyte and Agnes Duncan; and
* the 1686 burgess record identifying Marion Glasgow as his daughter.
