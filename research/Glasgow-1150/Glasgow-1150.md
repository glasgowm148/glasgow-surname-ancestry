== Biography ==
'''James Glasgow''' was born before 16 June 1641, probably in Scotland. His precise date and place of birth have not been identified.

He became a weaver and burgess of Edinburgh.

=== Apprenticeship and burgess status ===

On 16 June 1641 James, explicitly described as a son of '''Robert Glasgow, servant to William Crichtoun in Wester Duddingston''', was apprenticed to William Sclaitter, weaver.<ref name="Apprentice1641">Francis J. Grant, ed., ''Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh, 1583–1666'' (Scottish Record Society, 1906), printed p. 71, [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso28scotuoft/page/71/mode/2up scan]: “Glasgow, James, son to Robert G., servitor to William Crichtoun, in Wester Duddingston, with William Sclaitter, weaver 16 June 1641.”</ref>

James was admitted as a burgess of Edinburgh on 20 June 1649. The entry identifies him as a weaver, states that he had been apprenticed to the deceased William Sklatter, and repeats the apprenticeship date of 16 June 1641.<ref name="Burgess1649">Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=218 ''Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses and Guild-Brethren, 1406–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1929), printed p. 208: “Glasgow, James, B., weiver, as p. to umq. Wm. Sklaitter, weiver, B. (16 June 1641) 20 June 1649.”</ref>

The matching master and exact apprenticeship date establish that the 1641 apprentice was the later Edinburgh weaver and burgess.

=== Marriages ===

James married first '''[[Whyte-2772|Bessie Whyte]]''' at Edinburgh on 6 July 1649.<ref name="EdinburghMarriages">Henry Paton, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_marriages_for_the_parish_of_edinburgh_1595-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=277 ''Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1905), printed p. 267: “Glasgow, James, weaver; Bessie Whyte 6 July 1649” and “James, weaver; Agnes Duncan 11 Nov. 1653.”</ref>

He married second '''[[Duncan-16106|Agnes Duncan]]''' at Edinburgh on 11 November 1653.<ref name="EdinburghMarriages" />

The matching name, occupation and parish, together with the sequential dates, identify the bridegroom in both entries as the same James Glasgow.

=== Children ===

James's known children were:

* '''[[Glasgow-3097|Helen Glasgow]]''', daughter of Bessie Whyte;
* '''[[Glasgow-3099|Jonet Glasgow]]''', daughter of Bessie Whyte;
* '''[[Glasgow-1374|Robert Glasgow]]''', son of Agnes Duncan;<ref name="RobertBaptism">“Scotland, Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950,” database, ''FamilySearch'', [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VRSV-T1T Robert Glasgow, baptised 5 May 1655, Edinburgh], son of James Glasgow and Agnes Duncan.</ref>
* '''[[Glasgow-3143|Marion Glasgow]]''', daughter of Agnes Duncan;
* '''[[Glasgow-1373|Bethia Glasgow]]''', daughter of Agnes Duncan; and
* '''[[Glasgow-1057|John Glasgow]]''', son of Agnes Duncan.

The individual baptismal records should be consulted for the evidence identifying each child's mother.

Marion Glasgow married William Calder at Edinburgh on 29 June 1686.<ref name="MarionMarriage">Henry Paton, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_marriages_for_the_parish_of_edinburgh_1595-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=278 ''Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1905), printed p. 268: “Glasgow, Marion; William Calder 29 June 1686.”</ref>

On the following day William Calder was admitted as a burgess through his wife Marion, who was explicitly described as a daughter of James Glasgow, weaver and burgess.<ref name="CalderBurgess">Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=101 ''Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses and Guild-Brethren, 1406–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1929), printed p. 91: “Calder, Wm., B., weaver, be r. of w. Marion, dr. to James Glasgow, weaver, B. 30 June 1686.”</ref>

This directly establishes Marion as James's daughter.

James's date and place of death have not been identified. The maintained master-apprentice records place him alive on 18 October 1693; their full citations are retained below.

The adjacent 16 June 1646 date is Isobel Givan’s, not Alexander’s.

The documented Edinburgh weaver is the leading possible identity for this indexed James: trade, regional setting and the interval between his 1641 apprenticeship and 1649 burgess admission fit. The index itself does not give James’s own residence, father, spouse or role in the instrument. Alexander’s son [[Glasgow-3923|James Glasgow]], baptised at Inveresk in 1626, is a real rival; his father Alexander differs from this weaver’s apprenticeship-proved father Robert of Wester Duddingston. [[Glasgow-991|James Glasgow]], the earlier Fisherrow tailor, could have changed trade. [[Glasgow-3332|James Glasgow]], associated tentatively with the later Lindsaylands weaver, is a weaker comparison whose estimated birth is not a hard exclusion. [[Glasgow-3910|James Glasgow of Quothquan]] had died by 1624 and cannot be a living party in 1646, though an unread deed could mention a deceased predecessor. The instruments, not repeated index references, must decide capacity and kinship.
