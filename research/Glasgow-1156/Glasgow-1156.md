== Biography ==
'''Robert Glasgow''' was buried in the parish of Duddingston, Midlothian, Scotland, on '''17 January 1647'''.<ref name="RobertBurial">Church of Scotland, Old Parish Registers, Duddingston deaths and burials, burial of Robert Glasgow, 17 January 1647, parish number 684, reference 60/47, ''ScotlandsPeople''.</ref>

His date and place of birth have not been identified.

The burial entry does not state Robert's age, occupation, residence, spouse, parentage or children. It should not be assumed that every contemporary man named Robert Glasgow in Midlothian was the same person.

=== Duddingston father assessment — 17 October 2026 ===

[[Glasgow-1156|Robert Glasgow of Duddingston]] is now assessed as the probable father of [[Glasgow-1150|James Glasgow, Edinburgh weaver]], the Edinburgh weaver. Grant’s 16 June 1641 apprenticeship names James’s father Robert and places the entry at Wester Duddingston with William Crichtoun and master William Sclaitter. The 20 June 1649 burgess admission repeats Sclaitter’s name and the exact apprenticeship date, securely connecting the recorded son to the adult weaver. The Robert buried at Duddingston on 17 January 1647 fits the father’s name, parish and preceding generation, six years after the apprenticeship and before James’s independent household began in 1649. Taken together, the specific local father and the Duddingston burial favour one Robert over an otherwise unattested second contemporary Robert in that parish. The previous assessment treated the lack of a unique identifier as a reason to stop at plausible; the combined argument warrants probable, while remaining an inference.

The apprenticeship proves James’s father was Robert; identifying that father with the burial account [[Glasgow-1156|Robert Glasgow of Duddingston]] is probable rather than documented. The burial index gives no age, son, wife or occupation, and burial in the parish does not itself prove residence at Wester Duddingston. A second local Robert remains possible. The two craft entries are a linked identity chain, not two independent statements naming the father. The exact scope of “servitor” in the printed apprenticeship wording need not decide the inference: the entry supplies the father’s name and Duddingston association. No mother is identified, so Deborah Ivey is not upgraded. Robert’s unsupported Archibald-1097 ancestry and his compatible identification with Andrew-3163’s recorded son Robert-4006 remain separate questions. Neither the later Fisherrow sasine identity nor the Edinburgh weaver’s possible Lindsaylands continuation is needed for this father conclusion.

=== Profile identity ===

This profile represents the '''Robert Glasgow buried at Duddingston on 17 January 1647'''.

The burial is the only record presently identified that can be assigned to this profile with reasonable certainty. Other records involving men of the same name are discussed below as possible or rejected identifications.

Because the record is a burial entry rather than an identified death entry, Robert died on or before 17 January 1647. His precise date of death is unknown.

=== Birth ===

The structured birth year of about 1582 and the Glasgow birthplace are retained as uncertain working estimates.

No inspected record states Robert's age or birthplace or supports either estimate. They should not be treated as proved facts.

Although Robert was buried in Scotland, the burial record does not establish that he was born there.

=== Possible identification at Wester Duddingston ===

On 16 June 1641 '''[[Glasgow-1150|James Glasgow]]''', son of a Robert Glasgow who was servant to William Crichtoun in Wester Duddingston, was apprenticed to William Sclaitter, weaver.<ref name="JamesApprentice">Francis J. Grant, ed., [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso28scotuoft/page/71/mode/2up ''Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh, 1583–1666''] (Scottish Record Society, 1906), printed p. 71: “Glasgow, James, son to Robert G., servitor to William Crichtoun, in Wester Duddingston, with William Sclaitter, weaver 16 June 1641.”</ref>

The record directly proves that:

* James's father was named Robert Glasgow;
* the elder Robert was associated with Wester Duddingston in 1641;
* Robert was then a servant to William Crichtoun; and
* James was apprenticed to William Sclaitter.

The matching name, Duddingston association and compatible 1647 burial, combined with the exact 1641 apprenticeship cross-reference in the 1649 burgess admission, favour this Robert as the probable father of the Edinburgh weaver.

The identification is not proved. Neither record supplies Robert's age, wife, occupation beyond the 1641 description, or another distinguishing relationship that securely joins the two entries.

James is now assessed locally as a probable son. The live WikiTree father flag remains uncertain because the burial-to-father identity is inferred rather than documented.

=== James Glasgow the Edinburgh weaver ===

The apprentice named in 1641 was later admitted as a burgess of Edinburgh on 20 June 1649. The burgess entry identifies him as a weaver, names his deceased master William Sklaitter and repeats the exact apprenticeship date of 16 June 1641.<ref name="JamesBurgess">Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=218 ''Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses and Guild-Brethren, 1406–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1929), printed p. 208: “Glasgow, James, B., weiver, as p. to umq. Wm. Sklaitter, weiver, B. (16 June 1641) 20 June 1649.”</ref>

The matching master and exact apprenticeship date establish that the 1641 apprentice and the later Edinburgh burgess were the same James Glasgow.

This evidence strengthens the identification of James but does not provide additional information identifying his father with the man buried in 1647.

=== Distinct from the Inveresk James ===

The Edinburgh apprentice was not '''[[Glasgow-3923|James Glasgow]]''', who was baptised at Inveresk and Musselburgh on 19 March 1626 as a son of Alexander Glasgow and Jannet Clerk.<ref name="InvereskJames">“Scotland, Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950,” database, ''FamilySearch'', [https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VQ4V-GQW James Glasgow, baptised 19 March 1626, Inveresk and Musselburgh], son of Alexander Glasgow.</ref>

The 1641 apprenticeship explicitly names Robert Glasgow, not Alexander Glasgow, as the father of the Edinburgh weaver.

The two men should remain on separate profiles.

=== Possible father ===

'''[[Glasgow-1097|Archibald Glasgow]]''' is attached as Robert's uncertain father.

Archibald and his wife Isobell Neulands are documented acquiring a tenement in Glasgow on 21 May 1551.<ref name="Archibald1551">Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. 1, ''First Protocol Book of William Hegait, 1547–55'' (Glasgow: Carson & Nicol, 1894), protocol 84, 21 May 1551, printed p. 31: Elizabeth Beitht sold a tenement to “Archinbald Glasgw and Isobell Neulands, spouses.” Robert Glasgw was among the witnesses.</ref>

The record does not name any children of Archibald and Isobell. It also does not state that Robert Glasgw, one of the witnesses, was Archibald's brother, son or other relative.

The proposed father-and-son relationship is chronologically possible. Archibald was married by 1551, and Robert's birth date is unknown. A son born later in the sixteenth century could have been the Robert associated with Wester Duddingston in 1641 and buried there in 1647.

There is presently no direct evidence establishing the relationship. No baptism, testament, sasine, deed or other inspected contemporary record identifies Robert as Archibald's son.

There is likewise no known record identifying a different father for Robert, and no better-supported profile has been identified as a child of Archibald.

Archibald may therefore remain attached as Robert's father with the relationship marked '''uncertain'''. The relationship should not be described as proved, probable or likely without further evidence.


=== Validator chronology note ===
WikiTree's warning that [[Glasgow-1097|Archibald Glasgow]] died before Robert was born is not a valid reading of the structured data. Archibald's death is recorded as '''after 1560''', an open lower boundary rather than a death in 1560. Robert's 1582 birth is an uncertain estimate, and their father-son relationship is itself marked uncertain. No source currently proves when Archibald died or that Robert was his son.

=== Deborah Ivey ===

'''[[Ivey-2264|Deborah Ivey]]''' has previously been attached as Robert's wife.

No Scottish marriage, baptism, testament, sasine, court record or other contemporary source has been found identifying Deborah as Robert's wife or as the mother of James Glasgow.

The attachment appears to derive from later compiled pedigrees involving Glasgow, Glasco, Glascocke or Glasscock families rather than from records concerning the Duddingston man.

Deborah should be detached from this profile.

=== Distinct from the Edinburgh cordiner ===

'''[[Glasgow-3938|Robert Glasgow]]''' married Jonet or Janet Thomsone at Edinburgh on 1 July 1652. The marriage register identifies that Robert as a cordiner.<ref name="CordinerMarriage">Henry Paton, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_marriages_for_the_parish_of_edinburgh_1595-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=278 ''Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1905), printed p. 268: “Glasgow, Robert, cordiner; Janet Thomsone 1 July 1652.”</ref>

The Duddingston Robert was buried in January 1647 and therefore could not have been the bridegroom in July 1652.

The two men are conclusively distinct and should remain on separate profiles.

No evidence presently identifies the Edinburgh cordiner as a son of the Duddingston Robert.

=== Daniel Glasco ===

Daniel Glasco, husband of Mary Gamon, is documented in Lower Norfolk County, Virginia, through the will of his father-in-law John Gamon, dated 4 October 1693 and proved 15 March 1694.

No inspected record identifies Daniel as a son of the Robert buried at Duddingston.

The supposed relationship depends on unsourced submitted pedigrees and should be removed.

There is likewise no evidence that the Duddingston Robert:

* travelled to Virginia;
* had children in colonial America;
* belonged to the Glasco, Glascocke or Glasscock families;
* married Ann Johnson or Johnston; or
* participated in any Scottish-to-Virginia migration.

=== Occupation and social status ===

If the 1641 Wester Duddingston record concerns this Robert, he was described as a servant to William Crichtoun.

The term does not establish that Robert was a merchant, burgess, property owner or participant in civic government. No evidence presently supports those descriptions.

There is also no evidence that Robert migrated from the burgh of Glasgow to Edinburgh or Duddingston. His surname alone does not identify his place of origin.

=== Wife and other children ===

No inspected record identifies the wife of the Robert buried in 1647.

James Glasgow is the only proposed child supported by a contemporary record naming a father called Robert Glasgow in Wester Duddingston. The combined record argument now makes him a probable son of this burial account; no other child is upgraded.

No other children should be attached without independent evidence.
