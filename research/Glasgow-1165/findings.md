# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1165)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1165)

## Current conclusion

Glasgow-1165 is not a defensible match for the James Glasgow of Killycurragh in the 1836 probate index. Although the structured profile reports death on 17 August 1836 in Ireland, its biography quotes a family Bible entry for a person born 9 November 1808 who died 20 April 1823, and the opening sentence calls him John rather than James. More importantly, the family register identifies him within John Glasgow and Mary Arthur's separate family, while PRONI T1959/3 identifies the Killycurragh man as a probable son of William Glasgow of Killycurragh and Miss Cunningham. The profile supplies no Killycurragh connection.

Do not attach the 1836 Killycurragh occurrence until this profile's name and vital dates are reconstructed from the cited family Bible.

## Tyrone probate exclusions

The family Bible death of 20 April 1823 predates both the 1826 administration
bond for James Glasgow, gentleman of Oritor, and the 21 March 1836 probate-index
entry for James Glasgow of Killycarragh. A delayed probate entry cannot be
excluded by chronology alone. The family structures in PRONI T1959/3 provide
the stronger exclusion: the Oritor man was probably the unmarried son of
William Glasgow of Drumore and Anne Rutherford, while the Killycurragh man was
probably the son of William Glasgow of Killycurragh and Miss Cunningham.
Neither record should be attached to this son of John Glasgow and Mary Arthur.

## Original family-register image

The cited original [Glasgow Bible register image](https://hiltner.com/mckeown/histories/8479.jpeg)
records James as born 9 November 1808 and dying after a sudden illness on
20 April 1823. The image proves the date but does not state the place of death.

The live profile still needs three corrections:

* structured death `17 August 1836` to `20 April 1823`;
* the biography's opening name `John` to `James`;
* relocation of the 1826 Oritor and 1836 Killycurragh entries to separate
  person notes because the relationship-defined families differ; do not rely
  on the probate index dates alone as proof that both men survived him.

The current structured death location must be preserved until a specific
sourced replacement location is established; the Bible's silence is not a
reason to clear it.
