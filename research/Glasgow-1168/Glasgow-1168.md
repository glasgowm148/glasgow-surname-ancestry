== Biography ==

'''James Glasgow''' was a tenant at Corsinkell in the parish and barony of Stevenston, Ayrshire. He was probably born about 1515, although his precise date and place of birth have not been identified.

The estimate is based on the presence of an adult or near-adult son, also named James, at Corsinkell by 1559–1560. It is an approximate generational estimate rather than a date stated in any record.

=== Corsinkell ===

A reconstructed list of Cunninghame teind debtors for 1559–1560 names Katherine Campbell, James Glasgow younger and James Glasgow elder under Corsinkell in Stevenston parish.<ref name="TeindList">Margaret H. B. Sanderson, [https://aanhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/people-of-ayrshire.pdf#page=36 “The People of Sixteenth Century Ayrshire,”] ''Ayrshire Collections'' 14, no. 8 (1987), appendix, “The Cunninghame Teind Lists of 1559–60,” printed p. 331, citing Register of Acts and Decreets, vol. 22, fols. 102v–167v.</ref>

The designations “elder” and “younger” establish that two men named James Glasgow were present at the same holding. That record alone does not state their relationship.

On 9 February 1571 letters of reversion concerning an annual rent from the 40-shilling land of Corsinkell named the occupiers as James Glasgow elder, James Glasgow and John Glasgow, his sons, and Christian Campbell.<ref name="NRS1571">National Records of Scotland, Charter Chest of the Earls of Glencairn, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F5%2F59 GD39/5/59], letters of reversion concerning annual rent from the 40-shilling land of Corsinkell, barony of Stevenston, sheriffdom of Ayr, dated 9 February 1571.</ref>

This directly establishes that James elder had sons named James and John.

=== Children ===

James's record-confirmed sons were:

* '''[[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow, younger]]''', present with James elder at Corsinkell in 1559–1560 and explicitly identified as his son in 1571; and
* '''[[Glasgow-3193|John Glasgow]]''', the record-defined Corsinkell son explicitly identified in 1571.

[[Glasgow-3193|John Glasgow]] represents the record-defined Corsinkell son following the merger of Glasgow-4061 into his profile. James’s fatherhood of that son is documented. The additional Kilwinning/Burrowland/Dalgarven occurrences remain probable identity attributions by name, generation and northern Ayrshire setting; no reviewed deed connects those holdings.

[[Glasgow-3178|Agnes Glasgow]] was formerly attached as a daughter, but no reviewed source identifies her parents. James younger is the selected uncertain working father on generational and Stevenston-locality grounds; James elder remains an alternative if Agnes was older. No mother or sisterhood is inferred. The 1571 record names two sons and does not address daughters.

=== Death ===

James was living on 9 February 1571.<ref name="NRS1571" />

His date and place of death have not been identified. The supported death statement is therefore '''after 9 February 1571'''. Stevenston is a plausible uncertain death locality from his last documented residence, rather than a recorded place of death.

A death date in or after November 1580 appears to derive from conflation with a different Glasgow family and should not be applied to this profile without additional evidence.

== Research Notes ==

=== Birth estimate ===

The former estimate of about 1540 is too late.

James Glasgow younger was separately listed alongside James elder at Corsinkell in the 1559–1560 teind material.<ref name="TeindList" /> The 1571 record then directly identifies the younger James as the elder James's son.<ref name="NRS1571" />

If the younger James was at least about eighteen to twenty-one in 1559, he was born by approximately 1538–1541. Normal generational spacing would place his father's birth around 1510–1520.

About 1515 is used as an uncertain working estimate. A more conservative statement would be that James elder was born before about 1525.

=== Parentage ===

[[Glasgow-1098|William Glasgow]] is James’s leading possible father, assessed at '''38% subjective confidence''' in the 17 October 2026 review. William and his wife [[Brade-232|Esbell Brade]] had a married son [[Glasgow-1093|John Glasgow]] by 31 December 1534, placing them in a compatible generation for James’s estimated circa 1515 birth.<ref name="WilliamFamily1534">Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. IV, protocol 1196, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc08robegoog/page/n84/mode/2up printed p.72], 31 December 1534: William Glasgow and Esbell Brade conveyed property to their son John and his wife Jonet Spreull. Published abstract of the contemporary instrument.</ref> William’s own birth date is unknown.

The Glasgow civic and Mathie–Glencairn contacts described below provide a plausible social route to Corsinkell, but they do not distinguish William’s fatherhood from a collateral or other local origin. The relationship remains possible and uncertain. Alternatives include James as William’s nephew or another collateral, a son of the older Saltmarket John provisionally represented by [[Glasgow-1095|John Glasgow]], or a member of another local Glasgow household. No alternative parent is selected.

[[Brade-232|Isobel or Esbell Brade]] is assessed independently as James’s uncertain mother, at '''28% subjective confidence'''. Her marriage to William and maternity of John are documented; no record establishes her maternity of James.<ref name="WilliamFamily1534" /> She is the best named maternal candidate conditional on William’s fatherhood. Neither her birth year nor her birthplace is supplied by the 1534 record. These percentages express research judgment, not measured probabilities.

The earlier attachment to [[Glasgow-1093|John Glasgow]] is strongly disfavoured by the working generations. James’s circa 1515 and John’s circa 1510 births are estimates, so this is not an absolute exclusion based on recorded ages. John’s marriage by 1534 alone does not establish his age.<ref name="WilliamFamily1534" />

=== Wife or Campbell associates ===

Katherine Campbell was listed at Corsinkell with James elder and James younger in the 1559–1560 teind material.<ref name="TeindList" />

Christian Campbell was named among the occupiers in the 1571 archival catalogue description.<ref name="NRS1571" />

Neither source states that either woman was James's wife, daughter or other relative. Katherine and Christian should not be assumed to be the same person, and neither should be attached as James's spouse without further evidence.

=== Son James ===

The younger James named at Corsinkell is represented by '''[[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow]]'''.

The repeated holding, the elder/younger distinction and the 1571 statement of relationship make this identification well supported.

Earlier links to [[Glasgow-991|James Glasgow]] should not be restored unless separate evidence shows that profile represents the Corsinkell son.

=== Son John ===

The 1571 record directly proves that James elder had a son named John.<ref name="NRS1571" /> [[Glasgow-3193|John Glasgow]] is the surviving profile after the record-defined Corsinkell profile Glasgow-4061 was merged into it on 27 September 2026. James is directly identified as the father of the Corsinkell son named in the deed; the father relationship is confident for that record-defined son. His further Kilwinning identity remains separately qualified.

The merger also brings the Kilwinning/Burrowland/Dalgarven records under this one profile as a probable identity match by name, generation and Ayrshire setting. No inspected deed explicitly carries the Corsinkell son to those holdings, so that part of the combined identity remains qualified. The former link to [[Glasgow-1561|John Glasgow]] should not be used.

=== Landholding context ===

Corsinkell formed part of lands held under branches of the Cunninghame family.

A charter made at Kerelaw on 4 February 1527 and confirmed on 25 May 1536 granted David Cunningham of Bertaneholme the five-merk lands of Corsinkell and Middlepart with other Stevenston lands.<ref name="Corsinkell1536">''Registrum Magni Sigilli Regum Scotorum'', vol. 3, [https://archive.org/details/registrummagnisi03scot/page/133/mode/2up no. 596, printed pp. 133–134].</ref>

A further charter dated 11 July 1537 and confirmed on 4 August 1537 included two-and-a-half merk lands of Corsinkell in a grant to David Cunningham of Robertland.<ref name="Corsinkell1537">''Registrum Magni Sigilli Regum Scotorum'', vol. 3, [https://archive.org/details/registrummagnisi03scot/page/377/mode/2up no. 1698, printed p. 377].</ref>

These charters establish the superior-title history of Corsinkell but do not name James Glasgow, identify an earlier Glasgow tenant or prove a kinship relationship with the Cunninghames.

Later marriages between people named Glasgow and Cunningham do not establish James's ancestry or his relationship to those later families.

=== Mathie and Glencairn network ===

The 1559–60 teind list also places John Mathie at Ardeer Nether, Margaret Mathie at Templemuir of Corscraig and William Mathie at Middlepart, alongside [[Glasgow-3908|Matthew Glasgow]], [[Glasgow-3911|Michael Glasgow]] and the Corsinkell Glasgows.<ref name="TeindList" /> This repeated association has a city counterpart: David Mathy and [[Glasgow-1096|Robert Glasgow]] were fellow Glasgow burgesses witnessing the same sasines in 1554.<ref name="Mathie1554">Robert Renwick, ed., Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow, vol. 1, protocols 183–184, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc07robegoog/page/n90/mode/2up 10 May 1554, printed p. 63].</ref>

David’s son John is a plausible match for the writer admitted with the Earl of Glencairn’s burgess group in 1583. Tutory retours entered in 1607 identify the deceased John Mathie of Ibert’s full brother Matthew as a Glasgow burgess and name John’s lawful children James, David, Agnes and Margaret.<ref name="Ibert1607">Inquisitionum ad Capellam Domini Regis Retornatarum ... Abbreviatio, vol. 2, tutory retours 100–101, 20 January 1607, [https://books.google.com/books?id=PiVDAAAAcAAJ digitised volume]; underlying register, National Records of Scotland, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browsetreeview.aspx?reference=C22 C22/3].</ref> This provides a credible route between Robert’s Glasgow burgh family and the Glencairn estate community at Stevenston. It makes an association between the Glasgow households more plausible, although it does not identify James elder’s father.

The later Mathie identities include unresolved steps and postdate William. Their connected appearances describe one social corridor, rather than independent proofs of James’s ancestry. [[Glasgow-3911|Michael Glasgow]]’s possible placement as another son of William derives from James’s existing father hypothesis and Michael’s possible brother/cousin relationship to James. It cannot independently corroborate James’s father. Michael’s adult Glencairn witness occurrence in 1549 supports his generation and local association.<ref>National Records of Scotland, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F1%2F59 GD39/1/59], 28 January 1549, instrument of sasine in favour of Janet Cunningham and Alexander, Earl of Glencairn; official catalogue abstract, original manuscript not inspected.</ref>

=== The 1571 Glasgow burgess witness ===

The 9 February 1571 Corsinkell letters of reversion name Mr Adam Wallace, explicitly a '''burgess of Glasgow''', among the witnesses, alongside the Porterfields and an earl's servant.<ref name="NRS1571" /> This places a named Glasgow civic figure at the same transaction that identifies James elder and his sons. A later Glencairn record names a Mr Adam Wallace as one of Glasgow's bailies and William Wallace as his son; the matching name and city favour the same civic officer but do not establish his relationship to the Corsinkell Glasgows.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Earls of Glencairn, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F5%2F68 GD39/5/68], commission dated 26 April 1588 (catalogue description; original not inspected).</ref>

The 1571 witness is a direct city-to-estate legal contact, not evidence that James elder was a son of [[Glasgow-1098|William Glasgow]] or kin to Wallace. The reason Wallace witnessed this deed remains open.

=== Margaret Smith of Brekpleuch ===

The Glasgow Commissary testament index contains an entry dated 21 February 1632 for Margaret Smith, spouse of James Glasgow in Brekpleuch, parish of Stevenston.<ref name="MargaretSmith">Francis J. Grant, ed., [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso07scotuoft/page/460/mode/2up ''The Commissariot Record of Glasgow: Register of Testaments, 1547–1800''] (Scottish Record Society, 1901), printed p. 460.</ref>

That couple belongs to a later generation. The entry does not identify the James Glasgow of Brekpleuch as James elder, his son or another specific descendant.

=== Disputed origins ===

Online family trees have assigned James a birth in Midlothian, parents named John Glasgow and Nancy Glendinning, a wife named Margaret Edstoun, and a death in 1580.

No contemporary evidence cited on this profile supports those claims.

The 1580 testament of Margaret Glasgow, widow of John Edstoun of Croftmalloch, concerns a Midlothian family and does not establish a marriage to James of Corsinkell.

The Ayrshire and Midlothian families should be researched independently unless a record explicitly connects them.

=== Negative searches ===

No published Great Seal entry examined for Corsinkell identifies James's father, siblings or a preceding Glasgow tenant.

No relevant entry was found in the searched published Privy Seal registers under James Glasgow, surname variants or Corsinkell variants.

The printed Glasgow Commissary testament index contains no testament indexed under James Glasgow of Corsinkell.

These negative results narrow the available evidence but do not prove that no relevant record survives elsewhere or under a different spelling.

=== Possible collateral households ===
The 1559-60 Stevenston teind list places [[Glasgow-3908|Matthew Glasgow]] at Ardeer Nether and [[Glasgow-3911|Michael Glasgow]] at Brakpleuch in the same estate landscape as James elder and James younger at Corsankell.<ref>Margaret H. B. Sanderson, ''The People of Sixteenth Century Ayrshire'', appendix “The Cunninghame Teind Lists of 1559-60,” [https://aanhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/people-of-ayrshire.pdf#page=36 p. 331].</ref>

Matthew is linked as James's uncertain working son, although the same evidence also permits a nephew or younger collateral. The 1571 Corsankell writ proves sons James and John but does not purport to enumerate every child.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Charter Chest of the Earls of Glencairn, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F5%2F59 GD39/5/59], 9 February 1571.</ref> Michael was already an adult legal witness in the Glencairn network in 1549, so he is better assessed as James's probable brother or cousin rather than a son. Neither exact relationship is stated in a record.
