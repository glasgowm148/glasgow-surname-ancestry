# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1168), of Corsankell, Ayrshire](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1168)

## Current conclusion

James's own parentage remains unproved. [William Glasgow (Glasgow-1098), born
about 1500](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1098) has now replaced John
Glasgow-1093 as the attached father. William is chronologically possible, but no
record joins his Glasgow-burgh family to Corsankell; mark the relationship
uncertain.

The 1571 record proves two sons: **James Glasgow younger**, now represented by
[Glasgow-3921](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3921), and **John Glasgow**,
now represented directly by the record-defined
[Glasgow-4061](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4061). The separate
[Glasgow-3193](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3193) remains an uncertain
Kilwinning identity match. The old Glasgow-991 and Glasgow-1561
identifications remain only as explicit no-restore cautions.

## Chronology correction

The profile's present birth estimate of about 1540 is untenable for James
elder. In 1559-60 James younger was separately listed with James elder among
the Corsankell teind debtors/tenants, and in 1571 the younger James was directly
called the elder James's son. If the younger James was at least about 18-21 in
1559, he was born by about 1538-41. Normal generational spacing places James
elder's birth around **1510-20**. A practical WikiTree estimate is **about 1515,
uncertain**, or the more conservative **before about 1525**.

This dating also explains why John-1093 is contradicted as the father while
William-1098 remains chronologically possible.

## Mathie–Glencairn bridge — 30 August 2026

The same teind list contains John, Margaret and William Mathie as well as the
four Glasgow men in the Stevenston holdings. That overlap now has an earlier
city counterpart: David Mathy and Robert Glasgow were fellow burgesses and
co-witnesses in Glasgow in 1554. David's documented son John is a plausible
match for a writer who entered the burgess roll with the Earl of Glencairn's
group in 1583. A two-generation John Mathie household at Clerkland in 1600 also
fits two successive Glencairn servitors of that name in 1606.

This supplies a credible social and estate corridor between Robert's burgh
family and the Corsankell Glasgows. It makes some wider family association more
plausible, but it does not identify James elder's father. January 1607 tutory
retours now directly identify the deceased John Mathie of Ibert's full brother
Matthew, a Glasgow burgess, and John's lawful children James, David, Agnes and
Margaret. John of Ibert is a strong candidate for the Glencairn servant killed
in 1606. His testament remains the best immediate target for his wife,
executor and wider household. See the [focused analysis](../Glasgow-3908/mathie-glencairn-bridge-1554-1618.md).

## Negative searches

- The published Great Seal register for 1546-80 was searched for Corsankell
  variants and James/Glasgow variants. No entry was found identifying James's
  father, sibling or earlier Glasgow predecessor at Corsankell.
- The searchable published Privy Seal registers covering 1488-1574 were checked
  for `James Glasgow`, surname variants and Corsankell variants. No relevant
  personal or place entry was found. City/diocese of Glasgow index hits were
  excluded. OCR and Google Books search limitations mean this is useful negative
  coverage, not proof of absence.
- The printed Glasgow Commissariot testament index for 1547-1800 contains no
  testament indexed to James of Corsankell.
- The two pre-1559 Great Seal charters for Corsankell name Cunningham superiors,
  not occupiers, so they do not identify the Glasgow family's arrival or origin.
