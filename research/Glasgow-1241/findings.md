The Irish records define **two Dublin watchmakers named Philip Glasco**. The
elder was established in Christ Church Lane by 1750 and died in 1772; Philip
junior married Teresa Plunkett in 1768 and died in 1770. The live profile's
Scottish baptism and the Dublin elder are chronologically compatible, but no
reviewed record yet proves that the baptised child migrated to Dublin.

## Source findings

### Findmypast sweep — 7 October 2026

- **New: a son named in print.** *Hibernian Journal*, 11 January 1773, p.2 (BNA `BL/0000817/17730111/005`), repeated in the same paper on 8, 10 and 22 February, 3, 5 and 22 March and 7 and 9 April 1773, and in *Saunders's News-Letter*, 12 March 1773, p.3 (`BL/0001057/17730312/016`). Full-page OCR of the 5 March issue (`BL/0000817/17730305/010`) reads, with long-s normalised: "THOMAS GLASCO, who served Apprenticeship to his Father, Philip Glasco, late [of] Christ-Church-lane, Watch-maker, and since had several Years Practice in London, takes this Method of informing his Friends and the Public, that he carries on the usual [business] at his said Father's House". This is the first record found that names a child of a Christ Church Lane Philip Glasco and gives the son's trade history. "Late" in early 1773 fits the elder Philip, whose death the Dublin Gazette reported in October 1772; Philip junior had died in December 1770 and, born about 1740, is very unlikely to have had a son who had already finished an apprenticeship and several years in London. The advertisement does not say "senior", so the identification of the father with the elder Philip is strongly probable, not stated. It gives no Scottish origin and does not bridge the Uphall baptism.
- **New (index only): court and probate entries for Philip Glasco in Dublin.** Findmypast indexes Philip Glasco in the Equity Exchequer Bill Books on 9 March 1743 (vol. 45, p.185, `IRE/COURT/EXCHEQUER/0001369387`), on the same page as an **Ellinr Glasco** (`IRE/COURT/EXCHEQUER/0001369388`); in the Chancery Bill Books on 7 December 1749 (vol. 32, p.228, `IRE/COURT/CHANCERY/0000398412`); and in the Exchequer Bill Books on 20 December 1760 (vol. 62, p.154, `IRE/COURT/EXCHEQUER/0002199803`). The index gives no role, residence or trade. Ellinor is a candidate earlier wife or relative of the elder Philip (his marriage to the widow Mangan was reported in 1750), not proved.
- **New (index only):** a Chancery bill of 3 November 1777 (vol. 43, p.190) indexes **Philip Glasco** and **Thomas Glasco** on the same page (`IRE/COURT/CHANCERY/0001223970`, `IRE/COURT/CHANCERY/0001223973`). Both Philips were dead by then, so this is probably litigation over a Philip's estate with Thomas as a party; the roles must be read from the image (`IRE/COURT/CHANCERY/007635217/00808`).
- **Adds detail to the known 1772 will entry:** the Dublin diocesan wills and grants index (Deputy Keeper's 26th/30th/31st Reports) has two Philip Glasco entries for 1772 (`IRE/IET0090/0424/215`, `/216`) and two for 1777 (`/217`, `/218`). Findmypast transcribes no residence or grant type; the 1777 pair may be a later grant on the same estate.
- **Candidate, unproved:** the same index has **Catherine Glasco**, 1760 (`IRE/IET0090/0424/212`). The elder Philip's wife died in early 1760 (Faulkner's, 2 Feb 1760); if her forename was Catherine (as the attached Mangan-824 profile has it) this could be her grant, but Glasco-173's wife Catherine was also living in Dublin in 1760, so no assignment is made.


## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

Select Thomas Glasgow-1242 as the uncertain working father of Philip Glasco-146. The preferred model uses Glasco-146 as the elder Dublin watchmaker account, retaining the 1711 Scottish child Glasgow-1241 as a separate possible account of the same man. That child’s Uphall index names father Thomas; his existing research already retains the elder watchmaker as a compatible adult identity. The Glasco-146 account has the Christ Church Lane watchmaking association in 1752, an estimated birth about 1715 and a younger Philip in its tree family, which suit that provisional elder role. The four-year difference from the 1711 baptism is between a record and an unsupported estimate, not a concrete identity conflict. This materialises the existing Scottish candidate at low confidence; it does not prove migration or two distinct elder Philips.

The Dublin records name no Scottish parents, birthplace or apprenticeship, so the Scottish child and elder watchmaker may be different men. The fresh Glasco-146 biography itself mixes the 1752 watchmaker with junior notices of 1765,1768 and 1770. Its c.1715 birth and the c.1740 birth of Glasco-147 are tree estimates, not evidence selecting a generation. If Glasco-146 instead represents the junior, the elder Philip is an alternative father-generation placement and the Thomas link would need revision; no competing father is attached alongside it. The elder placement is a preferred working interpretation of the profile’s trade/address and family structure, not a claim that every event in that biography belongs to him. The junior’s 1765 unnamed-wife death,1768 Teresa Plunkett marriage and reported 1770 death remain with the younger documentary account Glasgow-3932. Glasco-147 is a strongly supported duplicate account of that younger man, but its quoted 1772 younger/inventory wording was not independently rechecked here and establishes neither the estimated birth nor a newly verified father clause; a grant date is not a death date. No merge, spouse transfer or new child attachment follows. Catherine Mangan is called Glasco-146’s mother in its prose but spouse in its structured family; the 1750 report describes the elder’s bride only as the widow Mangan, and the 1760 report gives no forename. Neither record proves maternity. Preserve the existing Mangan/Braccan and younger-parent fields as qualified leads; do not add a mother for Glasco-146. The father Thomas is common to the retained 1711 child records, while the 3 June Uphall versus 21 October Linlithgow Philip entries and their maternal readings remain unreconciled; Jean Duncan is not transferred as Glasco-146’s mother. The 1777 probate/court occurrence does not establish survival after 1772. The local elder account uses 1772 as an uncertain death year through the selected identity, retaining the old after 1772 value and all junior events in the comparison.

The Scottish child remains this account’s record-defined starting point; the selected Dublin connection does not prove two distinct elder sons called Philip.
