### Hugh collateral update, 13 August 2026

a concrete Hugh Glasgow household at Liminary in Ballyclug: Hugh junior married
Ann Wilson in 1852 and Eliza Glasgow married William Montgomery in 1854; both

marriage gives no residence and does not prove that all three named the same
Hugh.

The surviving 1851 County Antrim census search produced no Liminary household
or age-compatible elder Hugh. This is a survival/transcription limitation, not
evidence that the family was absent.

The records strengthen Hugh as Robert's probable brother only through the
Mary-as-niece identity. They do not yet justify a structured sibling link.

No inspected record links Robert Glasgow of Glenleslie to Adam Glasgow of
Inishrush or names Alexander `Glasgow-951` as his son. The direct chain for this
profile is the 1851 Glenleslie census followed by the newly found 1854
Glenleslie will index. The 1820 lease, 1827 tithe and 1831 census instead form a
secure Coltrim chain now represented separately by `Glasgow-3941`. No document
bridges those two identities or connects either man to the Robert holding 35
acres at Lisnagaver in 1834. Household adjacency also does not establish John
`Glasgow-1022` as Robert's father. The best present placement is instead the
**Dunaghy / Doonbought-Glenleslie tenant cluster**: an age-compatible Robert
Glasco was at Doonbought in 1825, while Robert's probable son of the same name
headed a Doonbought household in 1851 and Robert himself headed a neighbouring
Glenleslie household. This supports local family continuity but still does not
name Robert's parents. Leave both parents blank.

The close R-FT20271 pairing between Alexander `Glasgow-951` and the James J
Glasgow `Glasgow-2738` line does not place this Robert in either pedigree. No
direct male-line descendant of Robert has a confirmed Big Y result in the
project. The three workable paper models remain, in descending order of fit:

1. Robert and older Alexander `Glasgow-3904` were brothers or cousins below an
   unidentified Dunaghy/Bann Valley ancestor. This requires the fewest hidden
   relationships.
2. Robert was an early son of older Alexander. This requires older Alexander to
   have been born by about 1760 and probably requires more than one partnership.
3. Robert was an early son or younger brother of John `Glasgow-1022`. A son
   relationship requires a mother and union before John's reported 1791
   marriage to Rose.

None is a relationship to mark certain. Robert's parent fields should remain
blank until a direct record or a tested male-line descendant supplies the
missing bridge.

## Parentage conclusion

Robert is **unsupported and chronologically implausible** as Adam and Rose's son.
John `Glasgow-1022` is only a weak locality candidate: houses 21 and 23 were
nearby, but no record states kinship and Robert predates John's reported 1791
marriage. Do not attach either proposed father; leave both parents blank.

The more defensible tree placement is beneath an **unknown Dunaghy father**, in
the same local tenant network as [[Glasgow-3959|John Glasgow of Ballyboggy]],
the two James Glasgows at Ballybogy in 1825, and [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow of
Glenleslie]]. John `Glasgow-3959` is old enough to be an earlier ancestor rather
than Robert's father; no intermediate generation has been identified.

## Probable brother Hugh and niece Mary

The strongest reconstructed collateral relationship comes from Robert's
unmarried niece Mary in 1851. [Mary's 1862 marriage](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1862/09623/5489617.pdf)
names her father as **Hugh Glasgow, weaver**. Her later [death at Crankill in
1898](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/deaths_returns/deaths_1898/05842/4654852.pdf),
aged 79, is compatible with the niece's census age.

If [[Glasgow-3957|Mary (Glasgow) Montgomery]] is the census niece—and the name,
age, County Antrim origin, unmarried status, weaving occupation and Ballymena
district make her the strongest located match—Hugh was probably Robert's
brother. No compatible existing WikiTree profile was found for Hugh; a
[creation draft](../../surname-research/new-people/1862_Ireland_Ballymena_Hugh_Glasgow.md)
has been prepared. The attachment of [[Glasgow-3956|Joseph Glasgow]] as Mary's
father arose from a misreading and must be removed.

4. **Unproved:** the Robert beside Alexander at Lisnagaver in 1834 may have been

A final targeted search of publicly indexed 1853-55 newspaper and probate
material found no notice naming Robert's parents, origin or heirs. The 1854
Connor diocesan will index is terminal: PRONI states that the original document
was destroyed and no further information survives. The negative search does not
formally disprove Adam's paternity, but it leaves no positive Inishrush bridge.

### Best unified working model

The least contradictory present model places Robert and older Alexander in the
same broad generation below an unidentified earlier Bann Valley/Dunaghy Glasgow
ancestor, probably as brothers or first cousins. This is more coherent than
making either a son of [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow]] or
[[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow]], but is not yet a relationship to add.

The separate [[Glasgow-2385|Alexander Glasgow of Glenleslie]] adds limited

network. His current attachment to [[Glasgow-867|John Glasgow of Lisnagaver]]
is chronologically impossible on the recorded ages and must not be used as the
bridge.
