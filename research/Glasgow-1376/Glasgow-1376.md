== Biography ==
{{Notables Sticker}}
James Glasgow was an adult associated with Whitecastle in July 1672. His birth date and birthplace are unknown; about 1650 is an estimate from his adult role.

=== Whitecastle and the 1672 proceeding ===
The printed decree in Robert Wodrow’s 1721 History, volume I, appendix no. XLVII, pp.133–134, is dated 27 July 1672 in both its heading and Latin opening. It names “James Glasgow in Whitecastle” among people imprisoned for refusing the oath. The monetary fines there belong to Mr James Duncan and the Countess of Wigtoun, not to James Glasgow. William Crookshank’s 1751 summary, volume I p.285, places the same named group under 24 July 1672; preserve that day discrepancy and prefer the printed decree’s 27 July for this source account. Neither source supports the old 1670 date. The two books recount the same proceeding and are not independent evidence of two imprisonments.<ref name="WhitecastleDecree">Robert Wodrow, History of the Sufferings of the Church of Scotland (1721), vol.I, appendix no.XLVII, pp.133–134, decree headed 27 July 1672; printed page images freshly inspected [https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_the-history-of-the-suffe_wodrow-robert_1721_1/page/n794/mode/2up printed pp.133–134].</ref>

The surrounding Biggar/Boghall group names men in Biggar, Carwood, Edmonstoun, Worslyd and Carstairs. This favours the Whitecastle in Libberton parish over a location guessed merely from general Covenanter activity. J. L. Davidson’s published article title locates West Whitecastle Farm in Libberton, Lanarkshire (Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland 108, p.383; title checked in the journal’s search-indexed contents, full article not inspected). This supports the Libberton place identification, in the same parish setting as Quothquan and near Biggar’s Lindsaylands; it does not identify a seventeenth-century occupier. This is a probable interpretation of James’s residence, not a parish supplied literally by the decree. White Castle in Garvald and Bara, East Lothian, is a real alternative place. The decree also deals with a Perthshire group; document order alone does not put every accused person in Clydesdale.<ref name="WhitecastlePlaces">[https://journals.socantscot.org/index.php/psas/issue/view/239 Davidson, PSAS 108 (1979), p.383, published title naming West Whitecastle Farm, Libberton; journal contents indexed, article not inspected]; [https://portal.historicenvironment.scot/designation/SM756 Historic Environment Scotland, White Castle fort, Garvald and Bara, SM756].</ref>

The record supplies no wife, child, age, occupation or parent for James. Mr James Duncan, indweller at Duplin, and the Countess of Wigtoun are separately accused people in the same decree. Duncan's surname provides no evidence that Agnes Duncan was James Glasgow's mother.

=== Provisional Quothquan family ===
Select [[Glasgow-1086|John Glasgow]] of Quothquan as the uncertain local working father of [[Glasgow-1376|James Glasgow]]. The choice is conditional on the Whitecastle man’s possible identity with [[Glasgow-3332|James Glasgow]], provisionally compared with the weaver at Back of Lindsaylands whose testament was indexed in 1680. The newly supported probable Clydesdale setting supplies a positive regional bridge that the earlier review lacked. The same name, neighbouring Libberton/Biggar households and compatible adult dates favour that existing local account as the leading identity comparison. The 1690 apprentice entry names a deceased James Glasgow, weaver in Lindsayland, as father of the apprentice; that trade and place probably identify the 1680 testator, not the Whitecastle man directly. John is already the uncertain father attached to [[Glasgow-3332|James]]. Carry that family placement across the possible identity at its actual uncertainty, rather than inventing another father. John’s possible 1608 childhood would fit a son born in the 1630s–1650s; neither James birth estimate is a recorded age. This is a selected, revisable family hypothesis, not a parent clause in the 1672 decree.

There are two dependent gaps: Whitecastle James is not proved to be the Lindsaylands weaver, and John’s paternity of [[Glasgow-3332|James]] is itself unproved. John’s 1685 Quothquan testament names his widow but no child; the absence of James, if dead by 1680, would not exclude this model. [[Glasgow-3332|James]]’s possible identity with the 1624 weaver would invalidate this particular later-life route, because the 1624 decedent cannot be the man present in 1672. [[Glasgow-1150|James]], the Edinburgh weaver, remains an alternative adult identification for the later weaver rather than being excluded from an unsupported birth estimate; another local James or a different kinship degree remains possible. The old Marion-baptism role error supplies no positive parentage for James-1376, and the Duffe household [[Glasgow-2682|James]] is not merged. The 1738 Edinburgh Castle lead is unselected and incompatible with a death by 1680 if this weaver identity is right. No 1680 original was newly read: the maintained case has only the supplied wills index, and no linked manuscript or transcription. Neither a marriage nor a mother is supplied. Jonet Kinnie, John’s widow, is not automatically James’s mother. === The 1657 baptism and the Edinburgh families ===
The 1657 entry used to supply James's birth and his proposed James Glasgow/Agnes Duncan parents belongs to Marione Glesgow. James Glesgow is named in it as Marione's father. The corresponding Marion entry and the 1686 Edinburgh burgess evidence, which calls Marion a daughter of James Glasgow, weaver and burgess, corroborate that record role. The former 29 January 1657 birthday and those parents are not established for the Whitecastle man. The approximate 1650 estimate is not a recovered baptism.

The Edinburgh weaver [[Glasgow-1150|James Glasgow]] remains an adult identity comparison; the false use of his daughter's baptism does not establish either that he was the Whitecastle man or that he was that man's father. [[Glasgow-2682|James Glasgow]], husband of Isobell Duffe and father in Edinburgh from 1677 to 1691, remains a separate family account. No identity merge is selected.

=== Other James occurrences ===
The James Glasgow associated with Edinburgh Castle in the 1738 General Assembly acts remains an unresolved namesake comparison. If the Whitecastle man was the Lindsaylands weaver whose testament was indexed in 1680, he could not be the man acting in 1738. The 1680 index date is 10 November, not an established death date; the 11 October form in the earlier account is not preferred over the maintained archival index.
