Select the Edinburgh weaver and burgess James Glasgow-1150 as the probable father of the record-defined 1696 apprentice James Glasgow-4114, at 76% subjective confidence. Printed page 37 names the apprentice’s father as James Glasgow, weaver and burgess. Name, occupation, civic status and Edinburgh setting identify a particular established craftsman rather than just a same-name Scottish father. The maintained 1641 apprenticeship and 1649 burgess cross-reference securely establish James-1150 as a weaver and burgess; printed page 72 also records a James Glasgow, weaver and burgess, taking John Paterson as apprentice on 18 October 1693. The maintained burgess-roll review found one matching admission, although an index cannot rule out another man. This makes James-1150 the preferred father-profile match. The child account itself is defined by the 22 April 1696 entry, so no later-life identification is needed for that child.

The 76% is an individual research judgment for the whole father-profile identification, not a measured frequency. The published entry proves a father called James, weaver and burgess; it does not supply his apprenticeship date, wife, home address or age. Another matching craftsman, including a Lindsaylands man who also held burgess rights, remains possible. The circa-1680 child date and circa-1620 father date are estimates, not recorded ages; the nominal sixty-year interval makes late fatherhood relevant but is not a contradiction. James’s 1641 apprenticeship supplies no exact birth year. The younger James’s mother remains unknown: neither Bessie Whyte nor Agnes Duncan is transferred merely because she married the proposed father. The father’s existing probable Robert ancestry and all other family grades remain unchanged.

The earlier claim that the 1690 and 1696 apprentices must be different men is withdrawn. The 1690 entry explicitly calls its Lindsayland weaver father deceased and says that the apprenticeship was scored out. The 1696 entry omits a death qualification; silence does not prove that father alive. A cancelled cordiner apprenticeship followed by an apothecary apprenticeship six years later is compatible with one man, although not established. The matching apprentice/father names and father’s weaving trade support testing that possibility; burgess status is given only for the 1696 father and the two masters differ. Keep the two existing profiles distinct pending an identifier; do not create them again. If they prove to be one child, the selected James-1150 father would need reconsideration because the 1690 father was dead whereas the Edinburgh master appears in 1693. This alternative limits the father assessment rather than being erased.

James Glasgow-1376 is not excluded from the 1696 apprentice merely because his former 1657 birth was a misread parent role. Removing that false date supplies no identity conflict. His Whitecastle adult occurrence in 1672 makes a 1696 first apprenticeship less ordinary, and no trade or residence bridge identifies him; retain him as a weaker compatible comparison. The proposed father of Whitecastle James is unchanged. Other Edinburgh James households remain comparisons, without unproved mergers, sibling transfers or replacement birth dates.

## Earlier role-error assessment

Agnes Duncan are unsupported and result from a record-role error. The profile's
cited FamilySearch entry is for **Marione Glesgow**; "James Glesgow" appears in
it as her father. It is not a baptism or birth record for a son James.

Detach Glasgow-1376 from the household unless an independent record for a son
James is found. Do not merge this profile into the father merely from the
misread citation, because its later Whitecastle/Covenanter claims have not been
shown to describe the Edinburgh weaver.

The proposed merge with [Glasgow-2682](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2682)
is also rejected. Glasgow-2682 is a documented father with Isobell Duffe from
1677 to 1691. The false 1657 birth on this profile supplies no matching identity
fact, and no record connects the Whitecastle occurrence to the Duffe household.

## Whitecastle and Quothquan review — 17 October 2026

The printed decree in Robert Wodrow’s 1721 History, volume I, appendix no. XLVII, pp.133–134, is dated 27 July 1672 in both its heading and Latin opening. It names “James Glasgow in Whitecastle” among people imprisoned for refusing the oath. The monetary fines there belong to Mr James Duncan and the Countess of Wigtoun, not to James Glasgow. William Crookshank’s 1751 summary, volume I p.285, places the same named group under 24 July 1672; preserve that day discrepancy and prefer the printed decree’s 27 July for this source account. Neither source supports the old 1670 date. The two books recount the same proceeding and are not independent evidence of two imprisonments.

The surrounding Biggar/Boghall group names men in Biggar, Carwood, Edmonstoun, Worslyd and Carstairs. This favours the Whitecastle in Libberton parish over a location guessed merely from general Covenanter activity. J. L. Davidson’s published article title locates West Whitecastle Farm in Libberton, Lanarkshire (Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland 108, p.383; title checked in the journal’s search-indexed contents, full article not inspected). This supports the Libberton place identification, in the same parish setting as Quothquan and near Biggar’s Lindsaylands; it does not identify a seventeenth-century occupier. This is a probable interpretation of James’s residence, not a parish supplied literally by the decree. White Castle in Garvald and Bara, East Lothian, is a real alternative place. The decree also deals with a Perthshire group; document order alone does not put every accused person in Clydesdale.

Select John Glasgow-1086 of Quothquan as the uncertain local working father of James Glasgow-1376. The choice is conditional on the Whitecastle man’s possible identity with James Glasgow-3332, provisionally compared with the weaver at Back of Lindsaylands whose testament was indexed in 1680. The newly supported probable Clydesdale setting supplies a positive regional bridge that the earlier review lacked. The same name, neighbouring Libberton/Biggar households and compatible adult dates favour that existing local account as the leading identity comparison. The 1690 apprentice entry names a deceased James Glasgow, weaver in Lindsayland, as father of the apprentice; that trade and place probably identify the 1680 testator, not the Whitecastle man directly. John is already the uncertain father attached to James-3332. Carry that family placement across the possible identity at its actual uncertainty, rather than inventing another father. John’s possible 1608 childhood would fit a son born in the 1630s–1650s; neither James birth estimate is a recorded age. This is a selected, revisable family hypothesis, not a parent clause in the 1672 decree.

There are two dependent gaps: Whitecastle James is not proved to be the Lindsaylands weaver, and John’s paternity of James-3332 is itself unproved. John’s 1685 Quothquan testament names his widow but no child; the absence of James, if dead by 1680, would not exclude this model. James-3332’s possible identity with the 1624 weaver would invalidate this particular later-life route, because the 1624 decedent cannot be the man present in 1672. James-1150, the Edinburgh weaver, remains an alternative adult identification for the later weaver rather than being excluded from an unsupported birth estimate; another local James or a different kinship degree remains possible. The old Marion-baptism role error supplies no positive parentage for James-1376, and the Duffe household James-2682 is not merged. The 1738 Edinburgh Castle lead is unselected and incompatible with a death by 1680 if this weaver identity is right. No 1680 original was newly read: the maintained case has only the supplied wills index, and no linked manuscript or transcription. Neither a marriage nor a mother is supplied. Jonet Kinnie, John’s widow, is not automatically James’s mother. Keep both James profiles separate; they may represent one man, not two proved brothers.

The fixed historical cohort and all names, dates, places, spouses, existing father choices and live WikiTree fields are preserved. The circa-1650 birth on James-1376 is an estimate; the newly checked occurrence is in 1672, not a pre-1650 record. One selected uncertain father connects this previously isolated account locally. Supported and confident paternal measurements remain unchanged. Working counts concern retained profile accounts and may include the unresolved duplicate represented by the two James profiles; a gained connection is not another proved person or a proved pedigree. The 27/24 July discrepancy and imprisonment-versus-fine correction remain visible beside the family hypothesis.
