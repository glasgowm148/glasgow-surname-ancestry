## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

Patrick Glasgow-1440, represented by the Gordon Patrike baptism of 1667, is selected as a very uncertain working father of Patrick Glasgow-3920 of the Kilclone memorial. The memorial’s age 60 at death in May 1754 puts the younger Patrick’s birth around 1694. If the Gordon baptism was near birth, the older Patrick would have been about 27 then: an ordinary father–son interval. The matching Patrick/Patrike and Glasgow/Glasco name pair supplies a specific older candidate rather than an invented ancestor. This is a very weak generational model; neither name recurrence nor a feasible age establishes paternity. There is no reviewed deed, parish entry, probate clause, occupation or shared associate connecting Gordon or Stitchel to Kilclone. No migration or Scottish birthplace is newly established for the Meath man, and the catalogue is not a complete census of older Patricks.

The Gordon profile also gathers Patrick’s marriage entries with Helen Fairbairn/Fairbarne at Stitchel on 23 May 1707 and Eccles on 1 July 1707, followed by children in 1708–1716. Those adult events do not themselves identify the groom with the 1667 child. If that combined identity is correct, the proposed Meath son would predate the recorded 1707 marriage by about 13 years. Helen is therefore not selected as his mother. No earlier wife, earlier marriage, illegitimacy, second household or move is invented. The proposed father would require an unrecorded earlier child, and that is a real weakness of the model. The two 1707 dates remain separate recorded entries; their precise banns/ceremony relationship has not been newly checked. The supplied Gordon index reads 4 August 1667 while the structured birth field says 8 April 1667. This existing day/month conflict remains visible, and neither index is used as proof of an exact birthday.

The model keeps the two Patricks distinct. A man baptised in 1667 would have been at least about 86–87 in 1754, incompatible with the Kilclone memorial’s age 60 unless the memorial age is substantially wrong. No evidence justifies correcting that age by a generation. A father–son hypothesis fits the two dated accounts better than identifying them as one person, but another father or more distant relationship remains possible. The 1667 index names James as father of the Gordon child, not a father for the Meath Patrick. The modern James Glasgow-3107 identification and the Gordon account’s existing maternal and ancestral attachments retain their previous qualifications; this new edge does not independently prove them. The project’s reported Y-DNA affinity does not provide a named parent or establish that this particular untested Meath family belongs to a tested line.

The Kilclone inscription identifies Patrick as James’s father, and Richard and William as James’s brothers. Richard and William died on 1 November 1738; Richard’s age is 10 and William’s is printed with a query at about 7. Patrick died in May 1754 aged 60. These statements define the existing nuclear family and remain separate from the new proposed grandfather. The source used is a modern reproduction of a transcription reported in 1892, not a newly inspected photograph of the stone. The inscription supplies no mother, wife, occupation, birthplace or Scottish origin. Kilclone is the memorial context, not independent proof of Patrick’s birthplace or exact place of death.
