## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

Patrick Glasgow-1440, represented by the Gordon Patrike baptism of 1667, is selected as a very uncertain working father of Patrick Glasgow-3920 of the Kilclone memorial. The memorial’s age 60 at death in May 1754 puts the younger Patrick’s birth around 1694. If the Gordon baptism was near birth, the older Patrick would have been about 27 then: an ordinary father–son interval. The matching Patrick/Patrike and Glasgow/Glasco name pair supplies a specific older candidate rather than an invented ancestor. This is a very weak generational model; neither name recurrence nor a feasible age establishes paternity. There is no reviewed deed, parish entry, probate clause, occupation or shared associate connecting Gordon or Stitchel to Kilclone. No migration or Scottish birthplace is newly established for the Meath man, and the catalogue is not a complete census of older Patricks.

The Gordon profile also gathers Patrick’s marriage entries with Helen Fairbairn/Fairbarne at Stitchel on 23 May 1707 and Eccles on 1 July 1707, followed by children in 1708–1716. Those adult events do not themselves identify the groom with the 1667 child. If that combined identity is correct, the proposed Meath son would predate the recorded 1707 marriage by about 13 years. Helen is therefore not selected as his mother. No earlier wife, earlier marriage, illegitimacy, second household or move is invented. The proposed father would require an unrecorded earlier child, and that is a real weakness of the model. The two 1707 dates remain separate recorded entries; their precise banns/ceremony relationship has not been newly checked. The supplied Gordon index reads 4 August 1667 while the structured birth field says 8 April 1667. This existing day/month conflict remains visible, and neither index is used as proof of an exact birthday.

The model keeps the two Patricks distinct. A man baptised in 1667 would have been at least about 86–87 in 1754, incompatible with the Kilclone memorial’s age 60 unless the memorial age is substantially wrong. No evidence justifies correcting that age by a generation. A father–son hypothesis fits the two dated accounts better than identifying them as one person, but another father or more distant relationship remains possible. The 1667 index names James as father of the Gordon child, not a father for the Meath Patrick. The modern James Glasgow-3107 identification and the Gordon account’s existing maternal and ancestral attachments retain their previous qualifications; this new edge does not independently prove them. ## Gordon household review — 17 October 2026

Prefer one household: James Glasgow, indexed with Agnes Waitte at Polwarth on 18 June 1663, is probably the James who fathered the Gordon children John (1666), Patrike (1667), Jean (1671), Robert (1673), Agnes (1676) and Margrat (1679). The father’s repeated full name, the neighbouring Berwickshire parishes, the first child three years after marriage and the orderly thirteen-year child sequence form a coherent family. The daughter Agnes supplies modest naming support; it is not the deciding evidence. The maintained 1702 Gordon marriage citation for Jean also names father James, adding a compatible adult continuation for her. Read together, these favour a single local family over six isolated fathers or an unrelated Polwarth couple. No concrete competing contemporary Gordon James household has been identified in the maintained material. That is the scope of this comparison, not a claim to have exhausted the population.

The child indexes explicitly name James as father but omit the mother. Their named-father roles are documented for the record-defined children; identifying that father with the 1663 Polwarth husband is probable. Agnes Watt is separately assessed as probable mother of all six children on the marriage, locality and sustained family sequence. This is an inference, not literal maternity in the indexes; a later marriage or another local James remains possible. Jean’s later marriage and the later adult identities of John and Patrick remain separately assessed. Patrick’s proposed Kilclone son and migration, James’s own John Glasgow-1084 ancestry and the unrelated Kilwaughter James are not strengthened. No original register image was newly inspected: this review combines the previously accepted index entries and maintained citations.
