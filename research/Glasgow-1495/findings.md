# Findings: [Katherine Glasgow (Glasgow-1495), born about 1565, Scotland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1495)

## Current conclusion

The original testament identifies Katherine as the spouse of **Thomas Weir of
the parish of Stevenston**, says she died in **January 1603**, and was confirmed
in **May 1605**. The precise confirmation day is unresolved: the manuscript
appears to read **[26?] May 1605**, whereas Findmypast and Grant's printed
calendar give **9 May 1605**. Neither reading is a death or marriage date. The existing
[Thomas Weir profile (Weir-4172)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Weir-4172)
has now been repurposed for this record-defined Stevenston husband: its former
structured 1590 Aberdeen birth was replaced by an uncertain birth before 1580.
Retain that profile as Katherine's spouse and do not restore the unrelated
Aberdeen identity. The biography captured on 7 September still says that
Thomas was born about 1590, so the complete replacement remains required.
Katherine's captured structured death still uses 9 May 1605 as a ''before''
boundary; the original testament instead records her death in January 1603.

The attached uncertain father,
[James Glasgow younger (Glasgow-3921), born before 1542, Corsankell](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3921),
is chronologically and geographically plausible but remains unproved. The
testament calendar names no parent.

William, James and probably Jonet Weir were the three minor executors, with Thomas
acting in their names. Their shared surname, minority and Thomas's
representation make them **very probably Katherine and Thomas's children**.
The parentage remains probable rather than explicit because the surviving
wording does not call any of them son or daughter. Complete local and live
duplicate audits found no compatible profile for any of the three. Separate
creation-ready drafts now preserve the probable parentage and uncertain
Stevenston birth-location inference without presenting either as direct fact.
