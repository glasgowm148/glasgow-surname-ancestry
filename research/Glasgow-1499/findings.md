# Findings: Hugh Glasgow (Glasgow-1499), probable duplicate

Last updated: 2026-08-13

## Current conclusion

No independent record has been found for a separate Hugh Glasgow born about
1700 and dead in 1726. The only specific event on the profile is demonstrably
misassigned: Classon Porter's published transcription of the Larne and
Kilwaughter session list marks **James Glasgow** deceased in 1726, then records
**Hugh Glasgow** separately as dead on 8 July 1767.

The documented Hugh is [Hugh Glasgow (Glasgow-1503)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1503),
whose 1736 Larne lease, session membership, 1767 death and family monument define
a coherent life. Glasgow-1499 has no fact that distinguishes him from that man.
It is therefore a probable duplicate created from the misread 1726 annotation,
not evidence for another son of [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1081)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1081).

The profiles should be merged into Glasgow-1503. The surviving profile should
retain Glasgow-1503's sourced identity and uncertain parentage; the unsupported
James-1081 father should not replace its current uncertain Robert-1502 placement.

## Duplicate and relationship assessment

- **Glasgow-1499 = Glasgow-1503:** probable duplicate.
- **Glasgow-1499 as a separate son of Glasgow-1081:** unsupported; no unique
  record defines this person.
- **Glasgow-1503's father:** still unproved. Merging the duplicate does not prove
  either James-1081 or Robert-1502 as father.

## Recommended correction

Merge Glasgow-1499 into Glasgow-1503. Remove 1726 as Hugh's death because it
belongs to James Glasgow in the source. Keep 8 July 1767 for the documented Hugh
and preserve all citations on both profiles. Parentage remains uncertain.
