# Findings: [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1502), active 1699-1700, County Antrim, Ireland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1502)

Record every useful result and negative search here as it is found.
Do not treat an unsourced tree or naming pattern as proof.

Last updated: 2026-08-11

## Current conclusion

The profile conflates an older Robert active in 1699-1700 with
[Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1143), born about 1710, Kilwaughter, County Antrim](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1143),
who was still living in 1772. The older Robert must have been born before about
1678 if he was an adult congregational representative in 1699. No reviewed
record makes James Glasgow his father or Hugh, James, William and the younger
Robert his sons. All four listed child relationships are therefore
**tree-only and unproved**, not merely awaiting citation cleanup.

There is also a material locality problem with the current reconstruction.
The complete 1699-1700 congregational extracts consistently assign the older
Robert to the **South Side**, while **John Glasgow senior** represented
Kilwaughter in 1699-1700 and **James Glasgow** was appointed Kilwaughter's
overseer in May 1700. This does not prove that Robert could not later have lived
at Kilwaughter or fathered the four men, but it removes the supposed same-place
bridge on which the attachment has largely depended.

The family narrative from which the arrangement appears to derive is explicit
about this weakness: it describes the older Robert only as a `candidate` father,
says the four similarly aged men `may be brothers`, and later calls them
`presumed brothers`. Their ages, congregation and naming patterns define a useful
research cluster, not a proved family.

The proof search is not limited to records explicitly saying `son of Robert`.
Wills naming siblings, nephews or cousins; guardianships; inheritance and tenancy
succession; and a uniquely closed chain through spouses or collateral relatives
would also prove an upward link. No reviewed record of the four men, their known
children, spouses or collateral associates presently supplies such a chain. Their
joint leases, congregation membership and petition signatures prove association,
but do not uniquely determine kinship.

The public volunteer Registry of Deeds index was also checked for every exact
Glasgow entry from 1708 to 1800. Its early results reproduce the known Larne and
Kilwaughter people but contain no father, brother, uncle or grandfather statement
for this group. FamilySearch's full-text handwritten-deed index remains an
outstanding search because the volunteer index is incomplete.

The destroyed 1727 will was also sought through searchable references to Betham,
Crossle and Tenison Groves abstracts. The National Library of Ireland's
consolidated Genealogical Office/Betham index contains no `Glasgow` surname entry,
and no other abstract or copy was located. That closes the Betham route but is not
proof that no private or archival transcript exists. A
separate 29-entry Ancestry collection titled *The Glasgow Family of Kilwaugheter
Parish, County Antrim, Northern Ireland and America* derives from material at the
Alabama Department of Archives and History; its images need checking for genuinely
contemporary extracts rather than another rendering of the same reconstructed
pedigree.

## Best next proof targets

1. **NRS `GD154/505-534`, Irish estate (Kilwaughter), 1636-1712.** Request the
   item-level inventory and inspect tenant lists, leases, rentals, accounts and
   correspondence around 1699-1712 for Robert of the South Side, John senior and
   James of Kilwaughter, with a named son, heir, household or tenancy successor.
   Do not search only under Robert: the district evidence means John or James may
   be equally important household heads.
2. **PRONI `MIC/1B/6`, original Larne/Kilwaughter session books, 1721-1748.** Read
   the full minutes, admissions, discipline and accounts rather than only the
   baptism/marriage chronology. A phrase such as `Hugh, son of Robert Glasgow`
   would be direct evidence; merely appearing in the same congregation would not.
3. **PRONI Larne title chain:** `D300/2/1/100/3A-B`,
   `D2977/3A/4/81/72A-B`, the 1751/1767 endorsements and 1824 renewal. These may
   establish succession between Hugh and William's descendants, although the
   1736 memorial itself does not reach Robert-1502.
4. **FamilySearch Registry of Deeds full-text search.** Search unindexed memorials
   before 1750 for Glasgow plus Larne, Kilwaughter, Newtown and Agnew, then inspect
   originals for kin terms rather than treating co-occurrence as kinship.
5. **Alabama Department of Archives and History family-history manuscript.**
   Inspect the 29-page item catalogued by Ancestry as *The Glasgow Family of
   Kilwaugheter Parish...*, recording the provenance of every pre-1750 statement.
   A later pedigree is not proof, but a reproduced family Bible, letter, will
   abstract or researcher transcript could preserve evidence now missing in
   Ireland.
6. **PRONI `LPC/1037`, Hugh Shannon of Ballykeel, copy will dated 5 April 1776.**
   Obtain the full copy and record all beneficiaries, executors, witnesses,
   residences and property clauses. Hugh's marriage to Ann Glasgow makes this a
   valid collateral route, but only explicit relational or uniquely closed
   inheritance language can establish the older generation.

## Focused FamilySearch full-text result - 20 August 2026

(Glasgow-1505), but supplies no parent-child statement linking him to this

Robert's sons therefore remains an unproved tree reconstruction.
