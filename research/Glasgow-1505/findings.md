# Findings: [William Glasgow (Glasgow-1505), Larne, died about 1783](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1505)

Last updated: 2026-10-06

## Current conclusion

William is a real, distinct older Larne man. A published transcript of the
Larne and Kilwaughter congregational accounts probably identifies him as
`William Glasgow Major` when the mortcloth was lodged with him in 1734. A
derivative account of the session book (not Porter) has William handling
mortcloth money in 1739/40, 1741 and 1743. Registry
memorial 62131 independently places William as an adjoining Larne occupier in
March 1736. These adult occurrences cannot belong to [William Glasgow-3326](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3326),
born in 1730.

The reported birth about 1708 and death about 1783 remain estimates. The exact
St Cedma's inscription has not been recovered, and the derivative reconstruction
reports conflicting tombstone-derived birth years of 1699 and 1708. The 1783
probate-index entry is in the **Diocese of Connor**, not Belfast, and cannot be
assigned uniquely because the William born in 1730 was also an adult Larne man.

This Larne man should not be equated with the William at Killycurry in the 1766
Derryloran return or the William of Killycoragh in the 1770 probate index. No
record moves William-1505 from County Antrim to those County Tyrone localities.

Registry memorial 62131 independently places William Glasgow as an adjoining
occupier at Larne by March 1736. The inspected original memorial 63221 confirms
a **10 March 1736** lease to William and Hugh Glasgow jointly (`D300/2/1/100/3A-B`)
and a related lease with 1751 and 1767 new-life endorsements
(`D2977/3A/4/81/72A`); its measuring lives are royal lives, not family members. The same
1736 title was renewed to Elizabeth Glasgow, widow of Larne, in 1824. This is a
strong property association and a possible descendant chain, but none of the
catalogue abstracts states that William and Hugh were brothers or identifies
Elizabeth's husband.

William's current descendants remain useful working hypotheses, not a proved
line. James `Glasgow-1509`, born or recorded about 1737 and later associated
with the Larne rope trade, is a chronologically plausible uncertain son. A
derivative register chronology instead calls the 1737 James a son of Hugh, so
the original `MIC/1B/6` entry is decisive. James `Glasgow-3986`, born about
1773, is far more naturally a grandson or later descendant than William's
direct son, although an uncertain attachment remains acceptable while the
reported shared memorial is unresolved.

## WikiTree recommendations

1. Retain birth **about 1708** and death **about 1783** only as uncertain
   estimates; the securely evidenced range is probable adult activity from 1734
   and direct property/congregational activity from 1736 through 1743.
2. Use Larne as proved probate residence, not automatically as certain death
   place.
3. Remove any statement that the Killycurry William was `listed alongside
   James`; the return does not say that.
4. Remove or qualify any identification with the Derryloran/Killycoragh men.
5. Keep Robert-1502, James-1509 and James-3986 as uncertain working attachments
   if useful; do not describe the edges as proved. No wife has yet been identified.
6. Inspect the named lives and title recitals in the PRONI leases; do not infer
   brotherhood or descent from joint occupation alone.
7. Prioritise `MIC/1B/6`, the 1751/1767 lease endorsements, the 1824 renewal,
   the complete St Cedma's inscriptions and William-3324's surviving 1815 will
   (`D300/1/5/49`).

## Identity assessment — "William Glasgow Major", 6 October 2026

On the page image of [Porter, vol. 3 p. 230](https://archive.org/details/christianunitari0003unse/page/230/mode/1up) "Major" is printed in roman type directly after the surname, with no comma before it. In these printed minutes ranks come before a name ("Capt. Patrick Agnew", "Captain Shaw") and words that distinguish namesakes come after it ("John Kerr, sen.", "Robert Mea, jun.", "Alex. Miller, Elder", "Alex. Miller, younger"). "Major" may therefore mean "the elder" (possible; a rank, a byname or a copying slip cannot be excluded). If so, a second William Glasgow was known in the congregation in 1734, not necessarily an adult.

This is a research lead only. An older William would be one candidate father to test for the men born about 1700-1710, beside the Dublin "Wm Glasroe" of the 1704 baptism. The derivative chronology's two gravestone-derived birth years for William (1699 and 1708) are unverified.
