== Biography ==
'''James Glasgow''' was reportedly born in '''1737'''. A Larne or Kilwaughter congregational record is said to contain an entry for a James, son of [[Glasgow-1499|Hugh Glasgow]], at '''New Town, Larne''', in that year.

The original congregational entry, or a reliable full transcription, has not yet been cited on this profile. The exact birth date, place and identification of Hugh as James’s father should therefore be treated as probable rather than conclusively established.

James was a '''ropemaker''' at Larne, County Antrim. A lease dated 10 September 1760, registered on 24 November 1763, describes him as a ropemaker of Belfast and leases him a marked-out ropewalk at Gardenmore in Larne for thirty-one years from 1 November 1760. He agreed to build a house at the south end of the ropewalk.<ref name="Ropewalk1760">Registry of Deeds transcript, lease dated 10 September 1760 and registered 24 November 1763, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-S7XZ-8 FamilySearch image].</ref>

On 28 March 1768 James, described as a ropemaker of the Old Town of Larne, assigned the plot, workhouse and remainder of the same lease to John Hill for £50. The matching commencement date and rent identify it as the 1760 ropewalk.<ref name="Ropewalk1768">Registry of Deeds transcript, assignment dated 28 March 1768, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-DDXW FamilySearch image].</ref>


A memorial at '''St Cedma’s Church, Larne''', is reported to record James Glasgow with the dates '''1737–1802''' and to name his wife as '''Janet Shutter'''. A photograph or complete reliable transcription of the monument should be cited before the exact dates and marriage are treated as fully verified.

James is reported to have died in '''1802'''.

An Irish probate-index transcription records a '''James Glasgow of Larne''' in the Diocese of Belfast in '''1803'''.<ref>Irish probate index transcription supplied July 2026, entry for James Glasgow, Diocese of Belfast, residence Larne, 1803.</ref>

The agreement in name and residence, together with probate in the year after the reported death, makes this a strong match to the man commemorated at St Cedma’s.

The 1803 entry should be understood as a probate or estate event. It does not replace the reported 1802 death year. Probate occurring in the year following death would be ordinary.

The probate entry establishes Larne as James’s recorded residence during the estate proceedings. It does not by itself prove that Larne was his place of death.

=== Wife and family ===

James’s wife is reported on the St Cedma’s memorial as '''Janet Shutter'''.

The complete inscription should be obtained and cited because the memorial may also contain:

* Janet’s dates;
* the date or duration of the marriage;
* children buried in the same plot;
* other Glasgow relatives; or
* wording clarifying whether the monument was erected by descendants.

No inspected baptismal, marriage, will or probate record currently cited on this profile names James’s children.

The 1803 probate record may be important. An original will, administration bond or surviving abstract could identify:

* Janet as his widow;
* sons or daughters;
* married daughters or sons-in-law;
* executors or administrators;
* business partners;
* property at Larne or Blackcave; or
* other relatives.

== Research Notes ==
=== Identification and evidence ===

The following records probably concern the same man:

* the James, son of Hugh Glasgow, reportedly recorded at New Town or Larne in 1737;
* James Glasgow, ropemaker, documented in the 1760 ropewalk lease and 1768 assignment;
* James Glasgow commemorated at St Cedma’s with dates 1737–1802 and wife Janet Shutter; and
* James Glasgow of Larne whose estate entered Belfast probate in 1803.

The chronology and repeated Larne association make this identification plausible and probably correct.

The strongest direct contemporary evidence presently cited is the 1760 ropewalk lease and its 1768 assignment. The baptism and memorial evidence still require complete source citations, and the probate index does not name James’s father or wife.

The profile should therefore distinguish between:

* directly documented facts, including James’s ropemaking and Larne residence in the 1760–1773 records;
* strong identity matches, including the 1803 probate entry; and
* reported but not yet fully exposed evidence, including the 1737 congregational entry and St Cedma’s memorial.

=== Ropemaker evidence and identity distinction ===

A Registry of Deeds memorial dated 14 May 1766 also names James Glasgow, ropemaker of Larne, as a witness.<ref>[https://www.irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=204122 Registry of Deeds Index Project, memorial 204122], deed dated 14 May 1766.</ref> In 1773 Mr James Glasgow of Larne, gentleman, swore that he was aged upwards of thirty, placing his birth before 27 October 1743.<ref name="Larne1773">Registry of Deeds memorial and affidavit, sworn 27 October 1773, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-M72W-M FamilySearch image].</ref> Alexander Holmes's 1799 will later identifies a Larne occupant as James Glasgow, roper. The repeated occupation and place form a coherent sequence.

The 1768 assignment was witnessed by a '''different''' James Glasgow who was expressly described as a tanner. A 1778 mortgage and the 1786 Blackcave lease continue that tanner sequence and fit [[Glasgow-3991|James Glasgow (about 1747–1812)]]. The two tradesmen should not be conflated.

The reported 1737 congregational occurrence and the present uncertain placement under [[Glasgow-1505|William Glasgow]] remain competing parentage hypotheses until the original entry or another relationship record is inspected.

=== Research priorities ===

Further research should obtain:

* the original or fullest available congregational entry for the 1737 James, son of Hugh Glasgow;
* the name of the congregation and exact wording of that entry;
* a photograph or complete transcription of the St Cedma’s memorial;
* evidence supporting Janet Shutter’s identity and marriage to James;
* the exact nature of the 1803 Belfast probate entry;
* the original will, administration record or bond, if it survives;
* a Betham or other pre-destruction abstract of the estate;
* names of beneficiaries, executors, witnesses or sureties; and
* records identifying any children of James and Janet.

=== Research conclusion ===

James Glasgow was directly recorded as a ropemaker in the 1760 Larne ropewalk lease, the 1766 memorial and the 1768 assignment.

The 1803 Belfast probate entry for James Glasgow of Larne is a strong match to the man reportedly commemorated at St Cedma’s with dates 1737–1802. It supports Larne as his probate residence while remaining consistent with death in 1802.

The reported identification of his wife as Janet Shutter and his father as Hugh Glasgow is plausible, but the complete memorial and congregational record should be cited before those relationships and the exact dates are treated as certain.

=== Current Larne reconstruction ===

James is securely the Belfast/Larne ropemaker in the 1760 Gardenmore ropewalk lease and its 1768 assignment, and the 1773 affidavit places that adult James at Larne and over thirty. The 1768 document separately calls its witness James Glasgow a tanner; that is a different occupational record group, probably [[Glasgow-3991|James Glasgow]], and does not change this James's occupation. The 1784 lease registered in 1790 should remain unassigned pending inspection: its Larne witness is not safely this man merely from the name. James is a plausible earlier-generation candidate in the Larne reconstruction, including as a possible father of [[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow]] and possible kin of [[Glasgow-3904|older Alexander Glasgow]], but no record yet makes either relationship. His 1762 marriage to Janet Shutter postdates Adam's estimated 1758 birth, so Janet cannot be called Adam's mother. A William Glasgow, merchant, witnessed the 1760 lease, but is not yet definitely [[Glasgow-1505|William Glasgow]].<ref name="LarneCurrent">Registry of Deeds memorials for the 1760 lease, 1768 assignment and 1773 affidavit, cited above.</ref>
