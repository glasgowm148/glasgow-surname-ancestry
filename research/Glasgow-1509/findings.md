Reviewed: 2026-10-16

**Selected Larne/Inishrush family, 16 October 2026:** The older Larne ropemaker Glasgow-1509 is the uncertain working father of Adam Glasgow-3902 and older Alexander Glasgow-3904. This profile is the working identity for the **older Larne ropemaker/roper**,
not the monument son of Hugh and Ann now represented separately by
[James Glasgow (Glasgow-3991)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3991).
It is also already separate from James `Glasgow-1504`, the c.1707 husband of
Sarah Murray. The catalogue must not describe 1504 and 1509 as a conflated
profile.
The 1803 Connor probate is a plausible match to a James reportedly commemorated
as 1737-1802 with Janet Shutter, but neither the complete monument nor probate
contents has been recovered.

The 20 August deed reconstruction separates this ropemaker from the contemporary
Larne tanner: the 1768 ropewalk assignment names both men. The 1778 affidavit
and 1786 Blackcave tanner lease therefore do not evidence this profile. The
1784 lease, sworn and registered in 1790, also calls its witnessing James
Glasgow a **tanner**; it belongs in that separate sequence, not this ropemaker's.

An earlier deed memorial supplies a much stronger identification. On 27
October 1773 James Glasgow of Larne, gentleman, swore at Ballyshannon, County
Donegal, that he had witnessed the underlying articles of agreement and their
memorial. He expressly stated that he was aged upwards of thirty years, placing
his birth before 27 October 1743. That excludes James `Glasgow-3991`, whose
monument places his birth in 1747/48, and strongly supports assignment to this
older Larne James. The record also proves that James was literate and alive in
1773; it does not name relatives.

The derivative congregation chronology records a James under Hugh in 1737. That
man is chronologically capable of establishing a ropewalk in 1761, but the
original entry has not been inspected. The current uncertain placement under
William `Glasgow-1505` is also chronologically plausible but lacks a direct
father-son record. These are competing hypotheses; neither is presently preferred
strongly enough to remove a useful uncertain attachment.

James Glasgow, ropemaker, is independently documented at Larne in newspaper
advertisements in 1761 and as a deed witness in 1766. Alexander Holmes's 1799 will refers to
houses occupied by James Glasgow, apparently a roper, proving that a Larne
namesake remained there after James-1144 had died in South Carolina.

The reported 1762 marriage to Janet Shutter and their parentage of William
`Glasgow-3324` are **probable**, not proved. William's daughter **Jane Shutter
Glasgow** supplies unusually specific circumstantial support. James
`Glasgow-3986`, born about 1773, is a plausible later son and may explain why his
former direct attachment to the much older William-1505 looked generationally
compressed.

### Tested relationship hypothesis — 13 September 2026

**Hypothesis:** this identifiable 1737 Larne ropemaker was the father of Adam
Glasgow of Inishrush (`Glasgow-3902`); `Glasgow-3905` is only the existing
placeholder for that father role. Adam's c.1758 birth gives an ordinary age-21
father–son interval, and Adam's proved son James (c.1794) offers limited positive
naming support. The reported 1762 marriage to Janet Shutter is four years later
than Adam's birth, so the hypothesis requires an earlier mother, a premarital
birth, or an erroneous date; Janet must not be made Adam's mother. No inspected
record joins Larne to Inishrush or names the relationship. The two 1740 local
Hugh households and the older Adam-and-Maria naming model remain alternatives.

Older Alexander `Glasgow-3904` fits more naturally as Adam's possible brother
than son: James c.1737 could have been about 33 at Alexander's estimated c.1770
birth, whereas Adam c.1758 was too young for the existing chronology. This is
only a reconstruction, not a basis to merge profiles or alter attachments.

## Registry of Deeds identity correction — 20 August 2026

The Larne deed sequence separates the ropemaker from a contemporary tanner.
They must not be combined merely because both were named James Glasgow.

The record-defined ropemaker is the strongest match for this profile:

- A [lease dated 10 September 1760 and registered 24 November 1763](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-S7XZ-8) calls James Glasgow of Belfast a ropemaker and leases him a marked-out ropewalk at Gardenmore in Larne for 31 years from 1 November 1760. He agreed to build a house at the south end of the ropewalk. William Glasgow of Larne, merchant, and James Baggs, shoemaker, witnessed the lease.
- A [28 March 1768 assignment](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-DDXW) calls the leaseholder James Glasgow of the Old Town of Larne, **ropemaker**, and assigns the plot, workhouse and remainder of the same 31-year term to John Hill for £50. The matching commencement date and rent identify it as the 1760 ropewalk lease.
- The 1768 deed was witnessed by a **different** James Glasgow, explicitly described as a tanner. That witness is treated under `Glasgow-3991`.
- In the [1773 memorial and affidavit](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-M72W-M), Mr James Glasgow of Larne, gentleman, stated that he was aged upwards of thirty. This excludes `Glasgow-3991`, born about 1747–1748, and is compatible with the older ropemaker.
- Alexander Holmes's 1799 will later calls a Larne occupant James Glasgow a roper. The occupational continuity makes this profile the best match.

The 1778 tanner affidavit and 1786 Blackcave tanner lease should no longer be used as evidence for this profile. They form a separate occupational sequence that most probably belongs to `Glasgow-3991`.

The original [memorial 278037, image 437](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJ4-4SJF) now settles the former automated “turner” reading: its 14 January 1784 lease calls the Larne witness James Glasgow **tanner**; the memorial was sworn 17 August and registered 23 August 1790. It strengthens the separate tanner sequence but supplies no kinship evidence.

The [1790 Larne and Kilwaughter mills conveyance](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJ4-331B), witnessed by James Glasgow and Charles McGarel, is compatible with this man and includes Alexander Holmes among the executing parties. It gives James no occupation, however, so it remains probable rather than conclusive.

Both original occupation readings were independently checked. The Farmer wording does not overturn the Tanner reading in the other deed. Whether the two witnesses were the same man with different descriptions or namesakes remains unresolved. The farmer is not assigned to the tanner or to the South Carolina settler from this record alone; neither assignment supplies kinship.
