# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1509), 1737–1802, Larne](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1509)

Last updated: 2026-08-11

## Current conclusion

This profile is the working identity for the **older Larne ropemaker/roper**,
not the monument son of Hugh and Ann now represented separately by
[James Glasgow (Glasgow-3991)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3991).
It is also already separate from James `Glasgow-1504`, the c.1707 husband of
Sarah Murray. The catalogue must not describe 1504 and 1509 as a conflated
profile.
The 1803 Connor probate is a plausible match to a James reportedly commemorated
as 1737-1802 with Janet Shutter, but neither the complete monument nor probate
contents has been recovered.

The derivative congregation chronology records a James under Hugh in 1737. That
man is chronologically capable of establishing a ropewalk in 1761, but the
original entry has not been inspected. The current uncertain placement under
William `Glasgow-1505` is also chronologically plausible but lacks a direct
father-son record. These are competing hypotheses; neither is presently preferred
strongly enough to remove a useful uncertain attachment.

James Glasgow, ropemaker, is independently documented at Larne in newspaper
advertisements in 1761 and as a deed witness in 1766. A 1786 lease names James
Glasgow, tanner, at Larne. These may be the same James, but the occupation change
means identity should remain cautious. Alexander Holmes's 1799 will refers to
houses occupied by James Glasgow, apparently a roper, proving that a Larne
namesake remained there after James-1144 had died in South Carolina.

The reported 1762 marriage to Janet Shutter and their parentage of William
`Glasgow-3324` are **probable**, not proved. William's daughter **Jane Shutter
Glasgow** supplies unusually specific circumstantial support. James
`Glasgow-3986`, born about 1773, is a plausible later son and may explain why his
former direct attachment to the much older William-1505 looked generationally
compressed.

## WikiTree recommendations

1. Retain the 1803 probate event for the 1802 Larne man as a possible identity
   match, but do not use it to prove a father, wife or child.
2. Retain death in **1802** if supported by the memorial; do not replace it
   with the probate year.
3. Add Larne as death place only if the memorial or another death source says
   so, not from probate residence alone.
4. Keep Glasgow-3991 separate from this profile. Inspect the original 1737 entry,
   reported James-Janet monument and 1762 marriage before upgrading either the
   William-1505 or Hugh-1503 parent hypothesis.
5. Keep the ropemaker and tanner identities explicitly provisional until a lease,
   will, spouse or property succession joins them.
6. Retain Glasgow-3324 and Glasgow-3986 as reasonable working children, with the
   former assessed probable and the latter uncertain.

### Record-bearing passages to audit

- Husband of Janet Shutter — married 1762 in Non-Subscribing Presbyterian Church of Ireland at age 25
- James Glasgow was reportedly born in 1737. A Larne or Kilwaughter congregational record is said to contain an entry for a James, son of Hugh Glasgow, at New Town, Larne, in that year.
- The original congregational entry, or a reliable full transcription, has not yet been cited on this profile. The exact birth date, place and identification of Hugh as James’s father should therefore be treated as probable rather than conclusively established.
- On 1 November 1786, Randall William, Earl of Antrim, granted a counterpart lease to James Glasgow, tanner, of Larne, for approximately five acres at Blackcave. The lease was for eleven years at an annual rent of £14 5s. 7d.[1]
- A memorial at St Cedma’s Church, Larne, is reported to record James Glasgow with the dates 1737–1802 and to name his wife as Janet Shutter. A photograph or complete reliable transcription of the monument should be cited before the exact dates and marriage are treated as fully verified.
- James is reported to have died in 1802.
- An Irish probate-index transcription records a James Glasgow of Larne in the Diocese of Belfast in 1803.[2]
- The agreement in name and residence, together with probate in the year after the reported death, makes this a strong match to the man commemorated at St Cedma’s.
- The 1803 entry should be understood as a probate or estate event. It does not replace the reported 1802 death year. Probate occurring in the year following death would be ordinary.
- The probate entry establishes Larne as James’s recorded residence during the estate proceedings. It does not by itself prove that Larne was his place of death.
- James’s wife is reported on the St Cedma’s memorial as Janet Shutter.
- The complete inscription should be obtained and cited because the memorial may also contain:
- Janet’s dates; the date or duration of the marriage; children buried in the same plot; other Glasgow relatives; or wording clarifying whether the monument was erected by descendants.
- No inspected baptismal, marriage, will or probate record currently cited on this profile names James’s children.
- The 1803 probate record may be important. An original will, administration bond or surviving abstract could identify:
