== Biography ==
'''Father assessment, 17 October 2026:''' The Moneymore father account, [[Glasgow-1024|James Glasgow]], is assessed as the probable father of [[Glasgow-12|Robert]], [[Glasgow-649|Arthur]] and [[Glasgow-1543|Joseph]]. This is an inference from the specific family-history father attribution and their established brotherhood; other identity, ancestry and maternity questions retain their separate uncertainty.<ref name="MoneymoreFatherReview" />
'''Joseph Glasgow''' was born before 6 August 1765, probably during the 1750s and probably in County Londonderry, Ireland. His exact birth and parents remain unproved. He later lived in Wythe County, Virginia, where he executed his will on 2 April 1831. It was presented for probate on 11 April, by which date he was dead.

=== Birth and origin ===

The 1810 Wythe County census records Joseph's household with one white male aged 16–25, one white male aged 45 or over, one white female aged 45 or over, and five enslaved people. There was no male aged 26–44. Joseph, as the named householder, was therefore almost certainly the man aged at least 45, placing his birth before the census date of 6 August 1765.<ref name="Census1810">U.S. census, Wythe County, Virginia, 1810, Joseph Glasgow household; digitised McGhee transcription, [https://archive.org/details/censusof1810wyth00mcgh Internet Archive]. The original census image should be cited when obtained.</ref>

An estimate of about 1755 is compatible with that census and with Joseph's known brothers. It is not an exact recorded year. The former estimate of about 1735 is unsupported.

Joseph's brother [[Glasgow-12|Robert Glasgow]] testified in 1834 that he was born in County Derry in 1749 and migrated in 1769. Robert's pension claim was rejected because his service narrative conflicted with known wartime circumstances, but the declaration remains sworn evidence of Robert's claimed origin and age.<ref name="RobertPension">Robert Glasgow, Revolutionary War pension application R4057, declaration dated 28 July 1834, [https://revwarapps.org/r4057.pdf transcription and file extract].</ref> Because Joseph and Robert are independently proved brothers, County Londonderry is a reasonable but still uncertain birthplace for Joseph.

A Joseph “Glassgo” appears in a 1777 Pennsylvania militia compilation for Captain George Crawford's company and a Joseph Glasgow witnessed John Glasgow's 1779 Peters Township will. These may concern the later Wythe County man, but no record yet bridges the Pennsylvania and Virginia identities.<ref name="PennMilitia">"American Revolutionary Soldiers of Franklin County, Pennsylvania," entry for Joseph Glassgo in Captain George Crawford's company, [https://lfweb.franklincountypa.gov/WebLinkArchives/DocView.aspx?dbid=0&amp;id=3230907&amp;repo=FranklinCounty8 digitised compilation].</ref>

=== 1802 Brooke County record ===

A March 1802 Brooke County, Virginia, court record names a '''Joseph Glasgow''' among the members of an inquest.<ref name="Brooke1802">Brooke County, Virginia, court record, March 1802, naming Joseph Glasgow among members of an inquest, [https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QSQ-G9VR-ZJX9 FamilySearch image].</ref> The entry supplies no age, family relationship or other identifier connecting that man to Joseph of Wythe County. It is retained as a same-name candidate record rather than an event proved for this profile.

=== Proved brothers ===

A letter dated 6 November 1806 from “Joseph and Betty Glasgow” to [[Glasgow-649|Arthur Glasgow]] addresses Arthur as a brother and refers to “brother Robert.” The letter is printed by David Faris and should ultimately be checked against the original or Faris's stated provenance.<ref name="FarisLetter">David Faris, ''The Glasgow Family of Adams County, Ohio'' (1990), pp. 14–19; [https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567 WorldCat catalogue record].</ref>

Joseph's will independently identifies Arthur as his deceased brother by describing the younger Robert as “the son of my deceased brother Arthur” and as Joseph's nephew.<ref name="JosephWill">Wythe County, Virginia, will of Joseph Glasgow, executed 2 April and presented 11 April 1831; [https://swvirginians.wordpress.com/2014/12/12/joseph-glasgow-pulaski-co-va-will-of-1831/ earlier published transcription] and [https://freepages.genealogy.rootsweb.com/~swva/Joseph%20Glasgow%20Will%20of%201831.htm RootsWeb transcription]. See also [https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA305 Library of Virginia, Wythe County microfilm catalogue], Will Books 3–4 and Order Book 1830–1833.</ref>

Oren Frederic Morton likewise identifies Arthur, Robert and Joseph as three brothers who came to the colonies in the late eighteenth century.<ref name="Morton">Oren Frederic Morton, ''A History of Rockbridge County, Virginia'' (Staunton, Virginia, 1920), pp. 253–254, 304–306.</ref> Morton is a later published account and corroborates the family tradition; it is not independent proof of the brothers' parents or migration details.

The evidence therefore proves that Joseph, Arthur and Robert were brothers. It does not by itself identify their parents.

An agreement dated 19 September 1822 concerning Arthur's estate names Arthur's participating children and devisees: [[Glasgow-648|Joseph]], [[Glasgow-2070|John]], [[Glasgow-902|Robert]], Rebecca Glasgow, wife of John Kerr, and [[Glasgow-2071|Peggy or Margaret Glasgow]]. Joseph served as Arthur's acting executor. The agreement directly establishes those five as Arthur's children or devisees, although it need not be a complete list of every child Arthur had.<ref name="ArthurAgreement">Rockbridge County chancery papers, agreement concerning Arthur Glasgow's estate, 19 September 1822, [https://old.lva.virginia.gov/ChanceryImg/163-Rockbridge/BOX%20016/163_1833_005_0089.pdf Library of Virginia image].</ref>

=== Proposed parents ===

Faris's catalogue description calls Robert a son of “James Glasgow of Moneymore” and says Robert migrated with three brothers and a sister. Since Joseph and Robert are proved brothers, [[Glasgow-1024|James Glasgow of Moneymore]] is a plausible father for Joseph. This remains indirect and derivative: the accessible summary concerns Robert, and the underlying source for the father statement has not been inspected.<ref name="Faris">David Faris, ''The Glasgow Family of Adams County, Ohio'' (1990), [https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567 WorldCat catalogue description].</ref>

No independent record identifies [[Harkness-1149|Mary Harkness]] as Joseph's mother. Her attachment traces through a user-contributed compiled pedigree, Brøderbund World Family Tree, volume 2, tree 6075, rather than a parish record, will, deed, Bible or contemporary family paper.<ref name="WFT">[https://www.werelate.org/wiki/Person%3AArthur_Glasgow_%281%29 WeRelate derivative profile], citing Brøderbund World Family Tree, volume 2, tree 6075.</ref>

The attached parents should therefore remain uncertain. Mary Harkness is presently unsupported, while James is only a plausible indirect lead.

=== Other sibling leads ===

The will of '''[[Glasgow-4027|John Glasgow of Peters Township]]''' was dated 14 March and proved 3 September 1779. A published abstract names his wife Ann, daughters whose names appear as “Lates” or possibly Lettice and Agnes, son Joseph, and an unborn child. Witnesses included Joseph Glasgow, James Neely and Mary Arter. The index places the will in Cumberland County Will Book C, page 161.<ref name="JohnWill">Cumberland County, Pennsylvania, Will Book C, p. 161, will of [[Glasgow-4027|John Glasgow of Peters Township]], 1779; [https://www.sampubco.com/wills/pa/pacumberland01.htm published index/abstract]. The original will and probate packet have not been inspected.</ref>

John is a credible candidate for the otherwise unidentified third brother in Faris's migration account: Arthur and Joseph account for two brothers, and a Joseph Glasgow witnessed John's will in the same Pennsylvania network. Witnessing a will does not prove kinship; John is attached as an uncertain brother pending stronger evidence. Faris's unnamed immigrant sister likewise remains an unidentified sibling lead.

=== Betty and William Glasgow ===

The 1806 letter was jointly sent by “Joseph and Betty Glasgow.” This places a woman named Betty in Joseph's immediate household and makes her very likely his wife. Betty was commonly used for Elizabeth, but no located record establishes her formal name, maiden surname, marriage or parents. The woman aged at least 45 in the 1810 household may be Betty, but the census does not name her.

Joseph's will left the residue to the children of [[Glasgow-3678|William Glasgow]], describing William as the person “who was raised by me.” It did not call William his son. The male aged 16–25 in Joseph's 1810 household is compatible with William's estimated 1788 birth, but neither fact proves biological parentage. William may have been a son, nephew or another dependent relative; he should not be described as formally adopted without evidence.

Compiled material identifies William's wife as [[Patterson-13881|Sarah or Sally Patterson]]. William's reported 1865 civil death is a priority record because Pulaski County's surviving death register may name his parents, birthplace and informant.<ref name="PulaskiRegisters">[https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA231 Library of Virginia, Pulaski County microfilm catalogue], Register of Deaths, 1853–1870, and accompanying index.</ref>

=== Death and estate ===

Joseph signed his will on 2 April 1831. It was presented to the Wythe County court on 11 April after his death. He therefore died after executing the will and by the probate hearing. The record does not establish 11 April as his exact death date.<ref name="JosephWill" />

== Research Notes ==
=== Probable Moneymore father — 17 October 2026 ===

The existing Moneymore father account, Glasgow-1024, is assessed as the probable father of Robert Glasgow-12 and, by inference through their established brotherhood, Arthur Glasgow-649 and Joseph Glasgow-1543. The catalogue description of David Faris’s 1990 genealogy explicitly identifies the Adams County emigrant Robert (1749–1839) as a son of a James resident at Moneymore. It identifies a particular father and son, rather than offering a same-name local candidate. The family letter of 6 November 1806 printed by Faris names Arthur and Robert as Joseph’s brothers; Joseph’s 1831 will independently identifies Arthur as his deceased brother and the younger Robert as Arthur’s son. These sources distinguish the two Roberts and establish the sibling group to which the father attribution can reasonably extend. Robert’s pension supports the County Derry setting and 1769 migration. The approximate generations are compatible; the circa-1722 birth estimate is not independent evidence. The positive father attribution and established family group together warrant probable parentage, while remaining below documented or certain.

The father attribution survives here through a catalogue abstract of a retrospective genealogy; the book’s underlying parentage notes and a contemporary Irish parent record have not been inspected. The letter and will corroborate the sibling group, not the father’s name independently. Glasgow-1024 is used for its record-defined Moneymore father role: the inherited dates, Mary Harkness marriage, 1770 immigration, 1804 naturalisation, older James pedigree and proposed Corvanaghan identity are separate questions. The Corvanaghan identity and older father remain very uncertain. No probability is assigned to those claims by this review. The same father is inferred across the three established paternal-surname siblings; no evidence for different fathers has been identified. Other proposed siblings, including James-3305, John and Mary, and the proposed Adam connection are not upgraded.

Three existing father links change from an uncertain saved WikiTree flag and an ungraded/unknown local assessment to a formal probable local grade. No parent is newly attached, no live flag changes and no new working-tree component is created. All mothers remain separately uncertain; James’s own parents are unchanged. This is an inference from the combined existing evidence, not a newly discovered parent record.

=== Published family tradition ===

Morton states that “Arthur Glasgow was a descendant of Earl Glasgow, of Scotland,” gives information about Arthur's family through Alexander McNutt Glasgow, and says Glasgow Township was named for the family.<ref name="Morton" /> These statements are retained as part of the published family tradition. They do not establish Joseph's parents or a genealogical connection to an Earl of Glasgow.

=== Reassessment of the unidentified brother ===

Faris's catalogue summary says Robert migrated with three brothers and one sister.<ref name="FarisSiblingCount">David Faris, ''The Glasgow Family of Adams County, Ohio'' (1990), [https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567 WorldCat catalogue summary].</ref> Robert, Arthur and Joseph account for three of the four brothers.

A later Adams County family-history account explicitly identifies [[Glasgow-4027|John Glasgow of Peters Township]] as their brother and states that Joseph, Robert and Arthur moved to Virginia after John's death in 1779.<ref name="AdamsCountyJohn">Joyce Wilson, "Our Ancestors of Adams County," ''People's Defender'', 29 March 2024, [https://www.peoplesdefender.com/2024/03/29/our-ancestors-of-adams-county-3/ online article]. This is a secondary family-history account.</ref> Joseph's appearance as a witness to John's will provides a contemporary association, although witnessing alone does not prove kinship.<ref name="JohnWillIndex">Cumberland County, Pennsylvania, Will Book C, p. 161, will of John Glasgow; [https://www.sampubco.com/wills/pa/pacumberland01.htm published will-book index].</ref>

The matching sibling count makes John a strong candidate for the fourth brother. The original will and probate packet should still be examined for an explicit relationship before the relationship can be treated as proved. This evidence weakens the unsupported claim that [[Glasgow-3305|another James Glasgow]] was the additional brother who remained in Ireland.


=== Neely associate network ===

James Neely witnessed the 1779 will of [[Glasgow-4027|John Glasgow of Peters Township]].<ref name="NeelyWillNetwork">Cumberland County, Pennsylvania, Will Book C, p. 161, will of John Glasgow, dated 14 March 1779 and proved 3 September 1779; [https://www.sampubco.com/wills/pa/pacumberland01.htm will-book index].</ref>

[[Glasgow-563|William Glasgow]], son of Joseph's brother [[Glasgow-12|Robert]], later married [[Montgomery-6593|Rhoda '''Neely''' Montgomery]], and the couple reused Neely in the name of a daughter. A published Adams County account identifies William and Rhoda and also identifies John as brother of Robert, Arthur and Joseph.<ref name="AdamsCountyNeely">Joyce Wilson, "Our Ancestors of Adams County," ''People's Defender'', 29 March 2024, [https://www.peoplesdefender.com/2024/03/29/our-ancestors-of-adams-county-3/ online article]. This is a secondary family-history account.</ref>

This repeated associate surname strengthens the John-brother hypothesis as FAN-network evidence. It does not establish that James Neely was related to Rhoda or explain her middle name, so it cannot independently prove the relationship.
