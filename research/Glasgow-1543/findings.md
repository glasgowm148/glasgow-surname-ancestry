# Findings: [Joseph Glasgow (Glasgow-1543), Wythe County, Virginia](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1543)

Last updated: 2026-10-17

## Integrated conclusion

Joseph was born **before 6 August 1765**, based on the age structure of his named 1810 Wythe County household. About 1755 is a defensible working estimate, not a recorded birth year. County Londonderry is probable from his proved brother Robert's sworn origin, but Moneymore is not established for Joseph.

Joseph executed his will on 2 April 1831 and was dead when it was presented on 11 April. His death occurred between those events; 11 April is the probate date, not a proved death date.

## Current-profile evidence preserved

The newer live biography adds a March 1802 Brooke County, Virginia, inquest record naming a Joseph Glasgow. The FamilySearch image is preserved in the replacement draft. No age, relationship or other discriminating detail presently connects that man to Joseph of Wythe County, so it remains a same-name candidate rather than a proved residence or event.

Morton's statements that Arthur, Robert and Joseph were brothers, that the family descended from an Earl of Glasgow, and that Glasgow Township was named for the family are also preserved. The brother statement is consistent with stronger evidence; the aristocratic-descent tradition does not establish parentage.

## New kin and disambiguation

Arthur's 19 September 1822 estate agreement directly names Joseph, John, Robert, Rebecca wife of John Kerr, and Peggy or Margaret as participating children and devisees. Known WikiTree profiles are [Glasgow-648](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-648), [Glasgow-2070](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2070), [Glasgow-902](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-902), and [Glasgow-2071](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2071). Rebecca does not yet have an identified profile.

John Glasgow of Peters Township is sufficiently identified by his 1779 will to be a distinct historical person, but the present task does not create or attach him. His original probate packet should be obtained before using him as Joseph's brother.

## John of Peters Township as the fourth brother — 20 August 2026

Faris's catalogue summary says Robert immigrated with three brothers and one sister. Robert, Arthur and Joseph account for three of the four brothers.

A 2024 Adams County family-history article identifies John Glasgow of Peters Township, who died in 1779, as their brother and says that the surviving brothers Joseph, Robert and Arthur then moved to Virginia. Joseph was also a witness to John's will. This changes John from a merely plausible associate to a strong secondary sibling hypothesis, although the original will and probate packet have not yet supplied an explicit relationship.

## Source findings — 1810 Wythe County census household, 7 October 2026

The 1810 United States census for Wythe County, Virginia, records Joseph Glasgow's household as one free white male aged 16–25, one free white male aged 45 or over, one free white female aged 45 or over, and five enslaved people, with no male aged 26–44. As the named householder Joseph was almost certainly the man aged 45 or over, which gives the birth limit of before 6 August 1765 (the census date). The woman aged 45 or over may be Betty, and the young man is compatible with William ([Glasgow-3678](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3678)), but the census names neither. Seen as a published transcription: McGhee, [Census of 1810, Wythe County](https://archive.org/details/censusof1810wyth00mcgh) (Internet Archive); the original census image has not yet been cited. Grade: strong inference for the birth limit; identities of the other members unproved.

## Probable Moneymore father — 17 October 2026

The existing Moneymore father account, Glasgow-1024, is assessed as the probable father of Robert Glasgow-12 and, by inference through their established brotherhood, Arthur Glasgow-649 and Joseph Glasgow-1543. The catalogue description of David Faris’s 1990 genealogy explicitly identifies the Adams County emigrant Robert (1749–1839) as a son of a James resident at Moneymore. It identifies a particular father and son, rather than offering a same-name local candidate. The family letter of 6 November 1806 printed by Faris names Arthur and Robert as Joseph’s brothers; Joseph’s 1831 will independently identifies Arthur as his deceased brother and the younger Robert as Arthur’s son. These sources distinguish the two Roberts and establish the sibling group to which the father attribution can reasonably extend. Robert’s pension supports the County Derry setting and 1769 migration. The approximate generations are compatible; the circa-1722 birth estimate is not independent evidence. The positive father attribution and established family group together warrant probable parentage, while remaining below documented or certain.

The father attribution survives here through a catalogue abstract of a retrospective genealogy; the book’s underlying parentage notes and a contemporary Irish parent record have not been inspected. The letter and will corroborate the sibling group, not the father’s name independently. Glasgow-1024 is used for its record-defined Moneymore father role: the inherited dates, Mary Harkness marriage, 1770 immigration, 1804 naturalisation, older James pedigree and proposed Corvanaghan identity are separate questions. The Corvanaghan identity and older father remain very uncertain. No probability is assigned to those claims by this review. The same father is inferred across the three established paternal-surname siblings; no evidence for different fathers has been identified. Other proposed siblings, including James-3305, John and Mary, and the proposed Adam connection are not upgraded.

Three existing father links change from an uncertain saved WikiTree flag and an ungraded/unknown local assessment to a formal probable local grade. No parent is newly attached, no live flag changes and no new working-tree component is created. All mothers remain separately uncertain; James’s own parents are unchanged. This is an inference from the combined existing evidence, not a newly discovered parent record.
