== Biography ==
'''Edward Glasgow''' has no identified birth or baptismal record. The estimate of about 1570 and the broad birthplace Scotland are uncertain family-tree traditions. The often-repeated estimate of about 1583 is a narrower inference from his appearance as the father of a child baptised in 1615, not a recorded birth year. Either estimate is chronologically possible; his documented connection to Inveresk and Musselburgh includes his daughter’s 1609 index entry and his son’s 1615 baptism and does not prove that he was born there.

Edward is currently attached to [[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow]] as an '''uncertain''' son. No identified birth, baptismal, testamentary or other relational record names Edward's father. His proposed father is James younger of Corsankell, born about 1540, rather than that man’s father [[Glasgow-1168|James Glasgow elder]], born about 1515. The approximately 1540/1570 generations fit, but his own origin is unresolved; the existing father hypothesis is assessed at 18%, uncertain.

=== Known Children ===

Edward appears in the Inveresk and Musselburgh parish register as the father of:

* [[Glasgow-1558|Edward Glasgow]] – christened 6 June 1615
** ''Scotland Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950'':  [https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XTRN-75P FamilySearch Record]

The 1615 baptism proves that a man named Edward Glasgow was the child's father. It permits, but does not prove, an estimated birth in the later sixteenth century; it does not resolve the competing about-1570 and about-1583 estimates.

=== Context and Possible Family Connections ===

* [[Glasgow-994|Eduard Glasgow (1609–)]] is another individual baptized in the same parish, potentially a nephew or cousin.
* Edward’s activity in the Inveresk and Musselburgh area aligns him with a broader cluster of Glasgow family members recorded in early 17th-century Midlothian. This includes [[Glasgow-1148|Alexander Glasgow (abt.1600–bef.1700)]] and his documented sons and daughters born between 1626 and 1637.

=== Possible Fisherrow sasine appearances ===

An Edward Glasgow in Fischerraw shares reference 9/135 with [[Glasgow-1148|Alexander Glasgow]] and Alexander’s father James, tailor in Fischerraw and burgess of Musselburgh; the recording date is 8 July 1624.<ref name="EarlyFisherrow1624">Scottish Record Office, Indexes no.38, Edinburgh etc. sasines 1624–1630, printed pp.187–188; 8 July 1624, RS25 vol.9 fol.135, and 11 July 1625, vol.10 fol.171, are recording dates; printed pages visually inspected 15 October 2026, original instruments unread; [https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015072481057&seq=193].</ref> This Edward is a possible identity for the Inveresk father represented here. A man already father of a child in 1615 fits an adult property association in 1624, without relying on either the circa-1570 or circa-1583 birth estimate.

Edward also shares the later reference 33/258, recorded 14 February 1646, with Alexander and James Glasgow, weaver.<ref name="EarlyFisherrow1646">Scottish Record Office, Indexes no.44 (1958), Edinburgh etc. register of sasines 1644–1653, printed pp.159–160; RS25 33/258, recorded 14 February 1646; 33/360, 15 May 1646; 34/287, 10 February 1647; printed columns visually inspected 15 October 2026, original instruments unread; [https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015072481222&seq=167].</ref> The actual register instruments have not been inspected. They may identify a party, predecessor, witness or another capacity; the index association supplies neither a kinship nor proof of continued ownership.

[[Glasgow-994|Eduard Glasgow]], baptised in 1609 as James’s son, is a genuine alternative within James’s proposed household. [[Glasgow-1558|Edward Glasgow]], baptised in 1615 as this Edward’s son, is another alternative, particularly in 1646. A minor could be named in a land instrument, so the younger men are not excluded merely by age. These occurrences do not identify this Edward’s father or connect the Fisherrow family to Corsankell as a fact.

=== Record-specific identities and ancestry ===

For the Edward in Fischerraw at 8 July 1624, 9/135, this Edward is the preferred possible identity at 56%. He was already a father in the same parish in 1609 and 1615, whereas [[Glasgow-994|Eduard Glasgow]], indexed in 1609 as James’s child, would be approximately fifteen and [[Glasgow-1558|Edward Glasgow]], indexed in 1615, approximately nine if baptised near birth. A minor could be named in a land instrument; neither younger man is excluded. Comparative assessments are this Edward 56%, Eduard-994 30%, Edward-1558 8%, and another Edward 6%. These percentages express subjective judgments, not measured probabilities.<ref name="EarlyFisherrow1624" />

For 14 February 1646, 33/258, Eduard-994 is separately preferred at 43%. His approximately 37-year-old generation and his existing local James-family account offer a coherent household comparison. Edward-1558, approximately 31, remains a substantial alternative at 30%; this Edward remains an alternative at 20%, with another Edward at 7%. The circa-1570 estimate would make this Edward approximately 76, but the estimate cannot establish death or incapacity. Eduard’s index date is 27 February 1609, Inveresk and Musselburgh OPR 689/10/13; the accepted Findmypast transcripts give year and father James, without an exact day.<ref name="Eduard1609">National Records of Scotland, OPR 689/10/13, Inveresk and Musselburgh, supplied index entry 27 February 1609, Eduard Glasgow, father James Glasgow; original image uninspected. Findmypast, Scotland, Parish Births &amp; Baptisms 1564–1929, accepted transcripts [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=R_686174177&tab=this R_686174177] and [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=R_693575445&tab=this R_693575445], year 1609 and father James, no exact day.</ref><ref name="EarlyFisherrow1646" />

The index’s family clauses identify [[Glasgow-1148|Alexander]] as son of James, tailor in Fischerraw and burgess of Musselburgh. [[Bie-55|Christina Bye]] is James’s wife, and [[Clerk-296|Janet Clerk]] is Alexander’s wife. Identification of that James with [[Glasgow-991|James Glasgow]] and the resulting father relationship to Alexander remain probable, assessed at 84%; the alleged mother in the 1614 William baptism has not been independently verified. Neither Edward occurrence states kinship or transaction capacity. Alexander and James the weaver also share 33/360, recorded 15 May 1646, and 34/287, recorded 10 February 1647; these are not additional Edward appearances. The separate 1624 and 1646 preferences do not establish inheritance, continuing ownership, or two proved distinct Edwards.<ref name="EarlyFisherrow1624" /><ref name="EarlyFisherrow1646" />

The proposed Ayrshire origin remains weak. [[Glasgow-1494|Jonet Glasgow]]’s Stevenston testament index names her husband [[Logan-5900|George Logan]], not a father, and [[Glasgow-3539|Mary Glasgow]]’s Saltcoats testament index names her husband [[Montgomerie-601|James Montgomerie]], without a parent. Their uncertain attachments to Edward cannot independently establish his origin.<ref name="AyrshireWomen">Francis J. Grant, ed., ''The Commissariot Record of Glasgow: Register of Testaments, 1547–1800'' (Scottish Record Society, 1901): Jonet Glasgw, spouse to George Logane in Steinstoun, 2 September 1628, NRS CC9/7/22; Mary, spouse to James Montgomerie, sailor in Saltcoats, parish of Ardrossan, 24 October 1677, [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso07scotuoft/page/196/mode/2up printed p.196]. Printed index accounts, original testaments not inspected for this comparison.</ref> The 9 February 1571 deed proves James younger’s father was James elder; it says nothing about Edward.<ref name="Corsankell1571">National Records of Scotland, Glencairn papers, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F5%2F59 GD39/5/59], letters of reversion, 9 February 1571: Corsinkell occupiers James Glasgow elder and James Glasgow and John Glasgow, his sons; accepted archival catalogue description, original deed uninspected.</ref> The Fisherrow James is a near-contemporary father, not a better replacement father for Edward. A new Mureistoun ancestry would require both an unresolved Edward identity and an unsupported degree of kinship. [[Smith-374110|Margaret Smith]]’s maternity remains independently uncertain, and no record here establishes [[Hamilton-31734|Catherine Hamilton]] as Edward’s spouse.
