### Newspaper education and Belfast ministry, 4 October 2026

Adam D. Glasgow of Broughshane appears in the Royal Belfast Academical Institution school prizes in June 1830 and Belfast College examinations reported in May 1831. The 1831 report also names James Glasgow, Institution, in voluntary advanced mathematical examinations. Sources: [Belfast News-Letter, 29 June 1830, p.3, Royal Belfast Academical Institution School Department](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390569859/), [Belfast News-Letter, 6 May 1831, p.3, Belfast College report dated 30 April](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390545285/).

## Current conclusion

Adam Dickey Glasgow is a member of the nineteenth-century Ballymena family. He
cannot be conflated with Adam Glasgow of Inishrush, born about 1758, or used to
connect that earlier family to Victorian Robert Glasgow.



The 1830–1840 newspaper sequence makes this profile a strong candidate for Adam D. Glasgow of Broughshane, later Institution assistant and Berry Street Presbyterian minister. The middle name is not given in full in the inspected notices, so the attribution remains qualified. No parent or sibling certainty changes. ## Source finding — missionary designation, 1842

The 14 January 1842 *Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser* reports that the Presbytery of Belfast set apart four ministers as fellow-labourers in Hindostan with Rev. James Glasgow, including the separately named Rev. Adam Glasgow. The paragraph is headed “Designation of Four Missionaries of the General Assembly” and describes the service at Dr Cooke’s church, May Street, Belfast. Adam Dickie Glasgow-1781 is a possible match for Rev. Adam: his checked profile documents a Belfast ministry, but this notice supplies no middle name or other identifying bridge. The co-occurrence of Adam and James Glasgow establishes no kinship. [Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser, 14 January 1842, p.1](https://www.newspapers.com/image/1050136610/).

## Identity and relationship assessment — Belfast and India notices, 1840–1843 (5 October 2026)

Five visually checked Belfast News-Letter pages add a marriage notice for Rev. Adam Glasgow and Miss Jane Thompson (4 January 1842, reporting 30 December 1841), Berry-street ministerial activity at the Townsend-street soiree (21 August 1840), a platform-list mention at a Rosemary-street meeting (12 March 1841), an India missionary designation (14 January 1842), and an India Mission notice reading letters from Rev. Adam Glasgow (28 March 1843). The recurring Rev. Adam Glasgow name and the Belfast/India sequence are strongly compatible with Glasgow-1781, but none of these notices supplies “Dickie” or a unique profile identifier. The designation report says Adam was “brother of the Rev. Jas. Glasgow”; the journal and Knox Church history already document the missionary siblings, so the newspaper is corroborative and does not create a new relationship certainty.

The marriage notice says Jane Thompson married Rev. Adam Glasgow at the house of her uncle, Thomas Corbett, Esq., on May-street. The name is compatible with the existing tree-linked spouse Jane Fulton (Thompson), but the notice does not give Fulton or establish that profile match; the relationship remains unknown. Thomas is named as Jane’s uncle only; no family branch or parentage is stated. May-street is the uncle’s house, not a proved residence for Adam. No profile relationship status changes.
