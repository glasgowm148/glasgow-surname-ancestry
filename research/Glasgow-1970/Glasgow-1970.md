## Identity note

The 1879 ''History of Hillsdale County, Michigan'' identifies James's wife as a Mitchell and says they emigrated from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750 and settled near Cookstown.<ref>''History of Hillsdale County, Michigan'' (Philadelphia: Everts & Abbott, 1879), [https://archive.org/details/bad0928.0001.001.umich.edu/page/263/mode/1up p. 263].</ref> The family may duplicate [[Glasgow-3137|the 1766 Corvanaghan householder]], [[Glasgow-2988|Glasgow-2988]] or [[Glasgow-1024|James Glasgow of Moneymore]], but no source-level bridge currently proves one of those identities.

James Glasgow is the earliest named ancestor in a family account published in the 1879 ''History of Hillsdale County, Michigan''. The account states that his wife was a Mitchell and that the couple moved from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750, settling in County Tyrone near Cookstown.<ref name="Hillsdale1879">''History of Hillsdale County, Michigan'' (Philadelphia: Everts & Abbott, 1879), 263. [https://archive.org/details/bad0928.0001.001.umich.edu/page/263/mode/1up Page image].</ref>

Their son [[Glasgow-1968|John Glasgow]] became a farmer near Cookstown and raised seven children, four sons and three daughters. John's sons included [[Glasgow-1969|William Glasgow]], born about 1811, and [[Glasgow-1967|John B. Glasgow Jr.]], born 15 April 1814. William went to America in 1833 and married [[Glasgow-4025|Eliza Glasgow]] on 4 August 1836. William and John Jr. moved to Hillsdale County, Michigan, in 1842. Their father later joined the family there.<ref name="Hillsdale1879" />

== Research Notes ==

James is a candidate for comparison with [[Glasgow-2988|James Glasgow of Derryloran]] and [[Glasgow-1024|the reported James Glasgow of Moneymore]], but no source presently identifies them as the same person. Several men named James Glasgow lived in the Cookstown and Derryloran district in 1766.

The descendant chronology also cautions against a merger with Glasgow-1024. Traditions concerning that family place Arthur's mother and elder brothers in America by 1766 or Robert there by 1769, whereas this family's son John remained near Cookstown and raised children there. Further evidence would be needed to establish remarriage, return migration or an error in one of the accounts.
