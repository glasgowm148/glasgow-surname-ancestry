## Cookstown family reconstruction

The 1879 Hillsdale history explicitly gives the chain **James Glasgow and his
Mitchell wife -> son John Glasgow -> John's seven children**, including sons
William and John who settled in Michigan. Later 1888 Hillsdale sketches conflict
over whether John and Jane were born in Scotland or County Tyrone, so the
reported 1750 migration is not proof of birthplace or date.

The 19 December 1794 will-index entry for James Glasgow of Drummond is the best
present probate candidate for this progenitor. Drummond is a small Derryloran
townland near Cookstown, and the date is compatible with an adult son John.
The will does not survive and the index names no kin, so do not assign the event
as proved. PRONI `D1413/3a-b`, genealogical notes on the Glasgow family of
Cookstown, is the priority source for testing the Mitchell wife and son John.

## Descendant reconstruction and comparison — 20 August 2026

The 1879 *History of Hillsdale County, Michigan*, page 263, supplies a coherent descendant chain:

- James Glasgow married a Mitchell.
- They moved from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750 and settled in County Tyrone near Cookstown.
- Their son John was a farmer near Cookstown and raised seven children.
- John's second son William, born about 1811, went to America in 1833.
- William married Eliza Glasgow on 4 August 1836.
- William and his brother John moved to Hillsdale County in 1842.
- Their father John later joined the family and died there at an advanced age.
- John Jr. was born 15 April 1814.

### Comparison with Glasgow-1024 and Glasgow-2988

Glasgow-1970 is of the right generation and locality to be considered in the Moneymore problem. He cannot presently be identified with Glasgow-1024 because the Hillsdale account preserves a son and later family near Cookstown, while the other family's traditions place Arthur's mother and elder brothers in America by 1766 or Robert there by 1769. Identity would require a return to Ireland, another marriage, or an error in one of the traditions.

Glasgow-2988's 1766 Corvanaghan occurrence is geographically and chronologically compatible with the Mitchell-family progenitor, but the return gives no wife or children. Glasgow-2988's attached sons are themselves unsupported and cannot be used to distinguish the men. Keep Glasgow-1970 and Glasgow-2988 separate until a record connects the Corvanaghan householder to the Mitchell family.
