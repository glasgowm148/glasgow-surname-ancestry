# Findings: Archibald Glasgow (Glasgow-2304)

Last updated: 2026-10-07

## Current conclusion

Reviewed: 2026-10-29. Re-read against the later dated sections below; no change to this conclusion.

James Glasgow's will and the 1817 equity case strongly support Archibald as a son
of James and Mary. Gilders Creek gravestones name three children of Archibald and
Mary: James `Glasgow-3981` (1806-1824), Neomi `Glasgow-3979` (1797-1831), and
Rachel `Glasgow-3980` (1801-1855), wife of Isaac Keller. Their parentage is direct
gravestone evidence rather than merely inherited tree structure.

The 1818 estate index names Mary Glasgow as administrator of Archibald's estate,
which strongly corroborates the household but does not itself print “wife.”
Sarah Glasgow-3042's current father link is tree-only; her stone names her spouse,
not her parents.

Robert `Glasgow-2769`, later of Tennessee, is now the leading named candidate for
an additional son. Archibald's 1800 Newberry household contained exactly one male
under ten. None of the three gravestone-proved children fills that slot: Neomi was
female, Rachel was born in 1801 and James in 1806. Robert's combined census
evidence places his birth about 1790, within the 1800 category if he was born after
the census date or if his later reported age was approximate. This is a genuine
household fit, not proof of parentage.

The age bands require a more balanced reading. Combining the 1800 boy with
Robert's 1820 and 1840 brackets permits 5 August 1790–7 August 1794, if the
identity and ages are correct. The adult woman aged 16–25 in 1800 could have
been about sixteen to twenty at an early-1790s birth if near the older end of
her range. Her identity and maternity are not stated. This is a condition to
check, not a general chronological objection to the household. Robert's 1850
age of sixty requires a modest discrepancy with the earlier brackets.

The actual American reconstruction has a coherent migration sequence: James's
family is associated with Kilwaughter in 1772 and his will places him in South
Carolina in 1775; Robert later reported a South Carolina birth and served in
Tennessee in 1814. Robert is proposed only as James J's father, not the father
of the Irish Alexander, Thomas or Daniel. Their ages do not count against this
chain. [The full Larne comparison](/people/glasgow-951.html#larne-reconstruction)
separates the supported James-to-Archibald relationship from the two subsequent
working parent links.

## Original 1810 household reviewed — 30 October 2026

The original 1810 Newberry schedule, page 119 A, names Archd Glasgow. In the five ordered age columns (under 10, 10–15, 16–25, 26–44, 45 and over), males read 2, blank, blank, 1, blank; females read 1, 1, 1, 1, blank. The other-free column is blank and the enslaved-person tally is 6. Only the head is named. No male aged 10–25 is tallied, so the unnamed 1800 boy cannot be tracked through this household in 1810. An adult son could have left home; this is neither proof nor disproof that Tennessee Robert was Archibald’s son. Robert’s father hypothesis remains possible, 51%; no mother or identity is assigned to an unnamed slot.
