== Biography ==

'''John Glasgow''' was born about 1809 in County Antrim, Ireland. He was a farmer
at Cross in Ballyclug parish.

John married [[Gray-38338|Rachel Grey]] on 11 September 1865. The original
certificate describes him as a full-age bachelor and farmer of Cross and names
his father as '''John Glasgow, farmer'''. Rachel was unmarried and also of Cross;
her father was Robert Grey, farmer.<ref name="Marriage1865">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1865/11578/8260226.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil marriage register], John Glasgow and Rachel Grey, 11 September 1865.</ref>

Their son [[Glasgow-2408|James Glasgow]] was born at Cross on 16 February 1871.
The birth register names John as a farmer and Rachel by her former surname
Gray.<ref name="JamesBirth1871">[https://civilrecords.irishgenealogy.ie/churchrecords/images/birth_returns/births_1871/03313/2214261.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil birth register], James Glasgow, 16 February 1871.</ref>

John died at Cross on 27 January 1874, aged sixty-five. The register describes
him as a married farmer; Michael Glasgow of Cross was present at his death.<ref name="Death1874">[https://civilrecords.irishgenealogy.ie/churchrecords/images/deaths_returns/deaths_1874/020673/7253774.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil death register], John Glasgow, 27 January 1874.</ref>

== Research Notes ==

The marriage proves that John's father was named John and was a farmer, but it
does not identify him uniquely. [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow of Glenleslie]] is
one candidate. [[Glasgow-1657|John Glasgow of Cross]], who died in 1836, is the
probable father, assessed at '''84%'''. The 1865 father's name and occupation, exact Cross locality and compatible generation favour him over the Glenleslie candidate. The 1836 death notice explicitly places the elder John at Cross near Broughshane; the 1862 valuation lists a John Glasgow holding at Cross in Ballyclug parish. Neither record proves inheritance, and the marriage does not distinguish the father by age or wife. The percentage is an individual assessment of the father's identity, and no mother is identified by this argument.<ref name="CrossNotice1836">''Belfast Commercial Chronicle'', 10 August 1836, p. 3, deaths column; British Newspaper Archive article BL/0001287/18360810/010, John Glasgow of Cross near Broughshane.</ref><ref name="CrossValuation1862">''Griffith's Valuation'', Ballyclug parish, printed 23 January 1862, Cross townland, John Glasgow, lessor Henry Joy Tomb; Findmypast IRE/GRIFF/003/003068/001/1.</ref>

This John was not the married forty-year-old nephew recorded in the Glenleslie
household in 1851. The census nephew was recorded as married since 1839, while
this man's 1865 certificate explicitly calls him a bachelor.<ref name="Glenleslie1851">[https://durrushistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/1851irishcensusreturnscommadelimitedtextfilejul24_071.pdf ''1851 Irish Census Returns: detailed comma-delimited transcription''], Glenleslie household 21.</ref> The Glenleslie man remains an alternative father identity, but the Cross man is preferred. The older Cross John's own father remains unselected; this relationship assessment does not supply his ancestry.

The same marriage and death also have earlier GRONI index citations; these refer to the same events, rather than independent family evidence.<ref>[https://geni.nidirect.gov.uk/search/marriage GRONI civil marriage index], 11 September 1865, Ballymena district, M/1865/H1/215/4/87.</ref><ref>[https://geni.nidirect.gov.uk/search/death GRONI civil death index], John Glasgow, 27 January 1874, aged 65, Ballymena registration district, D/1874/24/1004/2/366.</ref>
