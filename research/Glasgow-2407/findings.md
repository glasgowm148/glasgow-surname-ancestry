## Current conclusion

John was a bachelor farmer at Cross, Ballyclug, when he married Rachel Grey in
1865. The original certificate names his father as John Glasgow, farmer. His
1874 death return gives age sixty-five, occupation farmer and residence Cross;
Michael Glasgow of Cross was present at death.

He is not the forty-year-old nephew in John `Glasgow-1022`'s 1851 household,
because that man was recorded as married since 1839. John `Glasgow-1022` remains
one possible father, but John `Glasgow-1657`, who was explicitly of Cross and
died in 1836, is the selected probable identity at **84%** (reviewed 17 October 2026). The exact Cross locality, compatible generation and continuing Glasgow holding in the 1862 valuation favour this man over the Glenleslie candidate. The marriage directly proves the father's name and occupation; the 84% is a subjective assessment of his identity, not of the certificate's wording. The father is already attached in the captured tree. This is a confidence-only review, not a new connection, and supersedes the earlier instruction to leave John parentless. No mother is assigned by this argument.

The older John's father remains unselected and unrated. His death at Cross in 1836 separates him from the similarly aged John-822 of Inishrush, who survived in Ohio until 1851. The 1862 holding has not been proved to be an inheritance; the exact Cross match remains the strongest positive discriminator.
