## Current conclusion

<!-- site-audit-2026-10-07:overall-conclusion -->
Nothing is yet documented about the life of Benjamin Glasgow in this research file. Two Belfast News-Letter items were considered for this profile and withdrawn on 6 October 2026: an 1850 subscription by Master Benjamin Glasgow of Cookstown, who cannot be fixed among three compatible Cookstown Benjamins, and an 1870 report of Benjamin Glasgow of Drapersfield lending a field at Cookstown, who is firmly Benjamin Glasgow (Glasgow-4340). This profile, earlier a possible match for the 1870 man, is now judged unlikely.

## Record findings and identity limits — 17 October 2026

A possible identification concerns Benjamin Glasgow, Drapersfield merchant. Benjamin Glasgow and William Glasgow were mill partners and plaintiffs in the 1858 watercourse action. Benjamin had approached John Sloan about weir levels in 1856. (Belfast News-Letter, 22 July 1858, p.3; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390574459/); Belfast News-Letter, 23 July 1858, p.4; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390575046/).) The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns Benjamin Glasgow, Drapersfield merchant. Benjamin Glasgow, Esq., Drapersfield, Cookstown, was a contact for the auction of Eary House and lands from the estate of the late William Magee. The advertisement does not explicitly call Benjamin an executor. (Belfast News-Letter, 22 December 1869, p.2; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/388287498/).) The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns Benjamin Glasgow, Drapersfield merchant. A contemporary July 1870 report credits Benjamin Glasgow of Drapersfield with granting use of a field for the 12 July Cookstown gathering. This postdates [Glasgow-2059](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2059)’s recorded death on 8 February 1870 and strongly supports a separate person. (Belfast News-Letter, 15 July 1870, p.4; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/387881589/).) The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns Benjamin Glasgow, Drapersfield merchant. The named witness Benjamin Glasgow identifies the late Robert Glasgow as his father, says he managed his father’s mill from 1850, and dates Robert’s death to 1856. Benjamin’s evidence describes a companion as “witness’s uncle, Mr. Glasgow, sen.”; identification as William belongs to the separate case context, not this quotation. (Belfast News-Letter, 22 July 1858, p.3, Benjamin’s sworn testimony; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390574459/).) Certain documentary father–son relationship; identification of Robert with [Glasgow-2656](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2656) or [Glasgow-3941](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3941) remains qualified. Counsel’s reference to Robert’s sons supports a qualified brother inference for co-plaintiff William. The uncle’s paternal or maternal side and all mothers are unstated. No parents are established for [John Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2058). The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns Benjamin Glasgow, Drapersfield merchant. Benjamin Glasgow of Drapersfield, Cookstown, married Wilhelmina, eldest daughter of Richard Pike, Esq., of Beechgrove, Dungannon, at Dungannon on 16 February 1864. (Belfast News-Letter, 18 February 1864, p.2; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/387828785/).) The original notice gives his wife and her father but no groom age or father. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns Benjamin Glasgow, Drapersfield merchant. Margaret George Glasgow, eldest daughter of Benjamin Glasgow of Hull, formerly Drapersfield, Cookstown, married W. Brooke Broadbent, formerly Manchester, in New York on 5 October 1891. (Belfast News-Letter, 16 October 1891, p.1; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/388748472/).) This directly bridges the father’s Hull and Drapersfield locations. The bride’s mother and age are unstated; W. is not expanded. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns Master Benjamin Glasgow, Cookstown school-support donor. Master Benjamin Glasgow of Cookstown is listed for one shilling towards support of scholars in the agricultural school in June 1850. (Belfast News-Letter, 28 June 1850, p. 2, agricultural-school scholars’ support; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390559842/).) A donor list, not proof that Benjamin attended the school. “Master” supplies no exact age. [Glasgow-2059](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2059) (reported born about 1835) and [Glasgow-2566](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2566) (about 1840) are compatible young catalogue namesakes; assignment to either, or to the Drapersfield Benjamin, is unresolved. The surgeon who died in 1833 is excluded. The identification remains uncertain.
