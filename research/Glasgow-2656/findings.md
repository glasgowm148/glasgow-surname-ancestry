(Banner of Ulster, 9 December 1854, p.2, BNA article BL/0001375/18541209/039; OCR checked.) The notice states that Robert was the son-in-law of John George's widow, and it places him at Tullyveagh. That ties the 1829 marriage of Robert Glasgow of Cookstown to Margaret, daughter of John George of Coagh, to the Tullyveagh Robert who died on 20 August 1856. It does not name Robert's children. The later names John George Glasgow (died at Drapersfield in 1870, aged 39) and Margaret George Glasgow fit this marriage, but repeating a name does not prove descent.
- **New: the Glasgow & Wood partnership at Drapersfield and Tullyveagh, 1850.** A notice from Glasgow & Wood, dated at Drapersfield Mill, Cookstown, on 6 July 1850, says the partners had "mutually agreed to dissolve Partnership" and offered "Drapersfield Flaxspinning Mill" and Tullyveagh Mills for sale by auction on 13 August 1850. Drapersfield (25a 3r 8p, a mill with 2,744 spindles) was "held by lease under the Worshipful the Company of Drapers, London, for a term of years, from 1847". Tullyveagh Farm, across the river (30a 2r 31p plus bog, with a dwelling-house and a bleach-mill), was held "in perpetuity under his Grace the Lord Primate" at £22 15s. The same advertisement ran in the Northern Whig, Banner of Ulster and Londonderry Standard, July–August 1850.)
