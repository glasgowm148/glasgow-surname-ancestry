== Biography ==

'''James J Glasgow''' was born about 1809 or 1810 in South Carolina. His exact birth date and parents have not been proved.

By 1845 James was living on a ridge in the sixth civil district of Giles County, Tennessee, near Donahue Creek and the waters of Richland Creek. A later deed referred to a title bond executed by Noah Hancock on 7 October 1845.<ref name="Deed1848">Giles County, Tennessee, Deed Book T, p. 211, Noah Hancock to James J. Glasgow, deed dated 9 November and registered 2 December 1848; [https://tngenweb.org/giles/deed-noah-hancock-to-james-glasgow/ transcription], TNGenWeb.</ref>

On 9 November 1848 Hancock conveyed the 100-acre tract to James for $300. The deed described it as the ridge on which James then resided.<ref name="Deed1848" /> On 6 April 1850 James sold the tract to John Tornerlin for $400. Joseph C. Rhea and James Anderson witnessed the deed.<ref name="Deed1850">Giles County, Tennessee, Deed Book U, p. 144, James J. Glasgow to John Tornerlin, deed dated and registered 6 April 1850; [https://tngenweb.org/giles/deed-james-j-glasgow-to-john-tornerlin-1850/ transcription], TNGenWeb.</ref>

James and '''Mary J Glasgow''' were enumerated together in the 1860 United States census.<ref name="Census1860">"United States Census, 1860," database with images, FamilySearch ([https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M6GF-RXZ record]), entry for Jas J Glasgow and Mary J Glasgow.</ref> This record supports the attached marriage, but does not identify either spouse's parents.

== Research Notes ==

=== Parentage ===

James is attached as an uncertain son of [[Glasgow-2769|Robert Glasgow]]. Robert's 1820 Giles County household contained one boy aged 10–15, compatible with James's estimated birth about 1809 or 1810.<ref name="Census1820">1820 United States census, Giles County, Tennessee, Robert Glasgow household; NARA microfilm publication M33, roll 124; [https://archive.org/details/populationsc18200124unit/page/n21/mode/1up original schedule, scan 21].</ref> James's later Giles County residence is also consistent with that placement. The census names only Robert, however, and neither Giles County deed names a parent or sibling. The relationship therefore remains uncertain.

FamilySearch has also placed James among the children of [[Glasgow-1982|Wilson Crossier Glasgow]] and [[McCracken-3711|Mary McCracken]]. The available estate evidence contradicts that placement. Mary's will gave Ruth one-seventh of her property, Mariah one-sixth of the remainder, and the residue to her “other five children,” establishing a total of seven children.<ref name="MaryWill">Newberry District, South Carolina, Will Book N, p. 1, will of Mary Glasgow, widow of Wilson Glasgow, dated 28 November 1837 and proved 22 October 1838; [https://web.archive.org/web/20101124184247id_/http://www.lifesweaving.com/Glasgow/Paper_WilsonGlasgowWife.html transcription]. The displayed recording date of July 1837 precedes execution and is evidently a transcription error.</ref> The index to Wilson's real-estate packet lists Mary with Robert, Ruth, Naomi, Matilda, Hiram, Almina and Maria—the widow and seven apparent children. James is absent.<ref name="WilsonEstate">Newberry County, South Carolina, real-estate packet 1-13, estate of Wilson Glasgow; [https://genealogytrails.com/scar/newberry/realestate.html index], entries for Wilson Glasgow and Mary, Robert, Ruth, Naomi, Matilda, Hiram, Almina and Maria.</ref>

The same FamilySearch family also includes Fleming Bates Glasgow, reportedly born in 1823. Wilson's gravestone records his death on 11 September 1817, six years before Fleming's birth, showing that the online child list combines at least two families.<ref name="GildersCreek">[https://genealogytrails.com/scar/newberry/gilders_creek_cem.htm Gilders Creek Cemetery survey], Newberry County, South Carolina, entries for Wilson Glasgow and Mary Glasgow.</ref>

The Wilson and Mary hypothesis was therefore rejected. Robert remains the leading working father, but a probate, deed, Bible, obituary or other record naming the relationship is still needed.

Robert's leading named father candidate is [[Glasgow-2304|Archibald Glasgow]]. Archibald's 1800 Newberry household contained one male under ten who is not accounted for by his three gravestone-proved children.<ref name="Archibald1800">"United States Census, 1800," Archibald Glasgow household, Newberry District, South Carolina, p. 75, NARA microfilm publication M32, roll 50; [https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XHRC-7BG FamilySearch record XHRC-7BG] and [https://genealogytrails.com/scar/newberry/1800nbycensusG.htm public household transcription].</ref> Robert's estimated birth and South Carolina origin fit that slot, but the census does not name the child and no record yet calls Robert Archibald's son. Archibald is therefore only a possible grandfather of James.

=== Y-DNA ===

A tested direct paternal descendant line reported through James belongs to haplogroup R-FT20271.<ref name="FTDNA">[https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview FamilyTreeDNA Glasgow Project], Y-DNA results, kit 1002232.</ref> This confirms James's broader paternal genetic cluster. It does not distinguish Robert from another man in that cluster as James's father.
