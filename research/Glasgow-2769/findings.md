# Findings: [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-2769), Tennessee](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2769)

Last updated: 2026-08-12

## Current conclusion

Robert was probably born about 1790 or 1791, not about 1780. The intersection
of the 1820 age 26–44 bracket and the 1840 age 40–49 bracket favours a birth
between about 1790 and 1794. The 1850 return gives his age as 60, but that may
have been an estimate or a rounding of 59 rather than an exact age.
[Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1983)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1983)
was detached as his father because the Gilders Creek gravestone proves that
Glasgow-1983 and Elizabeth already had a different son Robert,
[Glasgow-3982](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3982), who died in 1813.

The reported wife Nancy has not been identified by a contemporary record. The
19 January 1814 Delaware marriage of Robert Glasgow and Nancy Black belongs to
a different couple who remained in Delaware and raised children there. Nancy
Black must not be used as this Robert's wife.

Priscilla is Robert's strongest child identification. A female aged 15–19 in
Robert's 1840 household fits her estimated birth, and the 1850 census names
Priscilla Glascoe, 28, born Tennessee, in Robert Glaseoe's household. The 1850
census did not state relationships, so daughter remains a strong inference
rather than literal census wording. Emma and Jessie fit the other adult-child
slots in 1840 but are not named there. James J remains chronologically possible.
John, reportedly born about 1800, was detached because he would have been born
when Robert was at most about ten years old.

[Archibald Glasgow (Glasgow-2304)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2304)
is now the leading named father candidate, but is not proved. Archibald's 1800
Newberry household contained exactly one male under ten, a slot not occupied by
his three gravestone-proved children. Robert's best estimate of about 1790 can fit
that category. The only adult female was aged 16–25, however, making maternity by
Archibald's known wife Mary chronologically tight if Robert was born near 1790.
Archibald can be retained as a documented research hypothesis; Robert's parents
should remain blank unless the estate, census or another kinship record names them.

## Y-DNA project identification

Big Y-700 kit `1002232`, R-FT20271, descends through the documented line of
James J `Glasgow-2738`; James's attachment to Robert remains uncertain. The
tester tradition identifies the deeper root as this South Carolina-to-Tennessee
family. The kit is Alexander `Glasgow-951`'s closest basal FT20271 match at Y-111,
genetic distance 3, and the two share DYS714=27. This supports a paternal
relationship somewhere above the tested lines, but neither the STR match nor the
shared SNP identifies Robert's father.

## Candidate father assessment

Archibald `Glasgow-2304` ranks above the other named Newberry candidates because
the 1800 census supplies an actual unidentified boy of the correct broad age.
The hypothesis also makes geographic sense: Archibald belonged to the emigrant
family of James `Glasgow-1144`, while Robert was born in South Carolina and had
moved into Tennessee by the War of 1812. It remains only a moderate working
hypothesis. No record calls Robert Archibald's son, the 1810 age columns are still
unread, and the 1800 adult-female age is awkward.

  test the hypothesis against Archibald's estate 647 and original 1810 schedule.
