[[Category:Glasgow Name Study]]

== Biography ==
'''Robert Glasgow''' was master of the snow ''John and Archibald'', a vessel described in 1754 as "of Glasgow". He is securely documented only in February 1754. His birth, parentage, family and death have not been established.

=== Voyage of 1754 ===

<blockquote>"Last Tuesday arrived here, in Distress, the Snow John and Archibald, of Glasgow, Robert Glasgow, Master..."</blockquote><ref name="MarylandGazette1754">''The Maryland Gazette'', no. 466 (Annapolis, Maryland, 11 April 1754), report dated St John's, Antigua, 15 February 1754, Maryland State Archives, Maryland Gazette Collection MSA SC 2731, M1279, images 472–473; accessed 7 August 2026. [https://www.msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc4800/sc4872/001279/html/m1279-0472.html issue front page] and [https://www.msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc4800/sc4872/001279/html/m1279-0473.html report page].</ref>

The vessel had been sailing from Virginia to Glasgow. On 24 January 1754, at latitude 32°15′, it encountered a gale from W.S.W., sprang a leak and required both pumps to be worked continuously.<ref name="MarylandGazette1754" />

When men climbed the shrouds to set a double-reefed main topsail, lightning split the main-topmast and damaged the rigging. Two men were thrown into the sea and lost. Of two men struck on deck, one died immediately and the other was still unable to perform duty when the report was written. Six people remained aboard when they agreed to bear away for assistance at St John's, Antigua.<ref name="MarylandGazette1754" />

The same Antigua report was later printed in ''The Dublin Gazette''.<ref name="DublinGazette1754">''The Dublin Gazette'', no. 400 (Dublin, 25–29 June 1754), report from St John's, Antigua, dated 15 February 1754, Houses of the Oireachtas Library & Research Service, DGZ/1754/400; accessed 7 August 2026. [https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/DGZ-1754-400 digital item].</ref>

=== Earlier record of the vessel ===

A shipping report published in the ''Belfast News-Letter'' on 12 June 1739 lists a vessel named the ''John and Archibald'', Thomas Hendry master, among vessels described as "all of and from Irvine" carrying coal.<ref name="BelfastNewsLetter1739">''Belfast News-Letter'' (Belfast, 12 June 1739), shipping intelligence listing the ''John and Archibald'', Thomas Hendry master, among vessels described as "all of and from Irvine" carrying coal. The wording is presently taken from the British Newspaper Archive OCR/search extract; the original newspaper image has not yet been inspected. [https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/search-newspapers/results?keywords=%22John+and+Archibald%22 search results]; accessed 20 August 2026.</ref>

The unusual vessel name and western Scottish context make this a plausible earlier reference to the vessel later commanded by Robert Glasgow. The 1739 entry does not prove continuity of vessel, ownership or registration through 1754, and it supplies no information about Robert's identity or family.

The entry makes Irvine harbour, customs and burgh records a priority. Research should include Thomas Hendry and the owners and merchants associated with him, since a sequence of masters may connect the 1739 vessel with Robert Glasgow's command in 1754.

== Research Notes ==
=== Identity and family ===

The 1754 report gives no age, birthplace, parents, wife, children or later fate. The phrase "of Glasgow" describes the vessel; it is not evidence that Robert himself was born in Glasgow. His position as master does not support a defensible estimated birth year.

A separate Glencorse household is now documented more fully. Robert Glasgow married [[Davidson-20783|Margaret Davidson]] there on 8 June 1704.<ref name="GlencorseMarriage1704">Scotland's People, Church of Scotland old parish registers, marriage of Robert Glasgow and Margarett Davidson, 8 June 1704, Glencorse parish 687, reference 20/53.</ref> Indexed children of that couple include [[Glasgow-3492|Christian]] in 1708,<ref name="Christian1708">“Scotland, Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950,” database, ''FamilySearch'', [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VQ4J-CB3 Christian Glasgow, 1708].</ref> [[Glasgow-3491|John]] on 1 January 1710,<ref name="John1710">Scotland's People, Church of Scotland old parish registers, John Glasgow, 1 January 1710, parents Robert Glasgow and Margaret Davidson, Glencorse parish 687, reference 10/93.</ref> [[Glasgow-4062|Isobell]] on 26 December 1714,<ref name="Isobell1714">Scotland's People, Church of Scotland old parish registers, Isobell Glasgow, 26 December 1714, parents Robert Glasgow and Margaret Davidson, Glencorse parish 687, reference 10/106.</ref> and [[Glasgow-2868|James]] on 14 February 1720.<ref name="James1720">Scotland's People, Church of Scotland old parish registers, James Glasgow, 14 February 1720, parents Robert Glasgow and Margaret Davidsone, Glencorse parish 687, reference 10/121.</ref>

Those records prove the Glencorse family, but none gives Robert's occupation, age or parentage. They do not identify him as the master of the ''John and Archibald'' thirty-four years after the last listed baptism. No independently verified maritime, probate or parish record links the two men. [[Glasgow-3011|Robert Glasgow]], baptised at Glencorse on 29 December 1676 to John Glasgow and Margaret Currie, is a strong but unproved candidate for the Glencorse husband and father.<ref name="Robert1676">Scotland's People, Church of Scotland old parish registers, Robert Glasgow, 29 December 1676, parents John Glasgow and Margaret Currie, Glencorse parish 687, reference 10/8.</ref> Margaret Davidson is attached to that profile as an uncertain wife, and her former marriage link to this shipmaster has been removed. Christian, John and James remain attached here with uncertain father links pending a controlled correction. The Glencorse records do not establish them as the shipmaster's children.

No documentary evidence currently links this shipmaster to [[Glasgow-1026|William Glasgow]] or [[McCook-217|Ann McCook]]. The former birth-place claim at Irvine was also unsupported and has been cleared.

The current profile's only cited support for the dates 1690–1759 and a death at sea is an online memorial. No contemporary record cited there identifies the memorialised man as the master of the ''John and Archibald''.<ref name="FindAGrave">Find a Grave memorial 223820967, Robert Glasgow (1690–1759), described as "Buried or Lost at Sea"; [https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/223820967/robert_glasgow memorial].</ref>

The vessel's name does not establish the identities of its owners or any relationship between Robert and men named John or Archibald. Either interpretation remains speculation.

=== Priority Research ===

* '''Priority 1 — Virginia clearance and ownership.'''
** Search CO 5/1444, CO 5/1445 and CO 5/1446 for the vessel's late-1753 outward clearance. These cover the relevant Virginia naval districts through 1753 or 1756. CO 5/1447 begins on 27 March 1754 and is too late for the original clearance, although it may contain a later return or subsequent voyage. For every match, record the district, date, master, vessel type, tonnage, crew, owners, bonders, cargo, place built, stated home port and destination. [https://britishonlinearchives.com/collections/68/volumes/462/south-eastern-american-states-1689-1769 collection description].

* '''Priority 1 — Irvine vessel trail and customs.'''
** Search Irvine Outport and District records in CE71, held by Ayrshire Archives, together with Irvine harbour dues, burgh minutes, merchant and shipmaster bonds, and shipping protests from 1738–1755. Search the vessel, Robert Glasgow variants and Thomas Hendry/Hendrie/Henry. The aim is to establish a chain of masters and identify owners or merchants. [https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/customs-and-excise-records/ NRS customs guide].

* '''Priority 2 — Clyde customs records.'''
** Ask Glasgow City Archives to identify Glasgow Outport records in CE59 and Port Glasgow and Greenock records in CE60 covering 1739–1756. Search inward and outward accounts and letter books for the vessel and both known masters. A matching entry could identify the owners, tonnage, route, repairs and Robert's later activity. [https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/customs-and-excise-records/ NRS customs guide].

* '''Priority 2 — Testament and maritime litigation.'''
** Search the Scotland's People wills and testaments index, 1754–1780, for Robert Glasgow / Glasgo / Glascow described as a shipmaster, mariner, merchant or sailor. Search National Records of Scotland High Court of Admiralty records, including AC2 and AC6–AC11, particularly 1754–1757, for the vessel, owners, insurers, freight, salvage, repairs, wages or cargo disputes. A matching record may name relatives, owners or business associates. [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/search-records/legal-records/wills wills and testaments] and [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=AC High Court of Admiralty catalogue].

=== Current research ===

The 1754 report proves only a shipmaster Robert Glasgow aboard the ''John and Archibald''. It names no parents, wife or children. The Glencorse baptisms prove the three children belonged to parents named Robert Glasgow and Margaret Davidson, but do not prove that father was the captain. The attached William Glasgow/Ann McCook ancestry is unsupported and is not used to distinguish him from other Roberts.

The strongest present geographic lead is Irvine, Ayrshire: a possibly identical ''John and Archibald'' was trading from Irvine under Thomas Hendry in 1739, and the district had an established Glasgow-surname maritime community. No record yet identifies an Irvine Robert Glasgow as the 1754 master, so this is a research direction rather than an identification.

This profile remains separate from [[Glasgow-3407|Glasgow-3407]], but not because the current families overlap. The Glencorse household belongs to a father named Robert Glasgow; [[Glasgow-3011|the man baptised there in 1676]] is the leading candidate. A port, ownership, probate or maritime record is still needed to identify the shipmaster's family.
