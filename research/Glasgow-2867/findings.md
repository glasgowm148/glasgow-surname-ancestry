# Findings: [Captain Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-2867)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2867)

## Conclusion

The 15 February 1754 *Maryland Gazette* report proves only that a **Robert
Glasgow** was master of the snow *John and Archibald*. It names no parents, wife
or children, and it does not prove birthplace. The phrase “of Glasgow” in the
ship description applies to the vessel, not the person’s origin.

The Glencorse baptisms for Christian/John/James and the online-tree
parent/spouse claims are **not sufficient** to identify him as that shipmaster.
No original record yet links this Robert to Margaret Davidson, to children Christian,
John and James, to William Glasgow and Ann McCook, or to any shipmaster’s
family context.  

The unsupported marriage to Margaret Davidson was removed on 2 September 2026
only after Glasgow-3011 had been attached to her as the uncertain,
evidence-based candidate for the Glencorse husband. The three older child links
remain uncertain pending a controlled replacement rather than an unsupported
detachment. Treat the 1739 Irvine vessel trail as the strongest shipmaster
identity lead.

Do not merge with [Glasgow-3407](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3407); no
occupational, probate, or familial bridge has been documented.

## Source findings

### 1739 vessel continuity lead

On **12 June 1739**, the *Belfast News-Letter* listed a vessel named
*John and Archibald*, Thomas Hendry master, described as “all of and from Irvine”
and carrying coal.[^1] This is a plausible lead linking the 1739 vessel to the one
in 1754, but it is not proof by itself. The same name plus Irvine context is a
specific lead, not definitive identity proof.

Research priority now shifts from Glencorse/online-tree clustering to
Irvine maritime records and ownership chains.

=== Earlier record of the vessel ===

A shipping report published in the ''Belfast News-Letter'' on
12 June 1739 lists a vessel named the ''John and Archibald'',
Thomas Hendry master, among vessels described as “all of and from
Irvine” carrying coal.[^1]

The unusual vessel name and the western-Scottish context make this a
plausible earlier reference to the vessel later commanded by
Robert Glasgow. The 1739 entry does not, however, prove continuity
of vessel, ownership or registration through 1754. It supplies no
information about Robert’s identity or family.

The entry makes Irvine harbour, customs and burgh records a
priority. It also indicates that research should include Thomas
Hendry and the owners and merchants associated with him, since a
sequence of masters may connect the 1739 vessel with Robert Glasgow’s
command in 1754.

=== Identity and family ===

No primary record found to date identifies the parents, wife or
children of Robert Glasgow, master of the ''John and Archibald'' in
1754. No independently verified marriage, banns, testament, parish
entry or maritime record links him to Margaret Davidson.

The dates 1690–1759, the statement that he died or was lost at sea,
and the proposed William Glasgow and Ann McCook parentage derive
from online tree or memorial material rather than from a contemporary
record identifying the shipmaster.

The baptisms attributed to Christian, John and James may concern children
of a father named Robert Glasgow, but a same-name father is not sufficient
to identify the 1754 master. The original entries and related parish
records must be examined for residence, occupation, the mother’s name and
associates.

## Source findings — 1754 Antigua distress report, primary citations, 7 October 2026

The report dated St John's, Antigua, 15 February 1754 ("Last Tuesday arrived here, in Distress, the Snow John and Archibald, of Glasgow, Robert Glasgow, Master …") was printed in *The Maryland Gazette*, no. 466 (Annapolis, 11 April 1754), Maryland State Archives, Maryland Gazette Collection MSA SC 2731, M1279, images 472–473 ([front page](https://www.msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc4800/sc4872/001279/html/m1279-0472.html); [report page](https://www.msa.maryland.gov/megafile/msa/speccol/sc4800/sc4872/001279/html/m1279-0473.html)), and in *The Dublin Gazette*, no. 400 (25–29 June 1754), Oireachtas Library DGZ/1754/400 ([digital item](https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/DGZ-1754-400)). The reviewed draft records both page images as accessed on 7 August 2026.

The report says the snow was sailing from Virginia to Glasgow. On 24 January 1754, at latitude 32°15′, she met a W.S.W. gale and sprang a leak. Lightning split the main-topmast while men were setting a double-reefed main topsail; two men were thrown into the sea and lost, one man on deck was killed and another disabled. Six people remained aboard when they bore away for St John's, Antigua. **Proved** for the voyage and Robert's command only; the report gives no age, birthplace or family for him.
