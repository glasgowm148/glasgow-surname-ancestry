## Whitebank trial identity review — 30 October 2026

The held WikiTree biography transcribes [Find a Grave memorial 198800998](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/198800998/peter_glasgow): “Erected by JOHN and JAMES GLASGOW in MEMORY of PETER GLASGOW their father, late Tenant Whitebank”, who died 7 November 1812 aged 72. It continues with Jean Anderson his spouse, who died 7 January 1809 aged 73, and three of their children who died young. Jean's [companion memorial 198800999](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/198800999/jean-glasgow) represents the same physical stone, not independent corroboration. These are held derivative memorial readings; the original inscription has not been freshly read.

The [Scottish Indexes trial entry](https://www.scottishindexes.com/jcdetail.aspx?jcid=1797040&pid=179704079), re-read by the project researcher on 30 October 2026, identifies Peter Glasgow of Whitebank, tried for rioting on 9 October 1797, pleading not guilty, found guilty and sentenced to transportation; it cites **NRS JC26/1797/40**. It supplies no age, parent, occupation or spouse. The associated accused include Robert Lillie, blacksmith in Haddington, Christopher Kerr of Bankhead, James Richardson of Crooklaw and Elizabeth Wilson of Dunse. Their designations do not independently establish this Peter's family.

The full-name and specific Whitebank designation match is substantially more discriminating than the age and disappearance argument previously offered for Peter-1466. The memorial age places Peter-2878 at about 57 in 1797, which is compatible; Peter-1466 would be about 35, also compatible, so age alone does not choose between them. Whitebank tenancy and later commemoration in the Borders fit the accused's designation without requiring an invented occupation or relationship. No competing Peter of Whitebank has been established in the held evidence, but the index does not prove that the designation was unique. On the combined evidence, 94% is a subjective preferred identity assessment with residual same-name/same-place uncertainty, not a statistical calculation or proof supplied by two original records.

The 1812 memorial does not itself contradict a 1797 sentence: the index records the sentence, not its execution, destination, duration or any subsequent return. None of those events is asserted. The exact next test is the original trial papers and court minutes for identifying age, trade, relatives or residence, followed separately by records showing whether transportation occurred. The weaker Bathgate candidate remains documented rather than excluded merely for lacking a Whitebank field.

These approximate ages do not prove the record parents are the memorial adults. No exact John child profile is identified, no parent link is added, and no vital or grade changes.
