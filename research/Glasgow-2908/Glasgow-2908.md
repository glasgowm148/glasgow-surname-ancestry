== Biography ==

'''Samuel Glasgow''' was born about 1790 in County Londonderry, Ireland. The
1851 census reported him as sixty; his death registration reported him as
eighty-five in 1875.<ref name="Census1851">[https://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Rasharkin/Lisnagaver/34/ 1851 Census of Ireland], Lisnagaver household 34.</ref><ref name="Death1875">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view/?record_id=cide-7225943 IrishGenealogy.ie], civil death registration of Samuel Glasgow, 17 August 1875, Lisnagaver.</ref>

In 1851 Samuel was an unmarried linen weaver living in the Lisnagaver household
of his brother [[Glasgow-867|John Glasgow]]. The census explicitly describes
Samuel as John's brother.<ref name="Census1851" />

Samuel died at Lisnagaver on 17 August 1875. The civil register describes him
as a bachelor and weaver. Elizabeth Ross was present at his death; the record
does not state her relationship.<ref name="Death1875" />

The immediately preceding register entry records the death of eight-year-old
Robert Ross at Lisnagaver on 8 August 1875, with the same Elizabeth Ross as
informant. This is a useful household or neighbour clue, but neither entry
states Elizabeth's relationship to Samuel or Robert.<ref name="Death1875" />

== Research Notes ==

=== Father ===

John's original 1846 marriage register names John's father as '''John Glasgow,
farmer'''.<ref name="JohnMarriage1846">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1846/09306/5369439.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil marriage register], John Glasgow and Margaret Garvin, 16 November 1846.</ref>

On the ordinary meaning of the census relationship, the same older John was
probably Samuel's father. He is currently represented by [[Glasgow-1022|John
Glasgow]] as a possible working identification. Neither record distinguishes
that older farmer from every namesake, so the profile relationship must remain
uncertain. No inspected source identifies Samuel's mother.

Samuel was previously described as a son of [[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow]] and
Rose. The census-and-marriage chain instead points to a father named John, and
no record connects Samuel to Adam's Inishrush household.

Adjacent Glasgow households and consecutive holdings at Lisnagaver do not prove
that Alexander or Thomas Glasgow was another brother.
