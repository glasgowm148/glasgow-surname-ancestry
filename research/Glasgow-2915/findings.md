## Current conclusion

No inspected record proves that William Glasgow, reportedly born about 1760, was the son of [[Glasgow-2916]] or that he married Margaret Mitchell at Cookstown. The principal surviving Derryloran and Cookstown church registers listed by PRONI begin too late to record a 1760 baptism. The chain is therefore a research hypothesis, not a sourced lineage; preserve the present attachments until positive replacement evidence is found.

New evidence reviewed 4 October 2026: The 16 January 1793 Kildress reform meeting named an adult William Glasgow as delegate and subsequent chairman. This William’s reported c.1760 date and Cookstown district make him compatible with that role, but the record gives no age, precise residence, wife or father. It does not cure the existing father/spouse evidence gap.

### Findmypast sweep — 7 October 2026
- **New (index only):** Ireland, Outrage Reports 1836–1840 (TNA HO 100/255, image 393) indexes "William Glasgow" at "Orator" (Oritor), Kildress parish, County Tyrone, on 5 July 1838 (Findmypast IRE/HO100/020002). The index does not say whether William was a victim, a complainant or a witness, and it gives no age. A William Glasgow in Kildress in 1838 is compatible with the 1793 Kildress delegate, but also with the separate William Glasgow of Oritor whose will is dated about 1827 (PRONI T/1762), or with a younger man. Candidate only; read the report image before using it.
- **New (index only):** the Index of Irish Wills 1484–1858 has a will of William Glasgow proved in Armagh Diocese in 1836 (NAI IWR/1836/F/177, volume 4/236/27; Findmypast IRE/ENEC001/26356). The index gives no residence. It may be the probate of the Oritor will or of another William. It is not evidence for this profile until the residence is known.
- **Context:** in 1825 a Robert Glasgow and a William Glasgow acted as executors of an "R. Glasgow, deceased" of Cookstown (Belfast News-Letter, 22 July 1825, BL/0000038/18250722/088). Nothing in the notice links them to this William.
