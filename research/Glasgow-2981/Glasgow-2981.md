== Biography ==

'''James Glasgow''' was born about 1801 in County Antrim, Ireland. In the surviving 1851 census he was a fifty-year-old day labourer at Ballycregagh, Dunaghy, County Antrim.<ref name="Census1851">1851 Census of Ireland, County Antrim, Dunaghy parish, Ballycraigargh, household 29, James Glasgow household: [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QSQ-G94X-YD22 living household, image 251] and [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L94X-Y621 continuation and mortality return, image 252].</ref>

He is probably the '''James Glasgow junior''' assessed at Ballybogy in the same parish in 1825. The original tithe book records James junior separately from an unsuffixed James Glasgow, showing that two same-named occupiers were present.<ref name="BallybogyTithe1825">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, ''Tithe applotment for the parish of Dunaghy'', 1825, PRONI FIN/5/A/128, manuscript p. 25, Ballybogy entries for James Glasgow and James Glasgow junior; indexed transcription: W. Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/182030stithe/tithenorthantrimrevisionorder.pdf ''Data from the Tithe Applotment Books covering North Antrim, arranged in document order''], entries 6001–6002.</ref> James's age, his family's 1847 Glenleslie record and his 1851 Ballycregagh residence give him a substantially stronger Dunaghy identity chain than the other live candidates, although the tithe entry itself gives no age or relative.

James and [[Hamilton-25516|Peggy Hamilton]] were both recorded as having married in 1848. Peggy's unmarried sisters [[Hamilton-44032|Nancy Hamilton]] and [[Hamilton-44033|Jane Hamilton]] lived with them. The mortality portion of the return records [[Hamilton-44031|Robert Hamilton]] as James's father-in-law and [[Unknown-764457|Cathrin Hamilton]] as his mother-in-law, directly identifying them as Peggy's parents.<ref name="Census1851" />

An 1847 Ballymena Workhouse admission group records [[Unknown-764460|Mary Glasgow]], aged 34, married and deserted, at Glenleslie with Margaret, Boyd, William James and Mary Jane Glasgow. Their names, ages and locality correspond closely to four of James's children in 1851.<ref name="Workhouse1847">[https://www.billmacafee.com/otherrecords/ballymenaworkhouse.pdf William Macafee, "Admissions to Ballymena Workhouse: 13th May to 8th July 1847"], registrations 4406–4410, Mary, Boyd, William James, Mary Jane and Margaret Glasgow of Glenleslie (accessed 13 August 2026).</ref>

== Research Notes ==

The 1851 return records James and Peggy's marriage year as 1848. This means Peggy should not be treated as the proved mother of children born between about 1831 and 1846, but it does not make earlier biological maternity impossible. [[Unknown-764460|Mary]] of the 1847 workhouse group is the strongest identified candidate for James's earlier wife and the older children's mother, but no inspected marriage or other record explicitly names James as Mary's husband. Until a better-supported mother can be attached, Peggy should remain attached to those children with uncertain confidence.

The workhouse transcription says that its Mary Jane died on 27 November 1847, while the 1851 return records an eleven-year-old Mary Jane living with James. This could reflect a transcription or return error, or two same-named children, and should not be resolved without the original records.

The [http://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Dunaghy/Ballycregagh/29/ National Archives of Ireland household link] cited on the profile is preserved. The public detailed transcription supplies the accessible household data used here.

James was a son of [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow of Glenleslie]]. John's 1851 household directly calls eighteen-year-old [[Glasgow-3168|Ann Glasgow]] his granddaughter.<ref name="Glenleslie1851">[https://durrushistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/1851irishcensusreturnscommadelimitedtextfilejul24_071.pdf ''1851 Irish Census Returns: detailed comma-delimited transcription''], Glenleslie household 21.</ref> Ann's 1869 marriage then names her father as James Glasgow, weaver.<ref name="AnnMarriage1869">General Register Office of Ireland, [https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1869/11434/8200208.pdf civil marriage register], Ann Jane Glasgow and Patrick Russell, 24 November 1869.</ref> James's 1847 family group at Glenleslie and later household in adjoining Dunaghy provide the identity bridge between those explicit relationships. His mother remains unidentified.

The 1825 designation ''junior'' distinguishes James from the other Ballybogy occupier but does not say they were father and son. The unsuffixed James should not be attached as this man's father. [[Glasgow-1780|James McClure Glasgow]] was the other serious 1825 candidate, but his documented Broughshane–Clough clerical family and lack of any Dunaghy residence make him materially weaker. [[Glasgow-1702]] and [[Glasgow-1573]] remain separate similar-name profiles.
