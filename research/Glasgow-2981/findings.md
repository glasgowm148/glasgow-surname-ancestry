# Findings: Glasgow-2981

Last updated: 2026-08-25

## Current conclusion

The Glenleslie evidence now establishes a further family chain. In 1851,
[[Glasgow-3168|Ann Glasgow]], aged eighteen, was explicitly recorded as a
granddaughter of [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow]]. Her 1869 marriage names her
father as James Glasgow, weaver. Of the documented local candidates,
[[Glasgow-2981|James Glasgow]] is the James already tied to Glenleslie through
his family's 1847 Ballymena workhouse entries and to the adjoining Dunaghy
cluster in 1851. The combined records support James as John's son and Ann as
James's daughter. James's mother and Ann's mother remain unidentified.

[[Glasgow-2981|James Glasgow]] is directly identified as the fifty-year-old
Antrim-born head of household 29 at Ballycregagh in the surviving 1851 Dunaghy
census. The return calls John, William James, Mary Jane, Robert, Margaret,
Boyde and Andy his children. It records James and [[Hamilton-25516|Peggy
Hamilton]] as married in 1848 and identifies Peggy's parents and sisters.

James is also the strongest and probable identity for “James Glasgow junior”
at Ballybogy in the 1825 Dunaghy tithe book. His about-1801 birth, the 1847
Glenleslie family group and the 1851 Ballycregagh household form a parish-level
continuity. Rev. James McClure Glasgow belongs to the separately documented
Broughshane–Clough clerical family and has no Dunaghy record. The suffix does
not make the unsuffixed Ballybogy James his father.

The recorded 1848 marriage means Peggy should not be treated as the proved
mother of the children born before it, but it does not make earlier biological
maternity impossible. An 1847 Ballymena Workhouse admission group contains
Mary Glasgow, married and deserted, with Margaret, Boyd, William James and Mary
Jane at Glenleslie. The names and ages correspond closely to four of James's
1851 children. Mary is therefore the strongest identified earlier-wife and
mother candidate, although no marriage record or record explicitly naming
James as her husband has been found. Until Mary can safely replace Peggy on the
children's profiles, Peggy should remain attached but uncertain. Robert, aged
eleven months in 1851, was born after James and Peggy's recorded marriage and
is probably Peggy's son.

## Parentage and cluster placement

relationship chain is John's granddaughter Ann in the 1851 Glenleslie census,
Ann's father James in her 1869 marriage, and James's independently documented
Glenleslie/Dunaghy household network. No source identifies James's mother, so
he is not attached to [[Alexander-16967|Rose Alexander]].

The two existing similar-person leads, [[Glasgow-1702]] and [[Glasgow-1573]],
remain separate profiles. No record inspected in this pass establishes a
duplicate identity.

## Record findings and identity limits — 17 October 2026

A possible identification concerns James Glasgow, Belfast Juvenile Society secretary, 1830–1831. James Glasgow and Walter Moffat are named secretaries announcing the Belfast Juvenile Society’s sixth annual meeting at Rev. Dr Cooke’s May Street meeting-house on Monday 15 November 1830. In March 1831 Rev. Walter Moffatt and Mr James Glasgow attended a Knockbracken meeting as the Society’s deputation; Rev. Thomas Houston chaired it. (Belfast News-Letter, 12 November 1830, p.2, Belfast Juvenile Society sixth annual meeting announcement; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390573239/); Belfast News-Letter, 4 March 1831, p.4, Belfast Juvenile Society, Knockbracken meeting; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390543442/).) The repeated society and associates support one society account. The same Walter Moffat appears in the 1828 Institution report, making [Glasgow-1780](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1780) a compatible student/ministry-network candidate; this does not prove that the society secretary is the college student. The newspaper does not call James the president, chair or minister. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns James Glasgow, Belfast tobacco seller, 1822–23. James Glasgow offered leaf tobacco in three Belfast advertisements in 1822–23. The August 1822 notice directs buyers to him at James M’Master’s; the May 1823 notice locates samples at M’Master’s North Street premises. (The Irishman, 15 March 1822, p. 2, tobacco advertisement dated 9 March; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61259036/); The Irishman, 23 August 1822, p. 3, tobacco advertisement dated 20 August; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61260054/); The Irishman, 30 May 1823, p. 3, tobacco advertisement dated 27 May; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61261904/).) Repeated name, trade and contact support a consolidated seller account. M’Master’s premises are a commercial contact, not a proved home or owned property. No age, family or link to William Glasgow the grocer is stated. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns James Glasgow, Belfast tobacco seller, 1822–23. An auction notice describes the large shop and stores at No.32 Rosemary Street, Belfast, as formerly occupied by Mr James Glasgow. The lease interest was offered for sale on 1 April 1828; the notice dated 21 March gives 34 years unexpired from 1 November next and £30 late Irish currency yearly rent. (Belfast News-Letter, 1 April 1828, p.3, auction of shop and stores, No.32 Rosemary Street; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390544933/).) Former occupancy is explicit; ownership, trade and residence are not. The surname is certain, but identity with the 1822–23 tobacco seller, William the grocer or another James remains unproved. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns James Glasgow, Kilrea procession case, 1835–1836. James Glasgow was among the men indicted after a procession and alleged riot at Kilrea on 13 July 1835. The procession count was set aside under the amnesty; the trial proceeded on the riot charge and the jury immediately acquitted the prisoners. (Belfast News-Letter, 25 March 1836, p.1, Kilrea Orangemen, indictment and acquittal; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390543681/).) The court report establishes an accused surname bearer and acquittal, not a conviction, residence, occupation or kinship. Regional James Glasgow candidates remain unassigned. No connection to Alexander or Robert of Lisnagaver is supplied. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns James Glasgow, plaintiff in Dublin Exchequer, 1820. James Glasgow was plaintiff against Maxwell Lepper and Clotworthy Dobbin in a Court of Exchequer report headed Dublin, 22 June 1820. The defendants’ motion to rescind the contempt process was refused with costs and liberty for the plaintiff to continue process. (The Irishman, 30 June 1820, p. 2, Court of Exchequer, Dublin, 22 June; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61258711/).) The report gives a procedural decision, not final judgment on the dispute, and no age, occupation, residence or relatives for James. George Frederick Moulds is named as plaintiff’s agent. Identity with the later Belfast tobacco seller or any candidate is unproved. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns James Glasgow, plaintiff in Dublin Exchequer, 1820. A separate report lists James Glasgow on the petty jury for Friday 1 August 1828. He is a juror, not one of the accused in the following riot-and-assault report. (Belfast News-Letter, 5 August 1828, p.4, petty jury, Friday 1 August; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390549197/).) The name is James Glasgow, not the automated index’s combined William James Glasgow. No residence, occupation or kin is stated. This remains an unassigned legal occurrence; identity with the 1820 Exchequer plaintiff is unproved. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns James Glasgow, plaintiff in Dublin Exchequer, 1820. A report in the Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser states that the parties in cause and cross-cause included Robert Thompson of Newry, the Rev. Mr Walsh of Ballymena, and Mr Barbour of Lisburn; it then says “The following were the Jury” and lists James Glasgow. (Freeman's Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser, 3 November 1823, p. 3, trial report and jury list; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/1050060364/).) The original page confirms James Glasgow’s jury role in this reported trial. The newspaper issue is from Dublin, but the inspected passage does not show a court heading or venue. Newry, Ballymena and Lisburn describe other named parties, not the juror. The occurrence is compatible with, but not proved to be the same person as, the James Glasgow on the 1828 Belfast petty jury; identity with the 1820 Exchequer plaintiff is also unproved. The identification remains uncertain.
