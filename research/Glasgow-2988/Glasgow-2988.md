## Identity and possible duplicates

The 1766 religious census names a Protestant James Glasgow at Corvanaghan in Kildress parish.<ref name="Kildress1766">National Archives of Ireland, Parliamentary Return 667, 1766 religious census for Kildress, IHP/1/667, [https://virtualtreasury.ie/item/NAI-IHP-1-667 Virtual Treasury catalogue and original return]; [https://by2022-prod.adaptcentre.ie/loris/RCBL_23_176.JPG/full/full/0/default.jpg RCB Library transcript, p. 176].</ref> The return gives no age, wife, children or occupation and does not identify that householder as this profile. The same occurrence is represented by [[Glasgow-3137|Glasgow-3137]].

This profile may duplicate [[Glasgow-3137|the Corvanaghan householder]], [[Glasgow-1024|James Glasgow of Moneymore]], or [[Glasgow-1970|another profile attached to the Corvanaghan theory]]. Corvanaghan is about nine miles west of Moneymore, the estimated generation is compatible, and no later Irish record has been identified for the 1766 Corvanaghan man. No record yet connects him to Mary Harkness, Robert, Arthur, Joseph, the 1770 compiled immigration entry or the 1804 Pennsylvania naturalisation index.

The attached sons [[Glasgow-2989|James]] and [[Glasgow-2990|John]] are unproved. Two men called James Glasgo and one John Glasgo were recorded at Drumearn in adjacent Derryloran parish in 1766, so the names alone cannot establish a household.

## Do not add as fact

- The return names no son or father.
- Identity with Glasgow-1024, Glasgow-3137 or Glasgow-1970.

=== Comparison with the Cookstown Mitchell family ===

A separate family account identifies [[Glasgow-1970|James Glasgow]], whose wife was a Mitchell, as a settler near Cookstown after moving from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750. Their son [[Glasgow-1968|John Glasgow]] and grandsons [[Glasgow-1969|William Glasgow]] and [[Glasgow-1967|John B. Glasgow Jr.]] form a traceable Cookstown-to-Michigan line.<ref name="Hillsdale1879">''History of Hillsdale County, Michigan'' (Philadelphia: Everts & Abbott, 1879), 263. [https://archive.org/details/bad0928.0001.001.umich.edu/page/263/mode/1up Page image].</ref>

The 1766 return does not identify the Corvanaghan James's wife or children, so it cannot establish that he was the Mitchell-family progenitor. The sons presently attached to this profile are not independently documented and their estimated ages do not distinguish the candidates.

No post-1766 Irish occurrence has yet been assigned to this James. He therefore also remains a possible, but unproved, duplicate of [[Glasgow-1024|the reported James Glasgow of Moneymore]].
