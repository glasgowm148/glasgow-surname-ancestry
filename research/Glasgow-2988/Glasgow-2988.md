James Glasgow was a Protestant householder at Corvanaghan in Kildress parish, County Tyrone, in 1766.<ref name="Kildress1766">National Archives of Ireland, Parliamentary Return 667, 1766 religious census for Kildress, IHP/1/667, [https://virtualtreasury.ie/item/NAI-IHP-1-667 original return and catalogue]; [https://by2022-prod.adaptcentre.ie/loris/RCBL_23_176.JPG/full/full/0/default.jpg RCB Library MS 23 transcript, p. 176].</ref> The return gives no age, spouse, children, occupation, origin or later history. His birth is known only to have occurred before the return was compiled.

The district context comes from those Cookstown/Corvanaghan/Moneymore comparisons, not from the 1759 index. This is an inherited family hypothesis with no independent father record for the Mitchell progenitor.

No will, lease or baptism names the Cookstown James as the older James’s son. The reviewed PRONI detail says Drumcarn, County Tyrone, not Drumearn, and gives 17 January 1759 without age or kin; the modern profile identity and meaning of that date remain unresolved. It cannot corroborate Drumearn residence, a death date, or this father choice. The older and younger James accounts may themselves be duplicates rather than separate generations; if established, the father edge must collapse. The existing older pedigree and the Corvanaghan/Moneymore identity remain unproved. Multiple James and William householders appear in the 1740 Derryloran return, and two James men appear at Drumearn in 1766. Mary Harkness is not transferred to the Mitchell account, and Margaret Rutherford is not assigned as its mother. [[Glasgow-1970|James]], [[Glasgow-2988|James]], [[Glasgow-1024|James]] and the older [[Glasgow-1025|James]] remain separate possible overlapping reconstructions, not independently proved sons or households.

=== Identity and family ===

This merged profile represents the single James Glasgow occurrence at Corvanaghan in the 1766 Kildress return. The former profiles [[Glasgow-2988]] and [[Glasgow-2988]] duplicated that record.

The former estimate of birth about 1720, an origin in Derryloran and the attached sons [[Glasgow-2989|James]] and [[Glasgow-2990|John]] are not established by the return. Those relationships remain uncertain.

The 1740 Protestant householders' return names two men called James Glasgow in Derryloran, but gives no townlands, ages or relationships.<ref>W. Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/1740religiousreturnsulstername.pdf 1740 Ulster Protestant householders index], Derryloran entries.</ref> The 1766 Derryloran return separately names two men called Jas. Glasgo at Drumearn.<ref>National Archives of Ireland, IHP/1/665, [https://virtualtreasury.ie/item/NAI-IHP-1-665 1766 Derryloran religious return].</ref> None can be identified as this Corvanaghan householder from name alone.

=== Possible duplicates ===

This man is a possible duplicate of [[Glasgow-1024|James Glasgow of Moneymore]], the reported father of Robert Glasgow. Corvanaghan is about nine miles from Moneymore and the chronology is compatible, but no source connects the householder to Mary Harkness, the emigrant siblings, the 1770 compiled immigration entry or the 1804 Pennsylvania naturalisation applicant.

[[Glasgow-1970]] represents a separate family tradition in which James and his Mitchell wife migrated from Scotland to northern Ireland and settled near Cookstown. No record links that couple to Corvanaghan.

The 1826 Kildress tithe list names Glasgow occupiers at Muntober, Whitehill and Oritor, but none at Corvanaghan.<ref>[https://www.irishgenealogyhub.com/tyrone/tithe-applotment-books/parish-of-kildress.php Kildress tithe applotment transcription, 1826].</ref> This sixty-year gap does not prove death or emigration.


=== Comparison with the Cookstown Mitchell family ===

The 1879 ''History of Hillsdale County, Michigan'' identifies another James Glasgow of the same broad generation. It says James and his wife, whose surname was Mitchell, moved from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750 and settled in County Tyrone near Cookstown. Their son [[Glasgow-1968|John Glasgow]] farmed near Cookstown, and grandsons [[Glasgow-1969|William Glasgow]] and [[Glasgow-1967|John B. Glasgow Jr.]] later settled in Michigan.<ref name="HillsdaleMitchell">''History of Hillsdale County, Michigan'' (Philadelphia: Everts & Abbott, 1879), p. 263, [https://archive.org/details/bad0928.0001.001.umich.edu/page/263/mode/1up page image].</ref> That family is represented on WikiTree by [[Glasgow-1970|James Glasgow]] and [[Mitchell-34480|his Mitchell wife]].

The 1766 return does not identify the Corvanaghan James's wife or children, so it cannot establish that he was the Mitchell-family progenitor. The sons attached to this profile are not independently documented and their estimated ages cannot distinguish the candidates.

No post-1766 Irish occurrence has yet been assigned to the Corvanaghan householder. He therefore also remains a possible, but unproved, duplicate of [[Glasgow-1024|the reported James Glasgow of Moneymore]]. No merger among these profiles is presently supported by a source-level bridge.
