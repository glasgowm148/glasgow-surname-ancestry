### Record-bearing passages to audit

- John Glasgow was recorded in the 1766 Religious Census for the Parish of Derryloran, County Tyrone, Ireland. He was listed as a Protestant in a census aimed at assessing the religious composition of the population at that time.[1]
- Derryloran is a civil parish located near the town of Cookstown, in the eastern part of County Tyrone, Northern Ireland. At the time of the census, John Glasgow appeared in the household listings alongside two individuals named James Glasgow. Their exact relationship is uncertain, but it is likely that one of the James Glasgows was either John's father or brother.
- Although both James and John Glasgow were recorded in the 1766 Religious Census for Derryloran, there is no indication that they were heads of households. It is likely that Wm Glasgow, who appears in the 1740 records, was their father and head of the family. Their presence in the census suggests that the Glasgow family had an established Protestant presence in County Tyrone during a period of religious and political change in Ireland.
- It is not currently known whether John or James Glasgow had descendants recorded in later censuses or parish records, but their appearance in the 1766 census provides valuable evidence of their family's early presence in the region.
