## Source findings — Montgreenan double-marriage notice, 5 October 2026

Charlotte is a strong compatible candidate for the notice’s Charlotte Maria Cecilia, but identity remains unproved. Her distinctive names, current Drake surname and 1827 chronology fit the youngest daughter described as of Montgreenan.

The original [Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser, 2 September 1847, p. 4](https://www.newspapers.com/image/401331272/) reports two weddings at Paisley on “the 25th ult” (25 August 1847 inferred). Anne is explicitly the second daughter of the late Robert Glasgow Esq.; Rev George Drake, son of Rev John Drake, rector of Stourton, married Charlotte Maria Cecilia, explicitly the youngest daughter of the late Robert Glasgow Esq. of Montgreenan, Ayr. The source explicitly gives Robert Glasgow as father of both brides; it does not name either mother. The match to this profile remains a separate, qualified assessment. See the shared [documentary account](/research/subjects/newspapers-irish-1800-1850/#montgreenan-double-marriage-1847).

The [Aberdeen Journal, 19 December 1827, p. 4](https://www.newspapers.com/image/393027340/) independently reports an unnamed daughter born at Rome on 22 November to “Mrs Robertson Glasgow, of Montgreenan.” Its exact date, place and mother’s designation are consistent with Charlotte’s recorded birth and family context, but it does not name the child or father. This comparison supports Robertson-30564 as the probable father candidate: independent notices connect Mrs Robertson Glasgow to Montgreenan and a daughter born at Rome in 1827; a profile-cited transcription on [Mary Glasgow-3021](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3021) reports an 1841 census household with Robert Glasgow and Charlotte (10), though that census has not been independently inspected. Robertson-30564’s recorded Montgreenan succession and 1845 death fit the 1847 “late Robert.” Glasgow-1076 remains a weaker possible alternative: the 1835 appeal calls Mrs Robertson Glasgow his natural daughter and first heir, and his reported 1827 death is not a conflict with a 1847 notice calling the father “late.” No reviewed source definitively excludes him.
