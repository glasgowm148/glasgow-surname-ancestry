== Biography ==
'''John Glasgow''' was born about 1675 and is associated with Irvine, Ayrshire, Scotland. Both the date and place are part of a reconstruction and remain uncertain. This profile currently combines at least three same-name propositions which have not been proved to represent one man.

=== 1693 father proposition ===

An Irvine baptism dated '''8 October 1693''' records a son John to a father named John Glasgow and Agnes Martine.<ref name="Baptism1693">“Scotland Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950,” FamilySearch, John Glasgow and Agnes Martine, baptism of son John, 8 October 1693, Irvine 595/ M.</ref> This record proves that a couple with those names had a son John; it does not identify the father as the 1725 skipper or the 1740 bailie. [[Martine-274|Agnes Martine]] and [[Glasgow-1473|John Glasgow]] are retained on this combined profile as uncertain relationship candidates.

=== 1725 skipper proposition ===

In 1725, '''John Glasgow elder''', skipper in Irvine, purchased a one-sixteenth share of the ship ''Janet'' of Irvine, commanded by James Miller, for £7 sterling.<ref name="IrvineCatalogue">Irvine Burgh catalogue, 1725 ship-share entry. The catalogue PDF link formerly supplied by Ayrshire Archives returned HTTP 410 Gone when checked 26 August 2026; the source title and entry detail are retained pending recovery of a stable archive catalogue URL.</ref> The word “elder” distinguishes him from a younger namesake. It does not by itself prove that the younger John was his son, identify Agnes Martine as his wife, or connect him to the 1693 baptism family.

=== 1740 bailie proposition ===

A 1740 Irvine record names John Glasgow as a bailie in a legal summons against Elizabeth Cunninghame, widow of Robert Montgomerie of Broomland.<ref name="IrvineCatalogue" /> The shared name and Irvine setting make the 1725 skipper and 1740 bailie possible matches, but the cited records do not supply an occupation, family relationship or other bridge proving they were the same man.

=== Comparison with Glasgow-1434 ===

[[Glasgow-1434|John Glasgow]], baptised in 1679 at Kilwinning and later associated with Stevenston, remains an open candidate for part of this reconstruction. He would have been only about fourteen when the son John was baptised in 1693, which weighs against—but does not by itself disprove—the father proposition. The alleged duplicate-son conflict is not decisive because the skipper, bailie and family identifications on this profile are themselves reconstructed. The profiles should not be merged or detached without burgh or parish evidence linking the Kilwinning/Stevenston man to Irvine.

John's death remains unknown. If the 1740 bailie was the same person represented here, he was alive in that year; that equation is not proved.

== Research Notes ==
=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

[[Glasgow-3375|John Glasgow]] is the selected very uncertain working father of [[Glasgow-3018|John Glasgow]], treating the latter provisionally as the father named with Agnes Martine in the 1693 Irvine child entry. The route adopts the existing comparison with [[Glasgow-1434|John Glasgow]], indexed at Kilwinning on 6 July 1679 as child of John and Barbara Alason. Exact forename and surname, the neighbouring Ayrshire parishes, and a specific recorded child/parent pair make this a usable local model. It is not a merge or an identification of all events currently collected under either account. If the 1679 event was an infant baptism, that child would have been only fourteen by the 1693 entry. This is a serious weakness. Baptism of an older child would allow an older father, but no evidence establishes such a baptism; neither an earlier birth nor unusually young fatherhood is asserted as fact. The current circa-1675 birth is an inherited estimate, not independent support for the match.

[[Allison-6866|Barbara Alason / Allison]] is separately selected as the very uncertain working mother through the same proposed 1679-child identity. The indexed 1679 entry actually names Barbara Alason; this is not a mother invented from the father choice or supplied from the surname-only 1689 entry. The existing Allison profile represents that Alason/Allason household and is a compatible account. Her circa-1659 birth is also an estimate, and no date, birthplace or marriage is newly established. The mother identification and the adult John identification remain unproved. These two parent links depend on one child/adult comparison; they are not independent confirmations of the proposed family.

The Irvine source is the supplied Scotland’s People OPR index: JOHN GLASGOW, 08/10/1693, parish 595, reference 10 / 12, FR23, parents JOHN GLASGOW/AGNES MARTINE. The previous review interprets this as 8 October; the structured child field instead says 10 August 1693. Both readings remain visible, and an index from a combined births/baptisms collection does not establish a birthday. [[Glasgow-1473|John Glasgow]] is retained as the child-record account. Identification with the John Glasgow younger active as skipper in 1720, the 1719 father of Grizal, or the later bursary recipient remains separate and uncertain. Neither the elder/younger wording nor the new grandparents prove those adult identities or an elder skipper’s paternity. Agnes Martine remains the mother named in the 1693 record; no new spouse or parent-field certainty is assigned to the child account.

The 1725 elder skipper, 1729 Sailors’ Poor Box treasurer, 1734 deceased shipmaster and 1740 living bailie are not newly equated with the 1693 father. The 1734 catalogue describes heirs of a late shipmaster, while the 1740 entry names a living merchant/bailie; those occurrences cannot be one man. [[Glasgow-1077|John Glasgow]] remains a compatible candidate for the later bailie, independently of this parent model; the recorded 1690 child is not adopted as the 1693 adult father. The new parent fields are scoped to the 1693 father proposition, not an assertion that the composite biography is one proved life. All later occurrences and original archive references remain available without an invented death date or transfer of records between profiles.

The same 1679 child remains only possibly identical with the Stevenston father of John in 1701 and Adam in 1706. The alleged two-John-sons conflict does not justify excluding the Irvine match when those identities and the survival of the children are unproved. No intervening death, second baptism or remarriage is invented to reconcile the accounts. The already selected West Kilbride [[Glasgow-3161|John Glasgow]] route also uses this childhood comparison, so it provides no independent corroboration here. The retained adult accounts may be duplicates rather than distinct brothers. An older Irvine John, another local parent, or identification of the 1693 father with the older [[Glasgow-3375|John Glasgow]] rather than his proposed son remain alternatives; the last option would collapse the new father edge as an identity duplicate. The older John’s possible identity with minister [[Glasgow-1073|John Glasgow]] and earlier ancestry keep their previous uncertainty; this is not a new proved son of the minister. === Newly checked Irvine catalogue entries ===

* On 15 March 1729 a bond named “John Glasgow elder, skiller in Irvine,” as treasurer of the Sailors' Poor Box. The catalogue prints “skiller”; the underlying bond has not been inspected. His name, “elder” label, town and maritime association make him a strong candidate for the 1725 skipper, but the catalogue does not explicitly equate the men.<ref>Ayrshire Archives, ''Irvine Burgh Catalogue'' (2017), IB2/15/45, p. 160, 15 March 1729; [https://web.archive.org/web/20190919232657/https://ayrshirearchives.files.wordpress.com/2019/04/irvine-burgh-catalogue-2017-watermarked.pdf archived catalogue].</ref>
* On 30 January 1734 a ship share was sold from the heirs of the late John Glasgow, shipmaster. This man had died by that date. He is a plausible match for the 1725 skipper and 1729 sailors' fund treasurer, but those identities are not explicit in the catalogue.<ref>Ayrshire Archives, ''Irvine Burgh Catalogue'' (2017), IB2/14/52, p. 145, 30 January 1734; [https://web.archive.org/web/20190919232657/https://ayrshirearchives.files.wordpress.com/2019/04/irvine-burgh-catalogue-2017-watermarked.pdf archived catalogue].</ref>
* The 1740 legal-process entry calls its John Glasgow a bailie and merchant. He cannot be the shipmaster described as deceased by January 1734. Which of these men, if either, was the 1693 father or 1725 skipper remains to be established from the original records.<ref>Ayrshire Archives, ''Irvine Burgh Catalogue'' (2017), IB2/1/70, p. 70, 1740; [https://web.archive.org/web/20190919232657/https://ayrshirearchives.files.wordpress.com/2019/04/irvine-burgh-catalogue-2017-watermarked.pdf archived catalogue].</ref>

* [[Glasgow-1077|John Glasgow]], the later Irvine provost, has a documented 9 December 1690 Kilbirnie baptism and is a plausible candidate for the 1740 bailie entry. The same child cannot have been the adult father in the 1693 Irvine baptism attached as a separate proposition on this profile. The 1740 entry gives no parent, wife or birth identifier, so its assignment to Glasgow-1077 remains an identity hypothesis.<ref>Findmypast, Scotland Parish Births and Baptisms, record SCOT/OPR/BAP/3585655, Kilbirnie, 9 December 1690, John Glasgow; and Ayrshire Archives, ''Irvine Burgh Catalogue'' (2017), IB2/1/70, p. 70, 1740 bailie John Glasgow.</ref>
