# Findings: [John Glasgow (Glasgow-3018), Irvine reconstruction](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3018)

Last updated: 2026-09-26

## Current conclusion

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

**Selected very uncertain Irvine/Kilwinning parent pair, 16 October 2026:** John Glasgow-3018 is placed under John Glasgow-3375 and Barbara Allison-6866 only through possible identity with the 1679 Kilwinning child. Infant baptism would imply fatherhood at fourteen in 1693. The skipper/bailie and younger-John identities remain unresolved; both parent fields are conditional. [Evidence and alternatives](/branches/early-scottish-records/#irvine-kilwinning-working-family).

This profile combines at least three propositions: father John with Agnes
Martine in the 1693 baptism, `John Glasgow elder`, skipper in 1725, and bailie
in 1740. The records share name and Irvine, but the biography itself says the
1693 baptism may concern another John. The combined identity is not proved.

The newly checked 1734 ship-share catalogue describes a deceased John Glasgow,
shipmaster, while the 1740 legal-process entry names a living Bailie John
Glasgow, merchant. Those are distinct men. The 1740 bailie is a plausible
occurrence of the later provost on Glasgow-1077; that profile's 1690 baptism
rules him out as the adult father in 1693. Neither identification is assumed
for this profile without a direct record bridge.

[Glasgow-1434](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1434), baptised in 1679 at
Kilwinning and later associated with Stevenston, remains an open candidate for
part of this reconstruction. The alleged duplicate-son conflict is not enough
to exclude him, but his young age in 1693 and lack of a burgh-record bridge
prevent a merge.

## Source findings

The [1721 Irvine prize bond](https://electricscotland.com/history/gazetteer/MunimentsoftheroyalburghofIrvine02.pdf#page=160), printed in *Muniments of the Royal Burgh of Irvine*, II, p. 133, lists “John Glasgow for one” among residents pledging a half crown for the annual shooting prize. It could concern this profile's proposed 1725 skipper, but gives no trade, kin or other bridge. The reference was added to the live WikiTree Research Notes as an **unassigned same-name lead**, not a new identity conclusion.

The same volume's [1669–70 town account](https://electricscotland.com/history/gazetteer/MunimentsoftheroyalburghofIrvine02.pdf#page=299), p. 272, records the provost with “John Glasgow ffor stones to the bridge” on 21 July. Its date falls within the account opened 7 October 1669. The record does not identify the 1693 father, 1725 skipper or 1740 bailie. It establishes another earlier John occurrence at Irvine, with no demonstrated connection to this profile. The printed page images were checked on 26 September 2026.

The locally recovered *Irvine Burgh Catalogue* (2017) supplied three further entries, each checked against the PDF page image. **IB2/15/45, p. 160**, a bond of 15 March 1729, names “John Glasgow elder, skiller in Irvine,” as treasurer of the Sailors' Poor Box; “skiller” is the catalogue's printed word. He is a strong but unproved candidate for the 1725 “elder” skipper. **IB2/14/52, p. 145**, 30 January 1734, describes a ship share belonging to the heirs of the *late* John Glasgow, shipmaster. He died before that date and may have been the 1725 skipper or 1729 treasurer. **IB2/1/70, p. 70**, dated 1740, names Bailie John Glasgow, merchant, in a summons. The deceased 1734 shipmaster and living 1740 bailie are necessarily distinct, so the profile's combined 1725/1740 proposition cannot simply be assumed. Whether the 1725 skipper was the 1734 deceased shipmaster or the 1740 bailie remains to be tested in the original records. These references were added to the live WikiTree Research Notes without changing family links or existing vital fields. [Archived catalogue citation](https://web.archive.org/web/20190919232657/https://ayrshirearchives.files.wordpress.com/2019/04/irvine-burgh-catalogue-2017-watermarked.pdf); the remote archived file was incomplete when recovered locally, so the exact archive references are essential.

[Glasgow-1077](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1077), associated with later Irvine provost service, is a plausible candidate for the 1740 bailie. His [9 December 1690 Kilbirnie baptism](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FOPR%2FBAP%2F3585655&tab=this) rules him out as the adult father in the 1693 baptism proposition on Glasgow-3018. The bailie entry still does not prove identity with Glasgow-1077. This comparison was added to Glasgow-3018's live Research Notes.

## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

John Glasgow-3375 is the selected very uncertain working father of John Glasgow-3018, treating the latter provisionally as the father named with Agnes Martine in the 1693 Irvine child entry. The route adopts the existing comparison with John Glasgow-1434, indexed at Kilwinning on 6 July 1679 as child of John and Barbara Alason. Exact forename and surname, the neighbouring Ayrshire parishes, and a specific recorded child/parent pair make this a usable local model. It is not a merge or an identification of all events currently collected under either account. If the 1679 event was an infant baptism, that child would have been only fourteen by the 1693 entry. This is a serious weakness. Baptism of an older child would allow an older father, but no evidence establishes such a baptism; neither an earlier birth nor unusually young fatherhood is asserted as fact. The current circa-1675 birth is an inherited estimate, not independent support for the match.

Barbara Allison-6866 is separately selected as the very uncertain working mother through the same proposed 1679-child identity. The indexed 1679 entry actually names Barbara Alason; this is not a mother invented from the father choice or supplied from the surname-only 1689 entry. The existing Allison profile represents that Alason/Allason household and is a compatible account. Her circa-1659 birth is also an estimate, and no date, birthplace or marriage is newly established. The mother identification and the adult John identification remain unproved. These two parent links depend on one child/adult comparison; they are not independent confirmations of the proposed family.

The Irvine source is the supplied Scotland’s People OPR index: JOHN GLASGOW, 08/10/1693, parish 595, reference 10 / 12, FR23, parents JOHN GLASGOW/AGNES MARTINE. The previous review interprets this as 8 October; the structured child field instead says 10 August 1693. Both readings remain visible, and an index from a combined births/baptisms collection does not establish a birthday. John Glasgow-1473 is retained as the child-record account. Identification with the John Glasgow younger active as skipper in 1720, the 1719 father of Grizal, or the later bursary recipient remains separate and uncertain. Neither the elder/younger wording nor the new grandparents prove those adult identities or an elder skipper’s paternity. Agnes Martine remains the mother named in the 1693 record; no new spouse or parent-field certainty is assigned to the child account.

The 1725 elder skipper, 1729 Sailors’ Poor Box treasurer, 1734 deceased shipmaster and 1740 living bailie are not newly equated with the 1693 father. The 1734 catalogue describes heirs of a late shipmaster, while the 1740 entry names a living merchant/bailie; those occurrences cannot be one man. Glasgow-1077 remains a compatible candidate for the later bailie, independently of this parent model; the recorded 1690 child is not adopted as the 1693 adult father. The new parent fields are scoped to the 1693 father proposition, not an assertion that the composite biography is one proved life. All later occurrences and original archive references remain available without an invented death date or transfer of records between profiles.

The same 1679 child remains only possibly identical with the Stevenston father of John in 1701 and Adam in 1706. The alleged two-John-sons conflict does not justify excluding the Irvine match when those identities and the survival of the children are unproved. No intervening death, second baptism or remarriage is invented to reconcile the accounts. The already selected West Kilbride John Glasgow-3161 route also uses this childhood comparison, so it provides no independent corroboration here. The retained adult accounts may be duplicates rather than distinct brothers. An older Irvine John, another local parent, or identification of the 1693 father with the older John Glasgow-3375 rather than his proposed son remain alternatives; the last option would collapse the new father edge as an identity duplicate. The older John’s possible identity with minister Glasgow-1073 and earlier ancestry keep their previous uncertainty; this is not a new proved son of the minister.
