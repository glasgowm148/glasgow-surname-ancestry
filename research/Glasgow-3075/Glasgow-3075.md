== Biography ==

'''William Glasgow''' was a mealmaker in Leith. His son
[[Glasgow-3078|Archibald Glasgow]] was baptised in 1618 and was described in
1639 as the son of William Glasgow, mealmaker in Leith, when apprenticed to
Thomas Park, cordiner.<ref>Francis J. Grant, ed., ''Register of Apprentices of
the City of Edinburgh, 1583–1666'' (1906),
[https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso28scotuoft/page/71/mode/2up p.
71], 23 October 1639.</ref><ref name="Archibald1618">''Scotland Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950'', FamilySearch database, entry for Archbald Glascow, 1618.</ref> A mealmaker processed or sold grain meal within the urban food trade; the description does not necessarily mean that William was a miller.

William died in September 1644. His testament dative, confirmed 22 July 1646,
calls Archibald his lawful son and sole executor dative.<ref name="WilliamWill">National
Records of Scotland, Edinburgh Commissary Court, CC8/8/62, images 461–462,
testament dative of William Glasgow, mealmaker in Leith; [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/search-records/legal-records/wills Scotland's People wills search].</ref> Archibald's 1647
service of heir independently repeats the father-son relationship and both
men's Leith occupations.<ref>''Inquisitionum ad Capellam Domini Regis
Retornatarum... Abbreviatio'', vol. 2 (1811), Inquisitiones Generales no. 3278,
1 May 1647, original retour xix.128,
[https://archive.org/details/inquisitionumadc00grea_0 scan].</ref>

== Research Notes ==

[[Glasgow-3188|Ninian Glasgow]] remains only a possible father. A William
occurs in Ninian's damaged 1613 testament material, but the formerly quoted
''his sone'' wording cannot be securely recovered and no record identifies that
William as the Leith mealmaker.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Glasgow
Commissary Court, CC9/7/9, images 142–145, testament of Ninian Glasgow; working
transcription reinspection supplied 6 September 2026.</ref>

William's own testament does not name a brother John. The earlier derivative
reading which made John his brother and executor is superseded by the original,
which names Archibald as sole executor.

If William was Ninian's son, [[Meik-14|Jonet Meik]] is a possible mother. Her 1607 testament names Ninian as her spouse and [[Glasgow-1027|Robert Glasgow]] as her son, but does not name William.<ref name="Meik1607">National Records of Scotland, Glasgow Commissary Court, CC9/7/5, images 82–83, testament testamentar and inventory of Jonet Meik, recorded 27 August 1607.</ref> An earlier unidentified wife therefore cannot be excluded. The manuscript surname is Meik; “Melk” in electronic indexes is best treated as a transcription error.

A separate Glasgow High Kirk entry records William Glasgw, lawful son of a William Glasgw and Christian Scot, baptised on 8 November 1612; witnesses were John Allassoun and Mathew Sympsoun.<ref name="William1612">''Glasgow High Kirk Baptisms 1609–1777'', volume 1/962, baptism of William Glasgw, 8 November 1612.</ref> The elder William is chronologically compatible with the later Leith mealmaker, but no residence, occupation or relative identifies them as the same man, so Christian Scot remains only a possible wife.

William's testament records £20 Scots owed by Andro Glasgow in Woodhead and £2 10s associated with the late John Glasgow in England.<ref name="WilliamWill" /> These are financial connections, not proof that either man was William's relative.

William's Leith residence and Archibald's apprenticeship to an Edinburgh cordiner make four women recorded in South Leith or Edinburgh—[[Glasgow-3182|Barbara]], [[Glasgow-3598|Margaret]], [[Glasgow-3302|Barbara]] and [[Glasgow-3336|Isobel]]—useful collateral candidates. Their marriages into cordiner or tailor households fit the same locality and craft network,<ref>Henry Paton, ed., ''The Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595–1700'', [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_marriages_for_the_parish_of_edinburgh_1595-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=277 printed p. 267].</ref> but none of their marriage records names a father. They should not be attached as William's sisters or daughters without a direct relationship record.
