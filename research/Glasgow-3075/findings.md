## Current conclusion

Reviewed: 2026-10-15


Father assessment: [Ninian Glasgow (1550)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3188) is **possible, not proved as this William's father**. A William occurs in Ninian's damaged 1613 testament material, but the previously asserted `his sone` clause cannot be securely recovered. Neither occurrence identifies William as the later Leith mealmaker.

Child assessment: [Archibald Glasgow (Glasgow-3078), born 20 March 1618, South Leith, Midlothian, Scotland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3078) is **confirmed by four records**. His 1618 baptism names William as his father; his 1639 apprenticeship identifies that father as William Glasgow, mealmaker in Leith; William's 1646 testament calls Archibald his lawful son and sole executor dative; and Archibald's 1647 service of heir repeats the same relationship and occupations.

Two Findmypast indexes agree on the Leith marriage date, 10 July 1610, while recording the bride as Catherine Fraith or Cathrene Fraser. They index one event and do not independently establish a second marriage or resolve the disputed 1612 child.

The live Catherine profile records a birth year of 1600, but its only cited source is her child’s 1618 baptism. That citation does not establish Catherine’s birth year. The proposed 1610 bride identification therefore retains an explicit chronology question; no birth date or couple identity is proved by this review.

## Live-profile delta and exact reconstruction

Checked 19 July 2026. The structured child connection is already correct: [Archibald Glasgow (Glasgow-3078), born 20 March 1618, South Leith](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3078) is attached to this William. No new profile and no merge are required.

Add the 1639 apprenticeship and 1647 service of heir to William's biography. Together they prove that William the Leith mealmaker was Archibald's father, rather than merely a same-named baptismal parent. Keep William's own attachment to [Ninian Glasgow (Glasgow-3188), born about 1550, Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3188) uncertain: the damaged 1613 testament supports only a possible William relationship and does not identify him as the Leith man.

Correct William's death from September 1645 to September 1644. Replace the earlier derivative transcription that called John Glasgow his brother and executor: inspection of the original testament shows that Archibald Glasgow, William's lawful son, was the sole executor dative. No brother John is identified in that capacity.

### Paste-ready addition

William was the father of [[Glasgow-3078|Archibald Glasgow]]. On 23 October 1639 the Edinburgh apprentice register described Archibald as the son of William Glasgow, mealmaker in Leith, when he was apprenticed to Thomas Park, cordiner. On 1 May 1647 Archibald was served heir to William; the retour abstract again described Archibald as a shoemaker in Leith and William as a mealmaker in the same town, expressly calling William his father. These independent occupational and residence identifiers confirm that the adult Archibald was the son of this William.<ref>Francis J. ## Source findings — testament debts and the Meik testament, 7 October 2026

William's testament dative (NRS Edinburgh Commissary Court CC8/8/62, images 461–462, confirmed 22 July 1646) records £20 Scots owed by Andro Glasgow in Woodhead and £2 10s associated with the late John Glasgow in England. These are financial connections only; neither man is called a relative.

Jonet Meik's testament testamentar (NRS Glasgow Commissary Court CC9/7/5, images 82–83, recorded 27 August 1607) names Ninian Glasgow as her spouse and [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1027)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1027) as her son, but does not name William. If William was Ninian's son, Jonet is only a **possible** mother; an earlier wife cannot be excluded. The manuscript surname is Meik; "Melk" in electronic indexes is best treated as a transcription error. Seen as cited in the reviewed draft.

Recorded in `research/Glasgow-3075/Glasgow-3075.md` (8 September 2026); carried into this file on 7 October 2026.
