Child assessment: [Archibald Glasgow (Glasgow-3078), born 20 March 1618, South Leith, Midlothian, Scotland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3078) is **confirmed by four records**. His 1618 baptism names William as his father; his 1639 apprenticeship identifies that father as William Glasgow, mealmaker in Leith; William's 1646 testament calls Archibald his lawful son and sole executor dative; and Archibald's 1647 service of heir repeats the same relationship and occupations.

Two Findmypast indexes agree on the Leith marriage date, 10 July 1610, while recording the bride as Catherine Fraith or Cathrene Fraser. They index one event and do not independently establish a second marriage or resolve the disputed 1612 child.

The live Catherine profile records a birth year of 1600, but its only cited source is her child’s 1618 baptism. That citation does not establish Catherine’s birth year. The proposed 1610 bride identification therefore retains an explicit chronology question; no birth date or couple identity is proved by this review.

No brother John is identified in that capacity.

### Paste-ready addition

William was the father of [[Glasgow-3078|Archibald Glasgow]]. On 23 October 1639 the Edinburgh apprentice register described Archibald as the son of William Glasgow, mealmaker in Leith, when he was apprenticed to Thomas Park, cordiner. On 1 May 1647 Archibald was served heir to William; the retour abstract again described Archibald as a shoemaker in Leith and William as a mealmaker in the same town, expressly calling William his father. These independent occupational and residence identifiers confirm that the adult Archibald was the son of this William.<ref>Francis J. ## Source findings — testament debts and the Meik testament, 7 October 2026

William's testament dative (NRS Edinburgh Commissary Court CC8/8/62, images 461–462, confirmed 22 July 1646) records £20 Scots owed by Andro Glasgow in Woodhead and £2 10s associated with the late John Glasgow in England. These are financial connections only; neither man is called a relative.

Jonet Meik's testament testamentar (NRS Glasgow Commissary Court CC9/7/5, images 82–83, recorded 27 August 1607) names Ninian Glasgow as her spouse and [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1027)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1027) as her son, but does not name William. If William was Ninian's son, Jonet is only a **possible** mother; an earlier wife cannot be excluded. The manuscript surname is Meik; "Melk" in electronic indexes is best treated as a transcription error. Seen as cited in the reviewed draft.

Recorded in `research/Glasgow-3075/Glasgow-3075.md` (8 September 2026); carried into this file on 7 October 2026.

## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

Select William Glasgow-3075, the Leith mealmaker, as the low-confidence working father of Margaret Glasgow-3186, indexed marrying George Lyell at South Leith on 28 November 1633. William’s household is documented at Leith, with a proposed 10 July 1610 local marriage and a son Archibald baptised in 1618, securely linked to the adult mealmaker by the 1639 apprenticeship and 1647 service of heir. A daughter marrying in 1633 fits that established household generation. Shared surname, the same parish and a coherent marriage/child generation favour this named household over a newly invented father. No source calls Margaret his daughter. Her circa-1610 birth remains an inherited estimate and supplies no independent evidence of William’s paternity.

Retain the strongest specific alternative: Betham’s pedigree of Archibald Glasgow, Rathmalton clerk, whose will was dated April 1673, names Margaret as his only daughter, wife of an unnamed Cairns and mother of Thomas Cairns. The documentary father–daughter relationship is explicit, but identifying that Margaret with Glasgow-3186 remains unproved. A first Lyell marriage in 1633 permits a later Cairns marriage. The preferred William placement avoids requiring that unproved Scottish–Irish identity and remarriage. It also avoids tension with the probable identification of Archibald Glasgow-1101 as the child baptised in Glasgow in 1614 and the 1635 graduate: that child would be an implausible father of a 1633 bride. This is a ranking consideration, not an exclusion: the Scottish identity is unproved, and a baptism is not necessarily an infant’s birth. The new uncertain William field must not be used as a concrete father conflict to reject the Rathmalton candidate. A different South Leith Glasgow father also remains possible. No mother is selected: Catherine Fraith/Fraser’s 1610 bride identity and Christian Scot’s separate Glasgow household remain qualified. William’s testament names Archibald as lawful son and sole executor, not only child; omission of Margaret neither excludes her nor proves kinship.

William’s own possible Ninian father and the proved Archibald son remain unchanged.

### Possible Haddington household, 1639

Wm Glasgow is named as father of Issobell, born on 24 November and baptised on 26 November 1639 at Haddington, with mother indexed as Issobella Miral. (Findmypast/Scottish Genealogy Society, Issobell Glasgow, born 24 and baptised 26 November 1639, Haddington; OPR 709/1 p.208, transcript SCOT/OPR/BAP/4675742; original register not inspected; [Source](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FOPR%2FBAP%2F4675742).) The parental names belong directly to that indexed event; Miral has not been normalized to another surname without the original.

The Leith mealmaker is the leading possible existing identity for Wm because his established eastern-Lothian adult household and September 1644 death fit 1639. His Catherine Fraser and Christian Scot household comparisons neither prove nor by themselves exclude the Haddington occurrence; no remarriage, widowhood or overlapping marriage is inferred. [William Glasgow of Mid Calder](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1087) and [William Glasgow, baptised Inveresk in 1614](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3517), are genuine adult alternatives. No occupation or parish-to-parish bridge in the Haddington transcript chooses among them. The named child’s possible identification with [Issobell Glasgow of Mid Calder](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2454) is a separate, weak later-life comparison; it does not downgrade the direct parental statement in the Haddington event.
