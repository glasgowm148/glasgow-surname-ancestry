The 1696 apothecary apprentice and the 1699 youngest-son apprentice Thomas share the specific father description James Glasgow, weaver and burgess. Thomas’s maintained identification with the 1684 child of James and Isobell Duffe fits entry at almost fifteen. The supplied Edinburgh index gives a James to James Glasgow and Elizabeth Duffe on 16 March 1679, and another to James Glasgow and Issobell Duff on 18 April 1680. Either child fits the 1696 apprentice at seventeen or sixteen. Both childhood candidates therefore lead into a Duffe maternal household without requiring us to choose or merge the children.

The 72% rates this whole maternal identification and stays below the father’s 82%; the father, brother and mother assessments share evidence and must not be multiplied as independent support.
