== Biography ==
'''James Glasgow''' was an Irish landowner and esquire of '''Aughadenvarn''', County Leitrim, Ireland. His dates of birth and death are currently unknown, although he was probably born during the '''18th century'''.

James was married, but his wife's name has not been identified.

James and his wife had at least one known child:

* '''Jemima Glasgow''', born in County Leitrim. She married '''James Montgomery of Sea-View''', County Donegal, on '''1 January 1806'''.<ref name="Debrett">[https://archive.org/details/debrettsbaroneta25lond/page/n851/mode/2up John Debrett, ''Debrett's Baronetage, Knightage, and Companionage'', entry for Montgomery of the Hall, County Donegal.]</ref>

== Aghadonvane records ==

A lease dated 15 August 1779 and registered 10 August 1780 names '''James Glasgow of Aghadonvane, County Leitrim, esquire''' as lessee from Henry, Earl of Ely. The premises comprised the town and land transcribed as Aghteron, about 166 acres, with fishing and an eel weir on the River Bundrowse. The named measuring lives included James and two young sons of James Johnston of Kinlough.<ref name="AghadonvaneLease1779">Registry of Deeds transcript, lease dated 15 August 1779 and registered 10 August 1780, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJ4-39B5-N FamilySearch image].</ref>

Marriage articles dated 2 October 1779 for Patrick Smith and Honora McSharry were witnessed by '''James Glasgow of Aghadonvane, gentleman''', Edmond Connolly of Mullinaleck and Andrew McGowan of Moneen.<ref name="AghadonvaneMarriage1779">Registry of Deeds transcript, marriage articles dated 2 October 1779, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJ4-W935-7 FamilySearch image].</ref>

The uncommon residence and status securely identify both records with this James. They do not identify his wife or establish that the Johnston boys were relatives.

== Daughter's Marriage ==
Jemima Glasgow's marriage connected the Glasgow family of Aughadenvarn with the '''Montgomery family of Sea-View and the Hall''', a County Donegal branch which published pedigrees traced to the Montgomeries of Eglinton in Ayrshire, Scotland.<ref name="Montgomery120">[https://archive.org/details/genealogicalhist00mont/page/120/mode/1up Thomas Harrison Montgomery, ''A Genealogical History of the Family of Montgomery'', Philadelphia, 1863, p. 120.]</ref><ref name="Montgomery121">[https://archive.org/details/genealogicalhist00mont/page/121/mode/1up Thomas Harrison Montgomery, ''A Genealogical History of the Family of Montgomery'', Philadelphia, 1863, p. 121.]</ref>

Her husband, '''James Montgomery''', was born on '''1 March 1771''' and was associated with '''Sea-View''', County Donegal. He was the second son of '''Alexander Montgomery of the Hall''' and '''Mary Allen''' of the Castle Dobbs family of County Antrim.<ref name="Debrett" />

James Montgomery's elder brother was '''Sir Henry-Conyngham Montgomery''', an East India Company cavalry officer, Member of Parliament and the first '''Montgomery Baronet of the Hall'''. He was created a baronet on '''3 October 1808'''.<ref name="Montgomery121" />

The marriage therefore placed Jemima Glasgow within the immediate family of a newly created baronetcy and linked the Glasgows to a landed Montgomery family with military, parliamentary and East India Company connections.

== Children ==
James and Jemima Montgomery had at least two known children:

* '''Jemima Montgomery''', born at Sea-View, County Donegal, on '''23 October 1807'''.<ref name="Debrett" />
* '''Conyngham Montgomery''', born on '''2 January 1809'''.<ref name="Debrett" />

== Granddaughter: Baroness von Tautphoeus ==
James Glasgow's granddaughter, '''Jemima Montgomery''', became significant as both a member of the Bavarian nobility and a published novelist.

On '''29 January 1838''', she married '''Cajetan Joseph Friedrich, Baron von Tautphoeus''', of Marquartstein in Bavaria. He was a Bavarian government official and chamberlain to the King of Bavaria.<ref name="DIB">[https://www.dib.ie/biography/tautphoeus-jemima-montgomery-von-a8469 Dictionary of Irish Biography, “Tautphoeus, Jemima Montgomery von”.]</ref><ref name="UlsterBio">[https://www.newulsterbiography.co.uk/index.php/home/viewPerson/1924 Dictionary of Ulster Biography, “Montgomery, Jemima”.]</ref>

Through this marriage, James Glasgow's granddaughter became '''Baroness von Tautphoeus''' and spent most of her later life in Bavaria. She became known for English-language novels depicting Bavarian society, customs and scenery.<ref name="DIB" />

Her published works included:

* ''The Initials'' (1850)
* ''Cyrilla'' (1853)
* ''Quits'' (1857)
* ''At Odds'' (1863)

''The Initials'' became particularly popular, and William Makepeace Thackeray reportedly regarded her novels as among the best English-language representations of German life.<ref name="UlsterBio" />

Jemima and Baron von Tautphoeus had one known son:

* '''Rudolf Edgeworth Joseph von Tautphoeus''', born '''20 November 1838'''; died '''1 November 1885'''. He entered Bavarian diplomatic service and became the Bavarian minister at Rome.<ref name="DIB" />

Baron von Tautphoeus died shortly after their son, on '''14 November 1885'''. Jemima died at Munich on '''12 November 1893'''.<ref name="DIB" />

== Baronetcy Connection ==
'''Sir Henry-Conyngham Montgomery''', the elder brother of James Montgomery of Sea-View, entered the military service of the East India Company in '''1783''' and served as a cavalry officer for approximately twenty years.<ref name="Montgomery121" />

During the war against Tipu Sultan, he raised a force of cavalry and served with the combined army under Sir John Floyd. He later became inspecting field officer of yeomanry and volunteers in County Donegal, with the rank of lieutenant-colonel.<ref name="Montgomery121" />

He also served as a Member of Parliament and was created a baronet on '''3 October 1808'''. His seat was '''the Hall''', County Donegal.<ref name="Debrett" /><ref name="Montgomery121" />

Jemima Glasgow was therefore the sister-in-law of the first baronet, and her children were first cousins of the succeeding Montgomery baronets.

The principal direct evidence identifying Jemima Glasgow as James Glasgow's daughter is the entry for the Montgomery baronetcy in ''Debrett's Baronetage''. It records that James Montgomery of Sea-View married “Jemima, daughter of James Glasgow, of Aughadenvarn, County Leitrim, Esquire,” on 1 January 1806.<ref name="Debrett" />

The same entry records their children as Jemima, born 23 October 1807, and Conyngham, born 2 January 1809.<ref name="Debrett" />

The earlier version of this profile incorrectly gave Alexander Montgomery of the Hall's birth year as '''1757'''. ''Debrett's Baronetage'' gives his birth year as '''1727''', which is consistent with his marriage in 1762.<ref name="Debrett" />

The earlier claim that Robert Montgomery of Bonyglyn had six sons also appears to have resulted from a confused or unrelated pedigree. Thomas Harrison Montgomery's published account states that Robert Montgomery of Bonyglen left an '''only son''', Alexander Montgomery, born in 1700.<ref name="Montgomery120" />

The nineteenth-century pedigree describes an ancestor as “Hugh Montgomerie of Auchinhood and Bowhouse.” Contemporary records may distinguish Hugh of Auchinhood from Hugh in Bowhouse, so the intermediate Scottish generations should be treated cautiously until verified from original charters, sasines or probate records.

No record has yet been identified giving:

* James Glasgow's birth or death dates;
* his wife's identity;
* any children other than Jemima; or
* his title to Aghadonvane before the 1779 lease or its later succession.
