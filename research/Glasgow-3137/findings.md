## Current conclusion

An original 1766 religious census return directly establishes a Protestant householder named James Glasgow at Corvanaghan, Kildress parish. It does not supply his age, family, occupation, origin or later history. The profile's birth year 1750 is unsupported and should be replaced by “before 1766” or left unknown.

## Duplicate candidates

This record is presently the strongest documentary candidate for [Glasgow-1024](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1024), the later-reported “James Glasgow of Moneymore.” Corvanaghan is about nine miles from Moneymore and no post-1766 Irish record has been identified for this householder. The return gives none of the family or migration details needed to prove the identification.

[Glasgow-2988](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2988) uses this occurrence as a possible identity but has no record tying its estimated birth or children to the householder. [Glasgow-1970](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1970) has separate secondary evidence: an 1879 Hillsdale County history identifies James's wife as a Mitchell and says they migrated from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750 and settled near Cookstown. That family is compatible with the 1766 occurrence but is not proved to be the Corvanaghan household.
