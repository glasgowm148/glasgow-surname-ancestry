== Biography ==
'''Preferred working family, 17 October 2026:''' Andrew Glasgow-3163 and Elizat Stevinsoun-2 are now the selected '''uncertain local parents''' of Robert Glasgow-1156 through his possible identity with their testament-proved son Robert Glasgow-4006. Archibald Glasgow-1097 and Isobell Newlands-647 remain unselected uncertain/possible alternatives. The two Robert profiles remain separate; the recorded son’s documented parentage does not make the adult identification certain.<ref name="CorsounFamilyReview" />
'''Andrew Glasgow''' (written ''Andro Glasgw'') lived at a place read
uncertainly as “Corsoun,” in the parish read as “Calder Clair” or “Calder
Cleir,” within the sheriffdom of Edinburgh. His
testament testamentar and inventory was made on 6 February 1597 as written
(1597/8). The record says that he died the next day, 7 February, and was
confirmed on 10 July 1598.<ref name="Andrew1598">National Records of Scotland,
Edinburgh Commissary Court, CC8/8/32, images 123–124, testament testamentar and
inventory of Andro Glasgw, made 6 February 1597/8, death stated as 7 February,
confirmed 10 July 1598; working transcription of the original register images.
See also Scottish Record Society, ''Commissariot of Edinburgh: Register of
Testaments, 1514–1600'', [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso01scotuoft/page/n117/mode/1up
p. 108].</ref>

This was a ''testament testamentar'': Andrew made the will himself on the day
before his death. The introductory account, inventory and debts begin on image
123; the family provisions continue on image 124. A different testament begins
towards the foot of image 124 and is not part of Andrew's entry.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

=== Family and executors ===

Andrew's wife was '''Elizat Stevinsoun''' (Elizabeth Stevenson). The testament
expressly calls '''Robert Glasgow''' their son:

<blockquote>“Robart Glasgw his sone begottin be him vpoun the said Elizat his
wyff”</blockquote>

It also names Andrew's daughters '''Marion, Janet and Isobel'''. The dispositive
clause refers to “his said sone and his said [dochter… struck through] Marione,
Jonet, Isobell.”<ref name="Andrew1598" />

Andrew appointed Elizat and Robert to take charge of his goods, movable
property and debts receivable, and to pay his debts. He also named advisers
whose counsel they were to use. One readable adviser was '''James
Stevisoun'''. The shared surname makes him a useful lead for Elizat's family,
but the will does not call him her father, brother or other relative.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

=== Estate and financial network ===

The inventory describes a working rural household. It appears to include
three cows with calves, although the number is uncertain; two yearling cattle;
two horses valued at £10 each, £20 altogether; nine sheep; eight bolls of oats
in the barn and barnyard; four firlots of barley; household utensils and
furnishings; and Andrew's clothing. Several quantities, individual valuations
and the inventory total remain unresolved and should not be supplied from the
usual form of other testaments.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

The debts owed to Andrew include entries for '''John Mure''', an incompletely
identified widow of a deceased John, and '''James Stevisoun'''; some residences
and all of the relevant sums remain uncertain. The debts Andrew owed include a
payment associated with a brown horse, and less secure readings involving
Bartholomew Wilson, James Lyon and John Brown, David Lyon, James Stevisoun and
a laird whose designation may be Westhall. These are economic associates, not
proved relatives. The account then calculated the free estate, its division
into thirds and Andrew's own third, but the figures have not been securely
read.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

=== Distribution and land ===

Andrew directed that, once his debts had been paid, one third of the free
estate should go to Elizat and another third to Robert. His own third was to go
to a child if Elizat was pregnant by him. If she was not pregnant, that third
was to be divided equally among Robert, Marion, Janet and Isobel.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

Andrew provided that Elizat was to “bruik his [word unclear] land quhill his
said Robart cum to aige”: she was to possess or enjoy the land until Robert
reached legal age. The unclear word before ''land'' is not supplied here. This
is direct evidence of intended succession within Andrew's household, but the
record does not say from whom Andrew obtained the land or whether his interest
was heritable or tenanted.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

The will also allowed for the possibility that Elizat was pregnant: “incais his
said spous be with bairne to him.” That conditional wording does not establish
that another child was born. Andrew further directed that, if “Elizat his spous
do not hir pairt to his said bairnis,” she was to be removed from the
administration, protecting the children's interests.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

The closing clause records execution by a notary at Andrew's direction because
he could not write. The subsequent confirmation again names Elizat Stevinsoun
and Robert Glasgow. A further man, Johne Sandelandis, appears in the confirmation,
but the surviving reading does not establish a relationship to the family.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

=== Duddingston–Corsoun family review — 17 October 2026 ===

The preferred local working parents of the Duddingston Robert are now Andrew Glasgow and Elizat Stevinsoun, through possible identity with their recorded son Robert-4006. The accepted 1597/8 testament expressly names Robert as Andrew’s son by Elizat and makes provision until he reached age. A minor then can fit the adult generation of the Robert buried at Duddingston in 1647 and the probable father of James the Edinburgh weaver. The retained circa-1582 and 1597/before-1597 dates are estimates or bounds, not two exact birth records in conflict. On the maintained, queried Calder-area reading of Andrew’s residence, this offers a positive named-child family candidate in the relevant broad Lothian comparison. The earlier Archibald/Isobell pair has no record naming the Duddingston Robert as its child. Prefer the recorded Corsoun family as the uncertain route, with both former parents retained as alternatives.

The 1598 child and the Duddingston adult remain separate WikiTree profiles with an uncertain identity comparison; no merge is inferred. No holding, occupation, wife or named-relative bridge equates them, and the Corsoun/Calder place reading itself remains queried. A different local Robert, including the separate Ninian-son account Robert-1027, remains a comparison. Ninian’s son keeps his proved parents and is not conflated with Andrew’s named son. Elizat’s maternity is direct for the recorded child only; it is uncertain for the Duddingston adult because it depends on the same identity hypothesis. Archibald’s and Isobell’s biological parentage is not disproved. James-1150’s probable father assessment, all other selected families, and the adult’s existing vital fields remain unchanged. Two modern Robert accounts can represent one historical son; they are not independent evidence for two sons.

One paternal and one maternal working choice are replaced, affecting a child side of 64 pre-1850 profiles. The main view also reconciles already-completed profile redirects and the previously corrected public 1598 household; these are publication repairs, not new historical people or newly discovered records.

=== Calendar and locality ===

The manuscript year is 1597 in the Scottish record, while the confirmation was
in July 1598. Andrew's death should therefore be recorded as 7 February
1597/8, not 10 July 1598. The readings “Corsoun” and “Calder Clair/Cleir”
remain qualified until the handwriting is independently resolved.

The place has been rendered both Corsoun and Corstoun in derivative work. It
has not been safely equated with a modern settlement. The profile's structured
death place should reflect only the documented household locality, marked
uncertain, rather than the unsupported Kirknewton value.
