# Findings: Mary Glasgow (Glasgow-3167), wife of Robert Elder

Last updated: 2026-09-13

## Current conclusion

The 5 September master review confirms that Mary's original marriage was in the
Ballymoney registration district, not Ballymena. It directly proves Alexander
Glasgow, farmer, as her father. Her presence as informant at her mother Mary
Glasgow's 1892 Gortahar death supplies the strongest bridge to the later Elder
household.

The original 1871 civil marriage directly identifies Mary Glasgow, aged **28**, of
Lisnagaver as a daughter of **Alexander Glasgow, farmer**, and wife of Robert
Elder of Gortereghy, son of Nathaniel Elder. Her residence and Robert's later
succession to Alexander's valuation lot 25 make identification with
Alexander-951's family strong.

Robert's original death entry now closes the previous index-only gap. A married
farmer aged 64 died at Gortahar on 18 November 1894; Mary Elder of Gortahar,
described as a widow, informed the death. The register does not explicitly call
her his wife, but the exact place, compatible age and occupation, same-day widow
description, and her continuing Gortahar household make a competing identity
very unlikely.

Her stated age creates a substantial conflict with Maryan Glasgow, 17, recorded
in John `Glasgow-1022`'s household in 1851: Maryan would have been about 37 in
1871, rather than 28. Age alone does not conclusively exclude an identity when
reported ages conflict elsewhere in this family. The identification remains
possible but unproved; neither the marriage nor lot succession proves a
relationship between Alexander and John.

The original 1901 and 1911 Gortahar census forms make this the strongest located
continuation of the couple, though they do not independently name Mary's father.
Jane's original birth now supplies an independent civil-registration bridge:
**Jane Matilda Elder** was born at Gortahar on 16 September 1878 to Robert Elder
and Mary Elder, whose maiden surname was entered as **Glass**. The exact parents,
place and middle initial identify her as the later Jane M. Elder at high
confidence. Her birth fits the age 22 reported in 1901, but makes the age 26
reported in 1911 about six years too low.

The 1911 manuscript itself calls Robert J. Elder's two-year-old nephew **Samuel
Glasgow**. Samuel's original 1908 birth registration instead names him **Samuel
Elder**, son of Jane Elder, with no father recorded. These are conflicting
original records: the alternate surname is real reported usage, but it does not
identify Samuel's father or prove a Glasgow relationship.

The Mary Jane Elder who died at Lisnagaver in 1897 should not be merged with
Mary-3167 as established fact. If the Gortahar woman is the 1871 bride, Mary was
alive in 1911. The competing identity must be resolved through children's civil
births and death or probate evidence for the Gortahar couple.

## Corrected Gortahar household identification - 24 August 2026

Primary-index evidence substantially strengthens Mary's identification after her 1871 marriage. The 1901 census index records widowed farmer **Mary Elder**, aged 60, at Gortahar with children Mary, Robert and Jane.<ref>[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QPB2-KNM2 FamilySearch, 1901 Ireland census index], Mary Elder household, Gortahar.</ref> In 1911, Robert J. Elder headed the continuing Gortahar household with his widowed mother Mary, sister Jane and a nephew displayed as “Samuel Glasgow”.<ref>[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QYHR-52T2 FamilySearch, 1911 Ireland census index], Robert J. Elder household; [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QYHG-YHMM Mary Elder individual entry].</ref>

The original civil register instead records **Samuel Elder**, born 4 July 1908 at Gortahar to unmarried **Jane Elder**; the father's column is blank, and **Mary Elder**, Gortahar, was the informant.<ref>[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/birth_returns/births_1908/01647/1657700.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil birth register], Samuel Elder, 4 July 1908, Gortahar, Ballymoney district.</ref> This precisely fits the household nephew by age, residence and mother, but it records no Glasgow middle name or surname. Inspection of the original 1911 Form A now establishes that **Samuel Glasgow** is what the census household reported, not a derivative transcription mistake.<ref>[https://nationalarchives.ie/collections/search-the-census/census-record/#id=490671&c20_year=1911 National Archives of Ireland, 1911 census, Gortahar house 9], original Form A, image nai001389304.</ref> The two original records therefore preserve an unexplained alternate surname; neither names Samuel's father or proves that a Glasgow man was his father.

Together with Mary's 1871 marriage naming Alexander Glasgow, farmer, as her father and Mary Elder's presence at her mother Mary Glasgow's 1892 Gortahar death, the 1901-1911 household continuity makes the identification of the Gortahar widow with Alexander's daughter **high confidence**. Samuel's birth strengthens the internal Gortahar household continuity; the census adds direct evidence that the household reported his surname as Glasgow, but does not explain why or identify his father. The records do not identify Mary with the seventeen-year-old Maryan Glasgow in John Glasgow's Glenleslie household in 1851.

The original death return resolves the earlier index-only candidate. Robert Elder, married farmer, aged 64, died at Gortahar on 18 November 1894 of senile decay; Mary Elder, widow, of Gortahar was present and registered the death that day.<ref>[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/deaths_returns/deaths_1894/05952/4691448.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil death register], Robert Elder, Gortahar, 18 November 1894, entry 12.</ref> Although the return does not explicitly call Mary his wife, the exact household place, compatible age and occupation, her same-day widow description and the continuing widow household identify him as the 1871 husband at high confidence.

## Samuel Elder and Jane Elder - corrected 12 September 2026

The original 1911 census identifies **Jane M. Elder** as Robert J. Elder's unmarried sister, aged 26, and unequivocally records her nephew as **Samuel Glasgow**.<ref>[https://nationalarchives.ie/collections/search-the-census/census-record/#id=490671&c20_year=1911 National Archives of Ireland, 1911 census, Gortahar house 9], original Form A, image nai001389304.</ref> The original 1908 birth registration proves that Jane was Samuel's mother and records Mary Elder as informant, but names the child Samuel Elder and leaves his father blank. This is an original-to-original name conflict and unexplained alternate surname, not a transcription conflict or evidence identifying a Glasgow father.

The original 1901 Form A records Mary Elder, widowed farmer, with unmarried children Mary, Robert and Jane.<ref>[https://nationalarchives.ie/collections/search-the-census/census-record/#id=4575739&c20_year=1901 National Archives of Ireland, 1901 census, Gortahar house 7], original Form A, image nai000297950.</ref> Mary and Robert track strongly into 1911, but Jane's stated age changes from 22 to 26 across the decade; the identity is strong but the age discrepancy remains explicit.

The original civil birth register resolves Jane's identity and chronology. **Jane Matilda Elder** was born at Gortahar on 16 September 1878 to farmer Robert Elder and Mary Elder, with Mary's maiden surname entered as **Glass**; Robert, her father, reported the birth.<ref>[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/birth_returns/births_1878/02945/2078820.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil birth register], Jane Matilda Elder, 16 September 1878, Gortahar, entry 263.</ref> The exact parents, place and middle initial identify her with the later Jane M. Elder at high confidence. The date is consistent with age 22 in 1901 but makes the stated age 26 in 1911 too low by about six years. “Glass” is retained as the register's spelling and assessed as a variant or shortening compatible with Mary's documented Glasgow maiden name; it is not silently normalised in the transcription.

The age-compatible Ballymoney birth sweep did not find registrations for the other two census children. The inspected Mary Elder entries for 1873, 1874 and 1876, and Robert Elder entries for 1873–1876, name other parent couples. This is a bounded negative, not proof that the Gortahar births were unregistered: census ages can be inaccurate and indexing or registration gaps remain possible.

FamilySearch also incorrectly normalises Samuel's Ballymoney registration place to County Wicklow. The original register places his birth at Gortahar in the Ballymoney, County Antrim district and controls both the place and recorded name.
