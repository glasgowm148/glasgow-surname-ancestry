== Biography ==

'''Jonet (Janet) Glasgow''' was named as a daughter of [[Glasgow-3163|Andrew Glasgow of uncertain Corsoun]] in his testament, made on 6 February 1597/8. The provision names “his said sone and his said [dochter… struck through] Marione, Jonet, Isobell.” It proves a daughter Jonet was alive by that date. Her birth date and place are not recorded.<ref name="Andrew1598">National Records of Scotland, Edinburgh Commissary Court, CC8/8/32, images 123–124, testament testamentar and inventory of Andro Glasgw, made 6 February 1597/8, death stated as 7 February, confirmed 10 July 1598; corrected working transcription of the original register images. Printed index: Scottish Record Society, ''Commissariot of Edinburgh: Register of Testaments, 1514–1600'', [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso01scotuoft/page/n117/mode/1up p. 108].</ref>

The testament expressly calls [[Glasgow-4006|Robert]] Andrew's son by [[Stevinsoun-2|Elizat Stevinsoun]]. It does not separately identify Elizat as Jonet's mother; her maternal relationship remains uncertain.<ref name="Andrew1598" /> Jonet's named sisters were Marion and Isobel.

A Jonet Glasgow married [[Ramsy-9|Martin Ramsy]] at Mid Calder in 1624.<ref name="Ramsy1624">“Scotland, Marriages, 1561–1910,” FamilySearch, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XY9X-4PR Jonet Glasgow and Martin Ramsy, 1624], Mid Calder, Midlothian, Scotland.</ref> The marriage index names no father, sibling or Corsoun property.

== Research Notes ==

The confident father status applies to Andrew's testament-named daughter represented by this merged profile; the identification with the 1624 Mid Calder bride remains probable.<ref name="Andrew1598" />

The former Glasgow-4096 represented Andrew's testament-named daughter and this older profile represented the Mid Calder bride. They were merged as the leading identification: a woman of this uncommon surname and given name was recorded in the Calder district in 1598, and a woman of the same name married in the same district in 1624. The marriage does not directly identify Andrew's daughter, so the cross-record identity remains probable rather than proved. Seek the original marriage page, witnesses, succession deeds or a Ramsy family record linking the bride to Corsoun.

[[Glasgow-991|James Glasgow]] was previously attached as this bride's uncertain father from nearby Mid Calder baptisms in 1605, 1610 and 1616. Those entries do not name this Jonet as a daughter. Andrew's testament supplies the direct father statement for the woman represented by the merged profile, so James remains a superseded candidate, not a second documented father.

The older bride profile's circa-1600 birth estimate is too late for a woman already named in 1598. A birth before 6 February 1597/8 is the only supported boundary. Mid Calder and Corsoun are event and family localities, not documented birthplaces.

The [[Glasgow-3262|Jonat Glasgow]] named with [[Menzeis-28|David Menzeis]] as parents of [[Menzeis-27|Agnes Menzeis]] in a 7 December 1609 Mid Calder baptism is another possible identity for this Jonet.<ref name="Agnes1609">National Records of Scotland, Scotland's People, Old Parish Registers, Mid Calder, baptism of Agnes Menzeis, 7 December 1609, OPR 694/10/15; the indexed parents are David Menzeis and Jonat Glasgow. The entry is cited on the linked Menzeis family profiles; the original image has not been inspected for this comparison.</ref> The matching name and parish, followed by the 1624 Ramsy marriage, permit one woman who remarried. No reviewed record establishes that David had died or that the Ramsy bride was his widow, so the two profiles remain separate. Jonat Menzeis has no structured parents on WikiTree; Ninian Glasgow and Jonet Meik are a separate proposed placement, not evidence for this identity. An original marriage entry or probate record naming a former spouse would test the comparison.
