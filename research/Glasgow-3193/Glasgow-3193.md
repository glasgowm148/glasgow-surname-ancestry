== Biography ==
'''Ayrshire family reconstruction, 17 October 2026:''' [[Glasgow-1603|Hugh Glasgow]] is now placed as John’s '''uncertain working son''', on locality and generational reasoning. The 1571 deed does not name Hugh; grandson, collateral and other local fathers remain possible.<ref name="AyrshireHughFamily" />
'''John Glasgow''' was a son of [[Glasgow-1168|James Glasgow elder]] of Corsinkell, as directly stated in a 9 February 1571 letter of reversion. The document names both John and [[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow younger]] as James elder's sons and occupiers of the 40-shilling land of Corsinkell.<ref name="NRS1571">National Records of Scotland, Charter Chest of the Earls of Glencairn, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F5%2F59 GD39/5/59], letters of reversion concerning the 40-shilling land of Corsinkell, 9 February 1571. Official catalogue entry inspected 30 August 2026; the original is open but held off site and no manuscript image is available online.</ref> The document does not name their mother. John was alive on that date; his death is not recorded here. The confident father status applies to the record-defined Corsinkell son in this merged profile; it does not resolve the separate attribution of the Kilwinning appearances below.

The Cunninghame teind list for 1559–60 names a John Glasgow at Burrowland and names John Glasgow jointly with Agnes Wilson at Dalgarven, both in the Kilwinning district.<ref name="Teinds1559">Margaret H. B. Sanderson, ''The People of Sixteenth Century Ayrshire'', ''Ayrshire Collections'', vol. 14, no. 8 (Ayrshire Archaeological and Natural History Society, 1987), appendix “The Cunninghame Teind Lists of 1559-60,” [https://aanhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/people-of-ayrshire.pdf#page=38 p. 333]. Sanderson cites Scottish Record Office, Register of Acts and Decreets, vol. 22, fols. 102v-167v. See also [https://www.kilwinningheritage.org.uk/office/resources/documents/a-short-history-of-kilwinning-parish-2nd-ed.pdf ''A Short History of Kilwinning Parish''].</ref> Sanderson notes that a tenant could hold more than one property. These appearances may describe this John, but the list does not state his father and the Corsinkell document does not state his Kilwinning holdings. The combined regional pattern, rare name, overlapping period, and lack of a better identified local John make the identification probable rather than certain.

[[WIlson-130143|Agnes Wilson]] remains an uncertain spouse candidate. The teind list proves joint occupancy but does not call her John's wife. His birth before about 1540 is an adult-status estimate, and the earlier profile's Glasgow, Lanarkshire association was not based on a birth record. Burrowland, Dalgarven and Corsinkell are documented or proposed residences, not proved birth or death places.

== Research Notes ==
=== Preferred Ayrshire family — 17 October 2026 ===

Prefer [[Glasgow-3193|John Glasgow]] of the Corsankell/Kilwinning family as [[Glasgow-1603|Hugh Glasgow]]’s uncertain working father. This materializes the leading Ayrshire placement already argued in the maintained five-generation map. The 1571 Corsinkell deed expressly names John as James elder’s son; the earlier Kilwinning tenant appearances are attributed to him as probable. Hugh’s surviving profile account describes an Ayrshire progenitor, his son’s account is centred on Kilwinning, and the following households lie in Kilwinning, Ayr and Kilbirnie. These successive local settings favour the documented nearby family over the existing Mid Calder/Inveresk father attachment, which depends on an online tree and has no movement, household or property bridge. A John-to-Hugh-to-John sequence is compatible supporting context, not a naming rule. Father is selected as a revisable one-generation model, rather than leaving the previously preferred local route only in prose.

Hugh remains a tree-derived generation: no inspected contemporary record securely identifies him, names his father or establishes his own c.1600 birth. John’s before-1540 boundary means a birth for Hugh near 1600 would make him an older father, around 60 or more; a grandson or collateral position may fit better. Neither date is changed to fit. An earlier Hugh birth is possible but is not adopted as a new estimate, and no missing person is invented. The 1571 John is documented as a son, while his additional Kilwinning identity is only probable. John’s brother [[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow]], another local Glasgow family, an omitted generation and the former [[Glasgow-991|James Glasgow]] placement remain alternatives. This is stronger locality reasoning, not a probable or proved father assessment. The 1677 deed-index Hugh, preacher of the gospel, remains an unresolved identity comparison and supplies no evidence for this father choice.

The existing 137-profile pre-1850 working child side is rerouted from James of Mid Calder to John of Corsankell; descendants retain every earlier identity and relationship qualification. No mother, spouse, merge, new person or changed vital field is selected. Hugh-to-[[Glasgow-1604|John]] and the minister’s separate father and duplicate questions are unchanged. Shared Y-DNA can motivate the extended-family model but cannot identify this father. Both proposed fathers already belong to the same working component: cohort membership, working component and pair coverage, supported connectivity and confidently marked connectivity remain unchanged. This is a better-supported placement within the working tree, not a newly connected component or confidence upgrade. No new source inspection or live WikiTree save is claimed.

A later-sixteenth-century son of the earlier adult could have been a working sailor and father of two daughters by 1636. The choice gives the Saltcoats household a provisional place in an existing local John line, using geography and a feasible generation. It is a low-confidence selection, not a named parent clause or an identification of the two Johns.

No source names the sailor’s parents or age. His former c.1600 birth was an unsupported creation estimate; it is not replaced by an invented older birth to fit this hypothesis.
