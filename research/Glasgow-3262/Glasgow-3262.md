=== Selected local working father, 15 October 2026 ===
[[Glasgow-3163|Andrew Glasgow of Corsoun, Calder]] is the selected '''uncertain, low-confidence''' father in the local research tree. Jonat is the mother in a Mid Calder baptism in 1609. The existing comparison prefers her possible identity with Jonet Glasgow who married Martin Ramsy in the same parish in 1624, fifteen years later. That Jonet is already placed as Andrew’s testament daughter. Select Andrew as Jonat’s uncertain father through this local identity reconstruction. Her estimated 1585 generation fits Andrew’s household; the same name, parish and feasible remarriage sequence favour this model over the more distant Ninian household.

This combines two uncertain identity steps: the 1609 mother with the 1624 bride, and the bride with Andrew’s testament daughter. David Menzeis’s death and the bride’s widowhood are not recorded in the material reviewed. Jonat and Jonet may be different women; retain both profiles and do not count an identity merge. Ninian Glasgow remains a competing father hypothesis. No mother is attached.

This supersedes earlier advice to leave the comparison outside the local tree. It does not name a parent supplied by a new record or authorise a live parent attachment. No mother is inferred. [https://glasgow.phenotype.dev/branches/early-scottish-records/#early-collateral-joins Full reasoning and alternatives].

Agnes Menzeis, daughter of David Menzeis and Jonat Glasgow, was baptised at Mid Calder on 7 December 1609.<ref>Scotland’s People, Mid Calder OPR 694/10/15, as previously cited in the family research.</ref> A Jonet Glasgow married Martin Ramsy there on 23 March 1624.<ref>Scotland’s People OPR 694/10/47; FamilySearch [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XY9X-4PR marriage index XY9X-4PR]. This does not establish widowhood or identify the bride as the 1609 mother.</ref> Andrew’s 1598 testament names a daughter Jonet; her identification with the Ramsy bride remains a separate inference.<ref>National Records of Scotland, CC8/8/32, images 123–124, Andrew Glasgow testament, 1598; [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/search-records/legal-records/wills Scotland’s People wills collection], corrected existing family reading.</ref>
