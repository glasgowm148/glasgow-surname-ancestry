## Current conclusion

The leading new identity comparison is with [Jonet Glasgow, wife of Martin
Ramsy (Glasgow-3183)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3183). This
profile's 1609 Mid Calder baptism names Jonat Glasgow with David Menzeis;
the 1624 Mid Calder marriage names a Jonet Glasgow with Martin Ramsy. One
woman could have remarried in the intervening fifteen years, but no reviewed
record proves David's death or calls the Ramsy bride his widow. Keep the
profiles separate while testing that first marriage's end. If the women are
one, the separate probable identification of Glasgow-3183 with Andrew of
Corsoun's testament daughter would also affect this profile's parentage;
that is a chain of inferences, not a proved father link.

[Ninian Glasgow (Glasgow-3188)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3188)
and [Jonet Meik (Meik-14)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Meik-14) remain an
alternative working parent pair, without a direct relationship statement.

Ninian and Jonet are the correct generation; the daughter's forename `Jonat`
repeats her proposed mother's name. Jonat and David Menzeis had daughter Agnes
baptised at Mid Calder on 7 December 1609. Ninian's possible son William later
lived at Leith, supplying a credible family route east from Glasgow into the
Lothians. Jonet Meik's 1607 testament directly names son Robert and a Marion
Glasgow legatee, but does not call Jonat a daughter; that omission prevents a
confident edge but does not prove she was not an older or married daughter.

Assessment: **identity and parentage unresolved**. The Mid Calder continuity
is a stronger testable lead than the earlier name-and-craft based Ninian/Jonet
placement, but neither relationship is ready for a structured parent link.

**Live-state check, 27 September 2026:** Jonat's WikiTree profile has no
