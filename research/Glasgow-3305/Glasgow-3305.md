```wikitext
== Biography ==

'''James Glasgow''' has been proposed as a son of [[Glasgow-1024|James Glasgow of Moneymore]] and [[Harkness-1149|Mary Harkness]], and as a brother of [[Glasgow-12|Robert]], [[Glasgow-649|Arthur]] and [[Glasgow-1543|Joseph Glasgow]]. No located contemporary record establishes his birth, parents, siblings or residence. The profile therefore remains unsourced.

The estimate that he was born about 1740 at Moneymore appears to be a research inference. A later genealogy describes Robert as a son of "James Glasgow of Moneymore" who emigrated with three brothers and one sister, but the accessible catalogue summary does not name those siblings or say that another son James remained in Ireland.<ref name="Faris">David Faris, ''The Glasgow Family of Adams County, Ohio'' (1990), [https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567 WorldCat catalogue record]. The book and its citations have not yet been inspected.</ref>

=== Contemporary Irish records ===

A 1775 petition associated with the Cookstown dissenting congregation includes a signer named '''James Glasgow'''. Its archival reference is PRONI T/808/15307. The transcription supplies no age, townland, occupation, spouse or relationship, so it does not uniquely identify the signer as this profile.<ref name="Cookstown1775">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, T/808/15307, 1775 Cookstown congregation petition; [https://cotyroneireland.com/misc/cookstown3.html published transcription].</ref>

The 1766 religious return for Derryloran parish lists '''two''' householders called "Jas. Glasgo" at Drumearn, as well as other Glasgow households nearby. Either man, another local James, or this profile could have signed in 1775. The return gives no ages or family relationships.<ref name="Derryloran1766">National Archives of Ireland, IHP/1/665, religious return for Derryloran parish, 1766; [https://virtualtreasury.ie/item/NAI-IHP-1-665 Virtual Treasury catalogue record].</ref>

Cookstown and Drumearn evidence is not evidence of residence at Moneymore. The separate 1766 return for Moneymore and Ballygrooby names neither a Glasgow nor a Harkness household head.<ref name="Moneymore1766">[https://www.cotyroneireland.com/census/moneymore.html Religious Census of Moneymore, Ballygrooby and Artrea, 1766], transcription.</ref>

An 1807 probate-index occurrence names '''James Glasgow of Drumcarn, County Tyrone'''. The chronology and district make it relevant to this profile, but the surviving index gives no age, occupation or relationship and does not uniquely identify him.<ref name="Drumcarn1807">[https://apps.proni.gov.uk/ProniNames_IE/SearchPage.aspx PRONI Names, pre-1858 wills and administrations, James Glasgow of Drumcarn, County Tyrone, 1807].</ref>

=== Proposed emigrant family ===

Robert Glasgow's 1834 pension declaration states that he was born in County Derry in 1749 and migrated in 1769, but names neither parents nor siblings.<ref name="RobertPension">Robert Glasgow, Revolutionary War pension application R4057, [https://revwarapps.org/r4057.pdf transcription and file extract].</ref>

Other evidence connects Robert, Arthur and Joseph as a collateral group. Joseph's 1831 will expressly calls Arthur his deceased brother. None of the located records identifies this James as another brother.<ref name="JosephWill">Wythe County, Virginia, Will Book 4, will of Joseph Glasgow, 1831; Library of Virginia microfilm reel 14, [https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/ccmf/VA/VA305 catalogue].</ref>

=== Hawthorne will theory ===

The reported claim that a 1766 Hawthorne will named a nephew James Glasgow has not been verified from an image, page or complete abstract. Arthur Vicars's prerogative-will index instead lists '''James Hawthorne, merchant of Downpatrick, in 1762'''; it has no corresponding 1766 entry for him.<ref name="Vicars">Arthur Vicars, ''Index to the Prerogative Wills of Ireland, 1536–1810'' (1897), Hawthorne entries, [https://archive.org/stream/indextoprerogati00vica/indextoprerogati00vica_djvu.txt text edition].</ref>

The date discrepancy does not disprove the theory, since an old reference might confuse a will date, probate date, court or spelling. Until the complete Betham abstract is located, it establishes neither a Glasgow nephew nor this profile's identity.

No new profile should be created for the 1775 signer unless a record supplies a discriminating fact showing that he cannot be one of the existing local candidates.
```

## Required corrections

- Remove the statement that Moneymore borders County Down. County Londonderry
  and County Down are not adjacent.
- Recast the 1766 Hawthorne will identification as an unverified possibility.
  The current profile provides neither the abstract nor an archive reference.
- State that the 1740 birth, Moneymore residence, parents and claim that James
  remained in Ireland have not been independently documented.
- Do not use this profile as proof that the emigrant sibling group left a
  brother James in Ireland.

## Paste-ready Research Notes

```text
No contemporary record currently cited identifies this James. His estimated 1740 birth, Moneymore residence, attached parents and identification as the sibling who remained in Ireland derive from the biography of [[Glasgow-12|Robert Glasgow]], where the sibling statement is itself uncited. David Faris's 1990 genealogy says in its public catalogue summary that Robert emigrated with three brothers and one sister, but that summary does not name the siblings or identify another James who remained in Ireland.<ref>David Faris, ''The Glasgow Family of Adams County, Ohio'' (Baltimore: Gateway Press, 1990), WorldCat catalogue record 22731567, https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567 (accessed 21 July 2026).</ref>

This profile has also been proposed as the James Glasgow called a nephew in a 1766 Hawthorne will abstract. The complete abstract, archive reference and identifiers needed to distinguish that man from other contemporary James Glasgows have not been supplied. The identification remains unproved. Moneymore is in County Londonderry and does not border County Down.
```
