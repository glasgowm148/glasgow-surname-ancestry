## Current conclusion

PRONI `D300/1/5/49` contains a grant of probate and copy will for William Glasgow
of Larne, with the will dated 6 December 1815. This is the highest-value surviving
record for testing William's wife, children, occupation and relationship to the
older Larne household. The catalogue description alone does not expose its clauses.

A 28 November 1804 lease to William Glasgow of Larne, farmer, is a strong identity
candidate but contains no exposed family statement. Parentage under James-1509 and
Janet Shutter remains unproved until the register, monument or will wording is read.

A published 1899 transcription of the Larne Parish Churchyard monument directly
proves William's wife Elizabeth and daughters Jane Shutter, Eliza and Mary. The
distinctive name **Jane Shutter Glasgow** makes the proposed descent from James
Glasgow and Janet Shutter substantially stronger than a naming-pattern guess;
the parentage is **probable**, while the monument relationships within William's
own household are direct evidence.

An 1824 renewal of a Larne lease first made to William and Hugh Glasgow in 1736
names the new tenant as Elizabeth Glasgow, widow of Larne. She is a strong
candidate for this William's reported wife, but the catalogue does not name her
late husband. Read together with William's surviving 1815 will, the renewal is a
specific route to proving or rejecting the proposed descent from Hugh-1503.

catalogue descriptions are public, but the will clauses and renewal recitals

PRONI's [digitised-records inventory](https://www.proni.gov.uk/publications/digitised-and-born-digital-records-available-ecatalogue).

New evidence reviewed 4 October 2026: Inspected April–December 1792 Northern Star advertisements name Wm. Glasgow as master of the brig William on Belfast voyages to Rotterdam, Oporto and Bordeaux. They substantiate a real named captain corresponding to the inherited occupational suggestion, but do not prove that he was this Larne household’s William. The profile’s dated family monument and 1815 will remain the appropriate identity tests.
