== Research Notes ==
=== Whitecastle and Quothquan review — 17 October 2026 ===

'''Whitecastle comparison, 17 October 2026:''' the newly checked July 1672 legal occurrence supplies a probable Clydesdale setting for James-1376. He is now a leading possible identity comparison for the later Lindsaylands weaver. The 1680 identity and this profile’s John-1086 father remain uncertain; two James profiles do not prove two sons.

The printed decree in Robert Wodrow’s 1721 History, volume I, appendix no. XLVII, pp.133–134, is dated 27 July 1672 in both its heading and Latin opening. It names “James Glasgow in Whitecastle” among people imprisoned for refusing the oath. The monetary fines there belong to Mr James Duncan and the Countess of Wigtoun, not to James Glasgow. William Crookshank’s 1751 summary, volume I p.285, places the same named group under 24 July 1672; preserve that day discrepancy and prefer the printed decree’s 27 July for this source account. Neither source supports the old 1670 date. The two books recount the same proceeding and are not independent evidence of two imprisonments.

The surrounding Biggar/Boghall group names men in Biggar, Carwood, Edmonstoun, Worslyd and Carstairs. This favours the Whitecastle in Libberton parish over a location guessed merely from general Covenanter activity. J. L. Davidson’s published article title locates West Whitecastle Farm in Libberton, Lanarkshire (Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland 108, p.383; title checked in the journal’s search-indexed contents, full article not inspected). This supports the Libberton place identification, in the same parish setting as Quothquan and near Biggar’s Lindsaylands; it does not identify a seventeenth-century occupier. This is a probable interpretation of James’s residence, not a parish supplied literally by the decree. White Castle in Garvald and Bara, East Lothian, is a real alternative place. The decree also deals with a Perthshire group; document order alone does not put every accused person in Clydesdale.

Select John Glasgow-1086 of Quothquan as the uncertain local working father of James Glasgow-1376. The choice is conditional on the Whitecastle man’s possible identity with James Glasgow-3332, provisionally compared with the weaver at Back of Lindsaylands whose testament was indexed in 1680. The newly supported probable Clydesdale setting supplies a positive regional bridge that the earlier review lacked. The same name, neighbouring Libberton/Biggar households and compatible adult dates favour that existing local account as the leading identity comparison. The 1690 apprentice entry names a deceased James Glasgow, weaver in Lindsayland, as father of the apprentice; that trade and place probably identify the 1680 testator, not the Whitecastle man directly. John is already the uncertain father attached to James-3332. Carry that family placement across the possible identity at its actual uncertainty, rather than inventing another father. John’s possible 1608 childhood would fit a son born in the 1630s–1650s; neither James birth estimate is a recorded age. This is a selected, revisable family hypothesis, not a parent clause in the 1672 decree.

There are two dependent gaps: Whitecastle James is not proved to be the Lindsaylands weaver, and John’s paternity of James-3332 is itself unproved. John’s 1685 Quothquan testament names his widow but no child; the absence of James, if dead by 1680, would not exclude this model. James-3332’s possible identity with the 1624 weaver would invalidate this particular later-life route, because the 1624 decedent cannot be the man present in 1672. James-1150, the Edinburgh weaver, remains an alternative adult identification for the later weaver rather than being excluded from an unsupported birth estimate; another local James or a different kinship degree remains possible. The old Marion-baptism role error supplies no positive parentage for James-1376, and the Duffe household James-2682 is not merged. The 1738 Edinburgh Castle lead is unselected and incompatible with a death by 1680 if this weaver identity is right. No 1680 original was newly read: the maintained case has only the supplied wills index, and no linked manuscript or transcription. Neither a marriage nor a mother is supplied. Jonet Kinnie, John’s widow, is not automatically James’s mother. Keep both James profiles separate; they may represent one man, not two proved brothers.

=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

James [[Glasgow-3332]] is the selected uncertain working father of Robert [[Glasgow-3407]], who married Katherine Wilson at Carnwath in 1713 and is associated with son John [[Glasgow-2870]] at Libberton in 1722. This is conditional on James representing the weaver at Back of Lindsaylands whose testament was indexed in 1680. The adjacent Biggar, Libberton and Carnwath family setting supplies a specific local comparison. A James born around the inherited 1630/1634 estimates could father Robert in the 1660s or 1670s; a Robert born in the 1670s could marry in 1713 and father John in 1722 at ordinary adult ages. This provisional older-generation route is preferred to making the two overlapping Libberton Roberts sons of the same Johne. No record names Robert as the weaver’s child. Robert’s current circa-1690 birth estimate would be too late for a father dead by 1680; an earlier birth is required if this hypothesis is right. That estimate is unsupported, but the required revision remains a limitation, not evidence for the proposed father.

The 1680 index describes James as a weaver in the Back of Lindsaylands, parish of Biggar, with indexed testament date 10 November 1680, Lanark Commissary Court CC14/5/10, TD. That is a registration date, not an exact death date. The original has not been read and supplies no reviewed Robert clause. The 1690 Edinburgh apprenticeship names a deceased father James Glasgow, weaver in Lindsayland; the matching trade and locality make him a probable instance of the indexed weaver, but identification with [[Glasgow-3332]] remains only possible. The apprentice generation is another possible intermediary for the later family, not an additional person invented to complete a pedigree. James [[Glasgow-3332]]’s existing parentage, the separate 1648 Johne child identity, and the competing 1624 Quothquan-weaver identity retain their previous grades. The new father selection cannot itself prove any of those identities or be used circularly to reject the 1624 alternative. This particular parent model depends on the later weaver identity; the 1624 decedent would be an implausibly early direct father of this 1713–1722 household.

The Carnwath marriage citation gives Robert and Katherine Wilson in 1713; the captured 1722 Libberton baptism citation names Robert and Katharin Wilson as John’s parents. Those index citations define the proposed Wilson household but supply no paternal ancestry or age for Robert. The neighbouring Robert [[Glasgow-4168]] account combines Katherin Gibson in 1718 and Katherine Watson in 1720 and 1726. The Wilson event in 1722 falls between the Watson events. Consequently, a simple sequence of remarriages does not reconcile all maternal surnames. Transcription differences, record misassignment, overlapping families, or one man across some entries remain possible. No woman is equated with another, and no new marriage or mother is assigned. The two Robert accounts are retained separately; shared parish and forename do not prove identity.

Johne [[Glasgow-3093]] remains an unselected alternative father for the Carnwath Robert, particularly if Robert [[Glasgow-3407]] is eventually identified with the Gibson/Watson Robert [[Glasgow-4168]], whose selected father is already Johne. Johne’s birth around 1648 could fit fatherhood around 1690; however, no later record follows him from the Mid Calder child entry to Libberton. Giving him both Roberts would otherwise require two living sons with the same forename or an unproved identity between the accounts. The present choice avoids building that assumption into the default model. If James [[Glasgow-3332]]’s two father assignments and the Johne [[Glasgow-3093]] to Robert [[Glasgow-4168]] choice are right, Robert [[Glasgow-3407]] would be the latter Robert’s paternal uncle. That is a consequence of the conditional model, not independent evidence of kinship. Another unrecorded local father and a younger Lindsaylands generation remain possible. Captain Robert [[Glasgow-2867]] also remains a surviving identity comparison; his unsupported imported family is not a concrete exclusion, and no maritime or probate bridge identifies the Carnwath man with him.
