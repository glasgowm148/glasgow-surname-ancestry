# Findings: Glasgow-3332

## Current conclusion

Glasgow-3332 cannot be the James whose testament was recorded in 1624. He is a
chronological candidate for the 1680 Back of Lindsaylands weaver, but no reviewed
record joins the identities. His asserted family depends on the separate 1662
original. The 1685 Quothquan testament names no child and does not prove James
as a son of [[Glasgow-1086]].

## Next records — a son of the Lindsaylands weaver apprenticed at Edinburgh, 7 May 1690

On 7 May 1690 James Glasgow, **son of the deceased James Glasgow, weaver in Lindsayland**, was entered as apprentice to William Brown, cordiner and tanner in Portsburgh, burgess of Edinburgh; the entry was scored out (Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., *Register of Edinburgh Apprentices, 1666–1700*, Scottish Record Society, 1929, printed p. 37, [PDF](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/edinburgh_apprentices_~_1666_to_1700_~_1929.pdf); Findmypast transcript [SCOT/EDINBURGH/APPRENTICES/0005159/1](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FEDINBURGH%2FAPPRENTICES%2F0005159%2F1&tab=this)). Seen as a printed-edition transcription; the original register was not inspected. The deceased father is probably the James Glasgow, weaver at Back of Lindsaylands, Biggar parish, whose testament was registered at Lanark on 10 November 1680 (index entry above). A creation draft for the apprentice is held in `research/Glasgow-4131/`.

This bears on this profile only if Glasgow-3332 is the Lindsaylands weaver, which is unproved (chronology only). If a record bridges the two, the 1690 entry would add a son James. Grade: unresolved; no relationship is asserted for Glasgow-3332. The 1680 testament itself should be read for named children.
