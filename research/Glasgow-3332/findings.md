## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

Select Johne Glasgow-3093, indexed at Mid Calder in 1648 as son of Jamis Glasgow, as the very low-confidence working father of Robert Glasgow-4168, father at Libberton in 1718–1726. The preferred local route runs through Johne’s existing uncertain James-3332 father identity and that James’s possible identification with the Lindsaylands weaver indexed in 1680. Lindsaylands and Quothquan/Libberton supply the adjacent Lanarkshire family setting; Mid Calder is a separate parish and no migration is proved. A Johne born around 1648 could father Robert in the 1680s–1690s, allowing ordinary adult fatherhood at the 1718 baptism. This generation is preferred over assigning Robert directly to the older weaver, who was dead by 1680 if that identity is correct. It is a specific family hypothesis using recorded men, not a father clause in Robert’s records.

Johne’s only reviewed record is the 1648 child entry; no later occupation, residence, spouse or death identifies him with Libberton. The Jamis-to-Glasgow-3332 identity and the latter’s Lindsaylands identity are both uncertain. Its inherited circa 1634 birth would make him only about fourteen at Johne’s 1648 event if literal; that unsourced estimate must not establish or exclude parentage. The 1648 source names Jamis, not John-1084. The known parish association therefore belongs to a conditional family chain, not to an independently followed migration. James-3332 himself remains an alternative father if Robert was born before 1680 and fathered the later children at an older age. The son James apprenticed in Edinburgh in 1690, explicitly son of deceased James the Lindsaylands weaver, is a genuine alternative intermediate father or uncle; his existing held draft is retained, and no unrecorded generation is invented. The 1624 Quothquan weaver is a separate death account; its early death makes direct paternity of a 1718–1726 father much less plausible. Robert’s merged Gibson/Watson household remains a working combined account, not proof of remarriage or identity between the two women. No mother is selected for Robert, and no vital date is changed to fit the route.

This adds an uncertain grandson through the existing Johne account. It does not prove the Lindsaylands identity, turn the 1648 father into John-1084, or merge the 1624 and 1680 decedents.

## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

James Glasgow-3332 is the selected uncertain working father of Robert Glasgow-3407, who married Katherine Wilson at Carnwath in 1713 and is associated with son John Glasgow-2870 at Libberton in 1722. This is conditional on James representing the weaver at Back of Lindsaylands whose testament was indexed in 1680. The adjacent Biggar, Libberton and Carnwath family setting supplies a specific local comparison. A James born around the inherited 1630/1634 estimates could father Robert in the 1660s or 1670s; a Robert born in the 1670s could marry in 1713 and father John in 1722 at ordinary adult ages. This provisional older-generation route is preferred to making the two overlapping Libberton Roberts sons of the same Johne. No record names Robert as the weaver’s child. Robert’s current circa-1690 birth estimate would be too late for a father dead by 1680; an earlier birth is required if this hypothesis is right. That estimate is unsupported, but the required revision remains a limitation, not evidence for the proposed father.

The 1680 index describes James as a weaver in the Back of Lindsaylands, parish of Biggar, with indexed testament date 10 November 1680, Lanark Commissary Court CC14/5/10, TD. That is a registration date, not an exact death date. The original has not been read and supplies no reviewed Robert clause. The 1690 Edinburgh apprenticeship names a deceased father James Glasgow, weaver in Lindsayland; the matching trade and locality make him a probable instance of the indexed weaver, but identification with Glasgow-3332 remains only possible. The apprentice generation is another possible intermediary for the later family, not an additional person invented to complete a pedigree. James Glasgow-3332’s existing parentage, the separate 1648 Johne child identity, and the competing 1624 Quothquan-weaver identity retain their previous grades. The new father selection cannot itself prove any of those identities or be used circularly to reject the 1624 alternative. This particular parent model depends on the later weaver identity; the 1624 decedent would be an implausibly early direct father of this 1713–1722 household.

The Carnwath marriage citation gives Robert and Katherine Wilson in 1713; the captured 1722 Libberton baptism citation names Robert and Katharin Wilson as John’s parents. Those index citations define the proposed Wilson household but supply no paternal ancestry or age for Robert. The neighbouring Robert Glasgow-4168 account combines Katherin Gibson in 1718 and Katherine Watson in 1720 and 1726. The Wilson event in 1722 falls between the Watson events. Consequently, a simple sequence of remarriages does not reconcile all maternal surnames. Transcription differences, record misassignment, overlapping families, or one man across some entries remain possible. No woman is equated with another, and no new marriage or mother is assigned. The two Robert accounts are retained separately; shared parish and forename do not prove identity.

Johne Glasgow-3093 remains an unselected alternative father for the Carnwath Robert, particularly if Robert Glasgow-3407 is eventually identified with the Gibson/Watson Robert Glasgow-4168, whose selected father is already Johne. Johne’s birth around 1648 could fit fatherhood around 1690; however, no later record follows him from the Mid Calder child entry to Libberton. Giving him both Roberts would otherwise require two living sons with the same forename or an unproved identity between the accounts. The present choice avoids building that assumption into the default model. If James Glasgow-3332’s two father assignments and the Johne Glasgow-3093 to Robert Glasgow-4168 choice are right, Robert Glasgow-3407 would be the latter Robert’s paternal uncle. That is a consequence of the conditional model, not independent evidence of kinship. Another unrecorded local father and a younger Lindsaylands generation remain possible. Captain Robert Glasgow-2867 also remains a surviving identity comparison; his unsupported imported family is not a concrete exclusion, and no maritime or probate bridge identifies the Carnwath man with him.
