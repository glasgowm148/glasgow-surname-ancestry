== Biography ==
Robert Glasgow was born about 1690, probably in Scotland. He married Katherine Wilson in 1713 at Carnwath, Lanark, Scotland.<ref>Scotland, Marriages, 1561-1910. Indexing Project (Batch) Number: M11632-4, System Origin: Scotland-ODM, GS Film Number: 1042971, 0102895.</ref> Their son John Glasgow was christened on 23 September 1722 at Libberton, Lanark, Scotland.<ref>Scotland, Births and Baptisms, 1564-1950. MyHeritage.com [online database], accessed 2025. https://www.myheritage.com/research/record-30226-745324/john-glasgow-in-scotland-births-baptisms.</ref>

=== Libberton record-parent review — 17 October 2026 ===

'''Libberton child review, 17 October 2026:''' Robert is the '''documented named father in John’s 1722 baptism'''. The American continuation attributed to John and Robert’s own ancestry retain their uncertainty.

The accepted Findmypast transcript for John Glasgow’s Libberton baptism on 23 September 1722 explicitly names father Robert Glasgow and mother Katherin Wilson, with residence Sandyburgh: OPR 650/1, page 17, item 3, SCOT/OPR/BAP/4033399. The existing John profile is already assigned to this exact record by the completed person-level audit. [[Glasgow-3407|Robert Glasgow]] is the maintained Wilson-household father account and [[Wilson-127539|Katherine Wilson]] its mother account. Both recorded parent roles should therefore be documented locally, rather than left as unassessed tree fields. These are two explicit parental names in one baptism transcript, not a mother inferred from a husband’s surname. The existing Carnwath 1713 Robert–Katherine Wilson marriage citation is compatible household context; it is not needed to invent the 1722 parents or to treat every local Robert as the same man.

The documented assessment applies to the Scottish child and parents in the 1722 baptism. The later American identity attributed to John—Nottingham, Chester County, and a May 1769 death—remains unproved. The profile’s Bible and burial citations have not supplied an inspected migration, household or parent bridge from Libberton. No American newspaper attribution is reinstated. This does not establish that the American John had these parents. The source supplies a baptism date, not an independently proved birth date. Katherin in Findmypast and Katharin in the retained MyHeritage index are provider spelling variants of the record’s Wilson mother, not two independent maternal confirmations. This review reuses the captured transcript of 3 September 2026; no fresh register-image inspection is claimed.

The existing working branch below John contains 14 retained pre-1850 profile accounts. That is a measure of the current topology and includes the unresolved Scottish-to-American identity; the baptism does not prove the entire descendant chain. No person, parent attachment, identity merge, spouse or changed vital field is added. Robert’s own selected [[Glasgow-3332|James Glasgow]] father, his competing Johne route and his birth estimate remain uncertain. The separate Gibson/Watson Robert family remains separate; neither woman is equated with Wilson. Local record-parent assessments and reciprocal child descriptions are synchronized in both viewers. The raw WikiTree parent fields and saved certainty flags remain unchanged. Any live certainty change requires resolving its profile-identity scope; the prepared amendment adds the baptism evidence and that limit. Working connectivity is unchanged. The supported and confident paternal measures gain the existing Scottish father edge only; this is classification of previously ungraded record parentage, not a new ancestor or a proved American connection.

=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

James [[Glasgow-3332]] is the selected uncertain working father of Robert [[Glasgow-3407]], who married Katherine Wilson at Carnwath in 1713 and is associated with son John [[Glasgow-2870]] at Libberton in 1722. This is conditional on James representing the weaver at Back of Lindsaylands whose testament was indexed in 1680. The adjacent Biggar, Libberton and Carnwath family setting supplies a specific local comparison. A James born around the inherited 1630/1634 estimates could father Robert in the 1660s or 1670s; a Robert born in the 1670s could marry in 1713 and father John in 1722 at ordinary adult ages. This provisional older-generation route is preferred to making the two overlapping Libberton Roberts sons of the same Johne. No record names Robert as the weaver’s child. Robert’s current circa-1690 birth estimate would be too late for a father dead by 1680; an earlier birth is required if this hypothesis is right. That estimate is unsupported, but the required revision remains a limitation, not evidence for the proposed father.

The Carnwath marriage citation gives Robert and Katherine Wilson in 1713; the captured 1722 Libberton baptism citation names Robert and Katharin Wilson as John’s parents. Those index citations define the proposed Wilson household but supply no paternal ancestry or age for Robert. The neighbouring Robert [[Glasgow-4168]] account combines Katherin Gibson in 1718 and Katherine Wilson in the corrected 1720 reading, and Katherine Watson in 1726. The 1720 and 1722 entries now agree on Wilson: the older Watson–Wilson–Watson argument is superseded. The 1718 Gibson and 1726 Watson entries still require their own identity comparison. Transcription differences, record misassignment, overlapping families, or one man across some entries remain possible. The 30 October reconciliation selects Wilson-127539 as Agnes’s mother at 95% from her named baptism and the matching 1722 parental couple. Wilson is not equated with Gibson or Watson, and the two Robert accounts are retained separately. These child records do not identify Robert’s father.

The 1680 index describes James as a weaver in the Back of Lindsaylands, parish of Biggar, with indexed testament date 10 November 1680, Lanark Commissary Court CC14/5/10, TD. That is a registration date, not an exact death date. The original has not been read and supplies no reviewed Robert clause. The 1690 Edinburgh apprenticeship names a deceased father James Glasgow, weaver in Lindsayland; the matching trade and locality make him a probable instance of the indexed weaver, but identification with [[Glasgow-3332]] remains only possible. The apprentice generation is another possible intermediary for the later family, not an additional person invented to complete a pedigree. James [[Glasgow-3332]]’s existing parentage, the separate 1648 Johne child identity, and the competing 1624 Quothquan-weaver identity retain their previous grades. The new father selection cannot itself prove any of those identities or be used circularly to reject the 1624 alternative. This particular parent model depends on the later weaver identity; the 1624 decedent would be an implausibly early direct father of this 1713–1722 household.

Johne [[Glasgow-3093]] remains an unselected alternative father for the Carnwath Robert, particularly if Robert [[Glasgow-3407]] is eventually identified with the Gibson/Watson Robert [[Glasgow-4168]], whose selected father is already Johne. Johne’s birth around 1648 could fit fatherhood around 1690; however, no later record follows him from the Mid Calder child entry to Libberton. Giving him both Roberts would otherwise require two living sons with the same forename or an unproved identity between the accounts. The present choice avoids building that assumption into the default model. If James [[Glasgow-3332]]’s two father assignments and the Johne [[Glasgow-3093]] to Robert [[Glasgow-4168]] choice are right, Robert [[Glasgow-3407]] would be the latter Robert’s paternal uncle. That is a consequence of the conditional model, not independent evidence of kinship. Another unrecorded local father and a younger Lindsaylands generation remain possible. Captain Robert [[Glasgow-2867]] also remains a surviving identity comparison; his unsupported imported family is not a concrete exclusion, and no maritime or probate bridge identifies the Carnwath man with him.
