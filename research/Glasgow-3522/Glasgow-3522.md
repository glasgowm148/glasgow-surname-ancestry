== Biography ==
'''Murieston working route, 17 October 2026:''' William is selected as the record-only Mureistoun George’s '''uncertain father''', conditional on identifying that man with William’s already proved son [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]]. This does not establish two different George sons or prove that [[Glasgow-991|James Glasgow]] was William’s grandson.<ref name="MuriestonFatherReview" />
'''William Glasgow''', born circa 1540, was a citizen of Glasgow, Lanarkshire, during a period of significant civic and religious change. As the husband of '''[[Herbison-439|Margaret Herbesoun]]''', he was connected by marriage to one of Glasgow's influential families. A Glasgow protocol directly identifies him as the son of [[Glasgow-1093|John Glasgow (bef.1516-bef.1575)]], a contemporary burgess of the city. William died shortly before July 1586, leaving a minor son, [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]], as his heir.

=== Marriage and Social Standing ===
William married '''[[Herbison-439|Margaret Herbesoun]]''', a member of the prominent Herbertson family of Glasgow. This marriage connected the Glasgows to a powerful network within the city's merchant and clerical elite. Margaret's family had strong ties to the founders of Hutchesons' Hospital and Grammar School; her probable kinsman, the chaplain Sir William Herbertson, was a cousin of the Hutcheson brothers, who established these cornerstone Glasgow institutions.<ref>[https://www.electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow2_20.htm History of Glasgow, Chapter XXII], retrieved 12 Jul 2025.</ref> This connection underscores the family's respectable standing within Glasgow’s burgess class.

William and Margaret had at least one known son:
* [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]] (born c. 1565-1570)

[[Glasgow-1030|John Glasgow]] has also been proposed as William's son, but the 1586 protocols do not name or prove him. That proposed relationship remains uncertain.

=== Death and Family Estate ===
William died in Glasgow sometime before '''15 July 1586'''. On that date, his son, George, was formally declared his legal heir and took possession (''sasine'') of his father's property. The inherited property was described as a "back waste" (a back property or piece of undeveloped land) located on the west side of the main street leading from the Glasgow Cathedral to the market cross.<ref>Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. 9, protocol '''2903''', printed p. 103, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc01scogoog/page/n113/mode/2up 15 July 1586].</ref>

Just a few months later, on '''1 September 1586''', a second legal document provides a clearer picture of the family's circumstances. It records the sale of this inherited land by the young George Glasgow, who required the consent of his mother and legal guardians to proceed:
<blockquote>"George Glasgw, son and heir... with consent of '''Margaret Herbesoun, his mother''', for her liferent, and of '''Sir William Herbesoun''' and '''[[Glasgow-1029|Stephen Glasgow]]''', his curators, sold to John Angus..."<ref>Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. 9, protocol '''2916''', printed p. 107, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc01scogoog/page/n116/mode/2up 1 September 1586].</ref></blockquote>
This record is significant for several reasons:
* It confirms '''[[Herbison-439|Margaret Herbesoun]]''' as William's widow, who held a "liferent," a legal right to use and benefit from the property for the remainder of her life.
* It proves their son, '''[[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]]''', was still a minor (likely between 14 and 21 years old), as he required curators to legally act on his behalf.
* It identifies his powerful curators: '''Sir William Herbertson''' (a chaplain and likely his maternal uncle or close relative) and '''[[Glasgow-1029|Stephen Glasgow]]''', a trusted family associate or kinsman.

=== Murieston family review — 17 October 2026 ===

Prefer the named George of Mureistoun as [[Glasgow-991|James Glasgow]]’s probable father. George is recorded at Mureistoun in the 1586 Calder obligation; the accepted 1605 original-register reading places James’s household in probable Muirestoun and names an adult George Glasgow as witness to the baptism of James’s son George. The earlier review already assessed the two adult George occurrences as probably one man. The same uncommon surname, the probable same holding across nineteen years, and the older name-bearer’s personal participation in the younger household favour lineal family continuity. Father is the preferred degree: an older established man followed by James’s child-bearing household is a normal two-generation sequence, and the child’s George name is compatible with that reconstruction. This is a combined locality/household inference, not three independent proofs or a compulsory naming rule.

The 1605 witness’s residence and age are unstated, and Muirestoun remains a probable reading/location. Shared holding names do not prove a legal title transfer. Father, uncle, older brother, cousin and neighbour remain genuine alternatives. The 1586 obligation does not establish muster attendance, and neither record states that George was James’s father. The George name and witness role are supporting context, not direct kinship. [[Glasgow-3163|Andrew Glasgow]] remains an unselected uncertain father alternative; his 1597/8 testament names Robert, Marion, Jonet and Isobel, not James. The George comparison supplies a closer holding-and-witness association than the broader Andrew/Calder model, without proving that Andrew could not be James’s father.

For the working tree only, select [[Glasgow-3522|William Glasgow]] as George of Mureistoun’s uncertain father through the surviving possible identification with the Glasgow merchant [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]]. Protocol 2903 explicitly names that merchant’s deceased father William; protocol 2916 gives his mother and curator. The same rare full name and compatible 1586 generation make the merchant an existing-profile comparison for the Mureistoun man. The father proof belongs to the merchant: transferring it to the Mureistoun account depends entirely on the unproved identity. This is a testable named upstream route, not direct evidence of a Calder-to-Glasgow movement or a newly proved son of William.

The merchant was a minor in 1586, with the accepted approximate birth range 1565–1572. If James’s conventional circa-1575 birth were established, that would conflict with his being the merchant’s son. James’s birth is unrecorded: a later birth in the 1580s can fit the 1605 fatherhood and, toward the earlier end of George’s range, ordinary fatherhood in the previous generation. Those are compatibility examples, not new birth estimates. The 1598 marriage-contract comparison does not prove a first marriage or identify James’s mother. No date is altered to make this route fit. George-1099 stays a compatible candidate; there is no merge, no second distinct George son claimed for William, and no automatic transfer of Margaret Herbesoun or a wife as mother. If the two Georges are different men, William’s upstream placement must be reconsidered. The retained George Glascoe-36 and George-3171 comparisons remain open on their actual source limits.

=== Note on Parentage ===
On 24 August 1565, John Glasgow senior resigned a burgage tenement to "William Glesgw, his son," reserving liferent to John and Jonet Spreull, his spouse.<ref>Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. 3, protocol 847, printed p. 73, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc06scogoog/page/n85/mode/2up 24 August 1565].</ref> William had already appeared as an instrumentary witness on 9 June 1564 with Mychael Lyndesay, who also witnessed the 1565 father-to-son conveyance. In 1586 [[Glasgow-1029|Stephen Glasgow]] witnessed George Glasgow taking sasine as heir of the late William and then served as George's curator; a 1575 protocol directly identifies Stephen as another son of the late John and Jonet Spreull.<ref>Renwick, ''Abstracts'', vol. 7, protocol 2189, printed p. 110, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc00robegoog/page/n126/mode/2up 17 June 1575].</ref> No second living adult William Glasgow appears in the complete published Glasgow protocol series from William's first appearance through George's inheritance. The direct wording, repeated witness and record-proved sibling network make this profile's identification with John's son confident. On 31 December 1534 John's parents conveyed a back tenement to John and Janet Spreull, expressly named as his spouse.<ref>Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. 4, protocol 1196, printed p. 72, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc08robegoog/page/n84/mode/2up 31 December 1534].</ref> She remained John's spouse in the 1565 conveyance to his son William, and the 1575 protocol explicitly calls her the mother of John's son Stephen. Stephen then witnessed William's son's inheritance and served as that son's curator in 1586. William's estimated birth range fits this documented marriage, and no earlier wife or competing mother has been found. Together these records support Jonet as William's '''confident, inferred mother'''; the 1565 instrument alone does not expressly call her William's mother.

=== Note on Dates ===
William's birth year is not recorded. He witnessed a Glasgow sasine on '''9 June 1564''', and the pre-reform Scots-law minimum age of fourteen for an instrumentary witness gives a hard outer boundary of birth by 9 June 1550.<ref>Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', vol. 3, protocol '''795''', printed p. 54, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc06scogoog/page/n66/mode/2up 9 June 1564].</ref><ref>Scottish Law Commission, [https://www.scotlawcom.gov.uk/index.php/download_file/view/615/139 ''Report on Requirements of Writing''], para. 5.12.</ref> George's status as a male minor acting with curators in 1586 implies birth after 1 September 1565 and by 1 September 1572.<ref>Scottish Law Commission, [https://www.scotlawcom.gov.uk/files/2313/1419/8956/cm65_1.pdf ''Consultative Memorandum No. 65: Legal Capacity and Responsibility''], para. 2.1 and note 2.</ref> Taken together with the 1565 conveyance to William as John's son and fatherhood in the following decade, '''about 1540''', with a reasonable working range of roughly '''1535-1550''', is a better estimate than the former c. 1525 guess.
