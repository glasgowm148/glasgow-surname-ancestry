**Murieston working route, 17 October 2026:** William is selected as the record-only Mureistoun George’s **uncertain father**, conditional on identifying that man with William’s already proved son George Glasgow-1099. This does not establish two different George sons or prove that James Glasgow-991 was William’s grandson.

The dated review below supersedes earlier selected-father wording, while retaining its evidence and alternative.

Father assessment: [John Glasgow (Glasgow-1093), born about 1510,
Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1093) is **confident**. Protocol
847 directly calls William John's son. The profile identification is further
secured by the repeated Mychael Lyndesay witness in 1564-65 and by John's
record-proved son Stephen witnessing the 1586 entry of William's heir and then
acting as that heir's curator. A complete search of the published Glasgow
protocol series from William's first appearance through 1586 found no competing
living adult William Glasgow.

Mother assessment, **27 September 2026**: [Jonet Spreull
(Spreull-4)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Spreull-4) is now **confident as an
evidence-based inference**, saved and read back on WikiTree. Protocol 1196
establishes her marriage to John by 31 December 1534; protocol 847 still calls
her his spouse while John conveys land to his son William in 1565; protocol
2189 explicitly calls her mother of John's son Stephen in 1575. Stephen then
witnessed William's son's inheritance and served as his curator in 1586.
William's estimated birth fits the documented marriage, and no earlier wife or
competing mother has been found. The 1565 instrument alone does **not** call
Jonet William's mother; the grade rests on the combined family chronology and
records, and would need revisiting if evidence of an earlier union emerged.

William's son [George Glasgow (Glasgow-1099)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1099)
now has both parents marked confident on WikiTree. Protocols 2903 and 2916
directly identify William as his father and Margaret Herbesoun as his mother;
Margaret's parent status was saved and read back on 27 September 2026.

Birth-date assessment: William's live profile now uses **about 1540**. No record
supplies an exact birth year. William was an instrumentary
witness on 9 June 1564. The Scots-law minimum age of 14 for such a witness gives
a defensible outer bound of **born by 9 June 1550**. He received property as
John's son in 1565 and fathered George, who was aged about 14-20 in 1586 and was
therefore born between late 1565 and September 1572. Taken together, **about
1540**, with a reasonable range of roughly **1535-1550**, is a better working
estimate than 1525. It fits an adult witness/property recipient in 1564-65 and
fatherhood in the following decade without pretending that an exact birth year
has been found.

## Murieston preferred father — 17 October 2026

Prefer the named George of Mureistoun as James Glasgow-991’s probable father. George is recorded at Mureistoun in the 1586 Calder obligation; the accepted 1605 original-register reading places James’s household in probable Muirestoun and names an adult George Glasgow as witness to the baptism of James’s son George. The earlier review already assessed the two adult George occurrences as probably one man. The same uncommon surname, the probable same holding across nineteen years, and the older name-bearer’s personal participation in the younger household favour lineal family continuity. Father is the preferred degree: an older established man followed by James’s child-bearing household is a normal two-generation sequence, and the child’s George name is compatible with that reconstruction. This is a combined locality/household inference, not three independent proofs or a compulsory naming rule.

The 1605 witness’s residence and age are unstated, and Muirestoun remains a probable reading/location. Shared holding names do not prove a legal title transfer. Father, uncle, older brother, cousin and neighbour remain genuine alternatives. The 1586 obligation does not establish muster attendance, and neither record states that George was James’s father. The George name and witness role are supporting context, not direct kinship. Andrew Glasgow-3163 remains an unselected uncertain father alternative; his 1597/8 testament names Robert, Marion, Jonet and Isobel, not James. The George comparison supplies a closer holding-and-witness association than the broader Andrew/Calder model, without proving that Andrew could not be James’s father.

For the working tree only, select William Glasgow-3522 as George of Mureistoun’s uncertain father through the surviving possible identification with the Glasgow merchant George Glasgow-1099. Protocol 2903 explicitly names that merchant’s deceased father William; protocol 2916 gives his mother and curator. The same rare full name and compatible 1586 generation make the merchant an existing-profile comparison for the Mureistoun man. The father proof belongs to the merchant: transferring it to the Mureistoun account depends entirely on the unproved identity. This is a testable named upstream route, not direct evidence of a Calder-to-Glasgow movement or a newly proved son of William.

The merchant was a minor in 1586, with the accepted approximate birth range 1565–1572. If James’s conventional circa-1575 birth were established, that would conflict with his being the merchant’s son. James’s birth is unrecorded: a later birth in the 1580s can fit the 1605 fatherhood and, toward the earlier end of George’s range, ordinary fatherhood in the previous generation. Those are compatibility examples, not new birth estimates. The 1598 marriage-contract comparison does not prove a first marriage or identify James’s mother. No date is altered to make this route fit. George-1099 stays a compatible candidate; there is no merge, no second distinct George son claimed for William, and no automatic transfer of Margaret Herbesoun or a wife as mother. If the two Georges are different men, William’s upstream placement must be reconsidered. The retained George Glascoe-36 and George-3171 comparisons remain open on their actual source limits.

James’s former selected Andrew father becomes an uncertain alternative; George of Mureistoun becomes the preferred probable father. A separately uncertain William upstream link keeps the family model inspectable. The record-only George uses the existing complete sourced public subject account and is not a new WikiTree profile or an independently counted extra surname person. The 216-profile pre-1850 child side below James retains all its downstream uncertainty and possible duplicates. The working cohort’s connected components and connected pairs do not improve merely because this named intermediary is displayed. No live WikiTree save, new original inspection, mother, birth/death change or certainty upgrade is claimed.
