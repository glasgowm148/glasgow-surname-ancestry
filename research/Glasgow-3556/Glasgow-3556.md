== Biography ==

The date and place of birth of '''Thomas Glasgow''' are unknown. Because he was recorded as an adult in January 1684 and styled an esquire by 1689, he was probably born before about 1671. Lifford may be retained in the structured birth-place field as uncertain because it is his earliest stated locality, but the record does not prove that he was born there. No inspected record identifies his wife, children or parents.

On 22 January 1684 Thomas Glasgow was indexed as defendant in an Exchequer proceeding brought by a Bryson or Brison described as “of Raphoe.”<ref name="Exch1684">National Archives of Ireland, Equity Exchequer Bill Book 1684–1686, vol. 5, p. 102, 22 January 1684; [https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F007635147%2F00191&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001584141 image] and [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001584141&tab=this transcript].</ref>

In 1689 a printed list associated with the Irish attainder legislation named “Thomas Glasgow of Lifford, in the County of Donnegal, Esq.” among Protestants regarded as absent from Ireland or adherents of William of Orange.<ref name="King">''The State of the Protestants of Ireland under the Late King James's Government'', list of persons named in the attainder legislation, [https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/a47446.0001.001/1%3A6 p. 275].</ref> This establishes his Lifford association and designation, not his birthplace or ownership of every property later named.

On 2 March 1692 Thomas was a defendant in a Chancery proceeding in which Abraham Creighton was a plaintiff or petitioner.<ref name="Chan1692">National Archives of Ireland, Court of Chancery Bill Book 1692–1696, vol. 11, p. 36, 2 March 1692; [https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F007635177%2F00507&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F0000106395 image] and [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F0000106395&tab=this transcript].</ref>

On 23 December 1692 Thomas entered into a counterpart deed of partition with Colonel Abraham Creighton of Crom, County Fermanagh, and Abraham's son James Creighton concerning Donegal lands.<ref name="Partition1692">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/8/1, counterpart deed of partition, 23 December 1692.</ref>

On 25–26 May 1693 Thomas was described as “of Ballindroight” in a lease and release with Colonel Abraham Creighton concerning lands including Drumloane, Glencosh and Shannon in the Barony of Raphoe.<ref name="Lease1693">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/1/3, lease and release between Thomas Glasgow, or Glascow, of Ballindroight and Colonel Abraham Creighton, 25–26 May 1693.</ref>

On 26 May 1693 Thomas executed a performance bond and deed of defeasance for £4,000 in favour of Abraham Creighton.<ref name="Bond1693">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/3/25, bond and deed of defeasance, 26 May 1693.</ref> A counterpart release that day, for £400, concerned lands including Teroneal, Drumlane, Glancosh and Shannon in the Barony of Raphoe.<ref name="Release1693">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/7/1, counterpart release, 26 May 1693.</ref>

On 26 May 1694 Thomas was described as “of Shannon” when he issued a copy bond for performance of covenants in a release to Abraham Creighton.<ref name="Bond1694">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/11/16, copy bond for performance of covenant, 26 May 1694.</ref>

On 25 November 1695 Thomas appeared in a letter and memorandum concerning a joint bond of £6,000 with Hugh Hamill, Abraham Creighton, John Hamilton and John Johnston, all described as esquires.<ref name="Memo1695">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/3/25, letter and memorandum, 25 November 1695.</ref>

On 15 May 1701 Thomas Glasgow, Esquire, was indexed as plaintiff in an Exchequer bill against John Moffett, William Humphrys, Archibald Sinclair and Margaret Sinclair.<ref name="Exch1701">National Archives of Ireland, Equity Exchequer Bill Book 1700–1701, vol. 12, p. 121, 15 May 1701; [https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F007635145%2F00152&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001527519 image] and [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001527519&tab=this transcript]. The page includes June 1701 procedural annotations.</ref>

The rare surname, Esquire designation, exact Raphoe reference and repeated Abraham Creighton association strongly identify the court litigant with the man of Lifford, Ballindroight and Shannon. Thomas was alive after 15 May 1701. His death date and place remain unknown.

== Identity and status ==

Thomas participated in substantial legal and financial transactions and was repeatedly styled an esquire. The surviving catalogue descriptions do not establish his exact role in every instrument or prove that he beneficially owned every named estate. He is best described as an esquire and legal or property actor in the Donegal and Raphoe network.

[[Glasgow-3943|Thomas Glasgow]], known from a 1725 Dublin burial, has no event, associate or locality bridge to these proceedings. [[Glasgow-3964|Thomas Glascow]], a Dominican friar in Rome and Italy from 1691 through at least 1703, was a different man.

== Family and Relationship Research ==

No inspected record proves that Thomas was a son or other relative of [[Glasgow-1101|Archibald Glasgow of Ramelton]], or father of [[Glasgow-1136|Nathaniel Glasgow]] or [[Glasgow-3966|George Glasgow]]. Those existing relationships remain marked uncertain and should not be presented as proved. The proposed Donegal family structure is chronologically and geographically possible, but direct kinship evidence is missing.

Thomas should not be identified with [[Glasgow-1103|Thomas Glasgow]], baptised in Scotland in 1612, without a cross-channel identity record. A James Glasgow in Killybegs account material in 1728 is an adjacent-generation Donegal clue only; no source calls him Thomas's son or relative.

== Research Notes ==

The complete deeds and court bills may contain witnesses, signatures, property recitals or family language omitted from the indexes and catalogue descriptions. They remain the best sources for testing Thomas's family and tracing his estate after 1701.
