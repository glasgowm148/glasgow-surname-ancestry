== Biography ==
'''Killybegs working family, 17 October 2026:''' [[Glasgow-3001|James Glasgow]], the collector in the retrospective Killybegs tax-balance return, is selected as an '''uncertain working son'''. Archibald remains a possible older-family placement for James; Thomas’s probable father and other children keep their existing grades.<ref name="KillybegsFamilyReview" />
The date and place of birth of '''Thomas Glasgow''' are unknown. Because he was recorded as an adult in January 1684 and styled an esquire by 1689, he was probably born before about 1671. Lifford may be retained in the structured birth-place field as uncertain because it is his earliest stated locality, but the record does not prove that he was born there. The original 1713 Exchequer entry names Jane as the wife of a Thomas Glasgow, preferably this man. No child is assigned to Jane from that wording. His probable father is [[Glasgow-1101|Archibald Glasgow]], through the separately assessed identity of Betham’s named eldest son.

On 22 January 1684 Thomas Glasgow was indexed as defendant in an Exchequer proceeding brought by a Bryson or Brison described as “of Raphoe.”<ref name="Exch1684">National Archives of Ireland, Equity Exchequer Bill Book 1684–1686, vol. 5, p. 102, 22 January 1684; [https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F007635147%2F00191&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001584141 image] and [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001584141&tab=this transcript].</ref>

In 1689 a printed list associated with the Irish attainder legislation named “Thomas Glasgow of Lifford, in the County of Donnegal, Esq.” among Protestants regarded as absent from Ireland or adherents of William of Orange.<ref name="King">''The State of the Protestants of Ireland under the Late King James's Government'', list of persons named in the attainder legislation, [https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/a47446.0001.001/1%3A6 p. 275].</ref> This establishes his Lifford association and designation, not his birthplace or ownership of every property later named.

On 2 March 1692 Thomas was a defendant in a Chancery proceeding in which Abraham Creighton was a plaintiff or petitioner.<ref name="Chan1692">National Archives of Ireland, Court of Chancery Bill Book 1692–1696, vol. 11, p. 36, 2 March 1692; [https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F007635177%2F00507&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F0000106395 image] and [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F0000106395&tab=this transcript].</ref>

On 23 December 1692 Thomas entered into a counterpart deed of partition with Colonel Abraham Creighton of Crom, County Fermanagh, and Abraham's son James Creighton concerning Donegal lands.<ref name="Partition1692">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/8/1, counterpart deed of partition, 23 December 1692.</ref>

On 25–26 May 1693 Thomas was described as “of Ballindroight” in a lease and release with Colonel Abraham Creighton concerning lands including Drumloane, Glencosh and Shannon in the Barony of Raphoe.<ref name="Lease1693">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/1/3, lease and release between Thomas Glasgow, or Glascow, of Ballindroight and Colonel Abraham Creighton, 25–26 May 1693.</ref>

On 26 May 1693 Thomas executed a performance bond and deed of defeasance for £4,000 in favour of Abraham Creighton.<ref name="Bond1693">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/3/25, bond and deed of defeasance, 26 May 1693.</ref> A counterpart release that day, for £400, concerned lands including Teroneal, Drumlane, Glancosh and Shannon in the Barony of Raphoe.<ref name="Release1693">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/7/1, counterpart release, 26 May 1693.</ref>

On 26 May 1694 Thomas was described as “of Shannon” when he issued a copy bond for performance of covenants in a release to Abraham Creighton.<ref name="Bond1694">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/11/16, copy bond for performance of covenant, 26 May 1694.</ref>

On 25 November 1695 Thomas appeared in a letter and memorandum concerning a joint bond of £6,000 with Hugh Hamill, Abraham Creighton, John Hamilton and John Johnston, all described as esquires.<ref name="Memo1695">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1939/18/3/25, letter and memorandum, 25 November 1695.</ref>

On 15 May 1701 Thomas Glasgow, Esquire, was indexed as plaintiff in an Exchequer bill against John Moffett, William Humphrys, Archibald Sinclair and Margaret Sinclair.<ref name="Exch1701">National Archives of Ireland, Equity Exchequer Bill Book 1700–1701, vol. 12, p. 121, 15 May 1701; [https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F007635145%2F00152&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001527519 image] and [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001527519&tab=this transcript]. The page includes June 1701 procedural annotations.</ref>

The rare surname, Esquire designation, exact Raphoe reference and repeated Abraham Creighton association strongly identify the court litigant with the man of Lifford, Ballindroight and Shannon. Thomas was alive after 15 May 1701. His death date and place remain unknown.

== Identity and Status ==

The references to Thomas Glasgow of '''Lifford''', '''Ballindroight''' and '''Shannon''' almost certainly concern the same man.

The surname was uncommon, the locations were within the same Donegal and Raphoe property network, and the records form a continuous sequence of transactions involving Abraham and James Creighton between 1692 and 1695.

No inspected record explicitly states that the Thomas of Lifford was the same person as the Thomas of Ballindroight or Shannon. The identification is nevertheless strongly supported by the chronology, geography and repeated association with the Creightons.

Thomas was styled '''esquire''' and participated in substantial legal and financial transactions. The bonds, considerations and lands involved show that he was a significant actor in the Donegal property network.

The surviving catalogue descriptions do not establish his precise role in every transaction. Depending upon the instrument, he may have acted as:

* owner;
* purchaser;
* vendor;
* mortgagee;
* trustee;
* tenant;
* surety; or
* another party with a legal interest.

He should therefore be described as an esquire and legal or property actor. The records do not by themselves prove that he beneficially owned every estate or parcel named.

== Family and Relationship Research ==

The available records name Thomas Glasgow, Abraham Creighton, James Creighton and several other parties to the transactions.

The court and property records do not identify Thomas’s:

* father or mother;
* wife;
* sons or daughters;
* brothers or sisters;
* heirs;
* executors; or
* other stated relatives.

No family relationship between Thomas and the Creightons is given. Their repeated appearance together establishes a continuing legal and financial association, not kinship.

Betham’s April 1673 abstract explicitly names Thomas as eldest son of [[Glasgow-1101|Archibald Glasgow]]. Identification with this Lifford man is probable at 86%, although the abstract gives the son no age, residence, spouse or estate.

The chronology and geography allow Thomas to have been a son or younger relative of the Archibald Glasgow who served as a clergyman in County Donegal and whose will was proved in 1677. The named eldest son is the preferred identification, while another Thomas remains possible.

No inspected record proves that Thomas was the father of [[Glasgow-1136|Nathaniel Glasgow M.A. (died 1743)]].

No spouse or child should be attached to Thomas from chronology, geography, naming patterns or unsourced family trees alone.

== Distinction from Other Men Named Thomas Glasgow ==

Thomas should not be identified with [[Glasgow-1103|Thomas Glasgow]], the child baptised in Scotland in 1612, without evidence connecting that person to Lifford or County Donegal.

A man baptised in 1612 would have been approximately 77 years old in 1689 and over 80 during the property transactions of 1692–1695. That identity is chronologically possible but unlikely and is unsupported by any inspected cross-channel record.

The two profiles should remain separate unless a deed, will, migration record or other contemporary source establishes that they were the same person.

== Death ==

Thomas’s date and place of death are unknown.

He was alive on '''25 November 1695''', when he was named in the memorandum concerning the £6,000 joint bond.

No will, administration, burial entry or record naming his heirs has yet been identified.

His profile should therefore use:

* birth probably before 1671, based on adult status by 1689, with birthplace unknown; and
* death after 15 May 1701, place unknown, from the Exchequer proceeding.<ref name="Exch1701" />

The year 1670 and County Donegal should not be presented as a certain birth date and place without an independent source.

== Locations Associated ==

* '''Lifford, County Donegal''' — Thomas’s stated residence or association in the 1689 attainder list.
* '''Ballindroight, Barony of Raphoe, County Donegal''' — his stated residence in the May 1693 lease and release.
* '''Shannon, Barony of Raphoe, County Donegal''' — his stated residence in the May 1694 bond.
* '''Drumloane, Glencosh, Glancosh, Teroneal and Drumlane''' — lands named in the Creighton transactions.
* '''Crom, County Fermanagh''' — residence of Colonel Abraham Creighton, Thomas’s principal associate in the surviving deeds.

The place-name Ballindroight may represent Ballindrait or another historical spelling. The original deeds and contemporary maps should be consulted before standardising the location.

=== Current evidence assessment ===

The complete deeds and court bills may contain witnesses, signatures, property recitals or family language omitted from the indexes and catalogue descriptions. They remain the best sources for testing Thomas's family and tracing his estate after 1701.

The PRONI catalogue summaries may omit details contained in the original documents. Full copies could include:

* witnesses;
* seals and signatures;
* endorsements;
* former owners;
* inheritance recitals;
* adjoining tenants;
* trusts;
* marriage settlements;
* occupations;
* residences; or
* statements of family relationships.

The complete documents should therefore be inspected before concluding that they contain no genealogical evidence.

Particular attention should be given to phrases such as:

* “son and heir”;
* “natural and lawful son”;
* “brother”;
* “kinsman”;
* “heirs of the body”;
* “in consideration of marriage”;
* “for the advancement of”; and
