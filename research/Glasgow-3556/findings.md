## Current conclusion

Thomas is a genuine adult Donegal actor documented in a Raphoe-linked court
case in 1684, at Lifford in 1689, in the Raphoe/Ballindrait-Shannon property
network from 1692 to 1695, and as an Exchequer plaintiff on 15 May 1701. These records
do not prove an Irish birth, exact age, spouse, children or relationship to
Archibald of Ramelton, Nathaniel of Keady/Fintona, or the later Killybegs James.

A newly inspected Betham abstract of Archibald Glasgow of Rathmalton,
County Donegal, clerk, with will dated April 1673, explicitly names **Thomas
Glasgow as his eldest son**. Identifying this son with the Lifford/Raphoe man
is now **strongly probable**, superseding the former suggestion that their
degree of relationship was wholly unknown. The son has no stated residence,
age, wife or estate in the abstract, so that identity is still inferred.
The live father field was uncertain (10) on 30 September 2026 and remains so
pending a distinctive household, associate or estate bridge. Neither the
Scottish identity of Archibald nor a Thomas-to-Nathaniel descent follows from
this record.

The 1693 Ballindrait material also uses **Glascow**. Together with Archibald's
Glasgow/Glascoe forms, the compatible chronology and Donegal geography support
the father proposal; surname and proximity alone do not establish identity.

The three Dublin bill-book entries of 1684, 1692 and 1701 are a
high-confidence identity match. The 1684 image supplies an exact Raphoe bridge;
the 1692 image names his repeated associate Abraham Creighton; and the 1701
entry repeats the rare name and `Esq.` designation. Thomas was therefore alive
after 15 May 1701, superseding the former after-25-November-1695 boundary.

The newly inspected PRONI catalogue transcription of a c.1700 Lifford estate
paper supplies a more precise property history. Hugh Hamill sold Thomas
Glascoe, Esq., a previously mortgaged part of the lordship for £3,216, with
Abraham Creighton and John Hamilton as covenant sureties. Thomas later sued
Creighton; creditors seized his property interest and Creighton purchased it.
This is evidence of a specific purchase and debt history, not proof of an
inherited Ramelton estate. No parent, wife or Archibald-household relative is
named. The archival transcription was read; the manuscript image was not.
