## Current conclusion

Thomas is a genuine adult Donegal actor documented in a Raphoe-linked court
case in 1684, at Lifford in 1689, in the Raphoe/Ballindrait-Shannon property
network from 1692 to 1695, and as an Exchequer plaintiff on 15 May 1701. These records
do not prove an Irish birth, exact age, spouse, children or relationship to
Archibald of Ramelton, Nathaniel of Keady/Fintona, or the later Killybegs James.

The 1693 Ballindrait material is also published with the surname **Glascow**.
Together with Archibald's `Glasgow/Glascoe` forms, the chronology, uncommon
surname and Donegal geography make a close Archibald-Thomas relationship a
reasonable working hypothesis. They still do not establish father, uncle,
brother or another degree.

The three Dublin bill-book entries of 1684, 1692 and 1701 are a
high-confidence identity match. The 1684 image supplies an exact Raphoe bridge;
the 1692 image names his repeated associate Abraham Creighton; and the 1701
entry repeats the rare name and `Esq.` designation. Thomas was therefore alive
after 15 May 1701, superseding the former after-25-November-1695 boundary.
