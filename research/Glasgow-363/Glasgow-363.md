== Research Notes ==
=== Currituck will father review — 17 October 2026 ===

William’s 1746 Currituck will expressly names Caleb as his son and Abiah and Elizabeth as daughters. The existing Abiah and Elizabeth profiles share the named father, Currituck setting and compatible childhood generation; neither has a conflicting identity. Their father links are documented. The two Caleb profiles repeat the same FamilySearch identity and later Pasquotank will account, indicating possible duplicates. Both fit William’s named son sufficiently for probable father assessments, but they represent one historical son candidate, not two independently proved sons.

The will survives here through a checked published transcription; the manuscript has not been newly inspected. Abiah’s competing 1730/1738 estimates and Elizabeth’s 1741 date are not proved by it. Caleb’s conflicting 1726/circa-1740 births, 1781/1804 deaths and Gregory/Sawyer spouse accounts remain unresolved. The 1803 Pasquotank will does not name Lemuel; his very uncertain selected link to Caleb-363 and alternative Caleb-830 retain that grade. Susanah is William’s named wife, but the will does not establish her as mother of every child; no mother is upgraded. Lowdenner is not equated with the named Sarah or unborn child. William’s own father remains unproved.

Four existing profile father assessments are strengthened: two ungraded links become documented and two ungraded Caleb profile links become probable. These are three historical child roles, including one Caleb role represented by possible duplicate profiles. No new parent attachment, merge, live WikiTree save or vital-date correction is made. All other paternal and maternal grades remain unchanged.

=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

[[Glasgow-363|Caleb Glasgow, older Currituck account]] is selected as the very uncertain working father of [[Glasgow-2581|Lemuel Glasgow]]. The case combines a local surname household, a compatible older generation and the same Currituck–Pasquotank setting. Lemuel’s November 1833 pension declaration says he was born in Currituck, moved to Pasquotank as an infant and remained there until moving to Randolph about 1803. [[Glasgow-428|William Glasgow]]’s previously reviewed 1746 Currituck will names a son Caleb. The two modern Caleb accounts, [[Glasgow-363|Caleb Glasgow, older Currituck account]] and [[Glasgow-830|Caleb Glasgow, Pasquotank account]], both cite a Pasquotank will of 1803. These details make the established local Caleb family a useful preferred home for Lemuel over an otherwise unidentified father. They do not put Caleb in Lemuel’s infant household or name his parents.

The preference for [[Glasgow-363|Caleb Glasgow, older Currituck account]] is generational: its circa 1726 birth allows a father aged about 27 in 1753; [[Glasgow-830|Caleb Glasgow, Pasquotank account]]’s circa 1740 birth would give about 13. Neither Caleb birth is established by a birth record here, and Lemuel himself said he had no age record and only believed he was born in 1753. The younger estimate therefore lowers the second account’s preference without excluding it. No baptism, guardianship, deed or pension statement names Caleb as Lemuel’s father. A collateral connection to Caleb, another local Glasgow father or an unrelated Glasgow immigrant remains possible. This is a low-confidence named-family hypothesis, not a documented father clause.

The Caleb profiles may represent one man with conflicting dates rather than brothers. Both point to the same FamilySearch person K815-XBS and the same family-site account; those references are not independent corroboration. [[Glasgow-363|Caleb Glasgow, older Currituck account]] records 1781 as his death year but its biography transcribes a Pasquotank will dated 20 September 1803; [[Glasgow-830|Caleb Glasgow, Pasquotank account]] places Caleb’s death in 1804 and probate in 1808. The 1803 transcription names children Barhnda (as transcribed), James, Dorcas and Nancy, and an unnamed wife, but not Lemuel. That omission weakens a father hypothesis if this is the relevant testator, without proving that an older, separately established son could not have been omitted. No reason for an omission is established. The will’s modern-profile identity remains unresolved and neither death estimate is changed.

[[Glasgow-830|Caleb Glasgow, Pasquotank account]] remains an explicit unselected alternative father account, especially if its approximate birth is wrong or it is a duplicate of [[Glasgow-363|Caleb Glasgow, older Currituck account]]. No merge or second selected father is made. The 1746 son Caleb cannot yet be assigned uniquely between the two profiles from the will’s name alone. The existing Caleb children and the apparent duplicate Jesse accounts retain their current links and grades; this batch does not resolve their identities. The newly selected edge joins Lemuel’s two-child family to the existing working tree while retaining the uncertainty of the older Currituck pedigree.

[[Glasgow-428|William Glasgow]] is not selected as Lemuel’s father: the previously reviewed probate was in April 1747, roughly six years before Lemuel’s believed 1753 birth. The 1746 will’s expected unborn child cannot normally be a child born in 1753. Making William the father would require a material error in Lemuel’s recollected birth, for which this review supplies no evidence. William’s own father Daniel remains unproved by the inspected records; no certainty is added to that older connection. No mother is selected for Lemuel. [[Gregory-3163|Ruth Gregory]]’s existing attachment to Caleb and the Ruth Sawyer name in the other Caleb biography do not identify Lemuel’s mother. [[Dauge-19|Susannah Dauge]]’s recorded 1715 birth also conflicts with literal maternity of a child born in 1726; that inherited chronology needs its own review rather than being silently repaired here.

One very uncertain father is newly selected. No mother, merge, new person, changed vital field, original manuscript inspection, live WikiTree edit or confidence upgrade is claimed. The Caleb accounts remain separate pending identity review. Working connectivity measures retained profile accounts and selected hypotheses; they do not prove either the paternity or a unique historical person count. The confidently marked connectivity totals remain unchanged.
