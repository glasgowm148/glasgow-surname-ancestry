# Thomas M Glasgow — research findings

## Current conclusion

Reviewed 4 November 2026: Thomas’s father remains **Unknown**. His held census citations place his family at Abbeville in 1840 and 1850 and Calhoun County, Alabama, in 1860. [James Glasgow-803](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-803) is worth testing through the newly reconciled McClellan/Burdette/Hunter family, but no father is selected or probability assigned. The other [Abbeville James](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3753) remains a compatible comparison.

## Abbeville family comparison — 4 November 2026

The copied 1848 letter by [Mary Ann McClellan](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-818) mentions “Tom”; its editor separately calls a Thomas her brother. The body supplies no surname or explicit kin term for Tom. The family-register transcription instead calls its 1814 male James M., and its 1832 Bradford groom James N. Thomas’s Mary Ann/Polly Bradford household and the later child-name pattern are useful parallels, but the forename conflict is unresolved. The estate abstract’s six-child list does not name an apparently living Thomas. This is counterevidence to immediate selection, not proof of exclusion from a selective abstract. Do not rename those source entries or assign their exact dates to Thomas.

The original Abbeville estate packet 157/3423, indexed L01-351 in 1847, is the next test: inspect its full heir list, distributions and any adult James M/N or Thomas before evaluating the proposed family identity. The old SC Archives thumbnail endpoint returned 404; the alternative FamilySearch equity image returned an unauthenticated access error. Neither response establishes that the originals are lost. No father, mother, wife, birth or death is changed.
