# Findings: Glasgow-3896

Last updated: 2026-08-13

## Current answer

Daniel Glasgow's original 1871 will directly identifies David Glasgow of
the City of London as his son. It also identifies Daniel's late brother
Thomas Glasgow and nephew Daniel Neely Glasgow. The will names no Adam,
Rose, James, or Inishrush relative and does not support Daniel's current
attachment as a child of Adam Glasgow of Inishrush.

Daniel's original death registration now fixes his death at Kilrea on 16 October
1871, aged 75, and records him as a widowed watchmaker. It names Peter Glasgow as
the person present at death, but does not state Peter's relationship.

Daniel belongs in the **Bann Valley-Killoquin / Kilrea-Rasharkin cluster** with
his proved brother Thomas `Glasgow-987` and probable brother Alexander
`Glasgow-951`.

Follow-up on Thomas's son Daniel Neely has opened a potentially direct parent
route. The younger man's twins were born in September 1889 while their father,
a labourer, resided in New York. An Irish-born cemetery labourer named Daniel
Glascow died at Troy on 12 February 1931 (New York certificate 9941). He is a
strong but unproved candidate for Daniel Neely; his death certificate could
name Thomas and Mary Ann McCracken.

Live-profile audit **19 July 2026**: the structured parents have been changed to
Alexander Glasgow-3904 and Rosanna Unknown-761034, but the biography still has
the obsolete Adam section and the structured birth remains `before 1807`.
`Glasgow-951`. Older Alexander `Glasgow-3904` and Rosanna Unknown are useful
uncertain working parents, not proved parents.

## Source audit

- OCR for five consecutive weekly issues of *The Jewelers' Circular and
  Horological Review* from 13 December 1911 through 10 January 1912 was searched
  for `David Glasgow`, `D. Glasgow` and `Glasgow`. The only hits concerned the
  Scottish city; no death notice or obituary for David appeared. This is a
  bounded negative for those digitised issues, not evidence that no notice was
  published elsewhere.
- The British Horological Institute identifies David's obituary in *The
  Horological Journal*, volume 54 (1911-12), pages 75-76, but its digital archive
  requires membership. The Google Books and HathiTrust OCLC 49488259 holdings
  inspected expose later volumes, not volume 54. The cited obituary itself has
  therefore not been read and must not be treated as evidence for David's
  ancestry.

## Effect on Adam Glasgow parentage

Daniel belongs to a directly documented Kilrea-Lisnagaver collateral family whose
will-proved sibling is Thomas. No inspected source calls him Adam's son. Daniel
must not be used as a proved child or descendant of Adam when reconstructing
Adam's parents. His inferred birth about 1796 is compatible with Adam's household
chronologically, but compatibility is not relationship evidence.

Adam should not be retained above the older Alexander merely to connect the
cluster to Inishrush. Adam's wife Rose is chronologically excluded as the older
Alexander's mother, and no source identifies Adam as his father.

The will exposed the conflict in the former tree, which made Daniel Thomas's
uncle. The revised working tree places Daniel and Thomas together as brothers and
uses Alexander and Rosanna as uncertain parents. The will does not identify
which parent the brothers shared, so this parentage remains provisional.

## Identity cautions

- The will plus Daniel Neely's marriage now strongly identifies late brother
  Thomas as Thomas Glasgow of the Lisnagaver family; retain the two-record proof
  chain rather than citing either record alone.
- Do not infer Thomas and Daniel's parents from their sibling relationship.
- Do not identify Peter Glasgow as Daniel's son; the death register only says he
  was present at death.
- The will proves David's father, not his mother. Margaret Irwin remains uncertain
  as David's mother.

## Gortereghy placement update

[Griffith's Valuation for Rasharkin](https://www.failteromhat.com/griffiths/antrim/rasharkin.htm)
lists Daniel and Robert Glasgow at Gortereghy. Daniel is probably this Kilrea
watchmaker/farmer, but the name-only valuation is not a direct identification.
The Robert was most likely younger Robert `Glasgow-938`, proved son of Alexander
`Glasgow-951`, whose 1866 marriage links Lisnagaver and Gortereghy. If younger
Alexander was Daniel's brother, the two occupiers were uncle and nephew. This
supports the proposed Daniel-Alexander collateral relationship but does not prove it.

PRONI revision books `VAL/12/B/4/17A` and `17B` refine the land clue. They place
Robert Glasgow at house `18c` in 1864-65; the adjoining `18b` house belonged to
Daniel McCochrane, not Daniel Glasgow. See the [1864 scan](sources/proni_VAL-12-B-4-17A_gortereghy_folio21.jpg)
and [later scan](sources/proni_VAL-12-B-4-17B_gortereghy_folio22.jpg). Griffith's
name index still places a Daniel Glasgow somewhere in Gortereghy in 1862, but
his map reference has not been verified. The records therefore do not prove
that Daniel and Robert occupied adjoining houses, much less that they were
father and son. Probable uncle-nephew remains a qualified family reconstruction.
