== Biography ==
'''Adam Glasgow''' was born about 1758, calculated from his recorded age at death. His birthplace and parents have not been identified.

By 1831 Adam lived at Inishrush, in the civil parish of Tamlaght O'Crilly, County Londonderry. The census recorded one family in his household: three males and five females, all Presbyterians.<ref name="Census1831">[https://www.billmacafee.com/1831census/1831derrycensusnames.pdf William Macafee, ''Names of Householders Listed in the 1831 Census Returns for the County of Londonderry''], p. 231, record 28190: Adam Glasco, house 91, Inishrush, Tamlaght O'Crilly; one family containing three males and five females, all Presbyterian (accessed 16 July 2026).</ref>

A separate '''John Glasco''' headed the other Glasgow household recorded at Inishrush. His household also contained eight Presbyterians. The census provides no ages or relationships between the two households.<ref name="Census1831" />

On 3 October 1831 Adam gave notice that he intended to register at the Magherafelt General Sessions on 2 January 1832 as the holder of a £10 freehold comprising “Houses and Lands” in Inishrush, barony of Loughinsholin. The notice is direct evidence that Adam claimed a legally qualifying interest in the Inishrush property, but it does not prove that his registration was accepted or identify his landlord, predecessor, relatives or lease lives.<ref name="Freehold1831">[https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/viewer/bl/0001480/18311008/044/0003 ''Londonderry Sentinel'', 8 October 1831, p. 3, article 44, row 97], notice dated 3 October 1831 for registration at Magherafelt General Sessions on 2 January 1832: Adam Glasgow, Innisrush, £10 freehold, “Houses and Lands” in Innisrush, barony of Loughinsholin (inspected 10 August 2026).</ref>

The original 1833 Tithe Applotment Book lists '''Adam Glasgow''' first among 58 Inishrush landholders and gives his gross survey area as 11 Irish or Plantation acres, equivalent to about 17.82 statute acres. No John Glasgow appears in the complete Inishrush tithe list. This proves Adam had a tithe-assessed landholding and rules out a joint Adam-John tithe entry, but it does not show that John was absent from Inishrush or unrelated to Adam: the 1831 census independently records John's separate household, and tithe liability did not include every resident or householder.<ref name="Tithe1833">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, ''Tithe Applotment Book for Tamlaght O'Crilly parish'', 5 November 1833, PRONI FIN/5/A/254B, image 20, manuscript folio 16, Inishrush no. 1; duplicate full-page scan PRONI FIN/5/A/254A, images 42–44 (inspected 10 August 2026). The [https://www.nationalarchives.ie/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Family_History_Sources_NAI.pdf National Archives of Ireland's source guide] explains that tithe acreages are normally in Irish or Plantation measure, equivalent to 1.62 statute acres.</ref>

In the first surviving Valuation Revision Book, Adam's proved son [[Glasgow-3903|James Glasgow]] occupied Inishrush plot 29 in 1860–1863, measuring 19 acres 2 roods 10 perches, or 19.5625 statute acres.<ref name="JamesPlot29">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, Valuation Revision Book, PRONI VAL/12/B/34/10A, Inishrush plot 29, James Glasgow, 1860–1863.</ref> The later holding was therefore only about 1.74 statute acres, or 9.8 per cent, larger than Adam's converted tithe acreage. The close agreement, together with James's proved parentage and continued residence at Inishrush, strongly supports succession of substantially the same family farm. It does not establish identical boundaries because the tithe entry has no map reference and the two surveys differed in date, purpose and measurement.

Adam's wife was '''Rose''', whose maiden surname is unknown. Rose died on 31 July 1833 aged 66.<ref name="Gravestone">[https://www.oneirishrover.com/tamlaght-graveyard/ "Tamlaght O'Crilly Lower Graveyard"], transcription and photograph of the Glasgow gravestone recording Rose Glasgow, wife of Adam, who died 31 July 1833 aged 66; Adam Glasgow, who died 30 June 1844 aged 86; their children Eliza and James; and grandson William Glasgow (accessed 16 July 2026).</ref>

=== Children ===

The family gravestone proves two children of Adam and Rose:

* '''James Glasgow''', born about 1794 and died 11 December 1878 aged 84. The gravestone explicitly identifies him as their son.<ref name="Gravestone" />
* '''Eliza Glasgow''', who died aged 26. The gravestone identifies her as their daughter, but the surviving inscription does not preserve a clear death date.<ref name="Gravestone" />

A further documented daughter of Adam was:

* '''Nancy Glasgow''', who married John Kyle of Tyanee on 14 January 1842. The marriage notice described her as Adam's '''sixth daughter'''. The notice does not name Nancy's mother, although Rose is the likely mother.<ref name="NancyMarriage">[https://cotyrone.com/~inthenews/marriages-notices-1840-42/ "Marriage Notices 1840–42"], notice published 29 January 1842: John Kyle of Tyanee married Nancy, sixth daughter of Adam Glasgow of Inishrush, on 14 January 1842 (accessed 16 July 2026).</ref>

Nancy's description as Adam's sixth daughter proves that Adam had at least six daughters. Since Eliza and Nancy are the only daughters presently identified, at least four daughters remain unidentified.

The gravestone also records Adam's grandson '''William Glasgow''', who died on 21 November 1882 aged 27. It does not identify William's parents.<ref name="Gravestone" />

William's civil death registration describes him as a bachelor farmer of Inishrush and names '''Lindsey Glasgow''' as his brother and the person present at his death. The registration confirms an additional member of the later family but still does not identify their parents.<ref name="CivilDeaths">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/en/ IrishGenealogy.ie], civil death registrations for James Glasgow of Inishrush, December 1878, and William Glasgow of Inishrush, 21 November 1882.</ref>

=== Death and burial ===

Adam died on '''30 June 1844''', aged 86.<ref name="Gravestone" />

He was buried or commemorated with Rose in Tamlaght O'Crilly Lower Churchyard, also known as St Nossonus's Churchyard, near Inishrush, County Londonderry.

The gravestone does not state Adam's place of death. Inishrush is probable because he was recorded there in 1831 and was still described as being “of Inishrush” in Nancy's 1842 marriage notice.<ref name="Census1831" /><ref name="NancyMarriage" />

=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

[[Glasgow-1509]], the older Larne ropemaker, is selected as the uncertain working father of Adam of Inishrush ([[Glasgow-3902]]). This adopts the named-family comparison already tested in September. Adam’s gravestone age implies birth about 1758; James’s reported 1737 birth would give an ordinary age-21 generation. The original 1773 affidavit independently places the Larne James’s birth before October 1743, although it does not prove the reported 1737 year. Adam’s proved son James ([[Glasgow-3903]]) supplies limited positive naming support. James is a documented adult with a trade and property trail, making him a more useful provisional father than the recordless [[Glasgow-3905]] placeholder. No record names Adam as his child, and the naming argument is weak rather than corroboration of a parent clause.

The same James is selected, with very low confidence, as father of the reconstructed older Alexander ([[Glasgow-3904]]). This adopts the existing possible-brother route for Adam and Alexander. On the reported 1737 and estimated 1770 years, James would be about 33 at Alexander’s birth. Making Adam, born about 1758, Alexander’s father instead produces a twelve-year interval. Alexander’s circa 1770 birth is only an estimate, so that calculation does not exclude an Adam father: Alexander could have been born later. However, fatherhood of Daniel by about 1795/96 places Alexander in a generation born by roughly 1775–1778, leaving two short successive generations in the Adam-to-Alexander model. Adam’s wife [[Unknown-760890]], born about 1766/67 from her monument age, cannot be Alexander’s mother on that chronology. The sibling route is therefore preferred provisionally without inventing an earlier wife. Chronological economy does not prove brotherhood or a shared father; neither selected edge is independent support for the other.

A 1760 lease identifies James as a Belfast ropemaker taking a ropewalk at Gardenmore, Larne; the 1768 assignment identifies the same property. This is a real occupational and locality sequence, distinct from the contemporary Larne tanner. Adam is documented much later at Inishrush, and the monument does not state his birthplace. The estimated Inishrush birth cannot exclude a family from elsewhere, but no Belfast birth or Larne-to-Inishrush migration is newly assigned. The 1773 Larne gentleman’s age excludes the younger [[Glasgow-3991]] account; the identity assessment and separation of the two trades retain their previous grades. The reported James/[[Shutter-46]] marriage in 1762 is later than Adam’s birth. It cannot establish [[Shutter-46]] as Adam’s mother. An earlier union, premarital birth or inaccurate reported date would be needed if this father model is right; no such event or person is invented. [[Shutter-46]] is not selected as older Alexander’s mother either: the marriage report and the son identity are independently unproved.

The two local 1740 Tamlaght Hugh households, including [[Glasgow-3931]], and the older Adam-and-Maria naming model remain alternatives for Adam. A different unrecorded father remains possible. Neither Larne property succession nor the Inishrush household totals connect these generations. The 1831 return does not identify its unnamed occupants, and the later plot-29 succession is not evidence of James the ropemaker’s paternity. The shared [[Glasgow-3905]] field is a recently created research placeholder, not evidence that Adam, older Alexander and [[Glasgow-1367]] were brothers. It is not identified or merged with James. No father is extended to [[Glasgow-1367]], and no unnamed extra generation is added.

Older Alexander is himself reconstructed chiefly from late descendant testimony naming Thomas’s parents as Alexander and Rosanna. The 2007 account and its copied 2020 restatement are one source tradition, not independent witnesses. Daniel and Thomas are proved brothers, but their father’s name is not established by a contemporary parent record. The new James father therefore depends on that reconstructed older Alexander account. Younger [[Glasgow-951]] remains a separate, unresolved possible brother or cousin of Thomas and Daniel; the record-defined [[Glasgow-4090]] remains his leading current father comparison. This batch does not select older Alexander as younger Alexander’s father or place that younger family beneath James. No Rosanna surname or October 1822 death date is newly established. James’s own [[Glasgow-1505]] and [[Glasgow-1499]] ancestry alternatives and existing children retain their earlier assessments.

The proposed fathers remain unproved, and neither mother is identified by this reconstruction. === Birth ===

Adam's approximate birth year is calculated from the gravestone statement that he was aged 86 when he died in June 1844.

No baptism or other contemporary record has been found stating his birth date or place. His birth should therefore be recorded as '''about 1758''' and marked uncertain.

The available evidence does not prove that Adam was born in Ireland, County Londonderry, Tamlaght O'Crilly or Inishrush.

=== Possible 1799 reference ===

A County Londonderry declaration supporting the Act of Union includes an '''Adam Glasco''' among the subscribers in September 1799.

The original newspaper image gives no address or ditto mark after Adam's name. The apparent association with a nearby place in a derivative HTML abstract resulted from separate newspaper columns being flattened together.

The signatory may be Adam Glasgow of Inishrush, but the county-level entry does not prove his identity or residence and should not be used as evidence that he lived at Inishrush in 1799.<ref name="Union1799">''Belfast News-Letter'', 27 September 1799, original newspaper image reviewed during research; compare [https://www.kinsleuth.com/belnews/1790-1800.htm derivative name and place abstract] (abstract accessed 17 July 2026; original image rechecked 10 August 2026).</ref>

=== Parents ===

No reliable record has been found naming Adam's father or mother.

Two separate adult householders named '''Hugh Glasgow''' were recorded in Tamlaght O'Crilly parish in the 1740 Protestant household return.

One Hugh appears immediately before Hugh Ellis and James McCullough in the collector's sequence and within a broader group containing surnames independently associated with Inishrush. Both Hugh entries nevertheless have their townland recorded as “Not specified”, and collector order can cross localities.

Neither man can be described as an Inishrush resident or identified as Adam's father.<ref name="Hugh1740">[https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/1740religiousreturnsulstername.pdf William Macafee, ''1740 Protestant Householders' Return'']; [https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/books/Kilrea/Kilrea07.html collector-order transcription for Tamlaght O'Crilly], surviving typescript PRONI T/808/15258 (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

A newspaper notice dated 14 April 1772 names a '''Hugh Glasgow''' among inhabitants of Tamlaght O'Crilly parish who surrendered arms to Henry Ellis of Inishrush on 6 April.

The notice does not say that Hugh lived at Inishrush, distinguish him from either 1740 householder or name any family relationship.<ref name="Hugh1772">[https://arborealis.ca/records/newspapers/kilrea-news/1770-1779/ ''Belfast News-Letter'' extracts for the Kilrea area], notice dated 14 April 1772 (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

These records establish an earlier Hugh Glasgow presence in Adam's parish. They do not establish that Adam's father was named Hugh.

=== Hugh Glasgow of Pennsylvania ===

A younger Hugh Glasgow, later of Deer Township, Allegheny County, Pennsylvania, has been proposed as Adam's father.

Hugh signed his will on 29 March 1827. It was proved on 25 April 1831. The will names:

* sons John, Hugh, Alexander and Robert;
* daughter Nancy;
* granddaughter Sarah, daughter of Alexander;
* an unnamed wife.

John and Robert were appointed executors. Robert received the land and residue and was required to support his mother.

Adam was alive in Ireland when the will was made but was not named. His omission while four sons received bequests materially weakens the proposal that Hugh was his father.<ref name="HughWill">Allegheny County, Pennsylvania, Will Book 3, no. 252, pp. 492–493, [https://www.ancestry.com/imageviewer/collections/8802/images/005537856_00276?pid=1733435 will-book image]; corresponding loose probate packet, FamilySearch DGS 5538049, images 239–243, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L991-GV6H?cc=1999196&wc=9PMK-MNG%3A268499101%2C281697201 packet cover] (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

The omission is not a conclusive exclusion because the will does not state that every child was named. The later family account also names two children omitted from the will.

No positive record connects this Hugh to Adam.

=== Robert Glasgow proposal ===

[[Glasgow-550|Robert Glasgow]], whose profile currently uses estimated dates of about 1737–1787, has also been proposed as Adam's father.

Those dates and Robert's claimed County Londonderry origin are unsupported. No record found places him at Inishrush or identifies Adam as his son.

Robert should not replace Adam's unidentified father without a relational source.

A Robert Glasgow of Killwaughter, County Antrim, was already an adult married leaseholder in February 1738. His wife was Rachel, formerly Wilson. He cannot be the same person as someone born about 1737.<ref name="KillwaughterRobert">Registry of Deeds memorials [https://www.irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=74015 74015] and [https://www.irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=76724 76724] (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

A separate Robert Glasgow recorded as an Army medical officer in the West Indies was appointed surgeon on St Vincent in 1785, remained alive after 1787 and had a brother James at Beith, Scotland. This man also provides no evidence for Adam's parentage.<ref name="MilitaryRobert">[https://virtualtreasury.ie/item/DGZ-1785-4695 ''Dublin Gazette'', 13–15 December 1785, no. 4695]; [https://archive.org/details/rollofcommission00ande/page/61/mode/1up Johnston, ''Roll of Commissioned Officers in the Medical Service of the British Army'', 1917], p. 61 (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

=== Daniel Glasgow of Kilrea ===

'''Daniel Glasgow''' of Kilrea was formerly attached as an uncertain son of Adam, but the relationship is not supported by a contemporary record.

Daniel was established at Kilrea by 1827, when his wife was recorded as Margaret Irwin at the baptism of their son Robert.<ref name="RobertBaptism">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/KilreaP1/Baptisms/1827.html Richard John Torrens, "Kilrea First Presbyterian Church Records: Baptisms in 1827"], entry 146: Robert, son of Daniel Glasgow and Margaret Irwin of Kilrea, born 1 September and baptised 30 September 1827 (accessed 16 July 2026).</ref>

Daniel's registered will names:

* David Glasgow of the City of London as his son;
* Daniel Neely Glasgow as his nephew;
* Thomas Glasgow as his late brother.

The will does not name Adam, Rose, James, any person of Inishrush or any property there.<ref name="DanielWill">[https://apps.proni.gov.uk/willscalendar_ie/willssearch.aspx PRONI Will Calendars], registered will of Daniel Glasgow of Kilrea, 1871.</ref>

Daniel and Adam lived in the same general district, but proximity does not prove parentage. Current research instead places Daniel and his will-proved brother Thomas as plausible sons of Alexander Glasgow and Rosanna Shaw, based on a descendant account naming Alexander and Rosanna as Thomas's parents together with Daniel's will identifying Thomas as his brother.<ref name="DanielParentage">[https://www.genesreunited.co.uk/boards/board/general_topics/thread/465937 Genes Reunited descendant account], naming Alexander Glasgow and Rosanna Shaw as Thomas Glasgow's parents; considered with Daniel Glasgow's 1871 will naming Thomas as his late brother (reviewed 10 August 2026).</ref>

That placement is itself uncertain. If Alexander were eventually proved to be Adam's son, Daniel would be a possible grandson rather than a son. The present evidence does not support attaching Daniel directly to Adam.

=== John Glasgow of Inishrush ===

[[Glasgow-822|John Glasgow]] headed the other Glasgow household at Inishrush in 1831: house 2, containing four males and four females, all Presbyterians.<ref name="JohnCensus">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/Tamlaght/1831census_3.html Tamlaght O'Crilly 1831 census transcription], entry 1019: John Glasco, Inishrush house 2, four males and four females, all Presbyterian (reviewed 10 August 2026).</ref>

John's family Bible records that he was born on 10 April 1767 and married Mary Arthur on 21 May 1798. It names their nine children.<ref name="JohnBible">[https://hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I368&tree=tree1 John Glasgow family page and Bible images], Bible printed in 1797 (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

An 1847 letter written by John in Ohio recalls the family's beekeeping at Inishrush, confirming that he had personally lived there.<ref name="JohnLetter">[https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/documents/John%20Glasgow%20letter%20to%20Jane%20Glasgow%20Gray%203-20-1847.pdf John Glasgow to Jane Glasgow Gray, 20 March 1847], scan and transcript (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

John was only about nine years younger than Adam. He cannot plausibly have been Adam's son. The parish-wide 1831 transcription contains no other Glasgow or Glasco household in Tamlaght O'Crilly. Adam and John were not immediate neighbours by census house number, but their two same-townland households and the absence of another Glasgow household in the parish provide supportive evidence for a close collateral relationship, not proof of one.<ref name="Census1831" /><ref name="JohnCensus" />

The strongest current working hypothesis is that John was Adam's brother; cousin remains a live alternative. No accessible record explicitly calls them brothers or names a shared parent, so John should not be attached to Adam as a proved sibling.

John's sons were Adam, James, John and Arthur, and his second daughter was Maria. If John and Adam were brothers, this order is consistent with traditional naming patterns and makes an older '''Adam''' and '''Maria''' the leading candidate names for their shared parents. This is indirect naming evidence only and does not identify real people who can yet be attached.<ref name="NamingPattern">[https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/help-and-support/guides/forenames Scotland's People, “Forenames”], discussion of traditional Scottish naming patterns; considered with John's family Bible (reviewed 10 August 2026).</ref>

John's 1847 letter also reports transatlantic news that a '''James Glasgow''' had fathered a child by a daughter of Henry Barkly. A later marriage record identifies a 38-year-old Inishrush farmer named James Glasgow, son of another Adam Glasgow, who married Rosanna Barkley, daughter of Henry Barkley, on 17 March 1851. The exact names, generation and localities identify the letter's James far more strongly with the younger [[Glasgow-1623|James Glasgow]] than with Adam's older gravestone-proved son [[Glasgow-3903|James Glasgow]]. The 1847 report therefore should not be used as evidence for a relationship between John and Adam without a separate kinship record.<ref name="JohnLetter" /><ref name="JamesBarkleyMarriage">Marriage registration image, 17 March 1851: James Glasgow, aged 38, farmer of Inishrush, son of Adam Glasgow, and Rosanna Barkley, daughter of Henry Barkley; reviewed during research on 10 August 2026.</ref>

John's 1847 letter mentions receiving a letter from “Adam” in December 1846. This correspondent cannot have been Adam Glasgow born about 1758, who died in 1844. John had a son Adam born in 1804, who is the more likely correspondent.

A separate 1825 Kilrea Presbyterian marriage record names '''Margaret Glasgow''' as the mother of Mary Patton of Tyanee. Margaret belongs to the same Portglenone–Tyanee network and is a plausible sister or cousin of Adam and John. The record does not identify her relationship to either man, and identifying her as Adam's daughter would require an unusually early birth and is not supported.<ref name="Margaret1825">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/KilreaP1/Marriages/1825.html First Kilrea Presbyterian marriage register], 6 October 1825: Mary Patton of Tyanee, daughter of John Patton and Margaret Glasgow, married Wilson Gordon (reviewed 10 August 2026).</ref>

=== James Glasgow and the later Inishrush family ===

James is proved as Adam and Rose's son by the family gravestone.

James Glasgow of Inishrush, farmer, died in December 1878 aged 84. '''Henry Glasgow''' of Inishrush was present at the death. The civil registration does not state Henry's relationship to James.<ref name="CivilDeaths" />

The first surviving Valuation Revision Book records James on Inishrush plot 29 in 1860–1863 at exactly 19 acres, 2 roods and 10 perches (19.5625 statute acres). Compared with Adam's 1833 tithe holding of about 17.82 statute acres, the later farm was only 1.7425 statute acres, or about 9.8 per cent, larger. This strongly supports inheritance or continuation of substantially the same family holding, although parcel identity is not proved because Adam's tithe entry has no map number and the surveys differed in date, purpose and measurement.<ref name="Tithe1833" /><ref name="JamesPlot29" />

The Valuation Revision Books then show occupation of plot 29 passing:

* from James Glasgow;
* to Rose Anne Glasgow, inserted with an 1884 revision annotation;
* to a later Adam Glasgow, inserted with an 1893 annotation.

The holding remained 19 acres, 2 roods and 10 perches. These later records establish continuity of plot 29 but do not themselves state the relationships between James, Rose Anne and the later Adam.<ref name="Valuation">[https://apps.proni.gov.uk/Val12B/Search.aspx PRONI Valuation Revision Books], VAL/12/B/34/10A–E, Inishrush plot 29 (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

=== Record limitations ===

The surviving Drimbolg or Drumbolg church records do not include an eighteenth-century baptism register. Baptisms begin in 1895 and marriages in 1864.

PRONI ''MIC1C/15'' includes a session or discipline book covering 1809–1859. It may contain adult family context but cannot record Adam's baptism about 1758.<ref name="DrimbolgRecords">[https://www.billmacafee.com/churchrecords/churchrecordsderry.pdf County Londonderry church-record inventory], Drumbolg or Drimbolg entry, PRONI MIC1C/15 (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

Neither the surviving 1766 Religious Census nor the surviving 1775 Dissenters' Petitions covers Tamlaght O'Crilly. Adam or another Glasgow's absence from those collections would therefore be a coverage gap rather than evidence of absence.<ref name="RecordCoverage">[https://www.findmypast.co.uk/discover/census-land-and-surveys/census/ireland-1766-religious-census Findmypast, “Ireland, 1766 Religious Census”]; [https://www.rootsireland.ie/derry-genealogy/derry-census-substitutes/ Roots Ireland, County Derry census-substitute coverage]; [https://www.findmypast.ie/discover/census-land-and-surveys/census/ireland-1775-dissenters-petitions Findmypast, “Ireland, 1775 Dissenters' Petitions”] (accessed 17 July 2026).</ref>

A search of PRONI's indexed pre-1840 freeholder records returned no Adam Glasgow or spelling variant and no Inishrush address result. The newly inspected 1831 newspaper notice nevertheless records Adam's intended registration of a £10 Inishrush freehold, demonstrating that the index search is incomplete for this question and cannot be used as evidence that he held no freehold.<ref name="Freehold1831" />

PRONI ''D1201/53'', an 1853 Encumbered Estates rental concerning the Hercules and Eliza Ellis estate at Inishrush, Shilling Hill and Drumane, contains no Glasgow in its indexed Inishrush entries. The material appears to concern a different portion of the intermixed townland and should not be used as evidence that Adam or his successors lacked an Inishrush holding.<ref name="Estate1853">PRONI D1201/53, Encumbered Estates rental for the Hercules and Eliza Ellis estate at Inishrush, Shilling Hill and Drumane, 14 April 1853; collection index and rental reviewed 10 August 2026.</ref>

PRONI ''T1762'', “Glasgow family and Presbyterian papers,” is not an open Inishrush lead: its Glasgow-family item is a solicitor's copy of the will and codicil of William Glasgow, farmer of Oritor, County Tyrone, concerning Oritor lands.<ref name="T1762">PRONI T1762, item-level catalogue hierarchy, Glasgow-family item concerning William Glasgow of Oritor, County Tyrone (reviewed 10 August 2026).</ref> The Presbyterian Historical Society's ''G61'' Glasgow envelope is an enquirers' correspondence file rather than an exposed original record; its contents would need inspection before it could affect the tree.<ref name="PHS_G61">Presbyterian Historical Society of Ireland, G61 Glasgow envelope, catalogue description reviewed 10 August 2026.</ref>

A Virtual Record Treasury search found the known 1740 Hugh entries and Adam's 1831 census return but no record naming his father. This is a search of an indexed and reconstructed corpus, not all surviving or undigitised records.

=== Current conclusions ===

The evidence currently supports the following:

* Adam was born about 1758;
* his birthplace and parents are unknown;
* he lived at Inishrush by 1831;
* on 3 October 1831 he claimed a £10 freehold comprising houses and lands at Inishrush for intended registration at the January 1832 Magherafelt General Sessions;
* the original 1833 tithe book records him with 11 Irish/Plantation acres at Inishrush, about 17.82 statute acres;
* James's 1860–1863 plot 29 holding was 19.5625 statute acres, only about 9.8 per cent larger, strongly supporting continuation of substantially the same family farm without proving identical boundaries;
* Rose was his wife;
* James and Eliza were children of Adam and Rose;
* Nancy was Adam's sixth daughter, although her mother is not explicitly named;
* Adam had at least six daughters;
* William Glasgow was Adam's grandson, but William's parents remain unidentified;
* John Glasgow, born 1767, cannot have been Adam's son; brother is the leading working hypothesis, with cousin still possible, but no relationship is proved;
* if John and Adam were brothers, an older Adam and Maria are the leading candidate names for their shared parents based on John's naming pattern, not a proved parent pair;
* Daniel Glasgow of Kilrea should not be attached as Adam's son; the current alternative placement beneath Alexander Glasgow and Rosanna Shaw is itself uncertain;
* the earlier local Hugh Glasgow households are relevant parentage leads but cannot be attached.

=== Current research ===

Adam Glasgow is directly documented at Inishrush in 1831 and by the family gravestone. The gravestone names wife Rose, son James and daughter Eliza; Nancy's 1842 marriage notice calls her Adam's sixth daughter. Adam's father is not known.

A newly inspected original newspaper supplies a stronger land fact. On 3 October 1831 Adam served notice to register at the Magherafelt General Sessions on 2 January 1832 as the holder of a £10 freehold comprising <code>Houses and Lands</code> in Inishrush, barony of Loughinsholin. The notice proves that Adam claimed a legally qualifying interest in the Inishrush property; it does not show that registration was accepted or name his landlord, predecessor, relatives or lease lives.

The original 1833 tithe page gives Adam a gross survey area of 11 Irish or Plantation acres, equivalent to about 17.82 statute acres. A later James Glasgow occupied Inishrush plot 29, measuring 19 acres 2 roods 10 perches (19.5625 statute acres), but both James [[Glasgow-3903]] and James [[Glasgow-1623]] were then alive. The revision succession to Rose Anne strongly favours the younger James, whose wife was Rosanna Barkley. Plot 29 must therefore not be assigned to Adam's proved son or treated as inheritance of Adam's tithe farm without a further identifying record.

Only the proved child edges: James [[Glasgow-3903]], Eliza [[Glasgow-3906]], and Nancy [[Glasgow-3907]] as Adam's daughter with maternity uncertain are retained. Nancy's ordinal proves at least four additional unidentified daughters. Adam [[Glasgow-1622]], older Alexander [[Glasgow-3904]] and Robert [[Glasgow-1367]] are not proved children. Rose is chronologically contradicted as mother of older Alexander and Robert and must be detached from those edges.

Of the three proposed additional sons, Adam [[Glasgow-1622]] is the strongest working hypothesis. His proved son James was a farmer of Inishrush, the repeated name and chronology fit, and the three males in target Adam's 1831 household can coherently be reconstructed as elder Adam, proved son James [[Glasgow-3903]], and younger Adam. This is not a unique reconstruction: the census names no household members, and younger Adam's circa-1780 birth is itself estimated. The profile retains the edge only as uncertain unless a church, lease, probate or estate record names both generations.

Alexander [[Glasgow-3904]] remains only a weak paternal possibility. Making him father of Daniel by about 1795/96 requires Alexander to have been born by roughly 1775-78, leaving two unusually short successive generations from Adam, born about 1758. Rose cannot be Alexander's mother on that chronology. Robert [[Glasgow-1367]] is weaker still: his original 1851 census says he was born about 1778/79 in County Antrim, when Rose was about twelve, and places his documented family at Glenleslie. No record bridges him to Inishrush.

The parent placeholder [[Glasgow-3905]] should be retired as a genealogical person. The profile does not replace it by attaching Adam directly to Robert [[Glasgow-1143]]; no record provides that bridge.

The API provenance resolves the placeholder question: Adam's profile was created at 12:11:11 UTC on 16 July 2026, followed by [[Glasgow-3905]] at 12:43:34. The latter's own biography says it was created only as a research placeholder. It is not a second record for Adam and should not be merged into him. WikiTree merges are for two profiles representing the same person; The placeholder and leave Adam's father blank has been detached.

The catalogue's former top candidates were inflated by exact Inishrush points assigned to profiles whose sources provide only County Tyrone, County Londonderry, or <code>not far from Portglenone</code> traditions, and by treating unproved children as naming controls. Those mapped locations have been corrected. The best surviving record-defined father-generation leads are now the two 1740 Tamlaght O'Crilly Hugh households, including [[Glasgow-3931|Hugh Glasgow, entry 1195]]. Neither is identified as Adam's father.

After correction and rebuild, the five displayed automated father leads are [[Glasgow-1024|James Glasgow II]] (39), [[Glasgow-1143|Robert Glasgow]] (39), [[Glasgow-548|Hugh Glasgow]] (32), [[Glasgow-1025|James Glasgow]] (32), and [[Glasgow-1475|Adam Glasgow]] (30). None is a strong result after source review: James-1024 has no record connecting his family to Adam; Robert-1143's 1738 lease, congregational chronology and 1817 equity evidence define a Kilwaughter family whose surviving children were James and Ann; Hugh-548 is a late-tradition family head whose three reported sons do not include Adam; James-1025 belongs to the Drumearn cluster with no Inishrush bridge; and Adam-1475 is the record-defined 1706 Stevenston child with no evidence of Irish migration. The scores are useful name/network prompts, not evidence that any of these men was Adam's father.

The current working tree now places Daniel Glasgow and his will-proved brother Thomas as plausible sons of Alexander Glasgow and Rosanna Shaw. If Alexander was Adam's son, Daniel would be Adam's possible grandson rather than his son.

=== Current Larne reconstruction ===

Adam's Inishrush household, landholding and descendants remain separately documented; the present question is his unproved parentage. [[Glasgow-1509|James Glasgow]] of Larne is a plausible older-generation candidate, but the ropewalk and affidavit records do not name Adam. James's 1762 marriage to Janet Shutter is about four years after Adam's estimated birth, so Janet must not be assigned as Adam's mother. [[Glasgow-3904|Older Alexander Glasgow]] is more plausibly a possible brother or cousin than Adam's son: Adam's estimated 1758 birth would make him about twelve at Alexander's estimated 1770 birth, and Rose's reported age is still less compatible. The 1831 Inishrush census supports association with [[Glasgow-822|John Glasgow]], not brotherhood. Daniel and Thomas belong in the Alexander/Rosanna working cluster on their own evidence; no record joins that cluster structurally to Adam. These possibilities remain research hypotheses pending a record that names the relationship.<ref name="AdamCurrent">The cited monument, 1831 census, deeds and family material establish context but do not provide a parent statement for Adam.</ref>
