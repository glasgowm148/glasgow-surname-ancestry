Add the following after the 1831 census paragraph:

```text
On 3 October 1831 Adam served notice to register a freehold at the General Sessions to be held at Magherafelt on 2 January 1832. The published table describes him as "Adam Glasgow, Innisrush" and his qualification as "Houses and Lands" in Innisrush, barony of Loughinsholin, valued at £10.<ref name="Freehold1831">[https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.co.uk/viewer/bl/0001480/18311008/044/0003 ''Londonderry Sentinel''], 8 October 1831, p. 3, "List of Notices ... for Registry of Freeholds", row 97, Adam Glasgow; notice dated 3 October 1831. The [https://ulsterlees.azurewebsites.net/records/census/ transcribed heading for the Loughinshollen list] specifies the Magherafelt session and 2 January 1832 date.</ref>

The notice proves that Adam claimed a legally qualifying interest in the Inishrush farm. It is an application notice rather than proof that the claim was accepted, and it does not name his landlord, predecessor, relatives or any measuring lives in the lease.
```

```text
The original 1833 tithe applotment lists Adam first among the landholders of Inishrush and gives his gross survey area as 11 Irish or Plantation acres, equivalent to about 17.82 statute acres.<ref name="AdamTithe1833">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, ''Tithe Applotment Book for Tamlaght O'Crilly parish'', 5 November 1833, PRONI FIN/5/A/254B, image 20, manuscript folio 16, Inishrush no. 1, Adam Glasgow; full-page scan. ## Possible brother
```

[John Glasgow (`Glasgow-3927`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3927)
represents the separate 1831 Inishrush householder. He should not be attached to
`Glasgow-3905`: that profile is an artificial placeholder, not an identified
father. Adam and John may have been brothers, but the census states no
relationship and both should retain blank parents until a relational record is
found. The proposed identity with [John Glasgow
(`Glasgow-822`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-822) remains under review.

Adam was only about nine when John was born, so he cannot be John's father.
John's documented Inishrush history, Covenanter denomination, age and family
composition make him the strongest match for the Drimbolg elder and the second
1831 Inishrush Glasgow householder. The household composition is unusually close:
after married daughter Rose and deceased son James are removed, the Bible family
supplies exactly the four males recorded in 1831, while only one of five possible
females needs to have left home.

John's sons occur as Adam, James, John and Arthur. The first three positions fit
the traditional Scottish paternal-grandfather, maternal-grandfather and father
sequence, with father John correctly repeated third. Maria is the second daughter,
the traditional paternal-grandmother position. John's 1847 letter also relays
intimate news concerning the younger [[Glasgow-1623|James Glasgow]] and Henry Barkly's
Presbyterian family at adjoining Tyanee, where Adam's proved daughter Nancy had
married John Kyle. Together these points make brotherhood the best probable
placement. No record expressly names a shared parent, so cousin remains possible
and the relationship must remain uncertain.

Retain an older Adam and Maria as the leading candidate names for the brothers'
shared parents in Research Notes only. Do not attach or describe them as proved.
The second of the two 1740 Tamlaght O'Crilly Hugh households provides a cleaner
possible root for that collateral line than forcing the brothers under
[[Glasgow-549|John Glasgow]] or [[Glasgow-550|Robert Glasgow]]. No current record
distinguishes John from Robert as a direct-father alternative.
