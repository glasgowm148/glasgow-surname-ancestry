# Findings: Glasgow-3902

Last updated: 2026-08-19

## Current conclusion

Adam Glasgow is directly documented at Inishrush in 1831 and by the family
gravestone. The gravestone names wife Rose, son James and daughter Eliza; Nancy's
1842 marriage notice calls her Adam's sixth daughter. Adam's father is not known.

A newly inspected original newspaper supplies a stronger land fact. On 3 October
1831 Adam served notice to register at the Magherafelt General Sessions on
2 January 1832 as the
holder of a £10 freehold comprising `Houses and Lands` in Inishrush, barony of
Loughinsholin. The notice proves that Adam claimed a legally qualifying interest
in the Inishrush property; it does not show that registration was accepted or
name his landlord, predecessor, relatives or lease lives.

The original 1833 tithe page gives Adam a gross survey area of 11 Irish or
Plantation acres, equivalent to about 17.82 statute acres. A later James Glasgow
occupied Inishrush plot 29, measuring 19 acres 2 roods 10 perches (19.5625
statute acres), but both James `Glasgow-3903` and James `Glasgow-1623` were then
alive. The revision succession to Rose Anne strongly favours the younger James,
whose wife was Rosanna Barkley. Plot 29 must therefore not be assigned to Adam's
proved son or treated as inheritance of Adam's tithe farm without a further
identifying record.

Keep only the proved child edges: James `Glasgow-3903`, Eliza `Glasgow-3906`,
and Nancy `Glasgow-3907` as Adam's daughter with maternity uncertain. Nancy's
ordinal proves at least four additional unidentified daughters. Adam `Glasgow-1622`,
older Alexander `Glasgow-3904` and Robert `Glasgow-1367` are not proved children.
Rose is chronologically contradicted as mother of older Alexander and Robert and
must be detached from those edges.

Of the three proposed additional sons, Adam `Glasgow-1622` is the strongest
working hypothesis. His proved son James was a farmer of Inishrush, the repeated
name and chronology fit, and the three males in target Adam's 1831 household can
coherently be reconstructed as elder Adam, proved son James `Glasgow-3903`, and
younger Adam. This is not a unique reconstruction: the census names no household
members, and younger Adam's circa-1780 birth is itself estimated. Retain the edge
only as uncertain unless a church, lease, probate or estate record names both
generations.

Alexander `Glasgow-3904` remains only a weak paternal possibility. Making him
father of Daniel by about 1795/96 requires Alexander to have been born by roughly
1775-78, leaving two unusually short successive generations from Adam, born about
1758. Rose cannot be Alexander's mother on that chronology. Robert `Glasgow-1367`
is weaker still: his original 1851 census says he was born about 1778/79 in County
Antrim, when Rose was about twelve, and places his documented family at
Glenleslie. No record bridges him to Inishrush.

Retain the parent placeholder `Glasgow-3905` as an explicitly uncertain
structural link until the branch can be placed beneath a source-identified
ancestor. It is not a documented person or proof of Adam's father. Do not place
it beneath Robert `Glasgow-1143`; no record provides that bridge.

The API provenance resolves the placeholder question: Adam's profile was created
at 12:11:11 UTC on 16 July 2026, followed by `Glasgow-3905` at 12:43:34. The
latter's own biography says it was created only as a research placeholder. It is
not a second record for Adam and should not be merged into him. WikiTree merges
are for two profiles representing the same person. Preserve the placeholder only
as an uncertain topology aid, without inferred identity or vital facts.

The catalogue's former top candidates were inflated by exact Inishrush points
assigned to profiles whose sources provide only County Tyrone, County
Londonderry, or `not far from Portglenone` traditions, and by treating unproved
children as naming controls. Those mapped locations have been corrected. The
best surviving record-defined father-generation leads are now the two 1740
Tamlaght O'Crilly Hugh households, including [Hugh Glasgow, entry
1195](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3931). Neither is
identified as Adam's father.

After correction and rebuild, the five displayed automated father leads are

The current working tree now places Daniel Glasgow and his will-proved brother
Thomas as plausible sons of Alexander Glasgow and Rosanna Shaw. If Alexander was
Adam's son, Daniel would be Adam's possible grandson rather than his son.

## Direct-link audit with John Glasgow

A deeper search found no accessible record explicitly calling Adam and John
brothers or naming a shared parent. The strongest new evidence concerns John's
identity, not the relationship: a separate McKeown Bible records that John-822's
grandson was born at Inishrush in 1821 and baptised by Rev. Joseph Orr, the
Drimbolg minister. This independently connects John-822's family to the local
John recorded by that congregation in 1809 and 1827.

The full Adam gravestone names Rose, Eliza, James and grandson William but no
John; the complete 1847 letter contains no kinship term for Adam or James; and
the available 1801 part-Inishrush lease names other head lessees. The original
1799 newspaper image does **not** place `Adam Glasco` at Cloughole: it prints no
address or ditto mark after his name. The apparent grouping was produced by
flattening separate newspaper columns in a derivative HTML abstract. The
signatory could be Adam of Inishrush, but the county-level entry cannot prove
identity or residence. The 1796 flax-premium list's sole County Londonderry
Glasgow was a John at Desertoghill, a possible but unproved earlier record of
John-822.

Accordingly, uncertain brother remains the best connected placement because of
their ages, the two same-townland households, John's naming pattern and the
continuing family-news network. Cousin remains a live alternative. This is not a
confirmed relationship.

## Relationship to the Hugh-Melanchthon line

If John and Adam were brothers, the complete naming pattern points to shared
parents called **Adam and Maria**: John's first three sons were Adam, James and
John, while his second daughter was Maria. This model does not fit either
[John Glasgow (`Glasgow-549`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-549) or
[Robert Glasgow (`Glasgow-550`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-550) as
the father without treating the first-son pattern as an exception. Both men are
chronologically possible direct fathers, but no record distinguishes them.

An intervening older Adam cannot be a son of John-549 or Robert-550 and then the
father of Adam born about 1758: the generations are too compressed. The two
working models are therefore alternatives, not cumulative claims:

1. John-549 or Robert-550 directly fathered Adam and John; or
2. an older Adam and Maria directly fathered them. That older Adam could have
   descended from the other Hugh recorded in Tamlaght O'Crilly in 1740, or been
   an omitted son or close nephew of Hugh-548, but cannot have been a son of
   John-549 or Robert-550 before fathering Adam in 1758.

The public Glasgow Y-DNA table groups John's kit `N18544` with other U106 Glasgow
lines, but no displayed kit has a documented strict male-line descent from Hugh
`Glasgow-548`, John `Glasgow-549`, Robert `Glasgow-550`, or Hugh `Glasgow-540`.
The uncited phrase “Melanchthon branch” in an older WikiTree biography cannot be
used to choose a father until the tester and paper trail are identified.

The original Melanchthon narrative adds a material geographic bridge: it says
Hugh-540's eldest son John was born in 1780 on the family farm in County
Londonderry, `not far from` Portglenone. Together with the 1740 Hugh in the
Ellis/McCullough collector cluster and the 1772 parish Hugh, this makes descent
from or close collateral relationship to Hugh-548's family highly probable.
It does not identify the precise father. The older-Adam/Maria model is the best
internal fit to John's Bible. Entry 1272 is the better candidate for Hugh-548
because it immediately precedes Ellis and McCullough; the other 1740 Hugh offers
a cleaner collateral root for older Adam/Maria. If the choice is restricted to
the traditional brothers John-549 and Robert-550, both are possible but no
record or naming evidence securely distinguishes them.

John's complete Bible family contains no son named Hugh: his sons were Adam,
James, John and Arthur. This is useful negative naming evidence against an
immediate father named Hugh, but not against a more remote Hugh ancestor. His
daughter Margaret later had a son Hugh Wycoff Walkinshaw in 1840, so Hugh was
not wholly absent from John's descendants. Adam-3902's child list is incomplete:
the gravestone proves James and two daughters, while Nancy's description as his
sixth daughter proves several missing children. It is therefore not yet possible
to say that Adam had no son Hugh.

An unidentified son of John-549 or Robert-550 cannot be inserted between either
man and Adam-3902: men born about 1735-1737 could not have a son old enough to
father Adam in 1758. If John-549 or Robert-550 belongs above Adam, he must be
Adam's direct father. A chronologically viable missing-link alternative is an
otherwise unrecorded son Adam of Hugh-548 who married Maria and directly fathered
Adam-3902 and John-822, but this conflicts with the late family account's explicit
statement that Hugh-548 had only three sons. The other 1740 Hugh remains the
cleaner tentative root for an older Adam/Maria household.

## Tree effect

The parish-wide 1831 transcription contains only two Glasgow/Glasco households
in Tamlaght O'Crilly: Adam at Inishrush house 91 and John at Inishrush house 2.
Their separation by roughly 89 house numbers means the census does not show
them as immediate neighbours, but the absence of any other Glasgow household in
the return makes a close family or collateral relationship more plausible. This
is supportive locality evidence, not a parent or sibling record.

Daniel was removed as Adam's son on 17 July 2026 and attached with Thomas to
Alexander and Rosanna as uncertain parents. Adam's biography must no longer call
Daniel his son. Older Alexander `Glasgow-3904` should also be detached from Adam;
retain the connection only as a weak research hypothesis.

The record-defined Inishrush John is a strong identity match to `Glasgow-822`,
but his relationship to Adam remains unproved. Do not place either man beneath
the structural placeholder `Glasgow-3905`.

Preserve an older Adam and Maria as the leading candidate names for the shared
parents in Research Notes. Do not create or attach them as proved people until a
record names the relationship.
