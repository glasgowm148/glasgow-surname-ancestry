## Direct-link audit with John Glasgow

A deeper search found no accessible record explicitly calling Adam and John
brothers or naming a shared parent. The strongest new evidence concerns John's
identity, not the relationship: a separate McKeown Bible records that John-822's
grandson was born at Inishrush in 1821 and baptised by Rev. Joseph Orr, the
Drimbolg minister. This independently connects John-822's family to the local
John recorded by that congregation in 1809 and 1827.

The full Adam gravestone names Rose, Eliza, James and grandson William but no
John; the complete 1847 letter contains no kinship term for Adam or James; and
the available 1801 part-Inishrush lease names other head lessees. The original
1799 newspaper image does **not** place `Adam Glasco` at Cloughole: it prints no
address or ditto mark after his name. The apparent grouping was produced by
flattening separate newspaper columns in a derivative HTML abstract. The
signatory could be Adam of Inishrush, but the county-level entry cannot prove
identity or residence. The 1796 flax-premium list's sole County Londonderry
Glasgow was a John at Desertoghill, a possible but unproved earlier record of
John-822.

Accordingly, uncertain brother remains the best connected placement because of
their ages, the two same-townland households, John's naming pattern and the
continuing family-news network. Cousin remains a live alternative. This is not a
confirmed relationship.

## Relationship to the Hugh-Melanchthon line

If John and Adam were brothers, the complete naming pattern points to shared
parents called **Adam and Maria**: John's first three sons were Adam, James and
John, while his second daughter was Maria. This model does not fit either
[John Glasgow (`Glasgow-549`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-549) or
[Robert Glasgow (`Glasgow-550`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-550) as
the father without treating the first-son pattern as an exception. Both men are
chronologically possible direct fathers, but no record distinguishes them.

An intervening older Adam cannot be a son of John-549 or Robert-550 and then the
father of Adam born about 1758: the generations are too compressed. The two
working models are therefore alternatives, not cumulative claims:

1. John-549 or Robert-550 directly fathered Adam and John; or
2. an older Adam and Maria directly fathered them. That older Adam could have
   descended from the other Hugh recorded in Tamlaght O'Crilly in 1740, or been
   an omitted son or close nephew of Hugh-548, but cannot have been a son of
   John-549 or Robert-550 before fathering Adam in 1758.

The public Glasgow Y-DNA table groups John's kit `N18544` with other U106 Glasgow
lines, but no displayed kit has a documented strict male-line descent from Hugh
`Glasgow-548`, John `Glasgow-549`, Robert `Glasgow-550`, or Hugh `Glasgow-540`.
The uncited phrase “Melanchthon branch” in an older WikiTree biography cannot be
used to choose a father until the tester and paper trail are identified.

The original Melanchthon narrative adds a material geographic bridge: it says
Hugh-540's eldest son John was born in 1780 on the family farm in County
Londonderry, `not far from` Portglenone. Together with the 1740 Hugh in the
Ellis/McCullough collector cluster and the 1772 parish Hugh, this makes descent
from or close collateral relationship to Hugh-548's family highly probable.
It does not identify the precise father. The older-Adam/Maria model is the best
internal fit to John's Bible. Entry 1272 is the better candidate for Hugh-548
because it immediately precedes Ellis and McCullough; the other 1740 Hugh offers
a cleaner collateral root for older Adam/Maria. If the choice is restricted to
the traditional brothers John-549 and Robert-550, both are possible but no
record or naming evidence securely distinguishes them.

John's complete Bible family contains no son named Hugh: his sons were Adam,
James, John and Arthur. This is useful negative naming evidence against an
immediate father named Hugh, but not against a more remote Hugh ancestor. His
daughter Margaret later had a son Hugh Wycoff Walkinshaw in 1840, so Hugh was
not wholly absent from John's descendants. Adam-3902's child list is incomplete:
the gravestone proves James and two daughters, while Nancy's description as his
sixth daughter proves several missing children. It is therefore not yet possible
to say that Adam had no son Hugh.

An unidentified son of John-549 or Robert-550 cannot be inserted between either
man and Adam-3902: men born about 1735-1737 could not have a son old enough to
father Adam in 1758. If John-549 or Robert-550 belongs above Adam, he must be
Adam's direct father. A chronologically viable missing-link alternative is an
otherwise unrecorded son Adam of Hugh-548 who married Maria and directly fathered
Adam-3902 and John-822, but this conflicts with the late family account's explicit
statement that Hugh-548 had only three sons. The other 1740 Hugh remains the
cleaner tentative root for an older Adam/Maria household.
