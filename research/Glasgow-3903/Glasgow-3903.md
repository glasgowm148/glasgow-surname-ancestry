== Biography ==

'''James Glasgow''' was born about 1794, calculated from the age of eighty-four reported at his death. The Glasgow family monument at Tamlaght O'Crilly calls him a son of [[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow]] and Rose.<ref name="Grave">[https://www.oneirishrover.com/tamlaght-graveyard/ ''Tamlaght O'Crilly Lower Graveyard''], transcription of the Adam and Rose Glasgow family monument.</ref>

The leading civil record identifies James as a married farmer of Inishrush who died there on 12 December 1878, reportedly aged eighty-four. Henry Glasgow was present at the death; the register does not state Henry's relationship.<ref name="Death1878">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/deaths_returns/deaths_1878/020497/7192875.pdf General Register Office of Ireland, civil death register], James Glasgow, Inishrush, 12 December 1878, entry 138; [https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view/?record_id=cide-5928386 database entry].</ref>

The family monument transcribes the death as 11 December, one day earlier than the civil register.<ref name="Grave" /> The two sources concern the same death; the original civil-register date is used for the structured field and the one-day conflict is retained here.

Adam Glasgow's 1833 tithe entry gives a gross area of eleven Irish or Plantation acres, approximately 17.82 statute acres under the National Archives of Ireland's published conversion guidance.<ref name="AdamTithe">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, Tamlaght O'Crilly Tithe Applotment Book, PRONI FIN/5/A/254B, image 20, manuscript folio 16, Adam Glasgow at Inishrush; [https://www.nationalarchives.ie/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Family_History_Sources_NAI.pdf National Archives of Ireland, ''Family History Sources''], tithe-acreage guidance.</ref> James later occupied Inishrush plot 29 in the Valuation Revision Books, but the similar acreages and later occupancy are evidence of a property sequence, not a stated inheritance or proof that every James entry concerns this profile.<ref name="Valuation">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, Valuation Revision Books, Inishrush plot 29, VAL/12/B/34/10A–10D; [https://apps.proni.gov.uk/Val12B/Search.aspx PRONI Valuation Revision Books search].</ref>

== Research Notes ==

=== The two James identities ===

An original 1851 marriage records another—or possibly the same—James Glasgow as a 38-year-old bachelor farmer of Inishrush, son of Adam Glasgow. He married Rosanna Barkley, daughter of Henry Barkley.<ref name="Marriage1851">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view/?record_id=cima-3208068 IrishGenealogy.ie], marriage of James Glasgow and Rosanna Barkley, 17 March 1851.</ref> A man aged 38 in 1851 would have been about 65 or 66 in 1878, not 84. The records agree on name, father's name, occupation and locality but conflict by about nineteen years in age.

This profile represents the monument-defined son of Adam and Rose and the age-84 death. [[Glasgow-1623|James Glasgow]], the 1851 groom, remains a separate working profile while the one-man and two-man explanations are tested. The existing records do not justify merging them or creating two independently proved fathers named Adam.

=== Unproved associates ===

Henry Glasgow must not be attached as James's son merely because he informed the death. The later valuation sequence from James to Rose Anne and then Adam shows occupancy succession at Inishrush plot 29, not inheritance or kinship.

A 20 March 1847 family letter reported that a James Glasgow had a child by a servant who was a daughter of Henry Barkly.<ref name="Letter1847">[https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/documents/John%20Glasgow%20letter%20to%20Jane%20Glasgow%20Gray%203-20-1847.pdf John Glasgow to Jane Glasgow Gray], 20 March 1847.</ref> The 1851 James-and-Rosanna marriage is the closer documentary match. The letter does not name or sex the child, and the child should not be attached to this older James.

First Kilrea Presbyterian's 1828 register identifies Jane Barklie, daughter of Henry Barkly and Jane Galloway, marrying Peter Aikin. Henry's 1831 Tyanee household contained four females. This establishes a Barkly household in the relevant locality, but it does not identify Jane—or any particular daughter—as the servant in the letter.<ref name="BarklyContext">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/KilreaP1/Marriages/1828.html First Kilrea Presbyterian marriage transcription], Peter Aikin and Jane Barklie, 1828; [https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/Tamlaght/1831census_3.html Tamlaght O'Crilly 1831 census transcription], Henry Barkley at Tyanee; and [https://www.billmacafee.com/1831census/1831derrycensusnames.pdf William Macafee, ''Names of Householders Listed in the 1831 Census Returns for the County of Londonderry''].</ref>

Second Portglenone baptisms survive for 1845–49 under PRONI MIC1P/357, but the relevant pages have not been inspected. First Kilrea session minutes, PRONI MIC1P/87 (1825–1852), and Drimbolg Reformed Presbyterian session minutes, PRONI MIC1C/15 (1809–1859), are separate possible discipline-record targets. No negative conclusion should be drawn before those records are searched.<ref name="ChurchGuide">[https://www.billmacafee.com/churchrecords/churchrecordsderry.pdf William Macafee, ''Church Records for County Londonderry''], register and session-book coverage.</ref>
