## Suggested research note

John Glasgow's 20 March 1847 letter says that R. Mulhollan had sent Irish news
reporting a belief that James Glasgow had an illegitimate child by his servant, an
unnamed daughter of Henry Barkly. The report is near-contemporary hearsay. It does
not explicitly identify James by residence, name the mother or child, give a birth
date, or prove the child's sex. No inspected source identifies the child.

Source: [John Glasgow letter, 20 March 1847](https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/documents/John%20Glasgow%20letter%20to%20Jane%20Glasgow%20Gray%203-20-1847.pdf). The local identity audit is an internal research aid only, not a WikiTree source.

First Kilrea's 1828 marriage register identifies a real Henry Barkly household
at Tyanee: Jane Barklie, daughter of Henry Barkly and Jane Galloway, married
Peter Aikin. Henry's 1831 household contained four females. Jane is not thereby
identified as the servant in the letter; another daughter remains possible.

Source: [First Kilrea Presbyterian marriages, 1828](https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/KilreaP1/Marriages/1828.html),
entry for Peter Aikin and Jane Barklie; [1831 census names](https://www.billmacafee.com/1831census/1831derrycensusnames.pdf),
Henry Barkley at Tyanee.

Second Portglenone baptisms survive for the relevant years under PRONI
`MIC1P/357` / Coleraine reel 179, but no public image or transcript was found.
The reel has not been searched, so no baptism negative is justified. The
congregation's surviving session minutes begin in 1881 and cannot document an
1847 discipline case. The local register-access manifest is an internal research
aid only, not a WikiTree source.

Separate surviving discipline targets cover the event window: First Kilrea
Presbyterian session minutes `MIC1P/87` (1825–1852) and Drimbolg Reformed
Presbyterian `MIC1C/15` (1809–1859). Neither has yet been inspected for this
case, so no negative conclusion is justified.
