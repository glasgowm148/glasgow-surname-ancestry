## Larne parentage and the unresolved upstream connection

The 13 September catalogue review gives this older Alexander a coherent possible place **beside Adam of Inishrush**, rather than necessarily beneath him. If James of Larne (Glasgow-1509), reportedly born in 1737, fathered Adam about 1758 and this Alexander about 1770, the intervals are about 21 and 33 years. Adam born about 1758 would instead be only twelve at a 1770 Alexander birth, while Rose born about 1767 would be three. Their dates are estimates, but the sibling or cousin version avoids the extra conditions needed for an Adam-to-Alexander parent link. No record yet proves James as either man's father. [Full sourced comparison](/people/glasgow-951.html#larne-reconstruction).

The chain James-1509 → older Alexander-3904 → younger Alexander-951 was proposed on 13 September to accommodate the late account naming Alexander and Rosanna as Thomas's parents, if younger Alexander shared that family. It stood beside James-1509 → younger James-3986 → Alexander-951. Both were withdrawn as routes on 6 October 2026, because Alexander-951's probable father is John Glasgow, farmer, and each chain puts a different man in his place (see the assessment of that date below). Thomas and Daniel's proved brotherhood does not itself name their father.

The American branch is Robert-1143 → James-1144 → Archibald-2304 → possible Robert-2769 → possible James J-2738. The first two connections have concordant record support. The missing boy in Archibald's 1800 household, Robert's South Carolina birth and James J's later chronology give the next two a positive household-and-migration fit. Robert is proposed only as James J's father. The earlier hypothetical transatlantic common-father comparison is irrelevant to this model and is withdrawn.

The close descendant FT20271/STR connection makes the two branches useful comparisons. Their named common ancestor remains unresolved, including the proposed parentage of James-1509 and Robert-1143 within the older Larne family. DNA does not force a particular early-1700s man, but it supports pursuing this family reconstruction rather than treating the uncertain generations as grounds to abandon it.

## Parentage and cluster conclusion

Alexander remains **unproved** as Adam's son. Adam is a weak direct-father hypothesis; preserve existing uncertain attachments while comparing the sibling or cousin alternative. Rose, Adam's wife, presents a
serious chronology conflict: she was
born about 1767, while Alexander needed to be born by about 1778 at the latest to
father Daniel about 1795/96. Adam could only be a speculative young father by an earlier woman. No new maternal attachment or destructive tree change follows from this review. Brother, cousin or more distant collateral
is at least as plausible as Adam being the father.

The working family belongs in the standalone **Bann Valley-Killoquin / Kilrea-
Rasharkin cluster**. The 1834 occupier may be this older Alexander or his younger
namesake; the evidence does not choose. No record bridges this cluster to the
Kilwaughter Antrim Founders, Derry Main, Moneymore or Inishrush line.

Alexander should therefore be treated as the **earliest presently identifiable
head of this branch**, not forced beneath Adam of Inishrush or John of
Glenleslie. The best working reconstruction places [[Glasgow-3896|Daniel]] and
[[Glasgow-987|Thomas]] as uncertain sons because they are independently proved
brothers and descendant testimony names Alexander and Rosanna as Thomas's
parents. [[Glasgow-951|younger Alexander]] remains a plausible third brother,
but no contemporary record calls him their brother or names this older
Alexander as his father.

The 1834 Lisnagaver Robert does not presently supply Alexander's origin. The
best-supported placement for [[Glasgow-1367|Robert Glasgow of Glenleslie]] is a
separate Dunaghy / Doonbought-Glenleslie tenant cluster. If he was the 1834
Robert, he could have been Alexander's brother, cousin or temporary co-tenant;
the tithe alone cannot choose among those relationships.

No record currently selects a brother or first-cousin relationship between older Alexander and the unidentified Robert. Neither their relationship nor a shared generation should be supplied merely to complete an upstream diagram.

Two collateral records define additional Dunaghy relatives without establishing the older Alexander’s relationship to them.
[[Glasgow-1367|Robert]]'s age-matching 1851 niece is probably
[[Glasgow-3957|Mary (Glasgow) Montgomery]], whose 1862 marriage names her father
as **Hugh Glasgow, weaver**; Hugh is therefore a probable brother of Robert.
Separately, [[Glasgow-2385|Alexander Glasgow of Glenleslie]] named a John
Glasgow, labourer, as his father in 1847. This proves the Alexander given name
occurred within the Dunaghy network, but does not identify this older Alexander
or the common ancestor.

Rosanna should be represented as **Rosanna Unknown**, born before about 1780 if
she is retained as Daniel's mother. The `Shaw` surname is an inference from two
Kilrea clockmakers, not evidence of her birth family.

Alexander Glasgow-951's probable father is John Glasgow, farmer, named as the father of John Glasgow-867 in the 1846 marriage record. Any Larne connection for Alexander-951 must therefore join above John, not replace him. The chain James-1509 → this Alexander → Alexander-951 put this Alexander in John's place, and it is withdrawn as a route.

This Alexander and Rosanna remain a weaker rival parent hypothesis for Alexander-951. It rests on the late account naming them as Thomas's parents and on Alexander-951 having shared that family, which no record states.

Whether this Alexander could instead be John farmer's father is not decided by chronology: his birth "about 1770" is only an estimate, derived from Daniel's birth about 1795-96. No record suggests the relationship.

His possible place beside Adam of Inishrush, as a brother or cousin, is unchanged: a working comparison with no record. No WikiTree relationship is changed. [Full comparison](/people/glasgow-951.html#larne-reconstruction).

## Unresolved — "Alexander Glasgow, Ireland" in the Glasgow weavers' roll, 1816–17 (recorded 24 August 2026)

Appendix VII of *Old Glasgow Weavers* (Incorporation of Weavers of Glasgow, 2nd edition) lists **Alexander Glasgow, Ireland** among members entering in 1816–17; the appendix says that addresses generally reflect the address at admission. The entry gives no county, age, family, father or occupation, and the roll includes merchants and other non-resident members, so it does not show that he was a weaver.
