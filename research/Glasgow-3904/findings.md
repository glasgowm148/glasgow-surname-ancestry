# Findings: Glasgow-3904

Last updated: 2026-08-19

## Current answer

The best surviving record now identified for testing this reconstructed family
is PRONI's Ballymoney Reformed Presbyterian register,
`CR5/32/1/1/1-2` (1804-1844). Its society lists, baptisms, deaths and removals
span younger Alexander's and Thomas's probable births and Rosanna's reported
1822 death. A targeted surname-variant and family-group request was sent to
PRONI on 19 August 2026; no result has yet been received. The register is a
pending direct-evidence target, not present proof of the older couple.

No contemporary record identifies Alexander Glasgow as a son of Adam Glasgow of
Inishrush. The proposed older Alexander is reconstructed chiefly from a 2007
descendant account naming Alexander and Rosanna as parents of Thomas of
Lisnagaver. Daniel's original will and Daniel Neely's marriage now resolve the
former topology conflict by proving Daniel and Thomas were brothers. Daniel
and Thomas may remain uncertain children because they are proved brothers
and the descendant account names Thomas's parents. Younger Alexander
`Glasgow-951` may remain a probable brother and uncertain working child, but no
contemporary record proves the parent edge.

The three proposed children do not have equal support:

## DNA-constrained upstream model

Alexander `Glasgow-951` (kit B580327) and the James J Glasgow `Glasgow-2738`
line (kit 1002232) are confirmed R-FT20271 Big Y lines. They are genetic
distance 3 at Y-111, differ at four of 662 compared Big Y STRs, and share the
derived DYS714=27 state. The local SAPP model displays their likely branching
node at about 1750 with a 95% interval of 1700-1750. Because the model is
uncalibrated and misdates a known control relationship, the interval is a
documentary search window rather than proof that the common ancestor lived in a
particular decade.

If James J's present uncertain paper chain is substantially right—James J
`Glasgow-2738` to Robert `Glasgow-2769`, Archibald `Glasgow-2304`, James
`Glasgow-1144`, and Robert `Glasgow-1143`—the common ancestor with younger
Alexander must lie at or above James `Glasgow-1144`. James's will identifies his
children Robert, John, Archibald, Wilson, Margaret and Rachel; it does not name
a living son Alexander. Younger Alexander therefore should not simply be added
as another child of James. The DNA is more consistent with a collateral branch
descending from James's father or an earlier shared Glasgow ancestor.

The fewest-assumption working structure is:

```text
unidentified Glasgow man, born in the first half of the 1700s
├── line to James J Glasgow (the intermediate American links remain uncertain)
└── Bann Valley / Dunaghy collateral line
    ├── ? older Alexander Glasgow-3904
    │   ├── ? Daniel Glasgow-3896
    │   ├── ? Alexander Glasgow-951 — tested FT20271 line
    │   └── ? Thomas Glasgow-987
    ├── ? John Glasgow-1022
    └── ? Robert Glasgow-1367 and probable brother Hugh
```

This is a ranked research model, not a set of WikiTree relationships. The
marker-only kit reported for Robert `Glasgow-1143` is GD 10 from younger
Alexander at Y-111 and lacks a confirmed terminal Big Y result. It cannot be
used to turn any of the dotted lines above into a proved relationship.

## Parentage and cluster conclusion

Alexander remains **unproved** as Adam's son. Adam is only a weak research
hypothesis and should not be attached as father. Rose, Adam's wife, presents a
serious chronology conflict: she was
born about 1767, while Alexander needed to be born by about 1778 at the latest to
father Daniel about 1795/96. Adam could only be a speculative young father by an
earlier woman; detach Rose as mother. Brother, cousin or more distant collateral
is at least as plausible as Adam being the father.

The working family belongs in the standalone **Bann Valley-Killoquin / Kilrea-
Rasharkin cluster**. The 1834 occupier may be this older Alexander or his younger
namesake; the evidence does not choose. No record bridges this cluster to the
Kilwaughter Antrim Founders, Derry Main, Moneymore or Inishrush line.

Alexander should therefore be treated as the **earliest presently identifiable
head of this branch**, not forced beneath Adam of Inishrush or John of
Glenleslie. The best working reconstruction places [[Glasgow-3896|Daniel]] and
[[Glasgow-987|Thomas]] as uncertain sons because they are independently proved
brothers and descendant testimony names Alexander and Rosanna as Thomas's
parents. [[Glasgow-951|younger Alexander]] remains a plausible third brother,
but no contemporary record calls him their brother or names this older
Alexander as his father.

The 1834 Lisnagaver Robert does not presently supply Alexander's origin. The
best-supported placement for [[Glasgow-1367|Robert Glasgow of Glenleslie]] is a
separate Dunaghy / Doonbought-Glenleslie tenant cluster. If he was the 1834
Robert, he could have been Alexander's brother, cousin or temporary co-tenant;
the tithe alone cannot choose among those relationships.

The best unified working model places Alexander and Robert in the same broad
generation below an unidentified earlier Bann Valley/Dunaghy Glasgow ancestor,
probably as brothers or first cousins. This is preferable to forcing either man
beneath [[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow]] or [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow]], but it
is not yet a relationship to add to WikiTree.

Two collateral records make that model more coherent without proving it.
[[Glasgow-1367|Robert]]'s age-matching 1851 niece is probably
[[Glasgow-3957|Mary (Glasgow) Montgomery]], whose 1862 marriage names her father
as **Hugh Glasgow, weaver**; Hugh is therefore a probable brother of Robert.
Separately, [[Glasgow-2385|Alexander Glasgow of Glenleslie]] named a John
Glasgow, labourer, as his father in 1847. This proves the Alexander given name
occurred within the Dunaghy network, but does not identify this older Alexander
or the common ancestor.

Rosanna should be represented as **Rosanna Unknown**, born before about 1780 if
she is retained as Daniel's mother. The `Shaw` surname is an inference from two
Kilrea clockmakers, not evidence of her birth family.

lot 218 on 19 November 2013](https://www.the-saleroom.com/en-gb/auction-catalogues/john-nicholson/catalogue-id-2893472/lot-20016991),

tenant rental beginning in 1835. These are the best known records for testing
whether an Alexander, John or Robert Glasgow preceded the 1834 Lisnagaver
occupiers. The entries are not exposed and the present owner is unknown, so no
relationship is inferred from the auction descriptions.
