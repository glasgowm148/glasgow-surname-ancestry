== Biography ==

'''Unknown Glasgow''' is a research placeholder for the unidentified father of [[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow]] of Inishrush, County Londonderry. It does not represent a man identified in a surviving record. Adam's monument identifies his wife and several descendants but does not name either of his parents.<ref name="AdamGrave">[https://www.oneirishrover.com/tamlaght-graveyard/ ''Tamlaght O'Crilly Lower Graveyard''], transcription and photograph of the monument for Adam and Rose Glasgow.</ref>

No inspected baptism, marriage, will, gravestone, lease, census return or church record supplies this placeholder's name, dates, residence, wife, religion, occupation or death. Those details remain unknown.

== Research Notes ==

This placeholder and Adam's profile represent different generations and should not be merged. The placeholder may be retained only as an explicitly uncertain working connection while the branch is placed beneath a source-identified ancestor. It is not documentary proof that Adam had any particular father, and every relationship attached through it must remain uncertain.

The attachment to [[Glasgow-1143|Robert Glasgow]] and Rachel Wilson is unsupported. The available Wilson inheritance material identifies Robert and Rachel's documented family but does not identify Adam or an intermediate parent connecting this placeholder to them.<ref name="WilsonLitigation">[https://web.archive.org/web/20101124184247id_/http://www.lifesweaving.com/Glasgow/Paper_Reply.html Transcription of Robert Glasgow's sworn answer], 2 June 1817.</ref>

Two adult householders named Hugh Glasgow occur in the 1740 Protestant return for Tamlaght O'Crilly parish. They are the strongest record-defined earlier-generation search targets, but the return supplies no age, townland, wife, children or relationship to Adam. Neither Hugh can be assigned as Adam's father from this record.<ref name="Return1740">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/books/Kilrea/Kilrea07.html Transcript of the 1740 Protestant household return], Tamlaght O'Crilly entries for Hugh Glasgow; surviving typescript PRONI T/808/15258.</ref>

[[Glasgow-822|John Glasgow]] is probably the separate John Glasco enumerated at Inishrush in 1831, but the census does not say that John and Adam were brothers. Cousinship or another collateral relationship remains possible, and John should not be attached through this placeholder without a relational record.<ref name="Census1831">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/Tamlaght/1831census_3.html 1831 census transcription], Adam and John Glasco households at Inishrush.</ref>

Naming patterns in John's family make an older Adam and Maria a useful research hypothesis, not identified people. They must not be created or attached on naming evidence alone.

Separate records place a Robert Glasco at Coltrim in 1818 and a Robert Glasgow there in the 1827 Lissan tithe material. They define a collateral locality lead but do not identify this placeholder or connect that Robert to Adam.<ref>Colin Rynne, “Technological Continuity, Technological ‘Survival’: The Use of Horizontal Mills in Western Ireland, c. 1632–1940,” ''Industrial Archaeology Review'' 33:2 (2011), [https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272249982_Technological_Continuity_Technological_%27Survival%27_the_Use_of_Horizontal_Mills_in_Western_Ireland_c_1632-1940 online copy], citing the 1818 Coltrim occurrence; [https://www.cotyroneireland.com/tithe/lissan.html Lissan tithe transcription], based on PRONI FIN 5A/203.</ref>

=== Earlier research and source limits ===

Several sources concerning Adam and his descendants help define the family that needs to be connected, but none identifies Adam's father. These include the 1831 Inishrush household list, Nancy Glasgow's 1842 marriage notice, Irish civil records, PRONI will and valuation calendars, later Irish censuses and the 1929 Bellaghy estate sale schedule.<ref name="AdamContext">William Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/1831census/1831derrycensusnames.pdf ''Names of Householders Listed in the 1831 Census Returns for the County of Londonderry''], Tamlaght O'Crilly parish, Inishrush, Adam and John Glasco households; [https://cotyrone.com/~inthenews/marriages-notices-1840-42/ ''Marriage Notices 1840–42''], notice dated 29 January 1842 for Nancy, sixth daughter of Adam Glasgow; [https://apps.proni.gov.uk/willscalendar_ie/willssearch.aspx PRONI Will Calendars]; [https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/en/ IrishGenealogy.ie civil records]; [https://apps.proni.gov.uk/Val12B/Search.aspx PRONI Valuation Revision Books], Inishrush; [https://nationalarchives.ie/collections/search-the-census/ National Archives of Ireland census search]; [https://billmacafee.com/estates/bellaghy/lpcsbellaghyestate1929.pdf Bellaghy Estate Land Purchase Commission schedule], 1929.</ref>

The wider parish evidence establishes earlier Glasgow households and the historical setting, not parentage. The 1740 return records two Hugh Glasgow householders without townlands or relationships; later newspaper extracts record a Hugh in the parish in 1772, Inishrush estate notices and a possible Adam Glasco occurrence in 1799, but none bridges an earlier man to Adam of Inishrush.<ref name="ParishContext">William Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/1740religiousreturnsulstername.pdf 1740 Protestant Householders' Return], with the collector-order transcription cited above; [https://arborealis.ca/records/newspapers/kilrea-news/1770-1779/ Belfast News-Letter extracts for the Kilrea area, 1770–1779], notice dated 14 April 1772; [https://arborealis.ca/records/newspapers/kilrea-news/subject-index-1800/ Kilrea newspaper subject index] and [https://arborealis.ca/records/newspapers/kilrea-news/1760-1769/ extracts for 1760–1769]; [https://www.kinsleuth.com/belnews/1790-1800.htm Belfast News-Letter name and place abstracts, 1790–1800], entry dated 27 September 1799.</ref>

A late Glasgow family history proposes a Hugh Glasgow and Margaret McCullough family, but was written long after the events and contains chronological problems. The probate of the younger Hugh in Pennsylvania omits Adam, while Irish deeds distinguish Robert Glasgow of Killwaughter and Rachel Wilson from the Inishrush problem. Military records likewise show that the Robert Glasgow serving in the West Indies was a different, long-lived man. These sources test and weaken proposed fathers; they do not identify this placeholder.<ref name="RejectedCandidates">[https://www.wikitree.com/photo.php/0/0a/Glasgow-590.pdf William Melanchthon Glasgow family history], six-page scan; Allegheny County, Pennsylvania, Will Book 3, no. 252, pp. 492–493, [https://www.ancestry.com/imageviewer/collections/8802/images/005537856_00276?pid=1733435 will-book image] and FamilySearch DGS 5538049, images 239–243, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L991-GV6H?cc=1999196&wc=9PMK-MNG%3A268499101%2C281697201 probate packet]; Registry of Deeds memorials [https://www.irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=74015 74015] and [https://www.irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=76724 76724]; ''Dublin Gazette'', 13–15 December 1785, no. 4695, [https://virtualtreasury.ie/item/DGZ-1785-4695 Virtual Record Treasury copy]; Johnston, [https://archive.org/details/rollofcommission00ande/page/61/mode/1up ''Roll of Commissioned Officers in the Medical Service of the British Army''] (1917), p. 61.</ref>

John Glasgow's family Bible and 1847 letter prove John's dates and his own connection with Inishrush, but do not call him Adam's brother. Public Y-DNA comparisons are useful for selecting test lines but cannot name Adam's father: their ancestral labels are participant-supplied, and the published genetic distances do not establish a specific genealogical relationship.<ref name="JohnDNA">[https://hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I368&tree=tree1 John Glasgow family page and Bible images]; [https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/documents/John%20Glasgow%20letter%20to%20Jane%20Glasgow%20Gray%203-20-1847.pdf John Glasgow to Jane Glasgow Gray], 20 March 1847; [https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview FamilyTreeDNA Glasgow project results]; FamilyTreeDNA, [https://help.familytreedna.com/hc/en-us/articles/6019925167631-Understanding-Y-DNA-Genetic-Distance “Understanding Y-DNA Genetic Distance”]; Legacy Tree Genealogists, [https://www.legacytree.com/wp-content/uploads/T1508095.CLIENTGlasgow1508.report.public.pdf public Glasgow Y-DNA report] (2016).</ref>

Local land, church and family sources identify neighbouring or later Glasgow households without supplying Adam's ancestry. They include the 1734 Inishrush head-lease memorial and a study of Inishrush's rundale holdings; a 1825 Kilrea marriage naming Margaret Glasgow of Tyanee; census-search, tithe and 1851 census material; and the surviving church-record inventories and 1831 Reformed Presbyterian Synod minutes.<ref name="LocalContext">Registry of Deeds memorial [https://www.irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=57951 57951]; Desmond McCourt, [https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/364690144/The_rundale_system_in_Ireland.pdf ''The Rundale System in Ireland''] (Queen's University Belfast PhD thesis, 1950), p. 146 and Appendix B.IV no. 7; [https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/KilreaP1/Marriages/1825.html Kilrea First Presbyterian marriage-register transcript], entry dated 6 October 1825; National Archives of Ireland, [https://nationalarchives.ie/collections/search-the-census/census-search-forms-1841-and-1851/ Census Search Forms collection guide]; William Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/182030stithe/tithenorthantrim.pdf North Antrim tithe data], Rasharkin records 7501–7502; [https://durrushistory.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/1851irishcensusreturnscommadelimitedtextfilejul24_071.pdf 1851 Irish census transcription], Lisnagaver and Glenleslie households; [https://www.billmacafee.com/churchrecords/churchrecordsderry.pdf County Londonderry church-record inventory], Drumbolg entry; Reformed Presbyterian Church of Ireland, [https://archive.org/details/rpci-minutes-of-synod-1831 ''Minutes of Synod, 1831''].</ref>
