# Coltrim to New York collateral audit

Last updated: 2026-07-17

## Question

Can the Robert Glasgow recorded at Coltrim in 1827/1831 or James
Glasgow/Glasco, age 29, recorded at Coltrim before emigrating to New York in
1834 be connected by an explicit record to each other or to Adam Glasgow of
Inishrush?

## Identity controls

- Keep the 1827 Robert at **Coltrim** separate from the other 1827 Robert at
  **Drumnabragy**.
- Do not assume that James was Robert's son merely because Robert headed a
  large Coltrim household in 1831.
- A New York record must agree on enough of name, Irish birthplace, approximate
  birth year (about 1805), migration year (1834), religion, occupation, or
  associates to overcome the common-name problem.
- Do not merge the Coltrim Robert with WikiTree `Glasgow-550` or with any later
  Robert without a relational or otherwise identity-grade record.

## Findings

| Source | Finding | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Lissan Tithe Applotment Book index, 1827, PRONI `FIN 5A/203` | The transcription separately lists `GLASGOW Robert Coltrim` and `GLASGOW Robert Drumnabragy`; it also lists `GLASGOW John Drumeen (Drapers')`. | Direct evidence that two same-name Roberts were recorded in Lissan parish. The townland must be retained in every later comparison. |
| William Macafee, *Emigrants from Co. Londonderry 1834 to 1835 [Source: Ordnance Survey Memoirs]*, p. 32 (PDF page 31) | `James Glasgow [Glasco]`, male, age 29, of Coltrim, Lissan, Presbyterian, was recorded with destination New York in 1834. The row supplies no note or original-record number in the published table. | Near-original compiled evidence for a James born about 1805 leaving Coltrim. It states an intended destination, not a verified arrival or later residence. |
| Same table and page | `William Glasgow [Glasco]`, male, age 29, of Coltrim, Lissan, Presbyterian, was recorded with destination New York in 1835; table record 1263, original record 1658. | A second same-age Glasgow left the exact townland for the same destination one year later. This is a useful cluster lead, but the table does not call William James's brother or Robert's son. |
