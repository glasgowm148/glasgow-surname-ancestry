# Registry of Deeds original-access audit

Updated: 2026-07-17

## Scope and result

I sought original memorial images or authoritative abstracts for 193/462/128477, 215/276/141741, and 393/423/260245. No original memorial or full transcript was accessible in the present public/no-login session. Consequently, exact deed dates, consideration, full residences, parcels, witnesses, relationship clauses, and lives named in the leases remain unproved.

FamilySearch film mapping was established from the Registry transcript-volume table:

- 193/462/128477: DGS 007905840 (volumes 192-193)
- 215/276/141741: DGS 008088413 (volume 215)
- 393/423/260245: DGS 008093719 (volumes 392-393)

Each direct film route redirected to FamilySearch identity login in the existing research browser. No logged-in session was available. This is an access result, not evidence about deed contents.

The public FamilySearch Full Text Collections list showed 23 collections on 2026-07-17 and did not include Ireland or the Registry of Deeds. The experimental full-text search therefore provides no anonymous route to these memorials at present.

## 193/462/128477: Ellis to Dobbyn

**Indexed fact:** the volunteer Registry of Deeds townland index describes this memorial as Ellis to Dobbyn and indexes `Innishrushe + mill`. This is the only one of the three target entries for which an Inishrush locality is actually indexed.

**Independent contemporary context:** the Belfast News-Letter reported in July 1758 that Henry Ellis of Innishrush married Mrs Elizabeth Dobbin of Belfast. A December 1758 advertisement by Henry Ellis concerned the bleaching green and wash mill at Inishrush. These items corroborate an Ellis-Dobbin-Inishrush-mill network close in time, but they do not establish that memorial 128477 was a marriage settlement or identify its full parties.

**Parentage relevance:** potentially useful for reconstructing the Inishrush associates, but presently no direct link to Adam Glasgow, Hugh Glasgow, Margaret McCullough, or Tamlaght O'Crilly. The sparse index cannot reveal whether any Glasgow or McCullough appeared as a witness or named life in the full memorial.

## 215/276/141741: John and Robert McCullogh to Ellis

**Indexed fact:** a broad grantor/grantee names-index extraction gives `McCULLOGH John & Robt to ELLIS`, book 215, page 276, memorial 141741.

**Critical citation conflict:** the same compiled source assigns memorial 141741 to a different entry at book 214, page 436 (`McMURRAY-ELLIS`; the row displays memorials 141741 and 141742). Because these are derived name-index entries rather than images or transcripts, the conflict is unresolved. The 215/276 citation must not be used as evidence until checked against the original volume.

**Parentage relevance:** none currently. The entry supplies no county, townland, occupation, residence, witnesses, relationship, or Glasgow name. There is no evidence tying these McCulloghs to Inishrush or Tamlaght O'Crilly. Absence of those details from a sparse index is not proof that they are absent from the original memorial.

## 393/423/260245: William McCulloch to Ellis

**Indexed fact:** the broad names index gives only `McCULLOCH William to ELLIS`, book 393, page 423, memorial 260245.

**No valid localization:** the immediately preceding abstracted memorial, 393/422/260842, concerns David and Samuel McCulloch of Ballynease, County Londonderry, dated 19 September 1787. It is a different memorial, page, and number. Its adjacency cannot localize William McCulloch, prove a relationship, or connect 260245 with Inishrush. Ballynease is in Ballyscullion parish, not Tamlaght O'Crilly.

**Parentage relevance:** none currently. The index supplies no forename for Ellis and no county, land, residence, witness, relationship, Glasgow, or Inishrush detail. No kinship inference is justified.

## Source-quality conclusion

1. Memorial 128477 remains a genuine, localized Inishrush lead because the townland index and contemporary newspaper evidence independently converge on Ellis, Dobbin, Inishrush, and its mill.
2. Memorial 141741 is presently unusable because its citation conflicts inside the secondary index compilation.
3. Memorial 260245 is an unlocalized surname association. The neighbouring Ballynease memorial is not evidence about it.
4. None of the accessible material identifies Adam Glasgow's father or confirms Hugh and Margaret McCullough Glasgow as his parents.

## Rebellion Papers 620/42/15: Glasco financial papers

The Irish Manuscripts Commission's *Sources for Irish Women's History* database gives the authoritative catalogue-level entry:

- repository: National Archives of Ireland;
- collection: Rebellion Papers;
- reference: carton 620/42, item 15 (normally cited 620/42/15);
- date span: 1764-1798;
- description visible online: `Glasco, papers of, bills, accounts and financial...`.

The accessible catalogue and Virtual Record Treasury text give **no Glasco forename, residence, county, occupation, or relationship**. The fuller Treasury snippet says that the packet includes Mary Lynch's assignment of Trinity Lane premises to Abraham Taylor. It appears to describe a surname-level packet of financial material rather than a single named letter. The documents themselves were not accessible. The National Archives states that the Rebellion Papers are available on microfilm MFP 6/1-149; its public guide does not map this individual item to a reel.

The Virtual Treasury confirms that its Rebellion Papers resource is a digital version of the State Paper Office calendars, not a full transcription of every document. Its public search API exposed the item metadata and the Trinity Lane snippet, but not the original papers. Therefore no Glasco forename, residence, or relationship can responsibly be supplied from the calendar.

### Cluster assessment

Independent sources establish a dense, exact-spelling **Dublin Glasco commercial cluster** during and immediately around the packet's date span:

- newspaper extracts call Philip Glasco senior an eminent watchmaker in Christchurch Lane in 1750 and record his wife's death there in 1760;
- Philip Glasco junior, watchmaker of Christchurch Yard, married Teresa Plunkett in 1768 and died in Christchurch Lane in 1770;
- Watson's 1783 Dublin directory lists Thomas Glasco, watchmaker, at 20 Ormond Quay;
- Wilson's 1801 and 1804 Dublin directories list Mary Glasco, linen draper, at 64 Dame Street.

Those records, together with the Trinity Lane assignment, make the Dublin trade family a substantially better contextual match for a 1764-1798 file of `Glasco` bills and accounts than the presently known rural Londonderry target family. This remains a **moderate-confidence attribution to a cluster, not an identification of the papers' owner**: the entry does not name Philip, Thomas, Teresa, or Mary. It also contains no accessible reference to Londonderry, Inishrush, Tamlaght O'Crilly, Adam Glasgow, or a parent-child relationship. The file must not be used as evidence for Adam's parentage unless the microfilm supplies a relevant person or relationship.
