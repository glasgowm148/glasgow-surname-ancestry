## Letter correction and illegitimate-child lead

The letter's paragraphs must not be merged. The unidentified Adam's December 1846 account of acreage, fruit planting, and bees is most naturally an American report from John's son Adam, with “Inisrush” used only as a recollection. R. Mulhollan's separate report concerns western Canada and potato rot; the later sentence about James comes through McCaivey. It supplies a potentially important missing descendant of James, but currently supplies **no evidence about the elder Adam's father**.

Henry Barkley's identity is now substantially narrowed: Henry Barkly and wife Jane Galloway were parents of Jane Barklie by 1828, and Henry headed a seven-person Presbyterian household in Tyanee in 1831. The next test is civil/church marriage evidence for other Barkley daughters naming Henry, followed by baptism, maintenance, estate, or later death/marriage evidence for a child born about 1845-46.

One testable but currently **unproved** possibility is that Henry Glasgow, informant at James's death in 1878, was the reported child born about 1845-46 and was named for maternal grandfather Henry Barkley. Neither the death return nor any located marriage, baptism, probate, or newspaper item states that Henry was James's son. This cannot be used as a relationship fact without such a record.

## Kyle search result

An exhaustive civil-death index sweep for Nancy/Anne/Ann/Agnes Kyle in the Magherafelt district found no plausible death for Nancy Glasgow Kyle. The plausible alternatives are death before civil registration began in 1864, emigration, remarriage, or residence/death outside that district. Candidate John Kyle deaths in 1874 and 1892 do not identify a wife Nancy or connect their subjects to Adam Glasgow. No located Kyle record names Nancy's parents beyond the 1842 notice or names her grandparents.

The remaining Henry/Barkley hypothesis is worth retaining as a clearly labelled lead, not as a relationship conclusion.

### Strict male-line status

The gravestone proves `Adam (died 1844) -> James (died 1878)`. It does **not** prove `James -> later Adam`. The censuses and civil death prove `later Adam -> Adam D.` and strongly identify `later Adam -> Henry Downing`, but the first of these chains is disconnected from James at the crucial generation. The probate abstract alone cannot prove `Adam D. -> Edward William`.

Accordingly, none of adult Henry, Lindsey, or William is presently documented as James's son. There is no verified living strict male-line descendant and no public Y-DNA tester who can be assigned to this Inishrush line without adding an unsupported relationship.

## Reported Barkly daughter and illegitimate-child audit (16 July 2026)

The complete source-by-source log is preserved in [the Barkley child identity audit](sources/barkley_child_identity_audit_2026-07-16.tsv). The research question is narrower than whether the family story is plausible: it requires a record that identifies either Henry Barkly's unnamed daughter or the child whom R. Mulhollan reported by March 1847.

### What the letter proves

John Glasgow wrote that McCaivey brought transatlantic news: “we believe it that James Glasgow has an illegitimate heir, by his servant maid Henry Barkly daughter.” This is near-contemporary evidence that informants in the family's Irish circle believed a child existed. It is still second-hand, uses no forename for the mother or child, gives no birth date, and does not explicitly add “of Inishrush” to James's name. “Heir” should not be expanded into a legal finding or a male-sex statement without another source.

### Henry Barkly's documented family

| Person | Evidence | Status for the 1846-47 report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Henry Barkly of Tyanee | The 1831 census records three males and four females in his all-Presbyterian household. | Strong match for the father named in the letter, but household totals do not name his daughters. |
| Jane Galloway | Jane Barklie's 1828 marriage names Henry Barkly and Jane Galloway of Tyanee as her parents. | Proved wife/mother; no other source found expands her children. |
| Jane Barklie Aikin | Her 1828 marriage to Peter Aikin explicitly proves she was their daughter. | Weak mother candidate because she had been married for eighteen years; no evidence of widowhood, separation, or a Glasgow child was found. |
| Peter Barkley | His 1854 civil marriage names his father as Henry Barkley, farmer. | Likely son, not the servant daughter or reported child. |
| Other daughters | The 1831 household can accommodate additional daughters, but no name is supplied. | Their existence is plausible, not individually proved. A complete Kilrea marriage sweep and seven relevant civil returns identified no additional daughter naming Henry. |

Jane's 1828 entry says only “Portglenone congregation” and does not distinguish First from Second. First Portglenone's surviving baptisms stop in 1846 and resume in 1852, exactly spanning the likely birth; its surviving session minutes begin only in 1903. Second Portglenone baptisms survive for 1821-67 under PRONI `MIC1P/357`, but no public transcription of the target years was located. This is a genuine access gap, not a negative baptism search, and Second Portglenone may be the more important target precisely because its register covers 1846-47.

### The February 1847 Tyanee baptism is a false lead

The [Churchtown Presbyterian transcription](sources/tamlaght_presbyterian_baptisms.html) contains a striking same-place/same-time entry: on 22 February 1847, two-month-old **Francis Gamble** of Tyanee was baptized, with father entered as **Barkly** and mother as **Margret Quigley**.

The entry does not identify a Barkley mother or a Glasgow child. The register columns are child, father, mother, residence, and age. The transcriber expressly says fathers' surnames were entered only when they appear in the original; adjacent entries likewise give a child's surname and only the father's forename. The natural reading is therefore a child surnamed Gamble whose father was called Barkly, not a father surnamed Barkly.

Independent local records reinforce that reading. In 1829 Francis Gamble, son of Aaron Gamble and Grace? Barklie of Lislea, married; in 1840 Thomas Gamble, another son of Aaron and a Barklie mother, married; and Thomas later named a son **James Barkley Gamble**. Kilrea's communion roll also records James Gamble of Lislea as a theological student in 1838, while his grave calls him Rev. James B. Gamble. A derivative Ancestry tree expands the initial to Barkley, but that expansion is not needed for the exclusion. Barkley was demonstrably used as a given or middle name in this local Gamble cluster. Francis Gamble is therefore probably unrelated to the reported James Glasgow child.

| Candidate or reconstruction | Supporting facts | Contrary facts / missing proof | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adult Henry Glasgow, present at James's 1878 death | Correct surname; lived at Inishrush; adult by 1878 if born about 1846; could have been named for maternal grandfather Henry Barkly. | James's death return does not state Henry's relationship, age, birthplace, or parents; no later record securely follows him. | **Strongest named working candidate, but low documentary confidence. Not proved.** |
| Roseanne Glasgow was the Barkly daughter and later James's wife/partner | Born about 1823, so appropriate age; died a farmer's widow at Inishrush; plot 29 passed James -> Rose Anne -> Adam; her daughter Mary stayed there. A later union could explain both the servant report and later legitimate-looking household. | No record gives Roseanne's maiden surname or husband's name; no marriage was found; no source calls her Henry Barkly's daughter. | **Coherent reconstruction, but entirely unproved at the key maiden-name link.** |
| Jane Barklie Aikin was the mother | Proved daughter of Henry and geographically local. | Married since 1828; no widowhood/separation or Glasgow association found. | Weak candidate. |

| William, Lindsey, later Adam, or Mary of Inishrush | Close later household and plot associations. | Their estimated births in about 1853-56 are after the child was reported in March 1847. | Chronologically excluded as the reported child. |
| Francis Gamble, baptized 1847 | Exact townland and timing; father field reads Barkly. | Child is explicitly Gamble, mother is Margaret Quigley, and local Gamble records use Barkley as a given/middle name. | Strongly excluded as the reported Glasgow child. |

### Conclusion

No inspected source identifies the reported child by name. Adult Henry Glasgow at James's death is the best candidate produced by the evidence, and the companion theory that Roseanne was Henry Barkly's daughter is internally coherent. Both remain hypotheses assembled from timing, residence, naming, and property succession; neither is an explicit kinship record. They must not be entered as proved relationships or used as proof of Adam Glasgow's parentage.

The remaining record capable of changing that conclusion is an explicit entry in Second Portglenone baptisms (`MIC1P/357`), an unlocated copy/extract from the missing First Portglenone years, or a later civil/emigrant/estate record naming Henry Glasgow's parents or Roseanne's maiden surname. The current result is **identity unresolved**, not “no child existed.”

## Second Portglenone register and congregation audit (16 July 2026)

The source-by-source coverage, access, variant-name, and target-search results are preserved in [the Second Portglenone register access audit](sources/second_portglenone_register_access_audit_2026-07-16.tsv). This audit asked whether the relevant register actually survives, whether any public image or transcript could be searched now, and whether it survives elsewhere under another congregation name.

### Register identity and exact coverage

PRONI's current Guide to Church Records identifies **Second Portglenone Presbyterian Church, `MIC1P/357`** and gives:

- baptisms 1821-1867 and 1881-1910;
- marriages 1822-1910;
- session minutes 1881-1901, including communicants 1880-1883;
- Sunday school roll 1821-1867;
- stipend list 1829-1834 and stipend accounts 1829-1851; and
- later communicant and temperance records.

The reported child was already believed to exist by March 1847, so the target baptism years are inside the surviving first baptism volume. This is a positive survival finding, not an inference from an index.

Two derivative inventories need qualification. Bill Macafee reproduces the detailed PRONI coverage and gives Coleraine Library reel **179**. The Coleraine Family History Society inventory also gives film 179, but compresses the baptisms to 1822-1910 and thereby hides PRONI's 1868-1880 gap. It does not affect 1845-1849. Macafee's Sunday-school end year of 1967 conflicts with PRONI and Bann Valley's 1867 and is evidently a transcription or OCR error.

### Copies and public access

No public image, transcript, or citation for the 1845-1849 pages was found.

- Bann Valley's dedicated Second Portglenone page lists the record classes, but expressly gives **“none”** for its on-site dates for baptisms, marriages, minutes, and the Sunday school roll.
- RootsIreland's published Antrim source list does not include Second Portglenone Presbyterian baptisms. Its Portglenone baptism range is Roman Catholic. The Ulster Historical Foundation's 1845-1921 Second Portglenone line belongs to its marriage database and cannot be used to search the child's baptism.
- `MIC1P/357` is an onsite PRONI microfilm reference. A specialist PRONI access guide confirms that `MIC` records are read in the Search Room. PRONI also publishes a December 2025 list of digitised church records, but the linked DOCX could not be rendered through the available web interface, so the presence of an onsite digital surrogate was not verified. In either case, the catalogue does not expose the register images online.
- Coleraine/CFHS film 179 is a second onsite microfilm route, not a web database.

The absence of public pages means the name searches conducted here are only public-web negatives. They do **not** show that Glasgow, Barkly, Barkley, Barklie, or Barclay is absent from the baptism register.

### Congregation-name variants

Contemporary descriptions establish the naming context. Pigot's 1824 directory separately lists a Presbyterian meeting house and a **Seceding meeting house**. Lewis in 1837 separately describes a Synod of Ulster congregation and a **Seceding Synod** congregation. Those terms therefore belong in searches for early Second Portglenone material. The sources do not themselves print a later number beside each body, so the mapping of “Second” to the Seceding body should remain a historically supported search hypothesis rather than an explicit identity statement from either directory.

**Townhill is not a Second Portglenone alias.** The Presbyterian Church in Ireland's 2019 minutes state that Third Portglenone was established in 1839, was commonly called Townhill, and was formally renamed Townhill. Searches of PHSI, FamilySearch, WorldCat, UHF, and general web catalogues found no separate Second Portglenone register copy or transcript under Second, 2nd, Seceding meeting house, or Seceding Synod. Those are catalogue-surface negatives, not proof that no private transcript or uncatalogued extract exists.

### Evidential conclusion and remaining route

The target register survives and covers the correct years, but its contents were not inspectable online. No source located in this audit identifies the reported child or Henry Barkly's daughter. There are also **no 1845-1849 session minutes catalogued under `MIC1P/357`**: the surviving session series begins in 1881. Therefore a contemporary church-discipline or illegitimacy case cannot presently be pursued through this congregation's catalogued minutes.

The decisive unsearched material is now tightly defined:

1. Second Portglenone baptisms for roughly 1845-1849 on `MIC1P/357` / Coleraine film 179, checking Glasgow and every Barkly spelling and recording all entry fields and marginal annotations.
2. The Second Portglenone Sunday school roll through 1867, which could name a later-enrolled child but would not by itself prove parentage.
3. Stipend accounts through 1851, which could establish Glasgow/Barkly congregational or household association but are unlikely to name the child.

Until the reel is examined, **Henry Glasgow remains a candidate, not an identified child**, and no negative baptism claim is justified.

The Sentinel transcript calls its source “A Prescott, U.S. American paper,” while the matching merchant is demonstrably at Prescott, Canada West/Ontario. No competing Samuel Glasgow merchant in Prescott, Wisconsin, or another United States Prescott was located in web-indexed census, cemetery, newspaper, or local-history searches. Prescott, Wisconsin's early history also did not name a Glasgow among its early businesses or officers.

This makes the Ontario identification **very strong**, but the wording must not be silently corrected. It could reflect a transcriber's punctuation/geographic error, a cross-border reprint, or an unidentified United States namesake. Formal proof requires the original Sentinel item, the underlying Prescott obituary, or Samuel's Sandy Hill monument. The digitised *Prescott Journal* of Wisconsin was identified, but a complete issue-by-issue OCR search was not feasible; the United States result is therefore a qualified search negative, not an exclusion by exhaustive census work.

### WikiTree identity and relationship to the Irish candidates

No Prescott, Orena, Thomas Crichton, Grenville, or Sandy Hill match for this family exists in the local `glasgow-one-tree.xlsx` workbook, and no target profile was found in public WikiTree indexing. **No WikiTree ID can responsibly be assigned.** In particular:

- Prescott Robert is not `Glasgow-3905` Adam, who died in Ireland in 1844, and is not the proposed Robert `Glasgow-550`, born about 1737 and said to have died about 1787.
- Prescott Samuel is not `Glasgow-2908`, whose documented 1851 residence was still in Ireland.
- Neither Robert nor Samuel is `Glasgow-3896` Daniel, who remained at Kilrea through 1871.
- Daniel's documented son Robert was born/baptised in 1827. He cannot be the adult Robert building or owning the Prescott house about 1835 and fathering Samuel, who had already emigrated in 1831.

Samuel's notice supplies a valuable **Kilrea origin**, but it does not say Robert was born there or name any Irish relative. Robert could chronologically have belonged to an older Kilrea-area Glasgow generation, and the nearby John Glasco could be collateral kin, but neither proposition has documentary support. The Prescott cluster therefore does **not** identify Adam's father, prove a relationship to Adam of Inishrush, or overlap Daniel's known household.

### Conclusion

The best-supported reconstruction is: **Robert Glasgow, early Prescott shipbuilder (reported died 1856), was father of [[Glasgow-4010|Samuel Glasgow]], born about 1801 in Ireland, Kilrea emigrant of 1831 and Prescott general merchant (died 1866).** Samuel's identity is very strong from the original census and coincident name, place, occupation, migration and death evidence; Robert's paternity is strong local-history evidence but remains short of an original kinship record. No source yet gives Robert's parents, siblings or birthplace. This cluster is an important Kilrea collateral lead, not evidence for the parentage of [[Glasgow-3905|Adam Glasgow]]. Samuel's [created-profile record](../Glasgow-4010/1851_Canada_West_Prescott_Samuel_Glasgow.md) is resolved; [Robert](../../surname-research/new-people/1842_Canada_West_Prescott_Robert_Glasgow.md) remains on hold.
