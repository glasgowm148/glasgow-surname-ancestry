# Land, Civil, and Probate Research: Glasgow-3905

Last updated: 2026-07-16

## Objective

Trace the Inishrush Glasgow holding and civil-record family to identify the relationships among Adam Glasgow (died 1844), his proved son James (died 1878), and the later Adam, Henry, Lindsay, Rose Anne, and William. Priority is a contemporary record that names Adam's father.

## Research Log

- 2026-07-16: Extracted plot 29 from `VAL/12/B/34/10C-10F`; the recorded succession is James -> Rose Anne -> Adam, followed by Adam under the three estate lessors through the 1911-29 volume. No purchase/vesting notation is recorded there.
- 2026-07-16: Queried every Glasgow death indexed in Magherafelt for 1864-1922 and inspected the available register pages. Maria (1879, Kinturk), Eleanor (1883, Drumard), Martha Anne (1890, Dreenan), Robert (1917, Dorman), and William (1915, Dreenan) belong to other local households; none states a relationship to the Inishrush family.
- 2026-07-16: Current reconstructed plot-29 household is James Glasgow (born about 1794, died 1878), likely widow Rose Anne (born about 1823, died 1888), and likely children Adam (born about 1853, died 1916), William (born about 1855, died 1882), Mary (born about 1856, died 1889), and Lindsey (alive 1882). `Likely` is used because no found document yet explicitly calls the younger people children of James; Rose Anne -> Mary and William -> Lindsey are explicit.
- 2026-07-16: The 1847 report materially changes the working hypothesis. If Rose Anne was Henry Barkley's unnamed daughter, her estimated birth about 1823 fits a servant in the mid-1840s, and Henry Glasgow may have been the reported child: he was alive and at James's deathbed in 1878, and his name could honor maternal grandfather Henry Barkley. This is a coherent hypothesis only; no record yet explicitly calls Rose Anne a Barkley or Henry a son of James.
- 2026-07-16: No civil marriage of James Glasgow to a Rose/Rose Anne appears in the Magherafelt index after non-Catholic civil registration began in 1845. This is consistent with, but does not prove, the 1847 report that the child was illegitimate.
- 2026-07-16: Reconstructed the registered Inishrush head-lease chain using both the Registry of Deeds main index and its separate 703,000-entry townland index. The surviving indexed transactions consistently concern the Ellis middleman family and successors; no spelling of Glasgow appears as an Inishrush deed party. This is an important structural finding, not a dead end: Adam's predecessor must be sought in Conolly estate tenant-level records.
- 2026-07-16: The highest-yield records are now tightly specified: PRONI `D1062/1/5` (lease book, about 1730-1826), with special attention to entries from the 1764 re-letting onward; `D1062/1/2` (1838-83 rent ledger, which cites leases back to 1733); and `D1062/1/13` (1839-1941 renewal book, normally stating new lives, ages, and relationships). These volumes are catalogued but not digitally available. A Glasgow entry naming a life as `son of Hugh`, for example, would be direct contemporary proof; the currently accessible sources stop one documentary layer short of that entry.
- 2026-07-16: Traced McCourt's privately held 1950 Bellaghy sources to the Boyd solicitor collection now catalogued at PRONI. Macafee's inventory identifies the 1859 Barnes and Dougal traced-map volume as `D1062/1/16`; PRONI's current description does not enumerate that volume, so this is an exact catalogue discrepancy rather than proof that the maps are missing.
- 2026-07-16: Identified the exact Inishrush Griffith manuscript field book as PRONI `VAL/2/B/5/40C` (catalogued 1858). Neither it nor `D1062` appears in PRONI's comprehensive online-digital inventory.
- 2026-07-16: Tested geographic overlap between the remembered 1801 joint lease and later Glasgow plot 29. No defensible overlap can be made: the oral account says only `part of Inishrush`; McCourt says the strips were reorganised into new farms in 1859; and the cancelled `31A` sheet proves an Inishrush plot 29 can denote an unrelated 1a 3r 30p O'Neill holding.
- 2026-07-16: Corrected the Land Commission conclusion. `10F` continues the Earl of Strafford/Lord Deramore/Lady Louisa Trench as Adam Glasgow's lessors with no `in fee` notation, while Provisional List 1214 omits Glasgow. The two records establish omission, not earlier vesting. PRONI `FIN/48/3/503` (1925-29) is the next exact estate-sale file to inspect for the reason.
- 2026-07-16: Resolved the eCatalogue browse workflow and recovered the full `T877/216` item description. It is Thomas Conolly's 1 December 1800 renewable-for-ever lease to Joseph Courtney of the Inishrush mill and seven plantation acres at £10 5s rent. This eliminates it as McCourt's 1801 four-family partnership lease and as a direct Glasgow tenancy record. The catalogue omits named lives and witnesses and supplies no digital image; those details remain a physical-record question.

## Two-Hugh identity separation

### Conclusion

PRONI `T808/15258` proves that two separate Protestant householders named Hugh Glasgow were in Tamlaght O'Crilly parish in 1740. Both are high-confidence earlier-generation men and neither is a credible match for the emigrant `Glasgow-540`, born about 1739, whose original Pennsylvania will was signed in 1827. Making him an adult householder in 1740 would require both exceptional longevity and very late fatherhood for his children born in the 1780s-1790s. Either 1740 man could be the alleged older Hugh `Glasgow-548`, but no located record supplies enough identifiers to choose one.

The second Hugh, Macafee entry 1272, is in a collector cluster with strong independent Inishrush surname anchors. This is a geographic lead only. The return explicitly has no townland for either Hugh, and the sequence appears to cross localities.

### 1772 notice

The Belfast News-Letter notice dated Innisrush 6 April 1772 calls its signatories `Inhabitants of the Parish of Tamlaght O'Crilly` and says they surrendered arms to Henry Ellis of Inishrush. It names Hugh Glasgow, but does not call him of Inishrush or give age, occupation or relatives. Under the late traditional chronology, either the older Hugh (about 66) or younger Hugh (about 33) could be the subscriber. The list cannot separate them.

### Tradition audit

William Melanchthon Glasgow's history, written roughly 160 years later, claims the older Hugh was born in Tyrone about 1706, moved to Londonderry, worked as a weaver for McCullough, married Margaret McCullough in 1733, and had John, Robert and Hugh. Entry 1272's adjacency to Hugh Ellis and James McCullough is coherent with that story but does not prove it. The same history incorrectly says the younger Hugh died in 1824; his original will was signed in 1827. Its 1796 emigration paragraph follows the younger Hugh's household narrative and should not be assigned to the older Hugh.

## Ellis, Dobbin, and descendant-paper audit (2026-07-16)

### Result

The Ellis associative lead does not presently verify Hugh Glasgow's identity or marriage. Public catalogues and transcripts prove a substantial Ellis-Dobbin family and property network at Inishrush, but none calls a Glasgow or McCullough an Ellis relative, tenant, servant, beneficiary, executor, witness, debtor, or creditor. The four McCullough/McCulloch OCR hits reported in the 1874 Dobbin manuscript remain unusable without page context.

### Surviving records fixed precisely

- PRONI `T597/3`: will of Hercules Ellis of Innish Rush, dated 3 May 1750. NLI Sources `MS_UR_027542` exposes no contents. This is the best probate target.

### Evidential consequence

Collector adjacency in the 1740 householder return remains only an associative lead. It has not been corroborated by the accessible Ellis probate, deed, newspaper, catalogue, or descendant-paper material. Do not use Ellis proximity to select entry 1272 or to verify Margaret McCullough.
