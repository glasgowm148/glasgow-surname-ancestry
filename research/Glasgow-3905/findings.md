# Findings: Glasgow-3905

Last updated: 2026-08-19

## Current answer

`Glasgow-3905` is a **structural placeholder, not a documented person**. No
surviving record identifies Adam Glasgow's father or supplies a name, birth,
residence, wife, occupation, religion or death. Retain it only as an explicitly
uncertain structural placeholder until the branch can be placed beneath a
source-identified ancestor. It is not documentary proof of any parentage.

The WikiTree API supplies decisive profile provenance. [Adam Glasgow
(`Glasgow-3902`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3902) was created at
12:11:11 UTC on 16 July 2026. [Unknown Glasgow
(`Glasgow-3905`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3905) was created by the
same project account only 32 minutes later, at 12:43:34 UTC. Unknown's biography
expressly says that it is a research placeholder and `does not represent a man
identified in any surviving record`. It is therefore not an inherited profile,
an unidentified entry from a source, or an independent record duplicate of Adam.

Do **not** merge Unknown into Adam. WikiTree's [official merging
guidance](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Help:Merging) says that profiles are
merged when they represent the same person. These profiles were deliberately
created to represent different generations; merging them would falsely equate
Adam with his own hypothetical father. Preserve the placeholder only for working
topology, with every retained relationship marked uncertain. Do not infer a name,
vitals, spouse or parents, or treat the edge as evidence.

Its attachment as a child of Robert Glasgow-1143 and Rachel Wilson is unsupported.
The profile represents an unidentified Inishrush parent generation, while the
1817 Wilson inheritance litigation says Rachel died leaving James and Ann.
Do not use Robert and Rachel as identified parents without a relational source.

The two 1740 Hugh Glasgow householders establish an earlier local surname
population, not Adam's father. `Glasgow-3931` must not be attached as a tree
father; it can remain a research lead only. The
younger Hugh-540 is materially weakened by omitting living Adam from his 1827
will; Robert-550 remains undocumented. Older Adam and Maria are a naming-pattern
lead only and must not be created or attached.

John-822 is probably the separate John Glasco enumerated at Inishrush in 1831,
but no record says he and Adam were brothers. Cousin or another collateral
relationship remains viable. Any retained topology involving John or the
record-defined duplicate Glasgow-3927 must be marked uncertain and must not be
presented as a documented relationship. Do not place the placeholder beneath
Robert-1143 without a relational source.

## Catalogue and scoring audit

The automated system becomes useful only when run against Adam, not against the
placeholder. Its apparent leaders were [James Glasgow II
(`Glasgow-1024`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1024) and [Hugh Glasgow
(`Glasgow-548`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-548), both capped at 39,
followed by [Hugh Glasgow (`Glasgow-540`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-540)
and [Robert Glasgow (`Glasgow-550`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-550).
Manual review found two contaminated inputs:

* the child list included unproved [Alexander Glasgow
  (`Glasgow-3904`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3904) and [Robert
  Glasgow (`Glasgow-1367`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1367), causing

* the map gave exact Inishrush coordinates to Hugh-548, John-549, Robert-550,
  Hugh-540 and the placeholder even though the sources do not place those men
  there. Those rows supplied an artificial exact-locality match.

The map inputs have been corrected: the placeholder now has no mapped occurrence;
Hugh-548 is mapped only to his traditional County Tyrone origin; John-549 and
Robert-550 only to broad County Londonderry tradition; and Hugh-540 only to the
late `not far from Portglenone` description. The remaining name repetitions are
research clues, not independent evidence.

The rebuilt automated shortlist for Adam is [James Glasgow
II](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1024) (39), [Robert
Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1143) (39), [Hugh

remain better search targets because they are contemporary local occurrences,

After that audit, the strongest **record-defined** candidates are the two adult

## Candidate-father assessment — James Glasgow II

[James Glasgow II (Glasgow-1024)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1024)
is not a defensible father for this profile. The former 74-point score was
circular: [Arthur Glasgow (Glasgow-649)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-649)
appeared as an “exact sibling” only because the project hypothesis register
already labelled Arthur a *possible* brother of this placeholder. James is
attached as Arthur's father, so the scorer recycled one unproved hypothesis to
support another. No source identifies Arthur as the placeholder's brother.

The remaining evidence is insufficient:

* Robert Glasgow's 1834 pension declaration says only that he was born in
  County Derry in 1749 and came to America in 1769; it names no father,
  siblings or Moneymore residence.
* David Faris's 1990 catalogue summary calls Robert a son of James Glasgow of
  Moneymore and says Robert emigrated with three brothers and a sister. The
  protected book and its underlying citations have not been inspected, and the
  summary does not name an Adam or an unidentified sibling who remained in
  Ireland.
* Ellen Glasgow's much later account instead calls Arthur a sixteen-year-old
  ward who emigrated with his mother and elder brothers in 1766, and places the
  Irish family in Counties Down and Antrim. It does not name Arthur's father.
* The candidate's own reconstructed household is chronologically unreliable:
  James is estimated born in 1721, but attached son Joseph is estimated born in
  1735, when James would have been about fourteen and attached wife Mary
  Harkness about ten. The profile also combines a Pennsylvania naturalisation
  claim with a structured death at Moneymore.
* Arthur, Nancy and Eliza recurrences are descendant naming patterns, not
  relational records. Arthur is independently explained in the John-822 line
  by John's wife Mary Arthur.

The biological topology “James of Moneymore → unidentified child → Adam of
Inishrush” is not impossible if the estimated dates are changed, but nothing
found supports it. Do not attach James-1024 as this placeholder's father. The
catalogue now excludes structural placeholders from potential-parent scoring
and prevents a merely possible sibling from earning exact-sibling points.

## Additional evidence for the 1831 John identity

The family register for [John Glasgow, born 10 April 1767](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-822) and Mary Arthur records nine children: [Rose Ann Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-821) (4 March 1799), Maria (22 September 1800), Jane (5 October 1802), [Adam Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3373) (7 July 1804), Lavina, [James Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1165) (9 November 1808), Margaret, [John Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1617) (4 March 1813), and [Arthur Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1608) (1 July 1815). After married daughter Rose and deceased son James are removed, John and sons Adam, John and Arthur account for exactly four males. Mary and daughters Maria, Lavina and Margaret account for the four females. Jane was probably the unnamed wife in John Gray's separate two-person Presbyterian household at Boveedy: descendant evidence says she married John Gray in Ireland, and he remained a Boveedy landholder in 1833. This is a near-exact household reconstruction, although the census does not name Jane.

The son order Adam-James-John-Arthur fits the traditional paternal-grandfather, maternal-grandfather, father sequence, while Maria is the second daughter. This makes an older Adam and Maria a useful naming hypothesis for John's parents. It does not identify them, prove that Adam-3902 shared those parents or justify attaching John beneath this placeholder.

John's 20 March 1847 letter mentions a letter from "Adam" dated December 1846. Adam-3902 died on 30 June 1844, so the correspondent was probably John's son Adam-3373, not the older Inishrush Adam. The letter therefore adds evidence that the writer belonged to the same Inishrush family network. The report's James is now compellingly identified as younger James `Glasgow-1623`: his original 1851 marriage calls him a farmer of Inishrush and son of Adam, and calls his wife Rosanna a farm servant of Tyanee and daughter of Henry Barkley. This does not identify the reported child or connect Alexander `Glasgow-951` to the family.

Maria's documented marriage to Alexander Wallace and children born at Rasharkin in 1840 and 1842 provide a concrete Inishrush-to-Rasharkin connection, placing this collateral family in the same parish as the later Lisnagaver Glasgows.
