# Original-source audit

## What is proved about Adam

- Gravestone: Adam died 30 June 1844 aged 86; wife Rose died 31 July 1833 aged 66; the inscription names daughter Eliza, son James, and grandson William.
- 1831 census: Adam Glasco headed house 91 at Inishrush, containing three males and five females, all Presbyterian. No names or ages were recorded for the other seven people.
- 1842 marriage notice: Nancy was the sixth daughter of Adam Glasgow of Inishrush. The notice supplies no age or mother.
- 1833 tithe transcription: Adam was the only Glasgow occupier transcribed in Tamlaght O'Crilly.

## Candidate Robert `Glasgow-550`

- WikiTree fields: born about 1737, County Londonderry; died about 1787. No cited record supports either statement.
- WikiTree biography: discusses two 1831 Lissan households and explicitly frames their descent as a theory. It supplies no event concerning the candidate himself.
- William Melanchthon Glasgow history: late family tradition names Hugh's sons John, Robert, and Hugh. It gives Robert no dates, residence, wife, or descendants.
- Result: the candidate is a name in late tradition, not a documented County Londonderry individual.

## Robert of Loy, County Tyrone

- PRONI Names, Armagh Diocesan Wills: Robert Glasgow of Loy; will dated 19 May 1823.
- PRONI Names, Armagh Diocesan Administration Bonds: Robert Glasgow, gentleman, of Loy; 1825.
- Cookstown rent-roll transcription: Robert Glasgow paid rent of 4 pounds 10 shillings in 1786-1788.
- Registry of Deeds index: Robert Glasgow junior, gentleman, of Cookstown appears as a witness in 1817.
- Limitation: no record connects this man to Adam, and Loy is about 29 km from Inishrush. The main pre-1858 probate archive was destroyed in 1922; no copy of his will was found.

## Inishrush deeds

An exact and spelling-variant townland search found these relevant memorials:

- 57951, 29 March 1734: Conolly to Ellis and four other Inishrush gentlemen; named lives were Ellis, Wilson, and McCleland youths. No Glasgow.
- 128477: Ellis to Dobbyn.
- 143969 and 143970: Conolly/Murphy/Ellis transactions.
- 192358: Huey to Ellis.
- 269051 and 365181: Conolly to Ellis.
- 283410: Murphy to Ellis.
- 456488, 497435, and 503676: Inishrush mill and toll transactions, 1811-1820.
- 534422, 540207, 540252, 542089, 544742, and 545151: Inishrush transactions, 1821-1825.

No indexed principal party is named Glasgow. Townland indexing can omit under-tenants, occupiers, boundaries, and some witnesses, so the result is not proof that the family was absent.

## Directly supported children

- James: proved by gravestone.
- Eliza: proved by gravestone.
- Nancy: proved by marriage notice; description as sixth daughter proves at least five elder daughters.
- William: proved only as Adam's grandson; his parents are not named.
- Robert, John, Samuel, Alexander, and Daniel: no contemporary record found naming Adam as father. Their WikiTree attachments are hypotheses and cannot support a grandparental naming argument.

## Earlier local surname evidence

- 1740: two separated Hugh Glasgow entries in the Protestant household return for Tamlaght O'Crilly.
- 1772: one Hugh Glasgow named among parish inhabitants surrendering arms to Henry Ellis of Inishrush.
- 1796: no Glasgow in the Tamlaght O'Crilly flax-bounty list.
- 1831: Adam and John were the two Presbyterian Glasgow householders at Inishrush.

These records prove locality and continuity, not relationships.

William Melanchthon Glasgow's late account says Hugh `Glasgow-548` married
Margaret McCullough and that the eldest son of Hugh `Glasgow-540` was born on
the family farm near Portglenone in 1780. Read with the 1740 Hugh/Ellis/McCullough
collector cluster and the 1772 parish Hugh, this makes Adam and John probable
members of Hugh-548's extended family. It does not identify Adam's father.

## John born 1767 and the naming pattern

The family Bible records John and Mary Arthur's children in this order: Rose, Maria, Jane, Adam, Lavina, James, Margaret, John, Arthur. By sex, the sons are Adam, James, John and Arthur. The first three positions match the traditional Scottish paternal-grandfather, maternal-grandfather and father sequence, with father John correctly repeated third; Maria is the second daughter, the conventional paternal-grandmother position. The Bible does not state the relationships, so older Adam and Maria are leading candidate names rather than proved parents.

## DNA

The public surname project contains a Northern Irish paternal cluster, but no displayed tester is documented as a male-line descendant of target Adam or candidate Robert. It cannot resolve the parentage question.

## Proof status

- Proved: Adam's Inishrush identity, wife Rose, children James/Eliza/Nancy, at least six daughters, and an unidentified grandson William.
- Strong locality evidence: Hugh Glasgow in Adam's parish before and during Adam's youth.
- Possible only: Robert `Glasgow-550`, John `Glasgow-549`, or a Hugh as Adam's father.
- Unsupported as fact: candidate Robert's 1737 Londonderry birth and 1787 death; entering older Adam/Maria as proved parents; use of attached speculative sons to infer Adam's father. Older Adam/Maria remains the leading naming-pattern hypothesis.
- The second 1740 Hugh provides a cleaner possible root for older Adam/Maria than
  adding an unreported fourth son to Hugh-548. If choosing only between
  Hugh-548's traditionally named sons, the evidence does not distinguish
  John-549 from Robert-550. Do not insert the
  unidentified-father placeholder beneath either man; the additional generation
  is chronologically impossible before Adam's circa-1758 birth.
