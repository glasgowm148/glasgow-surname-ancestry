# Parentage assessment: Adam Glasgow of Inishrush

## Conclusion

**Adam Glasgow's father is not identified by the surviving online evidence.**
Adam and [John Glasgow (1767), Inishrush](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-822)
are probable brothers: they headed the only two Presbyterian Glasgow households
at Inishrush in 1831; John's family register nearly reconstructs his household
exactly; and his 1847 letter shows continued intimate knowledge of Adam's
Inishrush-Tyanee network. No record uses a kin term, so cousinship remains
possible.

The strongest pre-Adam evidence is for men named **Hugh Glasgow in Adam's own
parish**: two separate adult Hugh householders in the 1740 Protestant return and
an unidentified Hugh among Tamlaght O'Crilly inhabitants in 1772. One 1740 Hugh
immediately precedes Hugh Ellis and James McCullough in an Inishrush-linked
collector cluster. The late William Melanchthon Glasgow account independently
names Margaret McCullough as Hugh `Glasgow-548`'s wife and says their grandson
John was born on the family farm near Portglenone in 1780. The combined surname,
neighbour, parish and farm evidence makes Adam and John high-probability members
of Hugh-548's extended cluster, but no record supplies the intervening kinship.

The younger Hugh Glasgow's Allegheny will and loose packet are now precisely located. The packet is DGS 5538049 film images 239-243, bounded by the next cover at image 244; a separate full-resolution will-book scan makes the text readable. The 29 March 1827 will names sons John, Hugh, Alexander, and Robert, daughter Nancy, granddaughter Sarah, and an unnamed wife, but no Adam. Adam was alive, and each named son received property, so the omission is material evidence against younger Hugh as his father. It is not conclusive because the will never says it lists all children and also omits Clatworthy and Mary from the late seven-child account.

Recommended WikiTree treatment: detach and retire the unidentified-parent
placeholder; do not merge it into Adam, because the profiles do not represent
the same person. Leave Adam's parents blank and retain candidate names only in
Research Notes. An older Adam and Maria are the best internally consistent
parent-name model. The cleanest extension is for that older Adam to descend from
the *other* Hugh recorded in the parish in 1740; alternatively, he could have
been an omitted son or close nephew of Hugh-548. If the father must instead be
selected from Hugh-548's traditionally named sons, John `Glasgow-549` and Robert
`Glasgow-550` are both possible but the evidence does not distinguish them.

## Robert `Glasgow-550`: source audit

Robert's profile has two asserted facts: birth about 1737 in County Londonderry and death about 1787. Neither is supported by its biography or sources.

The cited family history by William Melanchthon Glasgow was written after 1892, about 160 years after the events it describes. The full six-page scan calls its early account tradition and says Hugh Glasgow had three sons, John, Robert, and Hugh. It gives Robert no birth date, residence, wife, children, occupation, or death. It therefore supports only a late tradition that a son named Robert existed, not the WikiTree dates or a connection to Adam.

The profile's other discussion concerns John of Dromain and Robert of Coltrim in the 1831 census. Those householders were later-generation men. The Coltrim Robert had eleven people in his household and was about 25 km from Inishrush. The census gives no age or parentage and cannot prove that either man descended from `Glasgow-550`.

## Actual records for men named Robert

Several real records were found, but none can be identified as Adam's father:

| Record | What it proves | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cookstown/Loy rent roll, 1786-1788 | A Robert Glasgow paid rent at Loy, County Tyrone | Birth, wife, children, or any Inishrush connection |
| Registry of Deeds evidence, 1817 | Robert Glasgow junior, gentleman, of Cookstown witnessed a deed | Relationship to Adam or to the older Robert of Loy |
| Armagh diocesan will index, 19 May 1823; administration bond, 1825 | Robert Glasgow, gentleman, of Loy made a will; estate administration followed | Contents of the destroyed will or identity with `Glasgow-550` |
| Flax Growers List, 1796 | A Robert Glasgow received flax seed in Lissan parish, County Tyrone | Age, townland, household, or identity with any other Robert |
| Lissan tithe index, 1827 | Roberts occupied land at Coltrim and Drumnabragy | Their ages, parents, or relationship to Adam |

Loy is about 29 km (18 miles) from Inishrush and is in County Tyrone. If the Loy records concern `Glasgow-550`, his profile's death about 1787 is wrong by at least 36 years. If they concern another man, they provide no evidence about `Glasgow-550`. Either way, they do not confirm Adam's parentage.

PRONI's official guidance explains that the main body of pre-1858 Irish wills and administrations was destroyed in 1922. The surviving indexes establish the Loy probate events, but no copy or abstract of this will was found online.

## Adam's documented family

The descendant scrape produced eight attached children, but attachment is not proof.

| Attached child | Evidence naming Adam as father | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Eliza, died aged 26 | Same gravestone calls her Adam and Rose's daughter | Proved |
| Nancy | 1842 marriage notice calls her Adam of Inishrush's sixth daughter | Proved; birth year not proved |
| Robert of Glenleslie, born about 1779 | None; 1851 census says Antrim-born, and Rose was about twelve in 1779 | Unsupported and chronologically implausible |
| John of Lisnagaver, born about 1801 | None; census says Antrim-born | Unproved |
| Samuel of Lisnagaver, born about 1790 | The 1851 census calls him John Glasgow's brother; the uniquely matched 1846 marriage of that John names father John Glasgow, farmer | Adam attachment strongly excluded; father John at high confidence, subject only to the unsupported possibility of paternal half-brothers |
| Alexander | A 2007 descendant post, no contemporary parent record | Unproved |
| Daniel of Kilrea | Records prove his own family and occupation, not his parents | Unproved |

The gravestone also names Adam's grandson William, who died in 1882 aged 27, but does not identify William's parents. Nancy's description as the sixth daughter proves a larger family whose names are mostly missing. The 1831 household totals (three males and five females) provide neither names nor ages. Consequently, the attached sons cannot be used to reverse-engineer Adam's father.

### Focused review of the four disputed sons

| Man | Result | Evidence strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Robert of Glenleslie (`Glasgow-1367`) | Not confirmed; the attachment is chronologically implausible | His 1851 census age implies birth about 1779 in County Antrim. Rose's gravestone age implies she was only about twelve. No earlier wife or parent statement has been found. |
| Alexander (`Glasgow-3904`) | Not confirmed | The older Alexander/Rosanna family is reconstructed from a 2007 descendant account and an unidentified 1834 tithe occupier. No contemporary record names Adam or Rose as his parent. |
| Samuel of Lisnagaver (`Glasgow-2908`) | Strongly excluded as Adam's son | Samuel is directly called brother of John in 1851; John's exactly matched original 1846 marriage names his father as John Glasgow, farmer. |
| Daniel of Kilrea (`Glasgow-3896`) | Not confirmed | Daniel's original will proves son David, late brother Thomas and nephew Daniel Neely Glasgow, but names no Adam, Rose, James or Inishrush property. |

Only Samuel has yielded a parent-level proof chain, and that chain points away
from Adam. None of the four can currently be used as a confirmed child of Adam.

There is also a decisive topology conflict in the current tree. Daniel's original
will calls Thomas his brother, while WikiTree makes Daniel a son of Adam and Thomas
a son of Adam's alleged son Alexander. That arrangement makes Daniel Thomas's uncle.
At least one of the Adam-Alexander, Adam-Daniel, or Alexander-Thomas attachments is
wrong. The direct will statement takes precedence over the late Alexander/Rosanna
account. See the [four-son assessment](four_sons_assessment.md).

## Local evidence before Adam

1. Two separate adult Hugh Glasgow householders appear in the 1740 Protestant return for Tamlaght O'Crilly. The second occurs immediately before Ellis and McCullough households in a sequence with several independently documented Inishrush surnames, but both Hugh entries have townland `Not specified` and the collector sequence can cross localities. Neither man can be younger Hugh `Glasgow-540` under his broadly supported chronology.
2. In April 1772 a Hugh Glasgow was among people described collectively as Tamlaght O'Crilly parish inhabitants surrendering arms to Henry Ellis of Inishrush. The notice does not say that Hugh lived at Inishrush and cannot distinguish the older Hugh, younger Hugh, or another namesake.
3. Newspaper notices show estate relettings at Inishrush in 1758, 1764, and 1792. The 1764 notice offered Inishrush and Inishrush Lile from 1 November but named no incoming or outgoing tenant. These dates identify lease-book targets, not a Glasgow tenancy.
4. PRONI `T877/216` is now identified as Thomas Conolly's 1800 lease of the Inishrush mill and seven plantation acres to Joseph Courtney. It is not the reported 1801 four-family lease and not a direct Glasgow tenancy record.
5. The exact-townland Registry of Deeds index contains the 1734 Inishrush head lease and seven further eighteenth-century Inishrush transactions. The named principal parties include Conolly, Ellis, Dobbyn, Murphy, and Huey, but no Glasgow. A subtenant could be omitted, so this is negative evidence rather than proof of absence.
6. The 1796 County Londonderry flax list has no Glasgow in Tamlaght O'Crilly. It was a bounty list, not a census.
7. Adam and John headed the two Presbyterian Glasgow households at Inishrush in 1831; Adam alone appears in the parish tithe transcription in 1833.

This chain supports a long-lived local surname cluster. It does not identify which Hugh was present in 1772, whether he was Adam's father or grandfather, or whether Adam arrived from elsewhere.

The late history says younger Hugh's son John was born on a County Londonderry farm near Portglenone and that the family emigrated. Hugh's original will now corrects that derivative chronology: it was signed 29 March 1827 and proved 25 April 1831, not preceded by a death in 1824. Its named family and omission of Adam materially weaken younger Hugh as father, as assessed above, but do not identify which 1740 Hugh household he belonged to.

## What the complete naming pattern contributes

John Glasgow born 1767 had his first daughter Rose at Inishrush in 1799. His family Bible orders the children Rose, Maria, Jane, Adam, Lavina, James, Margaret, John and Arthur. By sex, the sons were Adam, James, John and Arthur; the daughters began Rose, Maria and Jane.

Scotland's People describes the traditional sequence as first son for the paternal grandfather, second son for the maternal grandfather, third son for the father, and second daughter for the paternal grandmother. John's first three sons fit `Adam - James - John`; the third position correctly reproduces father John. Maria occupies the paternal-grandmother position. This internal fit makes older Adam and Maria a meaningful shared-parent hypothesis, not merely two isolated matching names. The third daughter Jane does not reproduce mother Mary, and naming customs were not universal, so it remains indirect evidence rather than a parent statement.

The same John also used Rose, Adam and James, the names of Adam of Inishrush, his wife and their proved son. John's son Arthur named his own first son Adam, and other descendants repeated Adam. These repetitions strengthen close kinship but cannot identify which person each child honoured.

## DNA result

The public Glasgow Y-DNA project shows several Northern Irish Glasgow lines in the broad `R-U106 > BY139800 > S14827 > FT4811 > FT32941 > FT25406` cluster. No displayed test is documented as a strict male-line descendant of Adam of Inishrush, and no test is documented from `Glasgow-550`. The project supports remote shared paternal ancestry but cannot choose among Robert, John, Hugh, or another man as Adam's father.

| Hypothesis | Direct support | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Other 1740 Hugh -> older Adam and Maria | Two distinct adult Hugh households are directly recorded; entry 1272 is the better Hugh-548 candidate because it adjoins McCullough, leaving entry 1195 as a separate local root | Best structural extension of the naming model; no record names the second Hugh's children or proves kinship between the two Hughs |
| Older Adam and Maria as shared parents | John's sons `Adam-James-John` fit paternal grandfather, maternal grandfather and father; Maria is the second daughter/paternal-grandmother position | Best internally consistent parent-name model; plausible, not proved |
| John `Glasgow-549` as direct father | Late account names him as Hugh-548's son; chronology and regional locality work | Possible, but the observed first-son name is Adam, not John; no direct advantage over Robert survives |
| Robert `Glasgow-550` as direct father | Late account names him as Hugh-548's son; chronology is possible | Possible; no Inishrush placement, wife, child or reliable dates; no evidence distinguishes him from John-549 |
| One of the 1740 Hugh householders as direct father | Direct earlier-generation parish presence | Possible but unidentifiable; the return gives no townland, age, wife or children |
| Younger Hugh `Glasgow-540` as direct father | His family farm was near Portglenone | Materially weakened: only about nineteen years older than Adam, and his 1827 will names four living sons but omits Adam |

An extra unnamed generation cannot be inserted between John-549 or Robert-550
and Adam born about 1758. If one of those men was Adam's ancestor, he must be the
direct father, not the father of another adult son who then fathered Adam. This
is why the placeholder `Glasgow-3905` must not be attached beneath either man.

## Remaining record capable of proving the case

The best surviving series is the townland-arranged Vintners lease book PRONI
`D1062/1/5` (about 1730-1826) and the Vintners' proportion rental material
`D2094/50-51`, `D2094/75-78`, and `T2825/C/19/1-13` (1775-1801). The official
catalogue descriptions were verified, but the documents are not digitized and
the catalogues expose no occupier names. A public selective transcription of
Lee-family Vintners entries contains no Inishrush or Glasgow entry; because it
is not a complete rental transcription, that is not negative evidence. Other
undigitized possibilities are Hercules Ellis's 1750 will (`T597/3`), Drimbolg
session records (`MIC1C/15`), and Ellis estate papers. None can honestly be
reported as evidence until its contents are examined.
