# The Mathie–Glasgow–Glencairn bridge, 1554–1618

## Conclusion

The Mathie surname now supplies a real documentary bridge between two early
Glasgow settings, although it does not yet prove a Glasgow pedigree.

- In 1554 David Mathy and Robert Glasgow were fellow Glasgow burgesses and
  witnesses to the same sasine.
- David's son John was recorded in 1571; a John Mathie was acting as a writer
  in 1574 and entered the burgess roll with the Earl of Glencairn's group in
  1583. These may be successive appearances of the same man.
- A John Mathie of Clerkland had a daughter Katherine and both a John senior
  and John junior in his family circle in 1600.
- An older John Mathie, Glencairn's servant, was killed in July 1606. A second
  John Mathie wrote a Glencairn bond that September. The published scholarly
  interpretation is that the second man was probably the victim's son.
- Tutory retours entered on 20 January 1607 identify the deceased John Mathie
  of Ibert's brother Matthew, a Glasgow burgess, and John's lawful children
  James, David, Agnes and Margaret. The timing, Glencairn ownership of
  Kilmaronock and the September bond's execution there make the Ibert man a
  strong candidate for the servant killed in July.
- Jonet Mathie, who married John Glasgow in 1618, may belong to this
  Clerkland–Glencairn household. The younger writer is the best current
  candidate for her father or close older kinsman, but no record yet names her
  father.

This provides a coherent institutional and family corridor from Robert
Glasgow's burgh circle to the Glencairn estate network in which Matthew,
Michael and the two Corsankell James Glasgows occur in 1559–60.

## Identity controls

The Glasgow cordiner family is separate. David Mathie and John Mathie,
cordiners and burgesses, were called brothers in 1607, and the burgess roll
later calls John the son and heir of deceased Allan Mathie. That John should
not be merged with David Mathy's son or the Glencairn writer.

The proposed sequence—David's son John, the 1574 writer, the 1583 Glencairn
burgess and John senior of Clerkland—is strong circumstantial reconstruction,
not direct identity proof. It is supported by name, occupation, chronology,
patronage and geography. The pairing of John senior/junior in 1600 with the
older and younger Glencairn servitors in 1606 is particularly persuasive.

The Ibert retours sharpen rather than close the question. Their John died at
exactly the right time to be the servant killed in July 1606, his holding lay
in Glencairn's Kilmaronock barony, and the later bond was executed there. His
brother Matthew was a Glasgow burgess. The tutory entries name four lawful
children but not Jonet or a son John. Because these were appointments of a
paternal tutor, an older child outside tutory could be absent; the omission
therefore weakens but does not eliminate either the proposed younger son John
or Jonet as an older daughter.

## Earlier Glasgow-area Mathie families

The Glasgow rental and protocol books show that Mathies were established
around the city well before 1554:

- Thomas Mathy witnessed at Monkland in 1503.
- William Mathy alias Johnson died by May 1513. The alias shows that `Mathy`
  was not always a stable hereditary surname.
- In 1527 a respite for the Kittycrocehill rising beside Glasgow named Mathy
  Thome and his sons Robert, John, Thomas and William Mathy. The wording may
  reflect patronymic naming rather than a settled surname.
- Gilbert Durand alias Mathy held land at Carmyle by 1527. In 1533 the rental
  directly names his brother Thomas, son William and daughter Marion. Thomas
  died without bodily heirs in 1545; his widow was Isobel Scott.
- John Mathy and wife Margaret Bell had a documented son Mungo in 1542.
- James Mathe held an urban parcel on the same South Port–Clyde Bridge street
  in which Robert and Archibald Glasgow were active in 1551.

These records establish several real Mathie households from which David could
have emerged, but none calls him a son or brother. The aliases and patronymic
forms also warn against collapsing every early Mathie into one family. Source:
the [Glasgow diocesan protocol and rental book](https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/8030/80303212.23.pdf),
especially rental-book pp. 88, 102–105, 120 and 127; and the 1527 Privy Seal
respite, no. 3787.

## Relevance to the early Glasgows

Three connected observations now matter:

1. Robert Glasgow and David Mathy had a direct civic association in 1554.
2. The Mathies subsequently entered the Earl of Glencairn's household network.
3. The 1559–60 Glencairn-area teind list includes three Mathies and at least
   four Glasgow men: Matthew at Ardeer Nether, Michael at Brakpleuch/Dubbs, and
   James elder and younger at Corsankell.

If John Glasgow who married Jonet in 1618 was Robert the fisher's son, as the
existing evidence presently suggests, their marriage may continue an
association between the descendants of the 1554 co-witnesses. If Jonet came
from the Clerkland household, it also links that city branch socially to the
same Glencairn network as the Stevenston/Corsankell Glasgows. Neither inference
proves that the Glasgow branches shared a father; it does identify the estate
and household records most likely to contain the missing bridge.

## Ranked hypotheses

1. **Moderate-to-strong:** John Mathie of Ibert was the Glencairn servant
   killed in July 1606. Date, place, lordship and the September bond align.
2. **Moderate:** the second John Mathie in 1606 was an older son/junior of the
   slain man and the junior named at Katherine's 1600 marriage. His absence
   from the tutory retours is compatible with, but does not prove, adulthood.
3. **Moderate:** John Mathie, son of David, was the writer recorded in 1574 and
   the Glencairn-sponsored burgess of 1583.
4. **Low-to-moderate:** Jonet Mathie was a daughter or close younger relative
   of the younger Glencairn writer.
5. **Low-to-moderate:** the 1618 Glasgow–Mathie marriage continued the civic
   relationship documented between Robert Glasgow and David Mathy in 1554.

## Best next records

1. Obtain the 1607 testament of **John Mathie of Ibert, Kilmaronock** and test
   the proved retours family against its spouse, executor, cautioners, debts
   and associates.
2. Inspect NRS `C22/3` for the full January 1607 tutory returns, including the
   children's ages or wording omitted from the printed abstracts.
3. Inspect the original 1618 Glasgow marriage entry for residence, witnesses
   or marginal detail absent from the index.
4. Search Glencairn papers for `Mathie`, `Mathy`, `Mathe`, Clerkland and Ibert,
   especially household wages, bonds and succession after July 1606.
5. Inspect the original 1559–60 teind litigation for the ordering, sureties and
   any omitted designations attached to the Ardeer and Corsankell entries.

## Negative findings

- No inspected record names Jonet Mathie's father. She is not among the four
  children named in John of Ibert's tutory retours.
- No inspected record directly identifies the 1571 son of David with the 1574,
  1583, 1600 or 1606 John occurrences.
- The teind list does not state that Matthew Glasgow and John Mathie were kin.
- The Mathie evidence does not yet prove a parent–child link between the
  Glasgow burgh and Corsankell families.
