== Biography ==
Michael Glasgow was probably born about 1525, perhaps in Ayrshire. The date and place are uncertain estimates based on his documented appearance as a witness at Stevenston in 1549.

On 28 January 1549 Michael Glasgow witnessed an instrument of sasine in favour of Janet Cunningham, daughter of John Cunningham of Caprington, and the heirs to be born between her and her husband Alexander, Earl of Glencairn, in the lands and barony of Stevenston and other lands. William Cunningham of Legland and Adam Mure were among the other witnesses.<ref name="NRS1549">National Records of Scotland, Charter Chest of the Earls of Glencairn, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F1%2F59 GD39/1/59], instrument of sasine in favour of Janet Cunningham and Alexander, Earl of Glencairn, 28 January 1549. The official catalogue entry was inspected; no manuscript image is available online.</ref>

In the Cunninghame teind lists of 1559–60, Michael Glasgow was listed with John Sparling, John Wilson, and Robert Wilson under Brakpleuch Over and Nether in the parish of Stevenston, Ayrshire.<ref name="Teinds1559">Margaret H. B. Sanderson, ''The People of Sixteenth Century Ayrshire'', ''Ayrshire Collections'', vol. 14, no. 8 (Ayrshire Archaeological and Natural History Society, 1987), appendix "The Cunninghame Teind Lists of 1559–60," [https://aanhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/people-of-ayrshire.pdf#page=36 p. 331], Brakpleuch Over and Nether entry. Sanderson identifies the underlying record as Scottish Record Office, Register of Acts and Decreets, vol. 22, fols. 102v–167v.</ref>

The uncommon name and the repeated Stevenston/Glencairn estate setting make it probable that the witness of 1549 and the tenant of 1559–60 were the same man. The records do not supply an occupation, patronymic, or exact property chain, so this identification remains an evidence-based inference.

=== Mathie–Glencairn network ===

The Stevenston records show a broader association rather than an isolated cluster. The 1559–60 list also names John, Margaret and William Mathie, [[Glasgow-3908|Matthew Glasgow]] and [[Glasgow-1168|James Glasgow elder]] with his son [[Glasgow-3921|James]], across neighbouring holdings.<ref name="Teinds1559"/> In Glasgow, David Mathy and [[Glasgow-1096|Robert Glasgow]] were fellow burgesses witnessing the same sasines in 1554.<ref name="Mathie1554">Robert Renwick, ed., Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow, vol. 1, protocols 183–184, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc07robegoog/page/n90/mode/2up 10 May 1554, printed p. 63].</ref>

David’s son John is a plausible match for a later writer in the Earl of Glencairn’s circle. Tutory retours entered in 1607 identify the deceased John Mathie of Ibert’s full brother Matthew as a Glasgow burgess and name John’s lawful children James, David, Agnes and Margaret.<ref name="Ibert1607">Inquisitionum ad Capellam Domini Regis Retornatarum ... Abbreviatio, vol. 2, tutory retours 100–101, 20 January 1607, [https://books.google.com/books?id=PiVDAAAAcAAJ digitised volume]; underlying register, National Records of Scotland, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browsetreeview.aspx?reference=C22 C22/3].</ref> This gives Michael’s estate circle a documented route into Glasgow burgh society, although it does not state how the Glasgow tenants were related.

== Research Notes ==
The same teind-list page also names a Michael Glasgow with Finlay Brown and James Morton under Dubbs.<ref name="Teinds1559"/> Sanderson warns that one tenant could hold more than one property. The Brakpleuch and Dubbs entries may therefore describe one Michael Glasgow or two contemporaries.

No reviewed source identifies Michael's parents, spouse, children, precise birthplace, or death. [[Glasgow-1168|James Glasgow]], [[Glasgow-3908|Matthew Glasgow]], and the other Glasgow surname bearers in the 1559 Stevenston rental form a local record cluster, but co-tenancy and locality do not prove kinship. No parent has therefore been attached.

This Michael is not [[Glasgow-1406|Michael Glasgow]], whose estimated birth was more than a century later.

=== Possible family placement ===
Michael is best placed as a probable brother or cousin, rather than a son, of [[Glasgow-1168|James Glasgow elder of Corsankell]]. Michael was already an adult legal witness in the Glencairn estate network on 28 January 1549,<ref>National Records of Scotland, Charter Chest of the Earls of Glencairn, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F1%2F59 GD39/1/59], 28 January 1549.</ref> and both men were present in the Stevenston-area tenancy landscape in 1559-60.<ref>Margaret H. B. Sanderson, ''The People of Sixteenth Century Ayrshire'', appendix “The Cunninghame Teind Lists of 1559-60,” [https://aanhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/people-of-ayrshire.pdf#page=36 p. 331].</ref>

The generation and repeated microregion support close collateral kinship, but no source identifies either man's father or the exact degree. No structured father should be inferred from this cluster.

=== Selected local working father, 15 October 2026 ===
[[Glasgow-1098|William Glasgow of Saltmarket]] is the selected '''uncertain, low-confidence''' father in the local research tree. The existing Stevenston study places Michael as a probable brother or cousin of James elder of Corsankell, rather than his son. Michael was an adult witness in the Glencairn estate network in 1549; both appear in the same small estate landscape in the 1559–60 teind list. Their approximate 1525 and 1515 generations fit brothers. Select the brother option provisionally and extend James’s already uncertain father William to Michael. This uses an existing older household without inventing an intervening person. The Mathie–Glencairn network supplies a credible route between the burgh family and these estates.

The brother-versus-cousin choice is unresolved, and James’s own father William is uncertain. No record calls Michael William’s son. A cousin through another burgh household, an older generation than the estimated 1525 birth, or a separate family remains possible. Brakpleuch and Dubbs may represent one Michael or two. No mother, exact birth or death is inferred.
