== Biography ==

'''William Glasgow''' lived at Quothquan (written “Quodquen”) in the parish of Libberton, Lanarkshire. His testament was recorded in the Lanark Commissary Court on 9 April 1673, in register CC14/5/9.<ref name="LanarkIndex">Francis J. Grant, ed., ''The Commissariot Record of Lanark: Register of Testaments, 1595–1800'' (Edinburgh: Scottish Record Society, 1903), printed p. 38, entry “Glasgow, William, in Quodquen,” [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/the_commissariot_record_of_lanark__register_of_testaments__1595-1800_~_1903.pdf#page=50 digital copy].</ref> He had died by that date.

The published index does not state his age, occupation, spouse, children or parents. All three original testament images were visually reviewed on 10 September 2026. The resulting selective working transcription is not a complete diplomatic transcription; no securely read spouse, parent, child or sibling clause was recovered. This does not establish that the partly unresolved text contains no family information.

=== Original-image review, 10 September 2026 ===

The original is National Records of Scotland, Lanark Commissary Court, '''CC14/5/9, images 24-26'''. William's entry begins in the lower part of image 24 and concludes above the next testament on image 26. It describes livestock, crops, household possessions and clothing, states that no debts were owed to the deceased, and lists debts owed by him. George Brown and John Neilson appear in the executor-dative administration and sworn inventory procedure; William Neilson in Biggar appears as cautioner. These administrative roles do not establish kinship.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Lanark Commissary Court, testament dative of William Glasgow, 9 April 1673, CC14/5/9, images 24-26; original images inspected 10 September 2026. [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/search-records/legal-records/wills ScotlandsPeople wills collection].</ref>

The opening locality and death-date wording remain partly obscured. Quothquan is retained from the maintained index identification, not a newly resolved manuscript reading. Retain the death boundary before 9 April 1673; do not convert the tentative manuscript month fragment into a firm date. Full financial transcription and clearer reading of the opening and executor appointment remain outstanding. No new family attachment or identification with the Cavers/Ulster William follows from this review.
