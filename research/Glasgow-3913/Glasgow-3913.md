== Biography ==
'''John Glasgow''', recorded as '''Jo Glascho''', was a taxed householder at Kilwaughter Demesne in Kilwaughter parish, upper Glenarm barony, County Antrim, in the 1669 Hearth Money Roll.<ref name="Hearth1669">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, 1669 County Antrim Hearth Money Roll, T/307/A; W. Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/1660shearthmoneyrolls/1669hearthmoneyrollsantrim.pdf transcription], records 5347–5348, PDF p. 36.</ref>

He is probably the same man who appears repeatedly in the Larne and Kilwaughter Presbyterian congregational records about 1699–1700/1 as '''John Glasgow, senior''', representing Kilwaughter. If this identification is correct, his documented career extended for more than thirty years.

The designation ''senior'' is genealogically significant: it strongly suggests that another, younger John Glasgow was then known within the local community. That younger man's identity and relationship remain unknown.

=== Kilwaughter in 1669 ===

The 1669 Hearth Money Roll records:

:'''Jo. Glascho'''
:'''James Glascho'''

as immediately consecutive householders at Kilwaughter Demesne.<ref name="Hearth1669" />

The abbreviation ''Jo.'' most probably represents John. The exact co-location and uncommon surname make a close relationship between John and [[Glasgow-1081|James Glasgow]] probable, although the tax roll gives no ages, household members or relationship.

=== John Glasgow senior, c. 1699–1700 ===

Classon Porter later published substantial extracts from an early record of the Larne and Kilwaughter congregation. Porter stated that he had been unable to locate the original in his day and was working from a transcript made or procured for Rev. D. Davison, portions of which had already been published in 1826. Porter arranged his quotations by subject rather than strict chronology.<ref name="Porter2">Classon Porter, "Congregational Memoirs—Larne and Kilwaughter," ''The Christian Unitarian'', vol. 2 (1863–1864), pp. 410–415; [https://archive.org/details/christianunitari0002unse Internet Archive]. Porter describes the provenance of the transcript on p. 410.</ref>

On 3 March 1699/1700 the congregational record appointed four men to approach the Presbytery concerning Rev. William Ogilvie. One was:

<blockquote>"John Glasgow, sen."</blockquote><ref name="Porter2" />

Porter immediately reproduced the Presbytery's minute for the same deputation. It names the same four men — Samuel Smealley, William Hare, John Glasgow and James Callwell — but calls him simply '''John Glasgow'''.<ref name="Porter2" />

This is useful identity evidence. ''Senior'' was a contextual qualifier; the same man could be recorded without it in another record of the same transaction.

On 17 May, John again appears as:

<blockquote>"John Glasgow, sen., for Kilwochter"</blockquote>

He and Patrick Agnew bound for Kilwaughter's £8 share of the minister's stipend, together with obligations for firing, ploughing and harrowing.<ref name="Porter2" />

John was also among the heads of families entrusted with considering the rebuilding of the meeting house.<ref name="Porter2" /> These entries establish that he was a substantial member of the Kilwaughter congregation and a representative of its householders. They do not establish that he held the office of elder.

The following year's material again records plain '''John Glasgow''' acting for Kilwaughter. On 27 February 1700 in the record's dating, John Glasgow and Robert White were chosen for Kilwaughter to inspect the minister's land. In the same meeting, Patrick Agnew and John Glasgow were selected for Kilwaughter to consider repairs to the minister's house.<ref name="Porter3">Classon Porter, "Congregational Memoirs—Larne and Kilwaughter," ''The Christian Unitarian'', vol. 3 (1864), pp. 15–18; [https://archive.org/details/christianunitari0003unse Internet Archive].</ref>

The date falls in the period conventionally written 1700/1 when double-dating pre-1752 records.

The recurrence of:

* the same uncommon name;
* the same Kilwaughter locality;
* John being explicitly styled ''senior'';
* plain John being paired again with Patrick Agnew;
* and [[Glasgow-1081|James Glasgow]] also appearing in Kilwaughter affairs about 1700;

makes it more probable that the congregational John was the '''same man''' as Jo Glascho, householder at Kilwaughter in 1669, rather than an undocumented son of that man.<ref name="Porter3" />

The identification remains inferential because no surviving entry explicitly says that the 1669 and c.1700 men were identical.

=== Family ===

[[Glasgow-1081|James Glasgow]] was recorded immediately beside John at Kilwaughter in 1669.<ref name="Hearth1669" /> About 1700 a James Glasgow was appointed overseer for Kilwaughter during work on the minister's house.<ref name="Porter3" />

The repeated John-and-James presence in the same small district strengthens the case that they belonged to one close family cluster. Their exact relationship remains unknown.

John is currently placed as an uncertain son of [[Glasgow-3970|Robert Glasgow]], the Robert ''Glaskow'' associated with Larne during the period of the 1641 rebellion.<ref name="RobertLarne">Trinity College Dublin, MS 838, fol. 153r, examination of Con O'Sheale, 21 April 1653, identifier 838153r210; Robert is described as "one Robert Glaskow liuing in Larne"; [https://1641dep.abdn.ac.uk/items/show/42821.html 1641 Depositions Project transcription].</ref>

That parentage remains a reconstruction from chronology, locality and surname continuity. No record names Robert as John's father.

John's wife is unknown.

The preferred identification is one James Glasgow from the Kilwaughter Demesne hearth household of 1669 to the Kilwaughter overseer of probably 29 May 1701 and the later elder marked deceased in 1726. Assess that continuity as probable rather than unresolved. The hearth roll places Jo and James Glascho consecutively in the same demesne, records 5347–5348. The later congregational extracts again contain both names acting for Kilwaughter: John senior in 1700 and an unmarked John in 1700/01, whose individual identity remains open, and James as overseer in probably 1701. John’s already-probable continuity supplies a coherent parallel ageing household, rather than leaving James’s later occurrence as an isolated name match. James’s later elder, 1722 commissioner and 1727 Kilwater probate-index accounts were already assessed as probably one man. The repeated local pairing, the particular district served and an ordinary adult generation in 1700/01 together favour continuity over an otherwise unattested replacement James. The preference uses the combined local sequence; no literal same-man clause is required.

The inference is not documented identity. The later men never occur together in the same reviewed minute, the 1669 roll does not state a kin relationship, and similar spellings are not independent identifiers. Kilwaughter Demesne and the later Kilwaughter district are compatible but not identical address statements. A father-and-son succession or another local James remains possible. Assuming an adult aged at least 21 in 1669 would make James at least about 74 in 1722 and 78 in 1726; those are conditional age calculations, not recorded ages or a new birth bound. His local commission in 1722 is compatible with advanced age, but the long career is the strongest reason for caution. No age at death, occupation, holding or named relative equates the occurrences. The missing 1701–1721 session sequence leaves a real gap. Porter’s 1699–1701 extracts are a printed transcription of a manuscript copy; the later extracts and lost probate index do not independently prove the earlier identity. The probable grade is an overall inference across the groups, not the product of independent probabilities. Neither the 1726 death annotation nor the 1727 probate is newly assigned to a separate earlier James; they remain events of the later elder whose identification with the 1669 man is now probable.

=== The younger John implied by "senior" ===

The repeated designation '''John Glasgow, sen.''' strongly suggests that another, younger John Glasgow was known to the congregation by about 1700.

The same group of congregational extracts uses other age qualifiers, including ''Robert Mea, jun.'' and ''John Kerr, sen.'', showing that such distinctions were actively used in the source.<ref name="Porter2" />

No located published extract explicitly names a '''John Glasgow junior''', however, and ''senior'' does not itself prove a father-son relationship. The younger namesake could have been:

* John's son;
* a nephew;
* a cousin;
* another younger member of the same Glasgow cluster.

'''A son is a plausible hypothesis, but not yet a relationship suitable for attachment without further evidence.'''

The important distinction is therefore:

:'''Probable:''' Jo Glascho of Kilwaughter in 1669 = John Glasgow senior of Kilwaughter c.1700.
:'''Possible:''' the younger John implied by ''senior'' was his son.
:'''Unknown:''' the younger John's identity and later history.

No reliable WikiTree profile has yet been identified for this younger namesake.

=== Could the c.1700 senior have been a son of the 1669 John? ===

It is chronologically possible but presently less likely.

A male householder in 1669 could readily still have been active about 1700. Even if John had been only about twenty-one in 1669, he would have been about fifty-two by 1700 — entirely compatible with being the established ''senior'' John, a head of family and congregational bondsman.

Making the c.1700 senior a son instead requires an additional same-name generation for which no record has yet been found. It would also require some other younger John to explain why that second-generation John was himself called ''senior''.

The simpler reconstruction fits all presently located evidence without adding an undocumented generation.

'''Current assessment: same person probable; father-son replacement possible but unsupported.'''

=== Parentage and Scottish hypothesis ===

The current uncertain father hypothesis should distinguish two separate questions.

[[Glasgow-3970|Robert Glasgow]] is the Robert ''Glaskow'' associated with Larne in the 1653 examination.<ref name="RobertLarne" /> He is a plausible earlier member of the Larne–Kilwaughter Glasgow family and is currently attached as John's uncertain father.

A separate [[Glasgow-1078|Robert Glasgow]] was recorded in Glasgow, Scotland, in 1618. That Scottish Robert has been proposed as identical with the Larne Robert, but no documentary migration or identity bridge has been found.

The two hypotheses should not be collapsed:

* Robert of Larne → John of Kilwaughter: '''possible; unproved'''.
* Scottish Robert → Robert of Larne: '''possible identity; unproved'''.

John's mother remains unknown.

=== Record survival ===

Porter's nineteenth-century publication is derivative: he stated that the original early congregational record could not then be found and that he was using a transcript.<ref name="Porter2" />

There is now a more concrete archival route. Modern scholarship identifies surviving Larne and Kilwaughter session material for 1699–1701 and 1720–1769 and cites '''PRONI MIC1B/6A/1B'''.<ref name="Whan">Robert Whan, ''The Presbyterians of Ulster, 1680–1730'' (Boydell Press, 2013), introduction and bibliography, identifying Larne and Kilwaughter session records for 1699–1701 and 1720–1769 and citing PRONI MIC1B/6A/1B; [https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C038A358FF15C0285190286F12E951A4/9781782041894bib_p202-230_CBO.pdf/bibliography.pdf Cambridge bibliography].</ref>

The early section should be inspected directly to determine its precise relationship to the transcript used by Porter and, more importantly, whether entries omitted from his topical extracts identify the younger John Glasgow.
