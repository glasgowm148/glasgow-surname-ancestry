# Findings: [Isobella Glasgow (Glasgow-3916), about 1530, Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3916)

Last live check: **19 August 2026**.

The reciprocal link to [Brady Family of Sauchie, Stirling & Easter Kennet — Research Notes](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Space:Brady_Family_of_Sauchie%2C_Stirling_%26_Easter_Kennet_%E2%80%94_Research_Notes) is live and verified because that page now includes Isobella's probable, explicitly uncertain placement as William Glasgow and Esbell Brade's daughter.

## Current conclusion

The profile correctly represents Isobella Glasgow, wife of Archibald
Herbisoune, and cites the 1563 property transaction. No reviewed source names
her parents. The attached [William Glasgow (Glasgow-1098), about 1500,
Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1098) and
[Isobel Brade (Brade-232), about 1500, Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Brade-232)
are now **probable on strong circumstantial evidence**, while the structured
links must remain uncertain. Isobella bears her proposed mother's forename. More
importantly, on 16 November 1564 her husband Archibald Herbisoune witnessed John
Glasgow senior's sale of the exact fore tenement which John had entered only
nineteen days earlier as heir of William Glasgow, explicitly his father. This
puts Isobella's husband directly into the proved son's inheritance transaction,
not merely the same broad burgh. A close in-law relationship is the simplest
explanation, but the act does not call Archibald John's brother-in-law and a
trusted neighbour or occupational associate remains possible.

The case is now materially stronger than that single witness appearance. On
20 February 1559/60 Archibald and Robert Glasgow jointly witnessed a Bridgegait
transaction. On 19 December 1573 Robert Glasgow, Archibald Herbertsone and John
Glasgow were again witnesses together and were collectively described as
**fishers**. The 1575 title history identifies the fisher Archibald through the
same William Maxwell and Marion Wilson who bought from Archibald and Isobella
in 1563. This repeated surname, craft and Bridgegait-property cluster strongly
supports Isobella being a sister of John and Robert and therefore a daughter of
William and Esbell. It remains circumstantial because no act uses a kin term.
