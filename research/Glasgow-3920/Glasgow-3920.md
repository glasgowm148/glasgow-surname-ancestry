== Biography ==
'''Patrick Glasco''' is named on a Kilclone,County Meath memorial transcribed in 1892. It records his death in May 1754 aged 60, implying birth about 1694. His son [[Glasgow-3924|James]] erected the stone for Patrick and James’s brothers [[Glasgow-3925|Richard]] and [[Glasgow-3926|William]], who died on 1 November 1738. Richard’s age was 10; William’s is printed with a query at about 7.<ref>Association for the Preservation of the Memorials of the Dead in Ireland,Kilclone inscription reported 1892, reproduced at [https://meathhistoryhub.ie/memorials-of-the-dead-journal/ Meath History Hub]. This is a published transcription, not a newly inspected photograph.</ref>

Patrick’s birthplace, wife and mother are unknown from this inscription. Kilclone supplies the memorial’s setting; it does not independently establish his birthplace or exact place of death.

[[Glasgow-1440|Patrick Glasgow of the Gordon 1667 account]] is selected locally as a very uncertain possible father. Their dates allow an ordinary generation, but no record links the Scottish and Meath households. The proposed son would predate the older account’s recorded 1707 marriage to Helen Fairbairn; Helen is not selected as his mother. No earlier union or migration is asserted as fact.<ref>[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XTBS-98C FamilySearch,Gordon Patrike Glasgow,1667]; [https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XYQ1-96T Patrick Glasgow/Helen Fairbairn,Stitchel,1707], existing parish-index citations. Neither names a Meath relative.</ref>

== Research Notes ==
=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

[[Glasgow-1440|Patrick / Patrike Glasgow of Gordon]], represented by the Gordon Patrike baptism of 1667, is selected as a very uncertain working father of [[Glasgow-3920|Patrick Glasco of the Kilclone memorial]] of the Kilclone memorial. The memorial’s age 60 at death in May 1754 puts the younger Patrick’s birth around 1694. If the Gordon baptism was near birth, the older Patrick would have been about 27 then: an ordinary father–son interval. The matching Patrick/Patrike and Glasgow/Glasco name pair supplies a specific older candidate rather than an invented ancestor. This is a very weak generational model; neither name recurrence nor a feasible age establishes paternity. There is no reviewed deed, parish entry, probate clause, occupation or shared associate connecting Gordon or Stitchel to Kilclone. No migration or Scottish birthplace is newly established for the Meath man, and the catalogue is not a complete census of older Patricks.

The model keeps the two Patricks distinct. A man baptised in 1667 would have been at least about 86–87 in 1754, incompatible with the Kilclone memorial’s age 60 unless the memorial age is substantially wrong. No evidence justifies correcting that age by a generation. A father–son hypothesis fits the two dated accounts better than identifying them as one person, but another father or more distant relationship remains possible. The 1667 index names James as father of the Gordon child, not a father for the Meath Patrick. The modern [[Glasgow-3107|Glasgow-3107]] identification and the Gordon account’s existing maternal and ancestral attachments retain their previous qualifications; this new edge does not independently prove them. The project’s reported Y-DNA affinity does not provide a named parent or establish that this particular untested Meath family belongs to a tested line.

The Gordon profile also gathers Patrick’s marriage entries with Helen Fairbairn/Fairbarne at Stitchel on 23 May 1707 and Eccles on 1 July 1707, followed by children in 1708–1716. Those adult events do not themselves identify the groom with the 1667 child. If that combined identity is correct, the proposed Meath son would predate the recorded 1707 marriage by about 13 years. Helen is therefore not selected as his mother. No earlier wife, earlier marriage, illegitimacy, second household or move is invented. The proposed father would require an unrecorded earlier child, and that is a real weakness of the model. The two 1707 dates remain separate recorded entries; their precise banns/ceremony relationship has not been newly checked. The supplied Gordon index reads 4 August 1667 while the structured birth field says 8 April 1667. This existing day/month conflict remains visible, and neither index is used as proof of an exact birthday.

The Kilclone inscription identifies Patrick as James’s father, and Richard and William as James’s brothers. Richard and William died on 1 November 1738; Richard’s age is 10 and William’s is printed with a query at about 7. Patrick died in May 1754 aged 60. These statements define the existing nuclear family and remain separate from the new proposed grandfather. The source used is a modern reproduction of a transcription reported in 1892, not a newly inspected photograph of the stone. The inscription supplies no mother, wife, occupation, birthplace or Scottish origin. Kilclone is the memorial context, not independent proof of Patrick’s birthplace or exact place of death. No parent-child confidence grade within the memorial family is changed.

[[Glasgow-3924|James Glasco, Kilclone memorial erector]] is the memorial erector, not a man proved to have died in 1736. The previously queued Findmypast memorial index gives 1736 for James (IRE/MEMDEAD/008199), but a stone he erected naming deaths in 1738 and 1754 cannot establish his death in 1736. Keep that index discrepancy visible and do not add a death year. James’s circa 1730 birth is an adult-status estimate, not a statement on the stone. His own later life and children remain unknown. The new Scottish grandfather is a hypothesis about his father’s origins; it supplies no new source naming James’s grandfather. No kinship with the Dublin watchmakers, Rathdowney families or an Ulster household is added.

One very uncertain father is newly selected, with no mother. This is a speculative working family connection, not a proved Scotland–Meath migration or an identity merge. The age 60 memorial reading, pre 1707 child problem, uncertain adult identity of the Scottish Patrick and all prior source limitations remain explicit.
