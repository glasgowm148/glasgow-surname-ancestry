## Relationship to Adam

Adam Glasgow (`Glasgow-3902`) was born about 1758. If the 1767 identity candidate
is correct, Adam was only about nine years older and cannot have been John's
father. The two men were separate Presbyterian household heads in the same small
townland. The child-name sequence, household reconstruction and 1847 Irish news
make brother the probable but still uncertain placement, with cousin the main
alternative. Leave both men's parents blank; `Glasgow-3905` is an artificial
placeholder and should not be used to represent the hypothesis.
Preserve an older Adam and Maria as the leading candidate names for their shared
parents, but do not enter those names structurally without a relational record.

in 1844.

### Earlier local network

A 6 April 1772 Tamlaght O'Crilly declaration lists Hugh Glasgow, Thos.
Mulhollan and James Berkley among people surrendering arms to Henry Ellis of
Inishrush. Glasgow-540 (born about 1739) is a plausible identity for that Hugh:
his age fits and the later family tradition places him in Ireland until his
1796 emigration. The notice does not identify him, however; two Hugh Glasgow
householders were already recorded in the parish in 1740, so the 1772 Hugh may
be Glasgow-540, his father, or another namesake. The Mulhollan and Berkley
names support a local information network behind John's 1847 letter, not a
parental or marital connection.

## Paste-ready biography

### Richardson Mulholland

John's daughter Lavina Glasgow, born 22 January 1806, is documented in the same
family compilation as the wife of Richardson Mulholland, later of Ontario. He
is therefore almost certainly the `R. Mulhollan` whose separate report in
John's 1847 letter concerned western Canada and the potato rot. McCaivey, not
Mulholland, carried the James/Barkly news from Ireland. The relationship is
useful for interpreting the letter but does not connect John to Adam or Hugh.

== Research Notes ==

John was a separate householder from [[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow]], who headed Inishrush house 91 in the same census.<ref name="Census1831" /> Adam was born about 1758. The two men's shared surname, townland and Presbyterian religion make them probable close relatives. Cousin remains possible because the census states no relationship. Their parents should remain blank: [[Glasgow-3905]] is an artificial structural placeholder, not an identified person.

A John Glasgow attended a Reformed Presbyterian session at Drumlamph in 1809 and was an elder present at Drimbolg on 19 July 1827.<ref name="Drimbolg">[https://www.oneirishrover.com/drimbolg-reformed-presbyterian-church/ "Drimbolg Reformed Presbyterian Church"], congregation history and session extracts (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref> Exact locality, denomination and chronology make him the strongest working identity for the 1831 householder, but neither church occurrence supplies an age or family relationship.

The family Bible of [[Glasgow-822|John Glasgow, born 1767]], records the sons in the order Adam, James, John and Arthur. Traditional Scottish naming placed the first son after the paternal grandfather, the second after the maternal grandfather and the third after the father.<ref name="NamingPattern">[https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/help-and-support/guides/forenames Scotland's People, "Forenames: traditional naming patterns"] (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref> The third position correctly reproduces father John, making the first-position Adam more significant than an isolated shared name. The daughters begin Rose, Maria and Jane; Maria occupies the traditional paternal-grandmother position, although the third daughter does not reproduce mother Mary's name. This supports, but does not prove, an older Adam and Maria as the parents of John and his probable brother Adam of Inishrush.

The same Bible family also closely reconstructs the census household. After married daughter Rose and deceased son James are removed, John and sons Adam, John and Arthur give exactly four males. Mary and daughters Maria, Jane, Lavina and Margaret give five possible females, so only one adult daughter needs to have been living elsewhere to produce the four females recorded in 1831.

[[Glasgow-822|John Glasgow, born 1767]], is a strong proposed identity match. His family register and later letter connect him personally to Inishrush.<ref name="JohnRegister">[https://hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I368&tree=tree1 John Glasgow family register and descendant page] (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref><ref name="JohnLetter">[https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/documents/John%20Glasgow%20letter%20to%20Jane%20Glasgow%20Gray%203-20-1847.pdf John Glasgow to Jane Glasgow Gray, 20 March 1847], scan and transcript (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref> The 1767 birth date remains the date recorded in the family register; it is not being challenged here. The 1830 Ohio census page's household head was aged forty to forty-nine, so he cannot be John-822, who would have been sixty-three. That does not make the census irrelevant: it may have been attached because one of John's sons or another household member appears there. The Bible's entry for son James's death in 1823 gives no place, so it cannot be used to exclude an Irish residence. These points remove the main objection to identifying Glasgow-822 with the 1831 Inishrush householder, but do not prove the identification. A merge should be proposed and left pending for review before completion.

A separate McKeown family Bible records that John-822's grandson John Glasgow McKeown was born at Inishrush on 17 April 1821 and baptised by Rev. Joseph Orr.<ref name="McKeownBible">[https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I1&amp;tree=tree1 John Glasgow McKeown family-Bible transcription] (accessed 21 July 2026).</ref> Orr was the Drimbolg-associated minister whose congregational history records a John Glasgow in 1809 and as an elder in 1827.<ref name="Drimbolg" /> This independently anchors John-822's daughter's household in the same townland and religious network, materially strengthening the proposed identity. Neither record names Adam or calls the men brothers.

John's 1847 letter also relayed Irish news that a James Glasgow reportedly had a child by a servant who was a daughter of Henry Barkly.<ref name="JohnLetter" /> [[Glasgow-3903|James Glasgow]], proved by gravestone to be Adam's son, remained a farmer at Inishrush.
