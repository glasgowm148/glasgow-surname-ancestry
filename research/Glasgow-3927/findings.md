# Findings: Glasgow-3927

Last updated: 2026-08-09

## Current assessment

Glasgow-3927 is the record-defined John Glasco who headed house 2 at Inishrush,
Tamlaght O'Crilly, in the 1831 census. The strongest proposed identity is
[John Glasgow, born 10 April 1767](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-822),
but the merge/identity review remains unresolved.

The identity case is now strong: a separate McKeown family Bible records that
John-822's grandson John Glasgow McKeown was born at Inishrush in 1821 and was
baptised by the Rev. Joseph Orr. Orr was the Drimbolg Reformed Presbyterian
minister whose surviving congregational history records a John Glasgow in 1809
and again as an elder in 1827. This independently joins John-822's family to the
same townland, congregation and period as the 1831 householder.

## Direct-link evidence audit: Adam and John

No inspected source explicitly calls [Adam Glasgow
(`Glasgow-3902`)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3902) John's brother,
cousin, father, son, co-lessee, heir, executor or witness. The available evidence
supports a shared local network and possible collateral kinship, but not an
attachable relationship.

## 1831 parish-wide Glasgow cluster

The complete Tamlaght O'Crilly 1831 transcription lists only two Glasgow/Glasco
households in the parish: John Glasco at Inishrush house 2 (four males and four
females) and Adam Glasco at Inishrush house 91 (three males and five females).
MacAfee transcribes Presbyterian while Torrens places the figures under Roman
Catholic, so the original return is required. They were not adjacent households, so this
does not prove a sibling relationship; however, it shows that the two men were
the only recorded Glasgow householders in the same townland/parish at that date.
The narrow local cluster supports close collateral kinship more than an isolated
same-surname coincidence.

The same return places Margaret Mulholland at Inishrush house 12, providing
additional local context for the Mulholland network mentioned in John's 1847
letter. It remains network evidence only.

The household-count case is now stronger than a generic near-match. John and
his three surviving sons reproduce all four males. Mary, Maria, Lavina
and Margaret reproduce all four females if Jane was already married to John
Gray at Boveedy; Rose was already married and James had died. Descendant material
specifically says Jane married John Gray in Ireland. No Alexander Wallace
household appears in the parish return, and the only Richard Mulholland household
has the wrong religion and composition for Lavina and her later husband.

The new evidence strengthens the case that John's Inishrush family belonged to a
long-running local Presbyterian network rather than being an isolated household.
The original Melanchthon account adds that Glasgow-540's eldest son was born in
1780 on the family farm near Portglenone. Combined with the 1740 Hugh in the
Ellis/McCullough cluster and the 1772 parish Hugh, this makes the family of
Glasgow-548 the high-probability extended cluster. It does not name John's
father. The account also says Hugh-540's household left many kin in Ireland in
1796, which supports a residual collateral branch without identifying it.

[Adam Glasgow-3902](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3902) is a plausible
brother or cousin, but the records prove neither relationship. Older Adam and
Maria remain naming-pattern search leads only. Glasgow-3927 should be detached
from placeholder Glasgow-3905 unless a relational record is found.

The Lavina–Richard connection is the most promising route to converting the
letter's network evidence into a person-level link. Priority records are their
marriage, the *Hawsbury*'s 1833 arrival, and Ontario records for Richard and
Lavinia. A confirmed marriage would show that “R. Mulhollan” could have been
John's son-in-law rather than merely a community correspondent.

## Research caution

Do not attach Mulhollan, Berkley/Barkly, Boggs, McCaivey or Hugh Henry as kin
from the letter or 1772 list. They are locality and migration-network leads only.
