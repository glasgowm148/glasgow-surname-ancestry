== Biography ==
'''Robert Glasgow''' was a cordiner in Edinburgh during the mid-seventeenth century. His date and place of birth have not been identified.

=== Marriage and occupation ===

Robert married '''[[Thomsone-50|Jonet Thomsone]]''' at Edinburgh, Midlothian, Scotland, on '''1 July 1652'''. The published Edinburgh marriage register prints her given name as Janet and describes Robert as a cordiner.<ref name="Marriage1652">Henry Paton, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_marriages_for_the_parish_of_edinburgh_1595-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=278 ''Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1905), printed p. 268: “Glasgow, Robert, cordiner; Janet Thomsone 1 July 1652.” Scotland's People, Edinburgh marriages 685/1, reference 440/13.</ref>

In Older Scots, a cordiner was a cordwainer or shoemaker.<ref name="CordinerDefinition">Dictionaries of the Scots Language, [https://dsl.ac.uk/entry/dost00057534 “Cordiner(e), n.”], ''Dictionary of the Older Scottish Tongue'', accessed 4 August 2026.</ref>

No inspected record identifies Robert's parents or any children.

Robert's date and place of death have not been identified. He was alive on 1 July 1652.

== Research Notes ==
=== Uncertain childhood of the 1652 Edinburgh cordiner —16 October2026 ===

Select Robert [[Glasgow-1027]] as the uncertain working father of the Edinburgh cordiner Robert [[Glasgow-3938]] through possible identity with the indexed Glasgow child Robert [[Glasgow-1078]], baptised on 17 November 1618. The published Edinburgh marriage register names Robert as a cordiner with Janet Thomsone on 1 July 1652. The older Robert is the probable identity of the Glasgow cordiner admitted burgess on 22 April 1613 and is the named father of the 1618 child. If baptised near birth, that child would have been about 33 at the July 1652 marriage. The matching name, compatible adult generation and same craft make this a useful preferred family comparison over an otherwise unidentified parent or a more distant cordiner kinsman. The trade association supplies a reason to select the family, but neither hereditary training nor a Glasgow-to-Edinburgh move is documented. The father selection is low confidence; it does not identify the child with the adult as an established fact.

Select Jonet [[Cunningham-20923]] as the independently assessed uncertain mother of the Edinburgh adult only through the same possible childhood identity. The 1618 baptism explicitly names Jonet Conynghame as mother of Robert [[Glasgow-1078]]. This is an explicit maternal clause for the child, not an inference from the older Robert’s marriage alone or from a Cunningham surname elsewhere. Its transfer to [[Glasgow-3938]] depends entirely on the uncertain identity and therefore remains uncertain. The father’s craft provides no independent evidence of maternity.

Keep Robert [[Glasgow-1078]], [[Glasgow-3938]] and the Larne Robert [[Glasgow-3970]] as separate profile accounts. Both adult accounts are now provisionally placed in the 1618 child’s family; this does not establish two distinct sons named Robert. The 1652 Edinburgh event and the 1653 Larne account are not simultaneous incompatible events on the available evidence: they could concern one mobile man or different namesakes. Neither a migration nor that combined identity is selected as proved. Robert [[Glasgow-1149]] remains the separate existing candidate for the Euphame Lorimer household. Her indexed children in 1651, 1659, 1662 and 1664 bracket the Thomsone marriage, favouring two Edinburgh men. The reported 1 March 1644 West Port sasine also names Eupham Lorimer and belongs to that household comparison; it does not give a West Port residence or wife to the 1652 bridegroom. The original sasine is uninspected. The 1618 child’s attached younger Cunningham spouse and 1645 James association do not prove his adult identity. A later Thomsone marriage could be a remarriage if that association is correct, but no death or remarriage sequence is supplied. Preserve the existing attachments and all namesake alternatives. [[Glasgow-3938]]’s before-1634 birth boundary is an age-derived profile estimate, not a recorded birth. It remains unchanged, as do the child’s baptism, the Larne estimate and all existing spouses, children and confidently marked links. The Edinburgh marriage gives no parents, age or children. No apprentice, burgess, witness, property or migration record joins these accounts. An unrelated cordiner or another son, nephew or cousin in the wider family remains possible. No identity merge, new original inspection, live WikiTree save or relationship-confidence upgrade is claimed. === Possible Edinburgh burgess in 1666 ===

On '''4 July 1666''' a Robert Glasgow, also described as a cordiner, was admitted as a burgess of Edinburgh by right of his wife Euphame, daughter of the late Andrew Lorimer, beltmaker and burgess.<ref name="Burgess1666">Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=218 ''Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses and Guild-Brethren, 1406–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1929), printed p. 208: “Glasgow, Robt., B., cordiner, by r. of w. Euphame, dr. to late Andrew Lorimer, beltmaker, B. 4 July 1666.”</ref>

Scotland's People indexes four children of a Robert Glasgow and Euphame Lorimer or Laurimer: Eupham, 8 May 1651 at Edinburgh (685/1 50/395); Kathrin, 3 February 1659 at St Cuthbert's (685/2 30/28); William, 29 November 1662 at St Cuthbert's (685/2 30/53); and Magdalene, 14 August 1664 at Edinburgh (685/1 60/377).<ref name="LorimerChildren">Scotland's People, Church of Scotland Old Parish Registers, births and baptisms: Eupham Glasgow, 8 May 1651, Edinburgh 685/1 50/395; Kathrin Glasgow, 3 February 1659, St Cuthbert's 685/2 30/28; William Glasgow, 29 November 1662, St Cuthbert's 685/2 30/53; Magdalene Glasgow, 14 August 1664, Edinburgh 685/1 60/377. Parents indexed as Robert Glasgow and Euphame Lorimer/Laurimer.</ref>

This persistent Lorimer household brackets the separate 1 July 1652 marriage of a cordiner Robert Glasgow to Jonet Thomsone. Combined with the 1666 burgess admission through Euphame, it makes the burgess very probably the father in the Lorimer household and strongly supports treating him as a different Robert from the 1652 bridegroom. The original images and cordiner records remain needed for proof, but the former “strong candidate” identification should no longer be preferred.

=== Birth, death and suffix ===

The former birth estimate of about 1612 is unsupported. No inspected record states Robert's age, birthplace or parentage.

For database purposes, his birth should be left unknown rather than estimated from compiled family trees. The only presently certain life range is that he was living on 1 July 1652. If the 1666 burgess is accepted as the same man, a death date after 4 July 1666 may be used, but should remain uncertain.

The former death estimate of about 1690 in Virginia is also unsupported. No burial, testament, probate record or other contemporary evidence has been supplied for that date or place.

The suffix “II” is a database label and does not appear in either inspected record. It should not be treated as part of Robert's name.

=== Parentage ===

'''[[Glasgow-1156|Robert Glasgow]]''' and '''[[Ivey-2264|Deborah Ivey]]''' have previously been attached as Robert's parents.

No inspected Scottish record identifies either person as his parent. The 1652 marriage entry names no parents. The possible 1666 burgess entry identifies Robert's wife and father-in-law, but not Robert's father or mother.

The elder Robert associated with Wester Duddingston in 1641 belongs to a separate record cluster. Shared surname, compatible chronology and residence in Midlothian do not establish a father-and-son relationship.

No Scottish record inspected identifies Deborah Ivey as the wife of the Wester Duddingston Robert or as the mother of this Robert. Both parental relationships should be removed unless contemporary evidence is found.

=== Children ===

No baptism, apprenticeship, burgess entry, testament, sasine or other inspected record identifies a child of Robert Glasgow and Jonet or Janet Thomsone.

The profile should not retain children merely because they appear in FamilySearch, Geni, Pedigree Resource File or other submitted family trees. Any proposed child requires an independent contemporary record linking that person to this couple.

=== Distinct from Robert Glasgow baptised in 1655 ===

This Robert was not '''[[Glasgow-1374|Robert Glasgow]]''', who was baptised at Edinburgh on 5 May 1655 as a son of James Glasgow and Agnes Duncan.<ref name="Robert1655Baptism">“Scotland, Births and Baptisms, 1564–1950,” database, ''FamilySearch'', [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:VRSV-T1T Robert Glasgow, baptised 5 May 1655, Edinburgh], son of James Glasgow and Agnes Duncan.</ref>

The present Robert married in 1652, nearly three years before that child's baptism. The two men must remain on separate profiles.

=== Unsupported Virginia identity ===

The 1652 Edinburgh marriage record should not be combined with colonial Glasco, Glascocke or Glasscock material without a direct migration, probate, land or parentage record.

Ann Johnson and the attributed colonial children should be removed from this profile.

=== Name of spouse ===

The published Edinburgh register prints the bride's name as '''Janet Thomsone'''. The attached WikiTree profile uses '''Jonet Thomsone'''.

The original parish-register image should be inspected before preferring one form over the other. The differing forms should not be treated as evidence for two separate wives.

=== North Leith namesake, 1694 ===

A poll-tax page for North Leith dated 3 October 1694 lists '''Robt Glasgow''' among four servants in the household of Alexander Ainslie, clerk to the glass manufactory.<ref name="NorthLeith1694">National Records of Scotland, E70/4/10, North Leith poll tax, 3 October 1694, image 11, ''Scotland's People Virtual Volumes'', [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/virtual-volumes/volume-images/volume_data-E70-4-10/REX01666?image_number=11 original image].</ref> A later history dates the entry to 1695 and describes Robert as a glassmaker, but the original says only "servant". On 16 July 1694 a Robert Glasgow gave a receipt in Ainslie's name for bottles supplied to the Earl of Breadalbane.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Breadalbane Muniments, GD112/15/59, item 24, 16 July 1694, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD112%2F15%2F59 catalogue description].</ref> The shared employer and year make him almost certainly the October servant and prove a functional glassworks-business role, though not that he was a skilled glassmaker.

This profile's Robert was an adult in 1652 and could have been alive in 1694, but no record connects the North Leith servant to Jonet or Janet Thomsone or to the cordiner trade. The occurrence is therefore an unresolved same-name candidate, not a continuation of this biography.


[[Glasgow-3011|Robert Glasgow]], baptised at Glencorse in 1676, is a stronger younger chronological candidate for the 1694 servant: he was seventeen, is the sole exact-name Robert baptism returned for Glencorse from 1640 through 1690, and a same-name man married there in 1704. There is no direct Ainslie or glassworks link.

Exact Scotland's People OPR searches found no Glasgow, Glasgo, Glascow, Glaskow or Glassgow baptism in the Leith parishes from 1694 to 1730, and no exact Robert Glasgow marriage there from 1694 to 1710 or burial from 1694 to 1730. Register survival and spelling limit those negative results.
