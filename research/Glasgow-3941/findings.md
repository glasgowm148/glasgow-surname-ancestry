## Source findings

### Loughinsholin freeholder comparison, 4 October 2026

The 16 August 1831 declaration lists Robert Glasgow and John Glasgow among freeholders of the barony printed Loughinshollen (modern Loughinsholin), County Londonderry, supporting Sir Robert Bateson. They are co-signatories; no kinship, occupation, age or individual townland is given. Castledawson and 4 July 1831 refer to the opposing meeting discussed in the declaration, not their homes or their signing date.

Robert Glasgow-3941, the Coltrim land occupier documented in 1820–1831, is a strong compatible regional candidate; the newspaper lacks a townland or family bridge to prove it. Glasgow-2656 of the Cookstown district remains another adult comparison. Glasgow-12 has an Ohio record sequence and is a weaker comparison despite Moneymore origin; the newspaper does not resolve his travel or retained Irish property. No Robert is identified as John’s father or brother, or as the surgeon’s father, from this signature.

## Current conclusion — newspaper identity review, 6 October 2026

- [The Northern Star, 1796-12-12, p.1](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61260706/): Robert Glasgow (subscriber to a reward fund) — **possible** match. The 1796 Flax Growers' list for County Tyrone (same year) contains exactly one Robert of the surname: 'Glasgow, Robert, Lissan'. Lissan parish adjoins Cookstown (Derryloran) to the north. Glasgow-3941 is built on that flax entry and later Lissan records (Drapers' Company lease of Coltrim 1820, Lissan tithe 1827, 1831 census at Coltrim, Presbyterian). So the name, year and district agree. It stays possible rather than probable for two reasons. A Cookstown reward subscription was a town and gentry list, and the Registry of Deeds has a 'Robert Glasgow junior, gent.' of Cookstown in 1817 (memorial 488398, cited in research/Glasgow-2915), which implies a Robert senior in Cookstown who has no profile and is an equally good fit. Also, the flax list does not record every resident. Other candidates: Glasgow-1189 (Robert, est. b. 1769 Tyrone, in New York by 1803) is possible, with no parish.
