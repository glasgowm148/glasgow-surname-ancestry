== Biography ==

'''John Glasgow''', recorded as '''Joh Glask''' in the surviving transcription, was a Protestant householder in Dunaghy parish, County Antrim, in 1740.<ref name="PHR1740">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, T808/15258, ''Names of 14,400 Protestant householders'', County Antrim, Dunaghy parish, 1740; William Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/1740protestantreturnsantrim.pdf surname transcription] and [https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/readmore1740protestantreturns.htm explanation of the surviving copy and collector order], accessed 6 August 2026.</ref>

On 1 November 1740, William Hamilton of Donnemanagh leased to '''John Glasgow and James Wier of Ballyboggy''' one third of the quarterland of Ballyboggey for three lives, at an annual rent of £6 13s. 4d.<ref name="BallyboggyLease">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D2223/15/56, counterpart lease, William Hamilton of Donnemanagh to James Wier and John Glasgow of Ballyboggy, one third of the quarterland of Ballyboggey, 1 November 1740; PRONI eCatalogue description, accessed 6 August 2026. The catalogue item is open but has no online digital image.</ref>

The same 1740 householder transcription places '''Thomas Alexander, Joh Glask and Joh Wier''' consecutively in the original collector order. This supports identification of Joh Glask with the Ballyboggy lessee, but it proves proximity or collection order rather than kinship.<ref name="PHR1740" />

No record examined names John's parents, wife or children. He should not be attached to a later County Antrim family solely because the surname recurs nearby.

== Research Notes ==

The lease catalogue names John's co-lessee as '''James Wier''', while the householder transcription has '''Joh Wier'''. The original lease should be inspected before deciding whether these were two different men or whether one forename was mistranscribed or miscatalogued.

The 1825 tithe later records two separate men, '''James Glasgow''' and '''James Glasgow junior''', at Ballybogy. Their identities remain unresolved and no compatible WikiTree profiles have yet been established. The 1851 census places [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow]] and [[Glasgow-1367|Robert Glasgow]] at neighbouring Glenleslie. These records make the 1740 tenant relevant to the local cluster but do not establish descent.

## Repository Weir/Wier connection audit — 6 August 2026

The repository's One Tree export and local research corpus were searched for
`Weir` and `Wier`, excluding the newspaper indexes. The audit found no locally
captured WikiTree profile representing James or John Wier of Ballyboggy and no
Antrim Weir/Wier profile of a compatible generation.

The complete locally captured descendant graph of [[Glasgow-1022|John Glasgow
of Glenleslie]] contains 75 descendants. None has a structured Weir/Wier spouse
or a Weir/Wier middle name. This is a bounded negative from the repository
snapshot, not proof that no intermarriage occurred.

The repository's positive Glasgow–Weir associations are separate clusters:

- [[Glasgow-1495|Katherine Glasgow]] and [[Weir-4172|Thomas Weir]] at
  Stevenston, Ayrshire, before May 1605;
- [[Glasgow-3187|Thomas Glasgow]] and [[Weir-3979|Catherine Weir]] in
  seventeenth-century Midlothian;
- [[Glasgow-32|Agnes Glasgow]] and [[Weir-293|Robert Weir]], whose profiles
  contain no usable dates, parents or locality connecting them to Ballyboggy;
- [[Glasgow-2054|Samuel Glasgow]] of Gortin, County Tyrone, whose Griffith-era
  lot was sublet in part to a James Weir; this is a later Tyrone tenancy rather
  than a Dunaghy relationship; and
- [[Glasgow-2052|Robert Hugh Glasgow]] of Gortin, County Tyrone, who married
  [[Weir-7468|Agnes Graham Lyle Weir]] at Motherwell, Scotland, in 1906.

Later Killycurragh profiles also preserve `Weir` as a middle name:
[[Glasgow-745|Sarah Adelaide Glasgow]] married [[Henry-6237|Silas Weir Henry]],
and [[Glasgow-1772|Margaret Elizabeth Glasgow]] married [[Tomb-123|Henry Weir
Tomb]]. These late Tyrone occurrences cannot identify the 1740 Ballyboggy
co-lessee.

The repository therefore supports only repeated **local surname association**
at Ballyboggy in 1740 and 1825. It does not yet support a Glasgow–Weir marriage,
descent from the 1740 co-lessees, or transfer of the same lease between the two
families. The original D2223/15/56 lease remains the decisive record because its
three named lives, witnesses and endorsements may supply the missing kinship.
