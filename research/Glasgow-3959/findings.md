# Findings: John Glasgow (Glasgow-3959), Ballyboggy

Last updated: 2026-08-11

## Current conclusion

John Glasgow of Ballyboggy is directly documented as an adult co-lessee in 1740.
The matching order of the Protestant Householders' Return and same-day Ballyboggy
lease groups strongly identifies him with `Joh Glask` in Dunaghy parish.

He is a possible, but weaker, candidate for the younger John implied by John
`Glasgow-3913` being called senior about 1700. Different districts, no shared
associates and the separate 1733 Killroot occurrence prevent attachment.

John is also the earliest directly documented member of the local
Ballyboggy-Glenleslie Glasgow-Alexander-Wier tenant network. Ballyboggy borders
Glenleslie. The 1740 householders' sequence `Thomas Alexander - Joh Glask - Joh
Wier` closely matches the separate same-day lease group. In 1825 James Glasgow
and James Glasgow junior occur in Ballyboggy among Alexander and Wier occupiers,
while Robert Glasco occurs separately at Doonbought in the same Dunaghy parish.
In 1851 adjacent Glenleslie households contain John Glasgow and Rose Alexander,
George Glasgow and Sarah Alexander, and John's brother-in-law William Alexander
by an unspecified route. A
further Dunaghy household at Cloughgaldenagh is already represented by William
`Glasgow-1387`, wife Ellin and their eight children; it is not an unprofiled
branch.

This is meaningful continuity of an extended local network, not a proved line
of descent. John-3959 could be an ancestor or collateral relative of John-1022,
and the two 1825 Jameses could bridge the generations, but father-son wording
has not been found. Existing uncertain upstream alternatives for John-1022 can
therefore remain open.

## Next proof target

1. Transcribe the three lives, ages, witnesses and endorsements in
   `D2223/15/56`.
2. Trace the 1825 James Glasgow and James Glasgow junior through
   estate rentals, valuation continuation, church records and probate.
3. Trace the eight children of William `Glasgow-1387` through marriages, deaths
   and migration records. Their records may distinguish William's father from
   the 1825 Dunaghy candidates.
4. Inspect the Crawford and Glasgow family papers, PRONI `D2003 [MIC128]`.
   Rev. A. R. Crawford compiled five family-history volumes after returning from
   Manchuria: three Crawford volumes and two Glasgow histories. Dr J. C. C.
   Crawford later supplied typescript copies and additional genealogical
   material. Request both Glasgow volumes and the later typescripts/additions,
   not merely a single “notebook.” This description is documented in the
   [Crawford Papers introduction](https://www.sommemidulster.org/dwd/pdfs/02002.pdf).
