# Findings: John Glasgow (Glasgow-3960), Gortigillagh/Gortaggle, 1728

Last updated: 2026-10-16

## Current conclusion

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

**Selected very uncertain Leitrim–Tyrone model, 16 October 2026:** John Glasgow-3960 is provisionally placed as a son of the traditional John Glasgow-551, using their existing estimated generations. Identity between the two Johns remains a live alternative. The 1728 witness record names no father; James Glasgow-3124’s existing paternal link remains unproved. [Evidence and alternatives](/branches/research-irish-belfast-donegal/#leitrim-tyrone-working-family).

Registry of Deeds memorial 38,213 records **John Glasgow, gentleman, of
Gortigillagh, County Leitrim**, as a witness on 30 May 1728. Gortigillagh is
strongly matched to modern Gortaggle in Annaduff parish. This is a genuine
same-generation Irish candidate for comparison with John-551, but no record
connects him to the traditional County Tyrone family.

Within the Registry of Deeds Index Project's currently indexed Glasgow entries,
he is the only named John before 1750. That is an index-bound observation, not
proof that no other John existed.

## Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026

John Glasgow-551, the John said in the later family history to have found a home in County Tyrone, is selected as a very uncertain working father of John Glasgow-3960, gentleman of Gortigillagh, County Leitrim, recorded in 1728. This develops the comparison already recorded in both case files. Keeping their current estimated generations separate gives about 1681 for the older John and about 1705 for the witness: a possible 24-year father–son interval. The proposed connection gives the Leitrim household a testable place alongside the early Irish family tradition. Its preference over an identity match depends on retaining those two estimated generations; neither birth year is independent evidence for paternity. The same uncommon surname and an early Irish setting support investigating kinship, but do not determine its degree. There is no named-father clause or documented Tyrone–Leitrim migration, and no place of birth is inferred from this link.

The first dated occurrence used here is 30 May 1728, registered 11 July: Registry of Deeds memorial 38213, volume 57, page 235. The existing derivative abstract names John Glasgow, gentleman of Gortigillagh, County Leitrim, witnessing a transaction between James Johnston of Cashellmenon, County Donegal, and Robert Johnston of Aghadunvane, County Leitrim, with Bartley McGowan and John Gilhuly. No original has been newly inspected. “1707” is an older inferred birth boundary from assuming an adult witness aged at least 21; it is not a 1707 record. The structured about-1705 birth and County Leitrim birthplace remain estimates, not documentary dates or proof of origin. The notes favour Gortaggle in Annaduff, supported by the near-contemporary Gortogill/Cortogill forms; the older biography favours Gortnasillagh in Rossinver. Both locality assessments remain visible. This parent model uses County Leitrim only and does not resolve the townland to manufacture geographic proximity.

Identity between the two Johns is a live alternative, already raised by the earlier review: the witness could have been much older than his present estimate. If that identity is established, this provisional father edge must be withdrawn as a duplicate-person construction, rather than retained as two generations. A brother, uncle, more distant relative, or another Glasgow family is also possible. The older John’s own descent from William Glasgow-591 comes from late family tradition and receives no new support here. His roughly 1681 birth was calculated from his son Hugh’s estimated generation; the broader 1656–1688 range in the profile is not a measured age. No wife or mother is supplied for either John.

James Glasgow-3124 of Aghadonvane already has John Glasgow-3960 attached as father, without a certainty mark. That existing link remains unproved and is not a new connection or a confidence upgrade. John’s 1728 Johnston/McGowan associations can be compared with James’s 1779 lease and marriage-articles witnesses: a repeated local social network supports investigating continuity, not the exact father–son relationship. The 15 August 1779 lease, registered 10 August 1780, names James as esquire of Aghadonvane and uses Robert and William, young sons of James Johnston of Kinlough, as measuring lives. Those lives are not called Glasgow relatives. The 2 October 1779 marriage articles name James Glasgow, Edmund/Edmond Connolly and Andrew McGowan as witnesses. James’s present circa-1750 birth would make the circa-1705 John about 45 at his birth; both dates are estimates. An older James or a different generation remains possible. No intervening ancestor is invented.

One very uncertain father is newly selected, conditional on treating the two John accounts as different generations. This is a working hypothesis, not a proved Tyrone–Leitrim family or an independently supported migration. The identity alternative, guessed dates, townland disagreement and unproved existing John–James link remain explicit. No merge, new mother, changed vital field, certainty upgrade or live WikiTree save is claimed. Connectivity counts retained profile accounts, including possible duplicates.
