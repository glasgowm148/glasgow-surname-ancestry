# Findings: James Glasgow (Glasgow-3967), Randalstown/Duneane ruling elder

Last updated: 2026-08-13

## Current conclusion

The published General Synod records directly place a ruling elder James
Glasgow beside William Taylor in 1698 and 1703, and a man of the same name and
office beside James Scott in 1705. The first two appearances are near-certainly
one man; the 1705 occurrence is probably the same man. The Synod entries record
representatives, not residences. Taylor also appears with different elders in
later Synods, so Robert King's appearance beside Taylor in 1705 does **not**
prove that James moved to Duneane or permanently changed congregations.

No wife or child of James has been proved. Existing uncertain relationships may
remain useful research links, but none should be presented as documentary
descent.

The later Bannside/Clough household of [[Glasgow-1657|John Glasgow]] and
[[McClure-6341|Jane McClure]] is a genuine western or mid-Antrim family with
documented children, including [[Glasgow-1780|Rev. James McClure Glasgow]]. It
is the most substantial later family found in the wider area, but no record
connects John to this elder. The surviving public description of the Crawford
and Glasgow papers begins that household with John and Jane rather than naming
John's parents. It is therefore a descendant candidate only in the broadest
sense, not a proposed attachment.

The western County Antrim `Glass` households are not presently evidence of his
children. MacAfee's 1669 transcription separately standardises Jo and James
`Glascho` of Kilwaughter as **Glasgow**, while recording an established `Glass`
population at Drummaul: John Glass at Andraid and John and Neile Glass at
Coolsythe. The 1740 Drummaul Adam Glass, John Glass and two Glass widows fit
continuity of that local Glass population better than migration from this James.
Do not normalise Glass to Glasgow without a record joining the surnames.

Henry `Glads` in Duneane in 1740 is the strongest unresolved spelling candidate
because of the unusual form and the same parish association, but the return
states no relationship and does not identify him as a Glasgow. No WikiTree
profile should be created or attached until the closest surviving return or a
second record resolves his surname and identity.

## Record-coverage limits

Ordinary local church registers do not reach James's likely child-bearing
period: Old Randalstown Presbyterian baptisms begin in 1853; Randalstown First
marriages in 1845; Randalstown Second baptisms/session minutes in 1850; and
Duneane Presbyterian baptisms in 1864 and marriages in 1845. Broad baptism
index searches for children born about 1695–1720 cannot resolve this family.

## Recommended profile correction

Use this wording in place of any claim that James moved to Duneane:

```text
The James Glasgow appearing beside James Scott of Duneane in 1705 is probably
the same man as William Taylor's elder in 1698 and 1703. The Synod entries list
ministerial and elder representatives; they do not record James's residence.
William Taylor appeared with different elders in later Synods, so the change
of representative does not prove that James moved to Duneane or permanently
transferred congregations.
```
