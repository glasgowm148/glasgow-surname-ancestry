== Biography ==
{{YDNA 700 Earliest Known Ancestor|R-FT20271}}

'''Robert Glasgow''', recorded as '''Robert Glaskow''' and '''Robert Glascow''', was associated with Larne, County Antrim, in four examinations taken in 1653 concerning the earlier death of Teige O'Sheale. The evidence establishes that a man of this name was locally remembered in connection with events following the outbreak of the 1641 rebellion. It does not establish his parentage, marriage, children or ultimate fate.<ref name="Con1653">Trinity College Dublin, MS 838, fol. 153r, examination of Con O'Sheale, Lisnagarvy, 21 April 1653, identifier 838153r210; 1641 Depositions Project, [https://1641dep.abdn.ac.uk/items/show/42821.html online transcription].</ref><ref name="Collogh1653">Trinity College Dublin, MS 838, fol. 208r, examination of Captain James Collogh, 1 June 1653, identifier 838208r259; 1641 Depositions Project, [https://1641dep.abdn.ac.uk/items/show/36082.html online transcription].</ref><ref name="Hill1653">Trinity College Dublin, MS 838, fol. 131r, examination of John Hill, 1 June 1653, identifier 838131r192; 1641 Depositions Project, [https://1641dep.abdn.ac.uk/items/show/42803.html online transcription].</ref><ref name="Willson1653">Trinity College Dublin, MS 838, fol. 218r, examination of William Willson, 1 June 1653, identifier 838218r266; 1641 Depositions Project, [https://1641dep.abdn.ac.uk/items/show/42875.html online transcription].</ref>

The four statements are not four independent confirmations. All are hearsay, and Con O'Sheale's version attributes the fatal shot to James Browne rather than Robert.

The genealogically useful wording is:

<blockquote>one Robert Glaskow liuing in Larne</blockquote><ref name="Con1653" />

This identifies Robert with Larne in the account. It does not name a wife, child, sibling, parent, occupation or property holding. The wording also need not mean that Robert was still alive or resident there when the examination was taken in April 1653; it may describe him at the time of the reported incident.

No examination of Robert, indictment against him or recorded judicial outcome has been located.

=== Date and origin ===

Robert's age and birthplace are unknown. The reported event occurred during the early rebellion period, when he was apparently old enough to travel in company with James Browne. A birth before about 1625 is therefore only a broad working estimate.

No evidence yet connects him with the separate [[Glasgow-1078|Robert Glasgow]] baptised at Glasgow High Kirk in 1618, or with any other Scottish Glasgow family.

=== Browne–McCullogh network and the 1653 transplantation list ===

Robert's only documented connection to this circle comes through the 1653 examinations: Con O'Sheale associated him with James Browne, while Captain James McCullogh repeated Jennett Woodsyde's accusation. Robert Browne's own examination named sons Hugh and James, and identified Hugh as a lieutenant in McCullogh's company, but it did not call Robert Glasgow a soldier, servant, tenant or relative.

A government declaration dated 23 May 1653 survives as British Library 816.m.17[74], Wing I341. In the Islandmagee, Magheramorne and Ballynure quarter, the surviving print names Hugh Hoomes and James Lawder, together with Captain James McCullogh; it does not name James Browne or Ensign William Stephenson.<ref name="UlsterPrint1653">Ireland, Commissioners for the Settling and Securing the Province of Ulster, ''By the Commissioners for the settling and securing the province of Vlster'' (Dublin: William Bladen, 1653), British Library 816.m.17[74], Wing I341; [https://catalog.folger.edu/record/538557 catalogue record describing the British Library original].</ref>

Robert M. Young's publication of William Pinkerton's manuscript-derived text instead places James Browne and Ensign William Stephenson in those two positions.<ref name="YoungTransplantation">Robert M. Young, ''Historical Notices of Old Belfast and its Vicinity'' (Belfast, 1896), pp. 80–86; [https://archive.org/details/historicalnotice00youn/page/80/mode/2up Internet Archive].</ref> Young also printed a contemporary commissioners' letter saying that a local paper had been circulated before the declaration arrived and that the print was withheld because of “the great mistake of the printing the names.” The letter does not identify the mistaken names. Browne's absence from the surviving print is therefore real, but his inclusion in an intended or working schedule remains disputed rather than disproved.<ref name="YoungTransplantation" />

The contemporary administrative dossier survives in Trinity College Dublin MS 844, formerly F.3.18. Henry Jones's draft material of 14 June 1653 is at fol. 841, with further proposals dated 30 June; the relevant folios and enclosures have not yet been collated against the two list states.<ref name="JonesMS844">Judith Mary Carroll, “The papers of Henry Jones” (MSc thesis, Aberystwyth University), p. 82; [https://pure.aber.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/26062006/Carroll_Judith_Mary_MSc.pdf online PDF].</ref>

This is supporting network evidence only. Robert appears in neither list state; no record places him in McCullogh's company; and nothing connects McCullogh or the Brownes with either Jonet Cunningham. The result does not materially strengthen the proposed identification with [[Glasgow-1078|Robert Glasgow baptised in 1618]].

=== A documented later Kilwaughter family ===

A later family can be established more securely.

The surviving Larne and Kilwaughter Presbyterian register records the baptism of [[Glasgow-1144|James Glasgow]], born 24 May 1731, as a son of [[Glasgow-1502|Robert Glasgow]] and Rachel Wilson.<ref name="James1731">Larne and Kilwaughter Non-Subscribing Presbyterian congregation, baptismal register beginning 1720, baptism of James Glasgow, son of Robert Glasgow and Rachel Wilson, born 24 May 1731; Public Record Office of Northern Ireland microfilm and congregational register series. The original register image should be cited when available.</ref>

A lease dated 13 February 1738, registered in 1742, was granted by William Agnew of Kilwaughter to [[Glasgow-1502|Robert Glasgow]]. It named three lives:

* Robert Glasgow;
* Rachel Glasgow alias Wilson, his wife;
* “James Glasgow junior”.

The property comprised land at Ballykeel and Upper Ballykeel, Kilwaughter.<ref name="Ballykeel1738">Registry of Deeds, Ireland, memorial 74015, book 105, p. 301, deed dated 13 February 1738 and registered 12 April 1742; William Agnew of Kilwaughter to Robert Glasgow of Kilwaughter, naming Rachel Glasgow alias Wilson and James Glasgow junior as additional lives.</ref>

The lease does not itself call James their son. Read with the 1731 baptism, however, the identification of “James Glasgow junior” as [[Glasgow-1144|James Glasgow]], son of Robert and Rachel, is strongly supported.

Sworn inheritance pleadings made in 1817 and 1818 by James's eldest son, also Robert Glasgow, state that Rachel Wilson married Robert Glasgow and left two children, James Glasgow and Ann Glasgow. The deponent identified James as his father and Rachel as his paternal grandmother. The pleadings arose from claims to the Wilson estate and included reference to a Ballykeel interest.<ref name="WilsonPleadings">South Carolina equity pleadings and associated Wilson–Glasgow inheritance papers, 1817–1818, sworn answers concerning the estate of Archibald Wilson. The pleadings identify Rachel Wilson as wife of Robert Glasgow; James Glasgow as their son; and the deponent Robert Glasgow as James's eldest son and Rachel's paternal grandson. Surviving online transcriptions should be checked against the original court papers.</ref>

The resulting documented chain is:

:'''[[Glasgow-1502|Robert Glasgow]]''' married Rachel Wilson
:* '''[[Glasgow-1144|James Glasgow]]''', born 24 May 1731
:** later Robert Glasgow, James's eldest son, who gave sworn evidence in 1817

This is a genuine documentary family connection, but it begins several generations after the 1653 Robert.

=== The later Robert was not the 1653 man ===

[[Glasgow-1502|Robert Glasgow]] was married to Rachel Wilson, fathered a child in 1731, held the Ballykeel lease in 1738 and was still appearing in congregational business in the 1740s.<ref name="James1731" /><ref name="Ballykeel1738" /><ref name="Porter" />

The Robert associated with the early-1640s incident was already old enough to accompany James Browne. Even at an implausibly young age of about fifteen in 1641, he would have been about 112 in 1738 and about 121 in 1747.

The two Roberts therefore should not be merged.

The later Robert could have been a son, grandson, great-grandson, nephew or other relative of the 1653 Robert. No record yet specifies which, if any, relationship applied.

=== 1666 shipmaster candidate ===

The Argyll Estates Archives catalogue identifies a receipt made in 1666 by a '''Robert Glasgow, master of the ''John of Portrush''''', for transporting barrels of coal for the earl of Argyll.<ref name="Portrush1666">Argyll Estates Archives, Campbell family, dukes of Argyll papers, “Various accounts and receipts,” 1666–1678, GB 3452 ARG-04-03-67-78, former reference PFV 67, catalogue p. 149, [https://www.argyll-papers.com/downloads/campbell-family-dukes-of-argyll-papers.pdf catalogue description]. The original receipt has not yet been inspected.</ref> A shipmaster working between the Scottish west coast and an Ulster-named vessel is chronologically compatible with both this Larne man and [[Glasgow-1078|Robert Glasgow baptised in 1618]]. The catalogue gives no age, residence, birthplace, kin or Larne connection, however, and the vessel name does not prove that its master lived at Portrush. He is therefore a relevant same-name candidate, not an identity bridge.

The surviving customs records do not presently supply an easy substitute for the receipt. National Records of Scotland guidance explains that early customs books may name masters and merchants, but the E72 port-book series begins in 1668 and no Ayr or Irvine customs book for 1666 has been located in the fragmentary E71 material.<ref>National Records of Scotland, [https://nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/customs-and-excise-records/ “Customs and excise records” research guide], accessed 31 August 2026.</ref> The original Argyll receipt is consequently the priority record for identifying this man.

An NRS catalogue search found a possible later west-coast occurrence. An instrument of resignation and sasine at Ayr dated 6 August 1688 was written by '''Robert Glasgow, servitor to the notary''' Alexander McDermeit, depute clerk of the burgh.<ref name="Ayr1688">National Records of Scotland, GD86/741, instrument of resignation and sasine, 6 August 1688, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browseDetails.aspx?reference=GD86%2F741 catalogue record].</ref> A literate former shipmaster might later have worked for a notary, but the records are twenty-two years apart and neither gives age, kin or origin. The hand and signature should be compared with the 1666 receipt before treating them as one man; neither occurrence is identified with Robert of Larne.

The original North Leith poll-tax page records another adult namesake on 3 October 1694. '''Robert Glasgow''' was one of four servants in the household of Alexander Ainslie, who was explicitly styled clerk to the glass manufactory.<ref name="Leith1694">National Records of Scotland, Scottish Exchequer poll tax, North Leith parish, E70/4/10, image 11, 3 October 1694; [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/virtual-volumes/volume-images/volume_data-E70-4-10/REX01666?image_number=11 original image], Scotland's People Virtual Volumes.</ref> A modern study describes Robert as a glassmaker and dates the tax to 1695, but the original page itself calls him only a servant and is dated 1694.<ref>Jill Turnbull, “The Scottish Glass Industry, 1610–1750” (PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh, 1999), pp. 171, 202, 324 and 350, [https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/fe29343e-a341-412e-85bd-96afca6214e5/content PDF].</ref> A second contemporary record almost certainly concerns the same man: on 16 July 1694 Robert Glasgow gave a receipt in Ainslie's name for bottles supplied to the Earl of Breadalbane.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Breadalbane Muniments, GD112/15/59, item 24, 16 July 1694, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD112%2F15%2F59 catalogue description].</ref> This proves a functional and probably trusted role in the glassworks business, though not the skilled occupation of glassmaker. Employment around the glassworks is plausible; a skilled trade and identity with the Larne, shipmaster or Ayr men are not proved.


A stronger younger chronological alternative now exists: [[Glasgow-3011|Robert Glasgow]] was baptised at Glencorse in 1676, was seventeen in 1694, and is the sole exact-name Robert Glasgow baptism returned for that parish from 1640 through 1690. A same-name man married at Glencorse in 1704. This makes him a plausible candidate for the North Leith servant, although no record links him to Ainslie or the glassworks. It further reduces the 1694 entry's value as continuity evidence for this much older Larne man.

Exact Scotland's People OPR searches found no Glasgow, Glasgo, Glascow, Glaskow or Glassgow baptism in the Leith parishes from 1694 to 1730, and no exact Robert Glasgow marriage there from 1694 to 1710 or burial from 1694 to 1730. Register survival and spelling limit those negative results.

=== Belfast maritime collateral candidate ===
[[Glasgow-3172|Captain James Glasgow of Belfast]] is a possible son or younger relative of Robert and a possible collateral of the later Kilwaughter householders. A Belfast-associated shipmaster active by 1667 fits the next generation after Robert's 1653 Larne occurrence, and Belfast, Larne and Kilwaughter form a plausible north-east Antrim maritime and estate corridor.<ref>Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, MIC19/2, maritime material naming Captain James Glasgow or Glascow as a Belfast-associated shipmaster in the late 1660s and 1670s; T/828/15, will extract for James Glasgow of Belfast, 1686.</ref>

This is locality and chronology, not a stated relationship. Captain James must also remain distinct from [[Glasgow-1081|James Glascho of Kilwaughter]], who was still active in the 1720s and was apparently deceased only about 1726.
