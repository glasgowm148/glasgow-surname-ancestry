# Descendant audit: Robert Glaskow of Larne (Glasgow-3970)

Last updated: 2026-08-31

Live update applied 19 August 2026: the profile now states that the repeated
1669 and about-1700 association makes Jo/John Glasgow-3913 and James
Glasgow-1081 probable close relatives without proving an exact kinship. Both
attached father links remain uncertain, and Larne is retained as an uncertain
death-place estimate rather than removed.

## Current conclusion

Robert Glaskow is directly documented as living at Larne in 1653. No inspected
record names his wife or child. The current links to James Glasgow (Glasgow-1081)
and John Glasgow (Glasgow-3913), and the subsequent chain through Robert
Glasgow-1502 to Robert Glasgow-1143, remain working tree relationships rather
than proved descent.

One relationship within the later group can now be ranked more firmly. Jo/John
and James `Glascho` were consecutive Kilwaughter Demesne householders in 1669
(original record numbers 5347-5348), then recur in the same district's
congregational business about thirty years later. John represented Kilwaughter
and James served as its overseer. This repeated association makes them probable
close relatives, but it does not prove that they were brothers or that either
was Robert's son.

The supposed grandson Hugh Glasgow-1499 does not add another supported branch.
The published session-list transcription assigns `deceased 1726` to James
Glasgow and separately records Hugh Glasgow dead on 8 July 1767. Hugh-1499's
1726 death was copied from James's line; no independent occurrence defines him.
He is a probable duplicate of the documented Hugh Glasgow-1503, not evidence
for a separate son of James-1081.

A full remote source pass found no documentary bridge from Robert to Jo/John or
James `Glascho` at Kilwaughter, nor to the later Robert who married Rachel
Wilson. The 1653 evidence remains four hearsay statements; one attributes the
shooting to James Browne rather than Robert. The early Larne Robert, the later
Robert/Rachel household and Scottish Robert `Glasgow-1078` must remain separate.

The first securely reconstructable later family in the district begins with
Robert Glasgow-1143 and Rachel Wilson-78088 at Kilwaughter. It is not yet proved
to descend from Robert-3970. Robert-1143's father remains unproved.

Robert Brown's own 1653 examination names the servants present in his house on
the massacre night as Robert Orr, John Hunter, Agnes Woodside and Marren Carr.
Robert Glasgow is not among them. This excludes the unsupported claim that he
was one of Brown's identifiable household servants present at the incident;
neighbourhood, militia/company or other association remains possible.

Brown also named sons Hugh and James. Hugh was a lieutenant in Captain
McCullogh's company, and Captain McCullogh later helped transmit the accusation
against Robert Glasgow. This makes a military or company-network connection a
better research lead than household service, but no examination names Glasgow
as a soldier.

A newly located 1666 record creates a more direct occupational lead. The
Argyll Estates Archives catalogue describes a receipt by **Robert Glasgow,
master of the *John of Portrush***, for transporting barrels of coal for the
earl of Argyll. It is held in bundle `GB 3452 ARG-04-03-67-78` (`PFV 67`). A
shipmaster active in the Scottish west-coast/Ulster trade is chronologically
compatible with both the Larne man and the Robert baptised in 1618, but the
catalogue supplies no residence, age, parentage or Larne connection for him.
The vessel name is evidence of a cross-channel trading context, not proof that
its master lived at Portrush or was Robert-3970.

The obvious customs follow-up is limited by record survival. NRS guidance says
the pre-1707 customs books can name shipmasters and merchants, but the `E72`
port-book series starts in 1668 and no Ayr or Irvine book for 1666 was located
in the fragmentary `E71` series. The published surviving Ulster port books are
for 1612–1615, not 1666. The original Argyll receipt is therefore the best
immediate source for identifying this shipmaster.

An NRS catalogue search found another later same-name occurrence in the same
broad west-coast region. On 6 August 1688 an instrument of resignation and
sasine at Ayr was written by **Robert Glasgow, servitor to the notary**
Alexander McDermeit, depute clerk of the burgh. The shipmaster and notarial
servitor could be the same literate man, but the records are twenty-two years
apart and neither gives age, kin or origin. This is a possible continuation to
test against handwriting and signature, not evidence that either was the Larne
Robert.

The original North Leith poll-tax page adds another adult namesake. On 3
October 1694 **Robert Glasgow** was listed among four servants of Alexander
Ainslie, clerk to the glass manufactory. A modern thesis calls Robert a
glassmaker and dates the entry 1695, but the original record itself says only
`servant` and is dated 1694. A second contemporary record almost certainly
concerns the same man: on 16 July 1694 Robert Glasgow gave a receipt in
Ainslie's name for bottles supplied to the Earl of Breadalbane (`GD112/15/59`,
item 24). This proves a functional connection to the glassworks business and a
trusted representative role, but not the skilled occupation of glassmaker. No
age, origin or kin links him to Larne, Ayr or the *John of Portrush*.

[Glasgow-3011](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3011), baptised at
Glencorse in 1676, is a stronger younger alternative: he was seventeen in 1694,
is the sole exact-name Robert baptism returned there from 1640 through 1690,
and a same-name man married there in 1704. No Ainslie or glassworks link is
proved, but this weakens the 1694 occurrence as continuity evidence for Larne.

Exact Leith OPR searches found no Glasgow/Glasgo/Glascow/Glaskow/Glassgow
baptism in 1694–1730 and no exact Robert Glasgow marriage in 1694–1710 or
burial in 1694–1730. Register survival and spelling limit the negative result.

## 1653 transplantation-list result

The exact printed declaration is now identified as British Library
`816.m.17[74]`, Wing I341: *By the Commissioners for the settling and securing
the province of Vlster* (Dublin: William Bladen, 1653), dated Carrickfergus,
23 May 1653. The EEBO-TCP diplomatic transcription of that surviving print
places Captain James McCullogh in the Islandmagee, Magheramorne and Ballynure
quarter, but gives the two disputed places to Hugh Hoomes and James Lawder.

Robert M. Young's 1896 publication of William Pinkerton's manuscript-derived
text instead gives those places to James Browne and Ensign William Stephenson.
This is a substantive two-name variant, not ordinary spelling variation.
Young also printed a contemporary commissioners' letter saying that a local
paper had already been circulated before the declaration arrived, and that the
print was not published because of “the great mistake of the printing the
names.” The letter does not identify the mistaken names. Browne's absence from
the surviving print is therefore real, but does not prove that he was absent
from every working or intended schedule.

This evidence refines Robert's local network but barely affects the identity
hypothesis. Robert appears in neither list state, no record puts him in
McCullogh's company, and nothing here connects McCullogh or the Brownes to
Jonet Conynghame. Reid, EEBO-TCP and modern scans derive from the same British
Library sheet; the Ulster Historical Foundation list derives from Young and is
not an independent witness.

The best resolving source is Trinity College Dublin MS 844 (formerly F.3.18),
especially fol. 841 and its neighbouring papers. It contains Henry Jones's
14 June 1653 draft material and a 30 June set of proposals. The UCD descriptive
catalogue now narrows Thomas Fitzpatrick's independent transcript to
`IE UCDA LA/12/5`, transcript pages 553–558: “Henry Jones – arrangements by him
and other Commissioners for transplantation of the Ulster-Scots,” copied from
TCD F.3.18. Fitzpatrick's manuscript biography, `LA/12/1`, chapter XIII,
pages 235–237, also treats the transplantation; `LA/12/3` is the associated
synopsis. `LA/12/6` begins with 1654 material and is no longer the priority.
None has yet been collated against the disputed names.

One supplied Wikimedia/Internet Archive scan link was checked and resolves to
an unrelated 21 October 1653 alehouse declaration, not this 23 May
transplantation sheet. It has therefore not been used as evidence.

The provisional FT25406/FT20271 connection between the tested William-attributed
and Robert-c.1710 Kilwaughter lines makes older shared paternal ancestry
plausible. It does not bridge Robert-3970 to the later family or permit William,
born about 1655, to be his father.

## New documentary result downstream

- Registry memorial 74015 (13 February 1738) names Rachel, formerly Wilson, as
  wife of Robert Glasgow of Kilwaughter.
- A derivative transcription of the surviving Larne/Kilwaughter register records
  Robert and Rachel as parents of James (1731), Ann (1733) and Rachel (1738).
- An 1817 South Carolina equity complaint and Robert Glasgow-1983's sworn answer
  identify Rachel as mother of James Glasgow-1144 and Ann Glasgow-1500.
- James's 1775 will and the equity case concordantly identify his widow Mary and
  six children: Robert, John, Archibald, Wilson, Margaret and Rachel.
- Margaret married John Kinard and Rachel married Robert McCrackin. Neither
  daughter has a matching profile in the current local One-Tree data.

These records prove or strongly support a large downstream family island. They
do not supply the missing bridge from Glasgow-1143 to Glasgow-1502, Glasgow-1081
or Glasgow-3970.

## Source findings: archive and estate

PRONI `T/808/14889` is the 1666 County Antrim subsidy roll, but no complete
public image or reliable full transcription was located. Its absence from
online name searches is therefore not a negative result. The 1669 hearth-roll
control records Jo and James `Glascho` consecutively at Kilwaughter Demesne and
Elizabeth `Glascho` at Glenarm Town; it states no relationships and separately
records people surnamed Glass.

The published [Agnew family papers](https://archive.org/stream/hereditarysheri01agnegoog/hereditarysheri01agnegoog_djvu.txt)
trace superior ownership of Ballykeel through an assignment to Patrick Agnew in
1622, confirmation to the younger Patrick in 1636, active estate management in
1645, a 1673 Kilwaughter lease, transfer of the Lochnaw interest in 1708, and
the later 1738 lease to Robert Glasgow. The printed 1645 extract names no
Glasgow but is explicitly incomplete. This clarifies the superior title; it
does not identify the missing Glasgow tenant succession.

The Registry of Deeds cluster adds William Glasgow as an adjoining Larne
occupier in 1736 ([memorial 62131](https://irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=62131)),
Robert and wife Rachel at Ballykeel in 1738 ([memorial 74015](https://irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=74015)),
Robert Glasgow, farmer of Kilwaughter, as a witness in 1741
([memorial 76724](https://irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=76724)), and James
Glasgow, ropemaker of Larne, in 1766
([memorial 204122](https://irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=204122)). These
form a persistent local surname cluster, not a pedigree. Memorial 74015 calls
Rachel Robert's wife but does not call James junior their son.

## Priority proof targets

1. Argyll Estates Archives `GB 3452 ARG-04-03-67-78`: inspect the original 1666 receipt for date, route, residence, associates, signature and endorsements.
2. NRS `GD86/741`: compare the 1688 Ayr instrument written by Robert Glasgow with the receipt's hand and signature.
3. TCD MS 844, fol. 841 and adjacent/enclosed papers; collate the names schedule against the print and Young.
4. UCD `IE UCDA LA/12/5`, transcript pp. 553–558; also `LA/12/1`, chapter XIII, pp. 235–237, and the `LA/12/3` synopsis.
5. NRS [Agnew papers `GD154/505–534`](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?di=y&k=Agnew+Estate&ko=a&reference=GD154&ro=s&st=1&tc=y&tl=n&tn=n&tp=n), Irish Kilwaughter estate material, 1636–1712; archive-only/off-site.
6. PRONI `D/282/2–160`, Kilwaughter leases, assignments, rentals and conveyances, prioritising 1647–1742.
7. PRONI `T/808/14889`, the undigitised 1666 County Antrim subsidy roll, searched parish by parish under Glasgow/Glascho/Glaskow/Glass variants.
8. PRONI `MIC/1B/6` and `MIC1B/6A/1B`, checked against derivative Larne/Kilwaughter register material.
9. Connor probate substitutes for James Glasgow of Kilwater, 1727.

`MIC331` is not a Glasgow-family source; PRONI identifies it as William Ewart
Gladstone papers. The superficially promising replacement `T1762` is mainly
later material, including the Oritor William Glasgow will/codicil, and is lower
priority than the estate, subsidy, church and probate collections. `D2003
[MIC128]` is likewise a later Clough/Ballymena Crawford-Glasgow family
collection, not a proved Larne/Kilwaughter bridge.

Replace the existing archive-priority paragraph with:

```text
The principal uninspected bridge records are the County Antrim subsidy roll
(PRONI T/808/14889), the Kilwaughter estate papers (PRONI D/282/2–160 and NRS
GD154/505–534), and the Larne and Kilwaughter congregational originals (PRONI

this research. T1762 is the correct Glasgow-family/Presbyterian collection
reference, but its exposed Glasgow material is substantially later and it is a
lower priority than the estate, subsidy, church and probate records. D2003

proved Larne/Kilwaughter bridge.
```

## 1666 shipmaster disposition

The Robert Glasgow who was master of the *John of Portrush* in 1666 should be
created as a distinct, unconnected profile. Larne and the vessel's Portrush
name supply relevant cross-channel context, but the vessel name is not a
residence and no occupation, family, signature or exact-event bridge connects
the 1653 Larne witness to the shipmaster.
## Focused FamilySearch full-text result - 20 August 2026

Exact-name searches restricted to Larne or Kilwaughter and the seventeenth
century produced no matching full-text record for Robert Glaskow, James
Glascho, or John Glascho. The useful results occur only in the later William
Glasgow generation: Larne leases in 1736 and 1737, and a 1744 deed whose
execution William personally proved in 1775. They do not bridge Robert to the
proposed children or grandchildren. The relationships descending from
Glasgow-3970 therefore remain uncertain.
