# Findings: James Glasgow (Glasgow-3971), Scottish record of 1645

Last updated: 2026-08-31

## Current conclusion

This profile preserves the Scottish James recorded in 1645. He is not presently
proved to be James Glascho of Kilwaughter (Glasgow-1081). The chronology permits
comparison, but no inspected record supplies migration, residence, occupation or
family evidence joining them.

A Scotland's People index search gives the exact entry as 25 November 1645,
parish Glasgow, OPR `644/1`, reference `40/56`, male, with parents indexed as
`ROT. GLASGOW/JONET CUNNYNGHAM`. The original image was purchased and inspected
on 31 August 2026. It names James's godfathers as **James Cunynghame and Johne
Muir**, but gives no occupation, residence, age or parentage for Robert. The
image therefore adds a useful Cunningham–Muir FAN cluster without identifying
the father as Robert Glasgow baptised in 1618.

The register's abbreviated opening formula is difficult, but the names are
legible as Robert Glasgow, Jonet Cunynghame, child James, and godfathers James
Cunynghame and Johne Muir. The image is preserved at
`sources/1645-11-25-james-glasgow-opr-644-1-40-56.jpg`.

## Index audit

A Scotland's People search for all Glasgow-surname baptisms in Glasgow city,
1610–1670, found the older Robert-and-Jonet couple's children in 1614, 1615,
1616, 1618, 1623, 1625 and 1627, followed by this isolated 1645 entry. No
intermediate Glasgow-surname baptism to parents Robert and Jonet was indexed.
The eighteen-year gap after 1627 makes one continuous parental couple less
likely, but does not prove a younger same-name couple.

The obvious James Cunningham, merchant and burgess admitted in 1629, had a
testament confirmed on 31 January 1642 and therefore cannot be the living
godfather in 1645. The burgess roll supplies several other same-name men,
including James Cunningham of Easter Garden and later entrants connected with
Dunoon and Cobblehaugh, but none is linked to this baptism.

The same-name problem is equally severe for Johne Muir. Testament entries
exclude a Glasgow tailor whose testament was confirmed in 1640 and a mariner
whose testament was confirmed in 1642. Later testaments show that men described
as a maltman, weaver, doctor and of Langside could have been alive in 1645.
The cordiner membership roll's early John Muir entry dates from 1585 and cannot
be selected merely because Robert Glasgow the elder was a cordiner. The
godfathers therefore remain unidentified.

## Best next proof targets

- Identification of godfathers James Cunynghame and Johne Muir in Glasgow
  burgess, testament, craft and property records.
- Kilwaughter estate succession naming an incoming Scottish tenant or relatives.
- Probate substitute for James Glasgow of Kilwater, 1727.
