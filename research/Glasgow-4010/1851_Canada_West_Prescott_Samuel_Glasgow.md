== Biography ==

'''Samuel Glasgow''' was born about 1801 in Ireland. A later death notice described him as formerly of Kilrea and said he emigrated in 1831.<ref name="Sentinel">[https://cotyrone.com/colondonderry/burial/CoLondonderry-DeathsSentinel-G-1829-1869.html County Londonderry ''Sentinel'' death-notice transcriptions], entry published 29 January 1867 for Samuel Glasgow (accessed 13 August 2026). Its “Prescott, U.S.” description is geographically anomalous; the matching merchant lived at Prescott, Canada West.</ref>

Samuel became a merchant at Prescott. The 1851 Canada Directory lists his Main Street general store, and the original 1851 census calls him an Irish-born Presbyterian storekeeper, aged 51 at next birthday.<ref name="Directory1851">Robert W. S. Mackay, [https://numerique.banq.qc.ca/patrimoine/details/52327/5139192 ''The Canada Directory''] (Montreal, 1851), Prescott entry for Samuel Glasgow.</ref><ref name="Census1851">Library and Archives Canada, [https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item/?app=Census1851&op=img&id=e002351566 1851 Census of Canada West, Prescott, page 31, lines 11–18, image e002351566], original schedule (accessed 13 August 2026).</ref>

All eight were Presbyterians.<ref name="Census1851" /> Orena's rare name and age recur in a transcription of the 1861 Prescott grammar-school census, associated with Samuel Glasgow, storekeeper.<ref name="Orena1861">[https://thepastaroundus.wordpress.com/2015/10/12/black-walnuts-squirrels-and-prescott-high-school/ “Black Walnuts, Squirrels and Prescott High School,” ''The Past Around Us''], 1861 entry for Orena Glasgow (accessed 13 August 2026).</ref> A local family history reports ten children, six surviving, and names later sons Thomas Crichton (born 1853) and Henry (born 1857, died young).<ref name="Grenville">Grenville County Historical Society, [https://www.grenvillecountyarchives.ca/uploads/1/4/7/7/147712809/jul_aug_2023_the_grenville_sentine__1_.pdf ''The Grenville Sentinel'', July–August 2023], “The Glasgow House,” pp. 3–4.</ref> Original births, baptisms or estate records are needed to prove individual relationships.

Fuller's 1866–67 directory lists Samuel as a general dealer on King Street.<ref name="Fuller">[https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/001075/f2/nlc008163.pdf ''Fuller's Counties of Leeds, Grenville, Lanark and Renfrew Directory for 1866 & 1867''], Prescott entry for Samuel Glasgow.</ref> His death notice said he died at Prescott after becoming a successful merchant, leaving a widow and wealthy family; local history dates the death to 1866 and identifies Sandy Hill Presbyterian Cemetery as the family burial place.<ref name="Sentinel" /><ref name="Grenville" />

Samuel was a brother of Robert Glasgow, the Prescott shipbuilder. Robert's will, dated 9 October 1849, gives one sixth each to his brothers Daniel, Samuel, John and Thomas, his sister Mary, and collectively the children of his brother James; it does not call James deceased.<ref name="RobertWill1849">Grenville County land records, DGS 8200419, image 35, registered will of Robert Glasgow of Prescott, dated 9 October 1849, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LWY9-S original image].</ref> Memorial 13 records a 30 October 1856 deed, registered 13 November 1856, by which Robert's widow Jane sold Samuel, merchant, property in which he held one undivided sixth share under Robert's will.<ref name="RobertWill1856">Grenville County land records, DGS 8200419, image 36, Memorial 13, dated 30 October 1856 and registered 13 November 1856, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LWYM-6 original image].</ref> Memorial 465, dated 30 July 1861 and registered 7 October, names Daniel of Kilrea, County Londonderry, watchmaker, and wife Margaret, and Thomas of Lisnagaver, County Antrim, farmer, and wife Mary Ann, in transfers to Samuel of two undivided sixth interests under Robert's will.<ref name="RobertWill1861">Grenville County land records, DGS 8200419, image 240, Memorial 465, registered 7 October 1861, printed p. 397, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LWB8-D original image].</ref> The deeds establish the stated Daniel, Thomas and Samuel as Robert's brothers by place, trade and wife; they do not identify the siblings' parents.

A further conveyance identifies Samuel's widow by her recorded name. On 25 March 1868, registered 18 August, Alexander Glasco of Rochester, New York, express agent and son of James Glasco formerly of Prescott, carpenter, conveyed an undivided eighteenth interest to '''Mary Eno Glasgow''', widow of the late Samuel Glasgow, for $166.66.<ref name="AlexanderGlasco1868">Grenville County land records, DGS 8200420, image 120, C114, dated 25 March 1868 and registered 18 August 1868, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQRC-Y original image].</ref> Eno is a recorded name in this deed, not a proved maiden name. This Alexander has not been identified with [[Glasgow-951|Alexander Glasgow]] of Lisnagaver.

== Research Notes ==

A later title-chain account calls Robert Samuel's father and says he left a house to six children.<ref name="Recorder">Theresa Taylor, [https://www.theresataylor.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Auction-all-for-others-Brockville-Recorder.pdf “Auction all for others,”] ''Brockville Recorder & Times'', 22 February 2017.</ref> The original memorials correct the relationship: Robert was Samuel's brother. They do not establish that James the carpenter named in later deeds was Robert's brother, and the apparent share fractions require a full reading of Robert's will before a complete inheritance reconstruction is stated.

A derivative Leeds and Grenville estate index gives a Samuel Glasgow estate year of 1875 and James E. Glasgow 1900–09. Neither provides an identity, file number or contents, so neither proves a death or relationship. The local One Tree export and public WikiTree indexing found no compatible profile; [[Glasgow-2908|Samuel Glasgow of Lisnagaver]] remained in Ireland in 1851, while other same-name profiles conflict in place, age or family.

The uncertain 1851 readings Alba and Lycania, and later tradition of a Robert born about 1835, remain leads rather than grounds for new child profiles.<ref name="AlbaLead">[https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item/?app=Census1851&op=img&id=e002351566 Underlying census image: Alba Glasgow].</ref><ref name="LycaniaLead">[https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item/?app=Census1851&op=img&id=e002351566 Underlying census image: Lycania Glasgow].</ref><ref name="RobertLead">[https://www.theresataylor.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Auction-all-for-others-Brockville-Recorder.pdf Underlying later account: Robert Glasgow].</ref>

Mary Black, formerly Mary Glasgow, is explicitly called the late Robert Glasgow's sister in a 15 September 1869 deed registered 1 July 1870. Her children—including John Black, Rose Ann Black, Jane Miller, Agnes Black and Thomasina Montgomery—conveyed their interests, together with the share inherited from their unmarried intestate sister Elizabeth Black, to Mary Eno Glasgow; the deed expressly cites Robert's will.<ref name="MaryBlack1870">Grenville County land records, DGS 8200420, image 277, C298, dated 15 September 1869 and registered 1 July 1870, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQTY-K original image].</ref>
