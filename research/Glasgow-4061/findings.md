# Findings: [John Glasgow (Glasgow-4061), son of James elder of Corsinkell](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4061)

**Current live state, checked 27 September 2026:** Glasgow-4061 redirects to
[[Glasgow-3193|John Glasgow of Kilwinning]]. NRS GD39/5/59 directly proves
that the Corsinkell John was a son of James elder; identifying him with the
Kilwinning tenant remains probable because the deed names neither Burrowland
nor Dalgarven. The separate-profile assessment below is retained as the
earlier research stage, not the current WikiTree state.

## Current conclusion

This is the record-defined John Glasgow expressly called a son of
[[Glasgow-1168|James Glasgow elder]] in the 9 February 1571 Corsinkell record.
The relationship is **direct and confident**. The same wording makes
[[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow younger]] his brother.

The earlier FamilySearch match to “John, in Bog of Steivinstoun” was based on a
row-alignment error and is withdrawn. That entry is dated 6 November 1685, not
17 April 1643, and is already represented by the index-confirmation record for
NRS `CC9/7/47`. The 17 April 1643 entry is a different John: “in Rashie
[FamilySearch transcript: Rashlic], par. of Kilbride.” The index gives no
relatives, the precise locality is unresolved, and the underlying testament
has not been examined. Do not identify him as Glasgow-4061 on the present
evidence. The structured profile now retains only the lower bound supported by
GD39/5/59: John was alive on 9 February 1571, so death is recorded as after
that date; no upper bound or exact death date is known.
[[Glasgow-3193|The John recorded at Burrowland and Dalgarven in 1559-60]]
remains a separate possible identity match, not proved by this evidence.

## Source findings
National Records of Scotland, Charter Chest of the Earls of Glencairn,
[GD39/5/59](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F5%2F59),
letters of reversion, 9 February 1571. The official catalogue describes the
40-shilling land of Corsinkell as occupied by “James Glasgow, elder James
Glasgow and John Glasgow, his sons, Christian Campbell.” Catalogue entry
inspected 30 August 2026; original open but off site, no online image.

FamilySearch, [*Glasgow. Wills 1547–1800*](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CS8K-D98V?view=index&keywords=Glasgow&searchForm=advanced&lang=en&groupId=M97N-2NT),
item `#007960708`, image 112, printed index p. 196. The heading and ditto marks
establish Glasgow as the surname for the John entries. “John, in Rashie
[transcript: Rashlic], par. of Kilbride,” is dated 17 April 1643; the separate
“John, in Bog of Steivinstoun” entry is dated 6 November 1685. The former has
not been matched to this profile; the latter is not a plausible match given
the profile’s record-defined 1571 context. These are index registration dates,
not stated death dates. The [Scotland’s People wills and testaments
guide](https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/guides/wills-and-testaments) explains
that the index date is when the testament was recorded and that the index omits
the deceased’s death date.

## Identity assessment

The FamilySearch John entries are not identified with Glasgow-4061. His 1571
record establishes only that he was alive on 9 February 1571. The structured
profile records death **after 9 February 1571** as this last-known-alive lower
bound; no upper bound, death place or exact age is known. Any age automatically
displayed from open date bounds is arithmetic only, not an independently
sourced age.
