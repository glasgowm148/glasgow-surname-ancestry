# Findings: Glasgow-4070

Last updated: 2026-09-23

## Current conclusion

[Nancy Glasgow (Glasgow-4070)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4070),
unmarried, "about 68" when she made her will at Plattsburgh, New York, on
15 January 1889 (born c.1820–21), died on 31 October 1891 and her will was
proved on 9 May 1892. Irish-born, she was a long-term servant in the Hiram and
Delia Walworth household in Plattsburgh (1860, 1870, 1880 censuses); Hiram
Walworth acted as her attorney in 1885 and executor in 1892.

**Her family of origin is the Rasharkin-parish Glasgow family of Lisnagaver.**
Her will names Margaret (Dysart) Kennedy as her niece and Robert Anderson as her
nephew; the Rasharkin Presbyterian register records Margaret as the daughter of
James Dysart and Jane Glascaw (Granagh, 1846) and Robert as the son of James
Anderson and Betty Glascaw (Drumack, 1834). The January 1892 probate citation
lists children of John Glasgow late of "Baladonly", Alexander Glasgow late of
Rasharkin, Robert Glasgow, Jane Dysart, Ann McClelland and Mary Thompson,
and names David, James and Samuel Anderson at Gortahar. It does not name
Elizabeth as their mother; that identification comes from the combined family
records. The will's explicit kin terms, the citation and the Irish records
support a **probable network of seven deceased sibling branches**, rather than
an express statement of every sibling relationship or an exhaustive pedigree.

Consequences:

- Nancy was probably a sister of [Alexander Glasgow (Glasgow-951)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-951)
  of Lisnagaver (c.1811–1888), whose children James, Mary and Ann fit the citation,
  and of [John Glasgow (Glasgow-867)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-867).
  James Garvin and Ann support John's identification; Lizzie remains unidentified
  and the cited Ballydonnelly residence is not independently explained.
- **John Glasgow, farmer**, named as John-867's father in 1846, is a conditional
  candidate for Nancy's and Alexander's father. Neither has a direct father
  statement; half-sibling relationships need not share the same father.
  [Glasgow-4090](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4090) carries the proposed
  father, but identifying that man with the 1796 Desertoghill flax-premium
  recipient is a separate, unproved identity hypothesis.
- The older Alexander-and-Rosanna tradition remains unsupported, but omission
  of Thomas-987's children does not disprove it or exclude Thomas, Daniel-3896
  or Robert-1367 categorically. The list's completeness is untested; the
  maintained reconstruction itself has an unexplained omission of Alexander's
  son Robert (whose later Scottish survival needs original-record verification).
  A person outside Nancy's sibling group could still be Alexander's half-sibling.
  This review supports no automatic parent replacement or removal.

The reconstructed birth span, Samuel c.1790 to Nancy c.1820–21, does not prove
that one father had two wives. Ages, full/half relationships and the identity
of each proposed sibling must be tested separately. Full argument and next
records: `research/Glasgow-951/findings.md`, section *Nancy Glasgow's next-of-kin
citation and Alexander's father*.

**23 September 2026 — father evaluation.** Records already held were reweighted, with no new record. The review rates **John Glasgow, farmer** (Glasgow-4090) as the *probable* father of Nancy, Alexander-951 and John-867. The notice treats Alexander's children as heirs, in the same class as the nieces and nephews named in the will. Every sibling branch with a recorded birth surname carries Glasgow, so the shared parent was the father. John-867's 1846 marriage names that father. Maternity and full/half blood remain unknown. The petition and heir schedule are still the decisive test. See `research/Glasgow-951/findings.md`, *Current conclusion — father evaluation, 23 September 2026*.

**21 September 2026 — evidence audit.** The saved 23 January newspaper image
was checked against its transcription. The sibling interpretation remains
probable; categorical exclusions and the automatic common-father inference
are withdrawn. The pre-1898 limit on collateral representation concerned
personal property; this citation concerns real and personal estate, so it
cannot be treated as a simple exhaustive personalty distribution list. See
[Matter of De Voe (1905), explaining the pre-1898 distinction](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/appellate-division/1905/107-ad-245.html).
The petition, heir schedule and service papers remain the priority evidence.

**Second printing of the citation and the siblings' records, 17 September 2026.** The *Plattsburgh Republican* printed the citation on 23 and 30 January and 13 February 1892 (Fulton History; copies and transcription in `sources/`). Its wording — "…the children of Mary Thompson whose names and places of residence are unknown; **to all other heirs or next of kin of Nancy Glasgow, deceased, if any; to** Mary E. Walworth … Robert Wallace Anderson, Dinah Anderson … the legatees, heirs and next of kin of the testator" — labels the Glasgow/Dysart/Anderson/McClelland/Thompson people as heirs and next of kin and the rest as legatees. Residences: Robert Dysart at Rasharkin; Sallie [Dysart] at Ballymena; David, James and Samuel Anderson at Gortahar; Glasgow children unknown.

Records for the proposed sibling branches (these records identify people; they do not independently prove every sibling relationship): Elizabeth Anderson d. 7 Sep 1868 Gortahar aged 66; Jane Dysart d. 13 Sep 1868 aged 64 (husband James Dysart's 1881 will names daughter Margaret Kennedy, son Robert, granddaughter Mary Anne McCaughern — daughter of Sarah/"Sallie" who married John McCaughern of Gortereghy in 1851); Ann McClelland = wife of Andrew McClelland, carpenter, Killyless, Craigs parish, whose sons Andrew (d. 1870) and Robert (m. 1873) lived in Bridgeton, Glasgow (Scottish civil records name "Ann Glasgow" as their mother); Robert Glasgow probably the man who died 31 Mar 1868 aged 71 (Ballymoney); Mary Thompson unidentified (candidate: Mary, widow of William Thompson, farmer, d. 1888 Tannybrake aged 73). Full detail and URLs: `research/Glasgow-951/research-log-2026-09-17-live.md` and the 17 September (part 2) section of `research/Glasgow-951/findings.md`.
