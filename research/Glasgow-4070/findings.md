## Current conclusion and new evidence — 29 September 2026

The **Plattsburgh Sentinel, 6 November 1891, p. 8**, reports Nancy's death at **Plattsburgh on 31 October 1891**, **aged about 75**, followed by a funeral on Monday afternoon at the **Peristrome Presbyterian church**. [Original newspaper page](https://www.nyshistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=plts18911106-01.1.8). The original PDF and an unaltered crop are saved in `record-excerpts/`. The notice names no relatives or birthplace. Its c.1815–16 birth implication conflicts with Nancy's will's c.1820–21 implication; neither approximate age resolves the conflict. The independently corroborated death date is stronger than the cemetery transcription alone. The maintained census indexes give still other ages: 30 in 1860, 50 in 1870 and 65 in 1880 ([MC7C-HHK](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MC7C-HHK), [M8FF-ZQD](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M8FF-ZQD), [MZZF-HDJ](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MZZF-HDJ)). These are not new original-image inspections in this review; they reinforce that neither selected birth estimate is a proved bound.

The [original probate docket](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:33SQ-GYCZ-X98?view=fullText) was visually checked: **DGS 005115656, image 34, vol. 6, p. 6**. It records a guardian for minor heirs at the May 1892 probate, Cabell McLean's appointment as collateral inheritance tax appraiser in March 1894, and his report filed **28 April 1894**. No parents, heir schedule or appraisal text appears in this entry. Robert Wallace Anderson, son of Nancy's deceased nephew, is a concrete younger beneficiary who could explain the guardian; the ward is not named in the docket. Nancy calls both generations nephew, so exact kin terms require independent relationship checks.

The broader [probate catalogue 223236](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/223236) includes minutes and letters beyond the will series. Neither catalogue proves that the petition or tax report was never filmed or does not survive. The G index in the earlier minutes volume DGS 005115623, image 12, and a scoped full-text Glasgow search yielded no entry; this was not an exhaustive estate-file search.

The specific Glenleslie daughter Nancy died in 1847 aged 36 and is conclusively a different person. A single-family model making both Nancys daughters of John-1022 needs an unexplained namesake daughter or a different sibling reconstruction. John farmer remains the probable shared-father model, with maternity and the Desertoghill identity unresolved. See Alexander's dated competing-theory table for the alternatives and exact search scope.

**Mary Thompson branch, 29 September:** the mother Mary Glasgon Thomson named in [Mary Thomson’s 1860 Old Monkland marriage index](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:6TLK-6T1J) is a new candidate only. ## Prior relationship assessment — consolidated family reconstruction

[Nancy Glasgow (Glasgow-4070)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4070),
unmarried, "about 68" when she made her will at Plattsburgh, New York, on
15 January 1889 (implying birth c.1820–21), was reported as about 75 at her death
on 31 October 1891 (implying c.1815–16). These approximate ages conflict. Her will was
proved on 9 May 1892. Irish-born, she was a long-term servant in the Hiram and
Delia Walworth household in Plattsburgh (1860, 1870, 1880 censuses); Hiram
Walworth acted as her attorney in 1885 and executor in 1892.

**Her family of origin is the Rasharkin-parish Glasgow family of Lisnagaver.**
Her will names Margaret (Dysart) Kennedy as her niece and Robert Anderson as her
nephew; the Rasharkin Presbyterian register records Margaret as the daughter of
James Dysart and Jane Glascaw (Granagh, 1846) and Robert as the son of James
Anderson and Betty Glascaw (Drumack, 1834). The January 1892 probate citation
lists children of John Glasgow late of "Baladonly", Alexander Glasgow late of
Rasharkin, Robert Glasgow, Jane Dysart, Ann McClelland and Mary Thompson,
and names David, James and Samuel Anderson at Gortahar. It does not name
Elizabeth as their mother; that identification comes from the combined family
records. The will's explicit kin terms, the citation and the Irish records
support a **probable network of seven deceased sibling branches**, rather than
an express statement of every sibling relationship or an exhaustive pedigree.

Consequences:

- Nancy was probably a sister of [Alexander Glasgow (Glasgow-951)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-951)
  of Lisnagaver (c.1811–1888), whose children James, Mary and Ann fit the citation,
  and of [John Glasgow (Glasgow-867)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-867).
  James Garvin and Ann support John's identification; Lizzie remains unidentified
  and the cited Ballydonnelly residence is not independently explained.
- **John Glasgow, farmer**, named as John-867's father in 1846, is a conditional
  candidate for Nancy's and Alexander's father. Neither has a direct father
  statement; half-sibling relationships need not share the same father.
  [Glasgow-4090](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4090) carries the proposed
  father, but identifying that man with the 1796 Desertoghill flax-premium
  recipient is a separate, unproved identity hypothesis.
- The older Alexander-and-Rosanna tradition remains unsupported, but omission
  of Thomas-987's children does not disprove it or exclude Thomas, Daniel-3896
  or Robert-1367 categorically. The list's completeness is untested; the
  citation itself omits Alexander's son Robert, who was living in December 1891
  (Belfast News-Letter, 6 January 1892; see the 6 October 2026 assessment below).
  A person outside Nancy's sibling group could still be Alexander's half-sibling.
  This review supports no automatic parent replacement or removal.

The reconstructed birth span, Samuel c.1790 to Nancy c.1820–21, does not prove
that one father had two wives. Ages, full/half relationships and the identity
of each proposed sibling must be tested separately. Full argument and next
records: `research/Glasgow-951/findings.md`, section *Nancy Glasgow's next-of-kin
citation and Alexander's father*.

**23 September 2026 — father evaluation.** Records already held were reweighted, with no new record. The review rates **John Glasgow, farmer** (Glasgow-4090) as the *probable* father of Nancy, Alexander-951 and John-867. The notice treats Alexander's children as heirs, in the same class as the nieces and nephews named in the will. Every sibling branch with a recorded birth surname carries Glasgow, so the shared parent was the father. John-867's 1846 marriage names that father. Maternity and full/half blood remain unknown. The petition and heir schedule are still the decisive test. See `research/Glasgow-951/findings.md`, *Current conclusion — father evaluation, 23 September 2026*.

**21 September 2026 — evidence audit.** The saved 23 January newspaper image
was checked against its transcription. The sibling interpretation remains
probable; categorical exclusions and the automatic common-father inference
are withdrawn. The pre-1898 limit on collateral representation concerned
personal property; this citation concerns real and personal estate, so it
cannot be treated as a simple exhaustive personalty distribution list. See
[Matter of De Voe (1905), explaining the pre-1898 distinction](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/appellate-division/1905/107-ad-245.html).
The petition, heir schedule and service papers remain the priority evidence.

**Second printing of the citation and the siblings' records, 17 September 2026.** The *Plattsburgh Republican* printed the citation on 23 and 30 January and 13 February 1892 (Fulton History; copies and transcription in `sources/`). Its wording — "…the children of Mary Thompson whose names and places of residence are unknown; **to all other heirs or next of kin of Nancy Glasgow, deceased, if any; to** Mary E. Walworth … Robert Wallace Anderson, Dinah Anderson … the legatees, heirs and next of kin of the testator" — labels the Glasgow/Dysart/Anderson/McClelland/Thompson people as heirs and next of kin and the rest as legatees. Residences: Robert Dysart at Rasharkin; Sallie [Dysart] at Ballymena; David, James and Samuel Anderson at Gortahar; Glasgow children unknown.

Records for the proposed sibling branches (these records identify people; they do not independently prove every sibling relationship): Elizabeth Anderson d. 7 Sep 1868 Gortahar aged 66; Jane Dysart d. 13 Sep 1868 aged 64 (husband James Dysart's 1881 will names daughter Margaret Kennedy, son Robert, granddaughter Mary Anne McCaughern — daughter of Sarah/"Sallie" who married John McCaughern of Gortereghy in 1851); Ann McClelland = wife of Andrew McClelland, carpenter, Killyless, Craigs parish, whose sons Andrew (d. 1870) and Robert (m. 1873) lived in Bridgeton, Glasgow (Scottish civil records name "Ann Glasgow" as their mother); Robert Glasgow probably the man who died 31 Mar 1868 aged 71 (Ballymoney); Mary Thompson unidentified (candidate: Mary, widow of William Thompson, farmer, d. 1888 Tannybrake aged 73). Full detail and URLs: `research/Glasgow-951/research-log-2026-09-17-live.md` and the 17 September (part 2) section of `research/Glasgow-951/findings.md`.

## Relationship assessment — completeness of the citation, 6 October 2026

Alexander's son Robert (Glasgow-938) was living when the citation was drawn: the [Belfast News-Letter of 6 January 1892](https://www.newspapers.com/image/388125998/) calls Jane Graham "wife of Robert Glasgow, formerly of Rasharkin", eight days before the citation of 14 January. The citation names only James, Mary and Ann as Alexander's children. It therefore left out a living member of a branch it did list, so its lists reflect partial knowledge and omissions from it carry little weight. The inclusion of Alexander's children still supports the sibling reading.

James Anderson, husband of the probable sister Elizabeth (Glasgow-4071), died at Gortahar on 18 April 1888, reported age 94 ([entry 333](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/deaths_returns/deaths_1888/06179/4766465.pdf)). His sons David, James and Samuel are the Gortahar Andersons of the citation.

## Identity assessment — Illinois James G. Glasgow excluded, 15 September 2026

The 1892 probate citation names "James G. Glasgow, Ann and Lizzie, the children of John Glasgow, late of Baladonly, county Antrim". A FamilySearch full-text search for "James G Glasgow" (81 hits, many duplicates) found mainly a Jo Daviess County, Illinois, land-record cluster for James G. Glasgow of Guilford Township, with wife Sarah, from the late 1860s to the 1890s. That man is represented on WikiTree by [Glasgow-3128](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3128), whose recorded father is a James Glasgow, not a John; targeted searches for James G. Glasgow with Antrim and for John Glasgow with Baladonly returned nothing. Glasgow-3128 is therefore a different man and should not be attached to Nancy's family.

The exclusion rests on the WikiTree-recorded father of Glasgow-3128 (a tree statement, not an inspected record) set against the citation's explicit father John. Later research identifies the citation's James G. with James Garvin Glasgow, son of [John Glasgow (Glasgow-867)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-867), which is consistent with this exclusion.

## Source findings — New Zealand residents named in the 1892 probate citation, 30 September 2026

The probate citation in Nancy's estate, dated 14 January 1892 and printed in the *Plattsburgh Republican* (23 and 30 January and 13 February 1892; transcription in `sources/plattsburgh_republican_citation_1892_transcription.md`) and the [*Plattsburgh Sentinel*, 26 February 1892, p.6](https://www.nyshistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=plts18920226-01.1.6), also names **Martha Dysart and Elizabeth Weir, "both of whom reside at Canterbury, New Zealand"**, and **Mary Jane, daughter of Jane Dysart, "supposed to be living at Canterbury, New Zealand"**. These complete the residences listed above (Rasharkin, Ballymena, Gortahar, West Troy).

The citation lists them among the people cited as heirs or next of kin but does not state how Martha Dysart or Elizabeth Weir were related to Nancy; that they belong to Jane (Glasgow) Dysart's family is an inference. The profile draft links Mary Jane to Dysart-1845 (Mary Jane (Dysart) Hampton); that identification comes from the draft, not from the citation. Glasgow-951's findings record New Zealand indexes for a Mary Jane Dysart who married Christopher Hampton in 1871 and a Mary Jane Hampton who died in 1881, and leave her identity with the 1837 Rasharkin child unresolved; if she is the same woman, the citation's "supposed to be living" was out of date. The New Zealand entries are a lead for the Dysart branch, not evidence for Nancy's parentage.
