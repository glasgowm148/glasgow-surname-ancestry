== Biography ==

'''Ann Glasgow''' married '''Andrew McClelland''', a carpenter, and the family
lived at Killyless, Craigs parish, County Antrim. No own-event record of Ann has
been found, but her children's records establish her name and identity.

Their son '''Andrew McClelland''', a weaver of Killyless, married '''Jane
McCracken''' of Ballyconnelly (daughter of William McCracken, labourer) at the
parish church of Craigs on 24 December 1860; the marriage names Andrew's father
as '''Andrew McClelland, carpenter'''.<ref name="Andrew1860">General Register
Office of Ireland, civil marriage register, Craigs parish church, 24 December
1860, entry 70: Andrew McClelland, full age, bachelor, weaver, of Killyless,
son of Andrew McClelland, carpenter; Jane McCracken, full age, spinster, of
Ballyconnelly, daughter of William McCracken, labourer; witnesses Samuel
Thompson and John Bacchus.
[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1860/09582/5474474.pdf
register image], group registration ID 3299926.</ref>

The same son died at Bridgeton, Lanarkshire, Scotland, on 17 January 1870,
aged 35 (born about 1835), an engine keeper; his death record names his parents
as '''Andrew McClelland and Ann Glasgow McClelland''' and his widow as Jane
McCracken.<ref name="AndrewDeath1870">Scotland civil registration, death,
17 January 1870, Bridgeton, Lanarkshire: Andrew McClelland, 35, engine keeper,
parents Andrew McClelland and Ann Glasgow, wife Jane McCracken.
[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XSSG-M9CW FamilySearch index record XSSG-M9CW],
"Scotland, Civil Registration, 1855–1875, 1881, 1891".</ref>

A second son '''Robert McLelland''', a carter aged 32 (born about 1841),
married '''Martha McLean''' (daughter of John McLean and Catherine McIlroy) on
18 July 1873; his record names his parents as '''Andrew McLelland and Ann
Glasgow McLelland'''.<ref name="RobertMarriage1873">Scotland civil
registration, marriage, 18 July 1873: Robert McLelland, 32, carter, parents
Andrew McLelland and Ann Glasgow; bride Martha McLean, 25, parents John McLean
and Catherine McIlroy.
[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:X95J-MXQP FamilySearch index record X95J-MXQP],
"Scotland, Civil Registration, 1855–1875, 1881, 1891".</ref>

Ann was almost certainly a sister of [[Glasgow-4070|Nancy Glasgow]] of
Plattsburgh, New York. The 14 January 1892 probate citation in Nancy's estate
lists "the children of Ann McClelland, whose names and places of residence are
unknown" among Nancy's heirs and next of kin, alongside the children of Nancy's
other siblings (the Glasgow, Dysart and Anderson families of Rasharkin parish,
County Antrim).<ref name="Citation1892">''The Plattsburgh Sentinel'',
26 February 1892, p. 6, legal notices (citation dated 14 January 1892,
returnable 9 May 1892): "the children of Ann McClelland whose names and places
of residence are unknown; the children of Mary Thompson whose names and places
of residence are unknown; to all other heirs or next of kin of Nancy Glasgow,
deceased".
[https://www.nyshistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=plts18920226-01.1.6 newspaper page].
A second printing appeared in ''The Plattsburgh Republican'', 23 and 30 January
and 13 February 1892.</ref>

The family's emigration to Glasgow, Scotland, between 1860 and 1863 — the same
Calton/Bridgeton district where Ann's probable nephew [[Glasgow-938|Robert
Glasgow]] settled — explains why Nancy's executors had no addresses for Ann's
children.

== Research Notes ==

Ann is reconstructed from her children's records and the probate citation; she
has no located own birth, marriage or death record (her marriage pre-dates
civil registration and does not appear in the surviving Rasharkin Presbyterian
transcription). Her LNAB Glasgow rests on (a) the sons' records naming her "Ann
Glasgow" and (b) the placement of "Ann McClelland" among Nancy Glasgow's
siblings in the citation. No marriage certificate directly proving "Ann
Glasgow m. Andrew McClelland" has been located. If she married again, or if her
maiden surname is wrong, the citation alone would still support her membership
of the sibling group.

The exact townland is uncertain. Killyless is the daughter-in-law's residence
at the 1860 marriage and the younger Andrew's own residence; the elder
Andrew's occupation (carpenter) is given in the same record.
