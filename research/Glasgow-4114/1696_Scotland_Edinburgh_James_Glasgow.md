== Biography ==
'''James Glasgow''' was probably born about 1680. This is an uncertain estimate from his 1696 apprenticeship; his age and birthplace were not recorded. Edinburgh is usable only as an uncertain location inferred from the apprenticeship and his father's burgess status.

On 22 April 1696 James, son of James Glasgow, weaver and burgess, was entered as apprentice to William Adam, apothecary and burgess of Edinburgh.<ref name="Apprentice1696">Henry Paton, ed., [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/edinburgh_apprentices_~_1666_to_1700_~_1929.pdf ''Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh, 1666–1700''] (Scottish Record Society, 1929), printed p. 37, entry dated 22 April 1696; see also Findmypast, [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FEDINBURGH%2FAPPRENTICES%2F0005161%2F1&tab=this transcript SCOT/EDINBURGH/APPRENTICES/0005161/1], captured 3 September 2026.</ref>

== Research Notes ==
=== Edinburgh apprentice family ===
[[Glasgow-3285|Thomas Glasgow]], the baxter apprentice entered on 11 October 1699, was probably James’s brother (79% subjective confidence). Both apprenticeship entries describe their father as James Glasgow, weaver and burgess of Edinburgh. Their entry dates are only three years apart, and Thomas is called the youngest son. Thomas’s identification with the child baptised to [[Glasgow-2682|James Glasgow]] and [[Duffe-18|Isobell Duffe]] in 1684 fits an apprenticeship at almost fifteen. The Duffe household’s sons James, baptised in 1679 and 1680, fit the 1696 entry at seventeen or sixteen. Neither is individually identified with this apprentice.

The shared paternal description supports brotherhood, but neither entry names a mother or address. Two matching craftsmen remain possible, and the Thomas apprenticeship-to-baptism match is an inference. [[Glasgow-1150|James the older Edinburgh weaver]] remains the preferred father-profile match at 76%. His possible identity with the Duffe husband is unresolved; Isobell is not thereby established as this James’s mother.

[[Duncan-16106|Agnes Duncan]], wife of James the weaver, was buried on 21 January 1674, before the Duffe children begin in 1677. Successive marriages are chronologically compatible. The older craftsman’s late fatherhood remains relevant, while the Duffe husband’s circa-1655 birth is an estimate rather than a recorded age. The possible identification with the cancelled 1690 apprentice [[Glasgow-4131|James Glasgow]] also remains open; that entry’s deceased father would require reconciliation with the master recorded in 1693.<ref name="EdinburghBrothersReview">[https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/edinburgh_apprentices_~_1666_to_1700_~_1929.pdf#page=47 Watson, Edinburgh Apprentices (1929), printed p.37, PDF p.47: James1696 and Thomas1699; printed page visually checked18 October2026]; [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_internments_~_greyfriars_churchyard_1658-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=268 Paton, Greyfriars interments1658–1700 (1902), printed p.252, PDF p.268: Agnes Duncan and James the weaver’s unnamed children; visually checked18 October2026]; [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/register_of_internments_~_greyfriars_churchyard_1658-1700_~_1898.pdf#page=212 Paton, same register, printed p.196, PDF p.212: Agnes Duncan, wife of James Glasgow,weaver,21 January1674; cross-index of same burial, not independent corroboration].</ref>


=== Apprentice family review —18 October2026 ===

James [[Glasgow-1150]] is selected as this apprentice’s new probable father,76%. The live structured father remains blank.

Select the Edinburgh weaver and burgess James [[Glasgow-1150]] as the probable father of the record-defined 1696 apprentice James [[Glasgow-4114]], at 76% subjective confidence. Printed page 37 names the apprentice’s father as James Glasgow, weaver and burgess. Name, occupation, civic status and Edinburgh setting identify a particular established craftsman rather than just a same-name Scottish father. The maintained 1641 apprenticeship and 1649 burgess cross-reference securely establish James-1150 as a weaver and burgess; printed page 72 also records a James Glasgow, weaver and burgess, taking John Paterson as apprentice on 18 October 1693. The maintained burgess-roll review found one matching admission, although an index cannot rule out another man. This makes James-1150 the preferred father-profile match. The child account itself is defined by the 22 April 1696 entry, so no later-life identification is needed for that child.

The 76% is an individual research judgment for the whole father-profile identification, not a measured frequency. The published entry proves a father called James, weaver and burgess; it does not supply his apprenticeship date, wife, home address or age. Another matching craftsman, including a Lindsaylands man who also held burgess rights, remains possible. The circa-1680 child date and circa-1620 father date are estimates, not recorded ages; the nominal sixty-year interval makes late fatherhood relevant but is not a contradiction. James’s 1641 apprenticeship supplies no exact birth year. The younger James’s mother remains unknown: neither Bessie Whyte nor Agnes Duncan is transferred merely because she married the proposed father. The father’s existing probable Robert ancestry and all other family grades remain unchanged.

The earlier claim that the 1690 and 1696 apprentices must be different men is withdrawn. The 1690 entry explicitly calls its Lindsayland weaver father deceased and says that the apprenticeship was scored out. The 1696 entry omits a death qualification; silence does not prove that father alive. A cancelled cordiner apprenticeship followed by an apothecary apprenticeship six years later is compatible with one man, although not established. The matching apprentice/father names and father’s weaving trade support testing that possibility; burgess status is given only for the 1696 father and the two masters differ. Keep the two existing profiles distinct pending an identifier; do not create them again. If they prove to be one child, the selected James-1150 father would need reconsideration because the 1690 father was dead whereas the Edinburgh master appears in 1693. This alternative limits the father assessment rather than being erased.

James [[Glasgow-1376]] is not excluded from the 1696 apprentice merely because his former 1657 birth was a misread parent role. Removing that false date supplies no identity conflict. His Whitecastle adult occurrence in 1672 makes a 1696 first apprenticeship less ordinary, and no trade or residence bridge identifies him; retain him as a weaker compatible comparison. The proposed father of Whitecastle James is unchanged. Other Edinburgh James households remain comparisons, without unproved mergers, sibling transfers or replacement birth dates.

This materialises a father already favoured in the apprentice’s live biography, whose structured Father field is still zero; it is a genuinely outstanding new father attachment, not a confidence correction to an attached father. Both apprentices were already created on WikiTree. Their record-only creation suggestions are retired in favour of those exact profiles. No live edit, new WikiTree person or new manuscript discovery is claimed. The inspected source is Watson’s 1929 printed register, not the original city register; earlier citations incorrectly named Henry Paton as editor.

Check the original indentures for cancellation, endorsement or a repeated identifier; compare the 1690 and 1696 apprentices before merging them. Seek the weaver’s testament or incorporation family entry for wife and sons. The 1696 master reads Wm. Adam, not the Findmypast transcript’s Adamson. A score-out does not establish a death.

The Findmypast transcript renders the master as William Adamson. The published register reads “Wm. Adam”; that reading is used here.

[[Glasgow-1150|James Glasgow]], the documented Edinburgh weaver and burgess, is probably the father. He was admitted burgess in 1649 and was still acting as a weaver, burgess and apprenticeship master in 1693. The published register also places a Robert, son of James Glasgow, weaver, in an apprenticeship in 1672 and a Thomas, youngest son of James Glasgow, weaver and burgess, in one in 1699. This is a coherent later-child group around the same burgh craftsman, but neither the 1696 nor 1699 entry names a mother. The father attachment should remain uncertain pending a baptism, testament or incorporation record with a second identifier.

This James is not the 1690 apprentice, whose father was explicitly deceased. [[Glasgow-1376|James Glasgow]] is excluded because his former 1657 birth was a record-role error based on Marion Glasgow's baptism and his remaining identity has no apothecary-apprenticeship evidence.
