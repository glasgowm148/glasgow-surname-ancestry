# James Glasgow, surgeon: will of 15 June 1716, proved 21 August 1719

Source: The National Archives, Prerogative Court of Canterbury, **PROB 11/569/479**, [Discovery D682397](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/details/r/D682397). Two supplied PDF pages; the will starts near the foot of PDF page 1 and continues at the top of page 2, followed by its probate. Other entries visible on those pages are separate wills.

## Verification and family identification — 12 November 2026

The supplied transcription below is retained with its uncertain readings. Both PDF pages have been visually inspected, and the decisive clauses naming **“my own Mother Isabell Duff”** and **“my Sister Jean”**, the surgeon/Swift/Sheerness description, the will date and probate date have been checked. This is partial verification of the supplied transcription, not a claim that every word or Latin expansion has been independently settled. The property wording, cancelled passage and tentative personal names remain unresolved.

The occupation, ship and station strongly match [James Glasgow-4124](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4124), the naval surgeon. [James Glasgow-2682](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2682) is his proposed father, the Edinburgh weaver and burgess, rather than this testator. The separate, previously checked 1733 Edinburgh testament index names the surgeon as son of that weaver and Isobel Duff. Its registration date must not be treated as proof that the surgeon was alive in 1733.

[Jean Glasgow-3088](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3088), indexed in Edinburgh on 13 April 1677 to James Glasgow and Issobell Duff, is a strong candidate for the sister. The mother, sister and Edinburgh context reinforce the family identification; the sister's forename alone does not prove her identity with that baptismal child. No father's name can reliably be extracted from the cancelled passage. The surgeon remains separate from the apprenticeship and childhood accounts pending their own identity review.

The testator was alive on 15 June 1716 and dead by probate on 21 August 1719. Neither date is an exact recorded death date.

## Supplied transcription

**The will is dated 15 June 1716 and was proved on 21 August 1719.** Below is James Glasgow’s entry, including the Latin probate.

I’ve added punctuation and paragraph breaks, expanded common abbreviations, and retained the original spelling where clear. **[Word?]** marks an uncertain reading.

## Will of James Glasgow

### Page 1

> **In the Name of God Amen.**
>
> The fifteenth day of June One thousand Seven hundred and Sixteen Year of our Lord, I James Glasgow, Surgeon of his Majesties Sloop the Swift at Shireness, Being very well in body and of perfect mind and memory, thanks be given unto God, therefore calling unto mind the mortality of my body and knowing that it is appointed for all Men once to dye, do make and ordain this my last Will and Testament, that is to say:
>
> Principally and first of all I give and recommend my Soul into the hands of God that gave it, And as for my body to the place appointed for it, not at all doubting of the Resurrection thereof at the last Day. And as touching such worldly Estate wherewith it hath pleased God to bless me in this Life, I give, devise and dispose of the same

### Page 2

> in the following manner and form:
>
> Imprimis I give and bequeath to my [cancelled passage, partly illegible] to my own Mother Isabell Duff all such [Silver land?] as of right belongs to me in North Britain, particularly in Edinburgh, as also out of my money in the publick funds of the Kingdom of Great Britain the Sume of One hundred pounds, which on her death is to fall to my Sister Jean and her heirs, and failing heirs of her body to the Poor of Lady Yesters Church Parish.
>
> Item I give to my Sister Jean the Sume of fifty pounds lawfull money of Great Britain out of the said publick funds.
>
> Item for the use of the Poor of Lady Yesters Church Parish at Edinburgh the Sume of fifty pounds lawfull money of Great Britain out of the said funds.
>
> Item to my very good friend John Barclay, Surgeon, whom I appoint Executor of this my last Will and Testament, the Sume of fifty pounds.
>
> Item to my very good friend John [Allison?] in Popings Court the Sume of Twenty pounds to him or his Wife. What remains belonging to me to be proportionably divided amongst the above named Persons.
>
> And I do hereby disallow, revoke and disannull all and every other former Testaments, Wills and Legacies, requests and Executors by me in any ways before this time named, willed and bequeathed, Ratifying and confirming this and no other to be my last Will and Testament.
>
> In Witness whereof I have hereunto set my hand and Seal day and year aforesaid.
>
> **James Glasgow.**
>
> Sign’d, Seal’d, publish’d, pronounc’d and declar’d by me the said James Glasgow in the presence of us the Subscribers as my last Will and Testament. Witness:
>
> John [Whinyard?]  
> Thomas Watts, Steward.

## Latin probate entry

*Common abbreviations expanded; the final “jurat.” is left unexpanded.*

> Probatum fuit hujusmodi Testamentum apud London Coram Venerabili Viro Johanne Andrew Legum Doctore Surrogato Venerabilis et egregij Viri Johannis Bettesworth Legum etiam Doctoris Curiae Prerogativae Cantuariensis Magistri Custodis sive Commissarij legitime constituti Vicesimo primo die mensis Augusti Anno Domini Millesimo Septingentesimo Decimo Nono Juramento Johannis Barclay Executoris unici in dicto Testamento nominati Cui commissa fuit Administratio omnium et singulorum bonorum jurium et creditorum dicti defuncti de bene et fideliter Administrando eadem ad Sancta Dei Evangelia jurat.

### English translation

> This will was proved at London on 21 August 1719 before the venerable John Andrew, Doctor of Laws, acting as surrogate for the venerable and distinguished John Bettesworth, also Doctor of Laws, lawfully appointed Master, Keeper or Commissary of the Prerogative Court of Canterbury, upon the oath of John Barclay, the sole executor named in the will. Administration of all the deceased’s goods, rights and credits was granted to him, having sworn upon the Holy Gospels to administer them faithfully and properly.

## Reading notes

- **Isabell Duff and Jean are clear readings.** He explicitly calls Isabell his mother and Jean his sister. Jean’s surname is not stated.
- **The crossed-out passage:** its opening may read “ffather”, but I cannot reliably recover a name from it.
- **“Silver land” is uncertain.** That is the closest visual reading, but the wording is unusual. I’ve left it marked rather than silently substituting something more plausible.
- **John [Allison?] and John [Whinyard?] remain tentative.** The second witness reads “Thos Watts Stewd”, expanded above as Thomas Watts, Steward.
- **“Shireness”** appears to be the manuscript spelling of Sheerness. **“Requests”** appears in the revocation clause, possibly in place of the usual “bequests”.
