# Findings: Glasgow-4127

## Current conclusion

Reviewed 21 October 2026. Fothergill’s printed bounty list gives John Glasgow’s Antigua grant on 10 March 1707/8 (10 March 1708 in modern year numbering), Money Book 19, p. 200. The Treasury calendar’s March 10&15 group records a £20 passage warrant for John as chaplain to Antigua, money orders dated 11 March and a direction dated 16 March; it cites Money BookXIX pp.200, 204, Order BookVII p. 120 and Disposition BookXIX p. 118. The grouped heading does not independently assign 15 March to John. This identifies a specific financial trail for the existing 1708 event. Fothergill and the calendar abstract the same underlying warrant and are not independent family testimony. Neither gives a parent, birthplace, age, ordination or proof of arrival. The printed list and calendar were inspected; the original Treasury manuscripts were not. John remains distinct from the 1800 Trinity student and the Kilbirnie Presbyterian minister.

## Earlier conclusion — retained

John Glasgow was a Church of England minister appointed to a cure in Antigua. He is named in a 1707/08 bounty list of emigrant ministers to America: "Glasgow, John, Antegoa, March 10, 1707-1708." The entry records the appointment and destination but no age, family or later life, so his birth is placed before 1707. No birthplace is recorded. The [Findmypast transcript](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=US%2FEMIGRANTMINISTERS%2F0394&tab=this) is the indexed source for this event. The live WikiTree profile records male sex at birth with uncertain status, inferred from the recorded given name. The cited transcript does not explicitly state sex.
