== Biography ==

'''James Glasgow''' was recorded at Killycarragh, County Tyrone, in the Public Record Office of Northern Ireland's index of pre-1858 wills and administrations. The entry is indexed under 21 March 1836.<ref name="Killycarragh1836">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, [https://apps.proni.gov.uk/ProniNames_IE/SearchPage.aspx PRONI Names], ''Pre-1858 Wills and Admons'', search result for James Glasgow, Killycarragh, 21 March 1836, accessed 7 September 2026. The result's item-detail URL is session-bound and the original probate document does not survive.</ref>

The index date should not be treated as an exact death date. The entry establishes a probate subject named James Glasgow and his recorded residence, but it supplies no age, occupation or relatives.<ref name="Killycarragh1836" />

A later family statement preserved as PRONI T1959/3 describes the writer's paternal grandfather, William Glasgow of Killycurragh, as the husband of an unidentified Miss Cunningham of Omagh and father of five sons and three daughters. The writer said that her father and a brother William were educated together; William became a physician in England. Three other sons—apparently Henry, James and Samuel—“lived and died on [the] farm in Killycurragh,” with Samuel remaining in their parents' house.<ref name="T1959">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, T1959/3, photocopy of an undated and unsigned Glasgow–Rutherford family letter preserved with papers of the 1890s; online transcription by Albert Rutherford reproduced by Gary Harding, [https://www.genealogy.com/forum/surnames/topics/rutherford/5445/ “Rutherfords and Glasgows in Co. Tyrone, Ireland”], 9 January 2005.</ref>

That relationship-defined James is the strongest and probable identity for the same-named Killycarragh probate subject. The letter's writer was born five months after her father, Rev. John Glasgow of Orritor, died in July 1801, placing the named James in the correct generation.<ref name="T1959" /> A Presbyterian-ministers compilation independently identifies John Glasgow of Orritor as the minister ordained at New Row, Coleraine, on 8 March 1796 and records his death on 13 July 1801.<ref name="RevJohn">[https://cotyrone.com/churchrecord/ministers-pres.html “Presbyterian Ministers in Co. Tyrone”], entry for John Glasgow of Orritor and New Row, Coleraine, citing his ordination on 8 March 1796 and death on 13 July 1801.</ref>

== Research Notes ==

The index spells the residence ''Killycarragh''. It is probably the townland now rendered Killycurragh in the Oritor electoral division and Derryloran parish. The T1959/3 statement independently places the writer's uncle James on the family farm at Killycurragh and says that he died there. The combined name, place, generation and death evidence supports one probable identity, although the original probate document is lost and the family letter is known only through an online transcription.

The St Luran register records a James Glasgow living at Orritor Craigs in 1829, when a child was baptised to him and Mary Anne of Drumcann.<ref name="Derryloran1829">Representative Church Body Library, [https://www.churchofireland.org/cmsfiles/pdf/AboutUs/library/AngRecord/DerryloranVol1.pdf ''Derryloran Parish Register, Volume 1''], baptism no. 466, 5 April 1829.</ref> He is a useful locality candidate for the 1836 estate, but the shared forename and neighbouring residence do not prove that the two records concern the same person.

=== Duplicate audit ===

The local One Tree export and live WikiTree data were searched again in September 2026. The strongest automated place-name result is demonstrably not the deceased man:

* [[Glasgow-1701|James Glasgow junior]] lived at Killycurragh but died there on 27 July 1875. His exact-place match is valuable for local context, not identity with the 1836 probate subject.
* [[Glasgow-1702|James Glasgow of Ballynasollus]] died in 1882 and cannot be this man.
* [[Glasgow-3444|James Glasgow of Cookstown]] died in 1866 and cannot be this man.
* [[Glasgow-1165|James Glasgow]] belongs to the separately documented family of John Glasgow and Mary Arthur. His family Bible records his death on 20 April 1823 and gives no Killycurragh connection. The earlier death alone would not exclude a delayed probate entry, but the incompatible relationship-defined family does.
* [[Glasgow-2989|James Glasgow of Derryloran]] is an older local candidate whose death is unresolved, but no source on the profile places him at Killycarragh or identifies him through relatives or property.

No current WikiTree profile is proved to represent this 1836 estate. The surviving index is too slight to show whether the subject was the 1829 Orritor Craigs householder, the older unresolved Derryloran man, or another member of the several contemporary Glasgow households.

The new family discriminator resolves the creation question even though it does not prove an exact birth date. None of the searched profiles represents the son of William Glasgow of Killycurragh and Miss Cunningham who lived and died on the family farm. [[Glasgow-2989|James of Derryloran]] is an older, relationship-free reconstruction, while the exact-place [[Glasgow-1701|James]] lived until 1875. Creating this man unconnected, with the probable family placement in Research Notes, avoids both conflation and omission.

[[Glasgow-1698|Samuel Glasgow]], born about 1800 and reported to have died at
Killycurragh in 1857, is the strongest live candidate for the brother Samuel
whom T1959/3 says remained in his parents' Killycurragh house. This is a useful
probable sibling identity, not proof of a shared parent attachment. By contrast,
[[Glasgow-1675|William Glasgow of Killycurragh]] became a Presbyterian elder and
emigrated to Ohio; that documented life does not fit the letter's physician
William who practised in London and Hull.
