== Biography ==
'''John Glasgow''' was a sailor in Saltcoats, within the parish of Ardrossan,
Ayrshire. He died in December 1636. His testament dative and inventory came
before Glasgow Commissary Court in April 1637.<ref name="JohnTestament">National
Records of Scotland, Glasgow Commissary Court, testament dative and inventory
of John Glasgow, sailor in Saltcoats, NRS CC9/7/27, register pp. 478–480;
death in December 1636 and court proceedings in April 1637; original register
re-read September 2026; locate by reference through the
[https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/search-records/legal-records/wills
Scotland's People wills search].</ref>

The proceeding names '''Isobell Gray''' as John's widow. It calls '''Katherine
Glasgow''' and '''Agnes Glasgow''' lawful children of the deceased, their
father. This directly establishes John as the girls' father and Isobell as his
widow. It does not expressly identify Isobell as their mother.<ref name="JohnTestament" /> Isobell gave up the inventory on the children's behalf.

A John Brown or Browne of Saltcoats appears in the estate accounting and a
sailor of that name and place appears in the closing security. These may be the
same man, but the document does not make that identity or any kinship explicit.
He should be treated only as a record-named financial and legal associate.<ref name="JohnTestament" />

The inventory may describe an old fishing boat and related equipment. The
manuscript reading of those goods is tentative and should not be treated as a
secure inventory transcription.<ref name="JohnTestament" />

== Research Notes ==
=== Saltcoats family: selected uncertain earlier father ===

Select John [[Glasgow-3193]], the recorded son at Corsankell in 1571 and probable Kilwinning tenant of 1559–60, as the Saltcoats sailor John [[Glasgow-4152]]’s uncertain working father. Saltcoats in Ardrossan adjoins the Stevenston–Kilwinning coastal parish cluster where this earlier Glasgow family is documented. A later-sixteenth-century son of the earlier adult could have been a working sailor and father of two daughters by 1636. The choice gives the Saltcoats household a provisional place in an existing local John line, using geography and a feasible generation. It is a low-confidence selection, not a named parent clause or an identification of the two Johns.

No source names the sailor’s parents or age. His former c.1600 birth was an unsupported creation estimate; it is not replaced by an invented older birth to fit this hypothesis. If a birth near 1600 is eventually established, the earlier John would have been an older father, and a grandson or more distant collateral placement could be more natural. Same given name is weak support and does not distinguish this candidate securely from [[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow younger]], another member of the local family, or an unrecorded father. The sailor could belong elsewhere in that family or to another branch. The 1559–60 Kilwinning attribution to the Corsankell John is itself probable, although the 1571 Corsankell family is directly recorded. The same-person alternative is less economical: an adult in 1559 would have been about 95 or older at the sailor’s death in 1636, assuming adulthood at eighteen. That is an illustrative age comparison, not a proved exclusion. The father-versus-grandfather degree remains open. No mother is assigned to the sailor or to his daughters; Agnes Wilson’s joint tenancy does not make her his mother, and Isobell Gray’s widowhood does not prove her maternity. The girls’ already-proved father links remain proved. === Nature and date of the record ===

A testament dative records court administration of an intestate estate. Its
relationship language is valuable, but it is not a personal will and does not
preserve statements made by John. The decisive manuscript wording describes
Katherine and Agnes as ''bairnes lawfull'' and continues ''to the defunct thair
father''; the closing security again calls them lawful bairns and names
Isobell as the deceased's relict. This is why John's fatherhood and Isobell's
widowhood are secure while her maternity is not explicit.<ref name="JohnTestament" />

The published testament index places John's entry under 11 April 1637; the day
in the original register has not been read securely in the current
transcription, so only April 1637 is used here.<ref>Francis
J. Grant, ed., [https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso07scotuoft/page/196/mode/2up?q=Glasgow%2C
''The Commissariot Record of Glasgow: Register of Testaments, 1547–1800'']
(Edinburgh: Scottish Record Society, 1901), p. 196, entry for John Glasgow,
sailor in Saltcoats.</ref>

=== Possible identity ===

[[Glasgow-1030|John Glasgow of Glasgow]] was compared because his death is
unknown and a later move or remarriage is chronologically possible. He married
[[Mathie-159|Jonet Mathie]] in
Glasgow in 1618 and their documented children were baptised there between 1619
and 1634. No inspected record connects that
household to Isobell Gray, Saltcoats, the sailor occupation, Katherine or
Agnes. He is excluded as the present match: name and chronological possibility
cannot override a different documented household without an occupation,
property, residence or explicit-family bridge.

=== Isobell Gray ===

No WikiTree profile is presently proved for John's widow. [[Gray-27093|Isobell
Gray]], baptised at Kirkcaldy in 1617, and [[Gray-27137|Isobel Gray]], baptised
at Dunfermline in 1614, are chronological candidates only. Neither profile has
a Saltcoats residence, Glasgow spouse, or child evidence linking her to this
estate. Do not attach either woman without an identifying record.
