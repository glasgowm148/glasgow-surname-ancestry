== Biography ==

'''Katherine Glasgow''' was a lawful child of [[Glasgow-4152|John Glasgow]], a sailor in
Saltcoats within Ardrossan parish. She and [[Glasgow-4151|Agnes Glasgow]] were named in
their father's testament dative and inventory when his estate came before
Glasgow Commissary Court in April 1637. Their father had died in December 1636.<ref name="JohnTestament">National Records of Scotland, Glasgow Commissary Court,
testament dative and inventory of John Glasgow, sailor in Saltcoats, NRS
CC9/7/27, register pp. 478–480; death in December 1636 and court proceedings in
April 1637; original register re-read September 2026; locate by reference
through the [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/search-records/legal-records/wills
Scotland's People wills search].</ref>

The proceeding calls Katherine and Agnes the lawful children of the deceased,
their father. It names '''Isobell Gray''' as John's widow and records that she
gave up the inventory on the children's behalf. This proves Katherine's father
and Isobell's marriage to him, but does not expressly call Isobell Katherine's
mother.<ref name="JohnTestament" />

== Research Notes ==

The testament does not state Katherine's age, birthplace, marriage or death.
Saltcoats is an uncertain creation-field birthplace inferred from her father's
residence, not a stated fact. The document's use of ''bairn'' proves a
parent-child relationship; it does not by itself establish that Katherine was
a young child in 1637.

[[Glasgow-1030]] has been excluded as the present identity of her father: his
documented Glasgow-burgh household has no Saltcoats, sailor, Isobell Gray,
Katherine or Agnes bridge. Create the distinct Saltcoats John first and attach
Katherine to that new profile with certain status.

=== Possible Dublin identity ===

[[Glasgow-3942|Katherine Glasgow]], whose birth and parents are unknown, was
buried at St Audoen, Dublin, on 3 November 1713. A Saltcoats child alive in
1637 could chronologically have survived to that date, and west-coast maritime
connections make movement to Ireland possible. Name and broad chronology do
not establish identity, however. With no age, Scottish origin, relative,
marriage or migration bridge, that profile is excluded as the present match;
later positive evidence could still justify a merge.<ref>Irish
Genealogy, Church of Ireland burial register, Katherin Glasgow, St Audoen,
Dublin, buried 3 November 1713, record identifier DU-CI-BU-25141, register p.
96, [https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/church/d-116-1-1-098.pdf record
image].</ref>
