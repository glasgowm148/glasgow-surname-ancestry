== Biography ==
'''Katherine Glasgow''' was a lawful child of [[Glasgow-4152|John Glasgow]], a sailor in
Saltcoats within Ardrossan parish. She and [[Glasgow-4151|Agnes Glasgow]] were named in
their father's testament dative and inventory when his estate came before
Glasgow Commissary Court in April 1637. Their father had died in December 1636.<ref name="JohnTestament">National Records of Scotland, Glasgow Commissary Court,
testament dative and inventory of John Glasgow, sailor in Saltcoats, NRS
CC9/7/27, register pp. 478–480; death in December 1636 and court proceedings in
April 1637; original register re-read September 2026; locate by reference
through the [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/search-records/legal-records/wills
Scotland's People wills search].</ref>

The proceeding calls Katherine and Agnes the lawful children of the deceased,
their father. It names '''Isobell Gray''' as John's widow and records that she
gave up the inventory on the children's behalf. This proves Katherine's father
and Isobell's marriage to him, but does not expressly call Isobell Katherine's
mother.<ref name="JohnTestament" />

== Research Notes ==
=== Saltcoats family: selected uncertain earlier father ===

Select John [[Glasgow-3193]], the recorded son at Corsankell in 1571 and probable Kilwinning tenant of 1559–60, as the Saltcoats sailor John [[Glasgow-4152]]’s uncertain working father. Saltcoats in Ardrossan adjoins the Stevenston–Kilwinning coastal parish cluster where this earlier Glasgow family is documented. A later-sixteenth-century son of the earlier adult could have been a working sailor and father of two daughters by 1636. The choice gives the Saltcoats household a provisional place in an existing local John line, using geography and a feasible generation. It is a low-confidence selection, not a named parent clause or an identification of the two Johns.

No source names the sailor’s parents or age. His former c.1600 birth was an unsupported creation estimate; it is not replaced by an invented older birth to fit this hypothesis. If a birth near 1600 is eventually established, the earlier John would have been an older father, and a grandson or more distant collateral placement could be more natural. Same given name is weak support and does not distinguish this candidate securely from [[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow younger]], another member of the local family, or an unrecorded father. The sailor could belong elsewhere in that family or to another branch. The 1559–60 Kilwinning attribution to the Corsankell John is itself probable, although the 1571 Corsankell family is directly recorded. The same-person alternative is less economical: an adult in 1559 would have been about 95 or older at the sailor’s death in 1636, assuming adulthood at eighteen. That is an illustrative age comparison, not a proved exclusion. The father-versus-grandfather degree remains open. No mother is assigned to the sailor or to his daughters; Agnes Wilson’s joint tenancy does not make her his mother, and Isobell Gray’s widowhood does not prove her maternity. The girls’ already-proved father links remain proved. The testament does not state Katherine's age, birthplace, marriage or death.
Saltcoats is an uncertain creation-field birthplace inferred from her father's
residence, not a stated fact. The document's use of ''bairn'' proves a
parent-child relationship; it does not by itself establish that Katherine was
a young child in 1637.

[[Glasgow-1030]] has been excluded as the present identity of her father: his
documented Glasgow-burgh household has no Saltcoats, sailor, Isobell Gray,
Katherine or Agnes bridge. The distinct Saltcoats father is [[Glasgow-4152]]. His already-confident attachment was verified on 28 September 2026.

=== Possible Dublin identity ===

[[Glasgow-3942|Katherine Glasgow]], whose birth and parents are unknown, was
buried at St Audoen, Dublin, on 3 November 1713. A Saltcoats child alive in
1637 could chronologically have survived to that date, and west-coast maritime
connections make movement to Ireland possible. Name and broad chronology do
not establish identity, however. With no age, Scottish origin, relative,
marriage or migration bridge, that profile is excluded as the present match;
later positive evidence could still justify a merge.<ref>Irish
Genealogy, Church of Ireland burial register, Katherin Glasgow, St Audoen,
Dublin, buried 3 November 1713, record identifier DU-CI-BU-25141, register p.
96, [https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/church/d-116-1-1-098.pdf record
image].</ref>
