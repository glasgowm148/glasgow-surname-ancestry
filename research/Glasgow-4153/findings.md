## Source findings — the 1637 testament and the Dublin namesake, 28 September 2026

National Records of Scotland, Glasgow Commissary Court, testament dative and inventory of John Glasgow, sailor in Saltcoats, `CC9/7/27`, register pp. 478–480 (original register re-read September 2026): John died in December 1636 and the estate came before the court in April 1637. The proceeding calls Katherine and [Agnes (Glasgow-4151)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4151) the lawful children of the deceased and names Isobell Gray as John's widow, who gave up the inventory on the children's behalf. This proves Katherine's father and Isobell's marriage to him; it does not call Isobell Katherine's mother, so no mother should be attached on that basis. The word *bairn* proves parentage, not that Katherine was young in 1637. Saltcoats as her birthplace is inferred from her father's residence.

[Glasgow-1030](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1030) is excluded as her father: his Glasgow-burgh household has no Saltcoats, sailor, Isobell Gray, Katherine or Agnes bridge. [Katherine Glasgow (Glasgow-3942)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3942), buried at St Audoen, Dublin, on 3 November 1713 ([register p. 96](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/church/d-116-1-1-098.pdf)), is chronologically possible but has no age, origin or relative linking her to Saltcoats, and is excluded as the present match.
