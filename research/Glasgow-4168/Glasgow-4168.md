== Biography ==
Robert Glasgow is named as a father in Libberton, Lanarkshire, parish baptism entries from 1718 to 1726:

* [[Glasgow-4130|Janet Glasgow]], baptised 22 April 1718, with mother [[Gibson-37713|Katherin Gibson]];<ref name="Janet1718">Findmypast, ''Scotland, Parish Births & Baptisms 1564–1929'', Libberton Old Parish Register, [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FOPR%2FBAP%2F4033250&tab=this Janet Glasgow, 22 April 1718]. The child profile retains the full index citation.</ref>
* [[Glasgow-4133|Agnes Glasgow]], baptised 21 August 1720, with mother [[Watson-53558|Katherine Watson]];<ref name="Agnes1720">Findmypast, ''Scotland, Parish Births & Baptisms 1564–1929'', Libberton Old Parish Register, [https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FOPR%2FBAP%2F4033335&tab=this Agnes Glasgow, 21 August 1720].</ref>
* [[Glasgow-4107|Elizabeth Glasgow]], baptised 10 June 1726, with mother Katherine Watson.<ref name="Elizabeth1726">Findmypast, ''Scotland, Parish Births & Baptisms 1564–1929'', Libberton Old Parish Register; the full entry is cited on [[Glasgow-4107]].</ref>

No birth, baptism or death record for this adult Robert has been identified. Birth before about 1699 is an age estimate based on the first child; Libberton is a recorded household parish, not a proved birthplace.

== Research Notes ==
=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

Select Johne [[Glasgow-3093]], indexed at Mid Calder in 1648 as son of Jamis Glasgow, as the very low-confidence working father of Robert [[Glasgow-4168]], father at Libberton in 1718–1726. The preferred local route runs through Johne’s existing uncertain James-3332 father identity and that James’s possible identification with the Lindsaylands weaver indexed in 1680. Lindsaylands and Quothquan/Libberton supply the adjacent Lanarkshire family setting; Mid Calder is a separate parish and no migration is proved. A Johne born around 1648 could father Robert in the 1680s–1690s, allowing ordinary adult fatherhood at the 1718 baptism. This generation is preferred over assigning Robert directly to the older weaver, who was dead by 1680 if that identity is correct. It is a specific family hypothesis using recorded men, not a father clause in Robert’s records.

Johne’s only reviewed record is the 1648 child entry; no later occupation, residence, spouse or death identifies him with Libberton. The Jamis-to-[[Glasgow-3332]] identity and the latter’s Lindsaylands identity are both uncertain. Its inherited circa 1634 birth would make him only about fourteen at Johne’s 1648 event if literal; that unsourced estimate must not establish or exclude parentage. The 1648 source names Jamis, not John-1084. The known parish association therefore belongs to a conditional family chain, not to an independently followed migration. James-3332 himself remains an alternative father if Robert was born before 1680 and fathered the later children at an older age. The son James apprenticed in Edinburgh in 1690, explicitly son of deceased James the Lindsaylands weaver, is a genuine alternative intermediate father or uncle; his existing held draft is retained, and no unrecorded generation is invented. The 1624 Quothquan weaver is a separate death account; its early death makes direct paternity of a 1718–1726 father much less plausible. Robert’s merged Gibson/Watson household remains a working combined account, not proof of remarriage or identity between the two women. No mother is selected for Robert, and no vital date is changed to fit the route. The former Glasgow-4169 profile represented the Robert named with Katherin Gibson in 1718. It was merged into this Robert-and-Katherine-Watson profile on 27 September 2026. The same father name and parish across the entries, with no distinct same-name adult identified, support treating them as one man. The maternal surnames differ. They may represent two women, a remarriage, or a register/transcription discrepancy. Both named women and all three child entries remain recorded; no marriage, death or surname change is asserted without further evidence.
