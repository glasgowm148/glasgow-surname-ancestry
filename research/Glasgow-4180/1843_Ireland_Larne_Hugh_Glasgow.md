== Biography ==
Hugh Glasgow was deceased by February 1843. A marriage settlement dated 10 February 1843 between John McGavock of Larne, innkeeper, and [[Glasgow-4181|Mary Glasgow]] of Larne, spinster, provided for a payment of £250 to McGavock by the trustees or executors of Hugh's will. An endorsed condition made the uses of certain premises dependent on that payment. The memorial's witness affidavit was dated 14 February and registration 18 February 1843.<ref name="Settlement1843">Registry of Deeds, Ireland, memorial of marriage settlement, John McGavock and Mary Glasgow, dated 10 February 1843, affidavit 14 February, registered 18 February 1843; FamilySearch, [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-FQ3Q opening image 499] and [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-F3BZ continuation image 500], original images inspected 21 September 2026. The record establishes Hugh's estate connection with Mary's intended marriage. It does not call him her father, state his residence, or give his age, will date or actual death date. His will has not yet been located.

== Research Notes ==
=== Uncertain parents and daughter ===
Hugh is attached to [[Glasgow-3991|James Glasgow]] as an '''uncertain son''', and [[Glasgow-4181|Mary]], the intended bride, is treated as his '''possible daughter'''. These are two separate, low-confidence working hypotheses; neither relationship is stated in the settlement.

James is the first candidate for an explicitly provisional placement. His Larne monument identifies him as a son of [[Glasgow-1499|Hugh Glasgow]] and Ann, and records his death on 14 April 1812 aged 64. His documented daughter [[Glasgow-3992|Ann]] died in 1832 aged 64. A further child Hugh born about 1770 fits that generation and repeats the grandfather's name, but no inspected record names such a son.<ref>"Armorial Sculptured Stones of the County Antrim," ''Ulster Journal of Archaeology'', second series, volume 6 (1900), [https://archive.org/details/ulsterjournalofa06ulstiala/page/47/mode/1up p. 47], Glasgow monument transcription.</ref> The naming and chronological fit are compatibility only, not independent proof. James's wife has not been assigned as Hugh's mother.

[[Glasgow-4179|John Glasgow]] is an alternative father candidate, but his own connection to elder Hugh is uncertain. [[Glasgow-3931|Hugh Glasgow]], a Tamlaght O'Crilly householder in 1740, remains another possibility. The settlement does not identify the testator's residence, so its Larne setting cannot exclude a man from elsewhere.

The proposed father-daughter relationship explains the estate provision, but Hugh could instead have been Mary's uncle, brother, grandfather or another benefactor. Mary may be identical to [[Glasgow-3989|Mary Glasgow]], the monument-proved daughter of [[Glasgow-3324|William Glasgow]]. If that identity is established, this proposed fatherhood must be withdrawn; William's documented relationship must be preserved.

=== Estimated dates and places ===
Birth about 1770 is solely a working generation estimate from the proposed father James and possible daughter, not a recorded date. The uncertain Larne birth and death locations are inferred from the proposed father's family and the bride/property context, not from a record of Hugh's birthplace, residence or death. The death boundary before 14 February 1843 follows the contemporary affidavit referring to the deceased testator.<ref name="Settlement1843" />

=== Further evidence needed ===
Locate Hugh's will or administration and trace Mary's half shares in Newtown Larne and the Pound Road property. A named heir, marriage register or earlier title could establish her relationship to Hugh and distinguish James from the alternative father candidates.
