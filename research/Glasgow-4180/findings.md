# Hugh Glasgow — deceased testator

## Current conclusion

Hugh Glasgow was deceased before 14 February 1843. His will’s trustees or executors were to pay £250 in connection with Mary Glasgow’s intended marriage to John McGavock. His placement as a son of James Glasgow-3991 and the bride’s placement as Hugh’s daughter are separate low-confidence hypotheses, neither stated in the deed.

## Identity and relationship assessments

The live attachment to James Glasgow-3991 is explicitly uncertain. No mother is assigned. Birth about 1770 is only a working generation estimate; Larne birth and death places are uncertain contextual inferences. The deed does not locate Hugh himself. John Glasgow-4179 and Hugh Glasgow-3931 remain alternative father candidates. The bride could be Hugh’s daughter, another relative or a beneficiary. Mary Glasgow-3989, monument-proved daughter of William Glasgow-3324, remains a possible duplicate of the bride: proving that identity would require withdrawing the proposed Hugh fatherhood.

## Priority actions

Locate Hugh’s will or administration and trace the bride’s half shares in Newtown Larne and Pound Road. Obtain explicit kinship wording and resolve the bride’s possible duplicate before treating either uncertain attachment as documented.
