# Findings: [Ann (Glasgow) McCracken (Glasgow-438)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-438)

Last updated: 2026-09-17

## New identity and relationship lead involving Ann Glasgow, 14 February 1867

The original civil birth return records Mary Ellen born at Lisnagaver to a
mother named Mary Glasgow, with no father entered. Ann Glasgow of Lisnagaver
was present at the birth and registered it. This is a new Glasgow-associated
record and is relevant to [[Glasgow-438|Ann Glasgow]] because the informant's
name and place match her documented 1851 household and later Lisnagaver
marriage residence.

The return does not identify the informant's age, parentage, or relationship to
the mother. It is therefore only a possible association with Ann-438, not proof
that she was the informant or that Mary Glasgow was her sister.

Source: [IrishGenealogy civil birth record](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view?record_id=f66be225e5-6827480),
[original 1867 birth return](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/birth_returns/births_1867/03501/2286677.pdf),
entry 214, Ballymoney union, County Antrim, accessed 17 September 2026.

## Current conclusion

Ann is strongly identified as the four-year-old daughter of Alexander and Mary
Glasgow in Lisnagaver in 1851. On 13 October 1876 she married Robert McCracken
at Ballymoney. The register calls her a full-age spinster of Lisnagaver and
names her father as **Alexander Glasgow, labourer**. This also corrects the
profile's birthplace from Lisnagarvey/Lisburn to **Lisnagaver, County Antrim**.
The occupational wording does not contradict Alexander's earlier description
as a farmer; later records can describe the same man by changed work or status.
The Nancy who informed Alexander's probable 1888 death may be Ann, but a focused
free search found no record calling Ann Nancy or placing her at Gortahar. The
death return explicitly places Nancy at **Gortahar**, but gives no age or
married surname. It calls her Alexander's **daughter**. [Nancy Glasgow
(Glasgow-1362)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1362), Thomas Glasgow's
daughter, is therefore not an equally simple identification: assigning her
would require an error in the stated relationship or further identity evidence.
Ann and an otherwise unidentified daughter remain possibilities; neither is
proved, and the informant identity remains unresolved at low confidence.

Their only presently identified child, William James McCracken, was born at
Lisnagaver on 21 September 1877. He died there the next day from debility from
birth; Ann was informant for both registrations. He therefore left no
descendants. No later child, secure later household, remarriage, or death has
yet been identified for Ann or her husband.

Robert's family can now be placed in a narrower **candidate McCracken
cluster**. Griffith's Valuation lists Robert and William McCracken at
Carnfinton, the reported birth townland of Thomas Glasgow's wife Mary Ann
McCracken. Later marriages identify several children of a William McCracken in
that townland. James William and Elizabeth are the strongest reconstructed
sibling pair because Elizabeth's 1889 marriage was witnessed by James
McCracken. Mary Ann is independently reinforced: she married George Mark, son
of Alexander Mark, and an Alexander Marks witnessed James William in 1886.
Ann's husband Robert may be another sibling, but his 1876 register
describes father William as a labourer rather than a farmer and gives Robert's
residence as Lisnagaver. Treat this as a strong lead, not a proved attachment.

## Affinal implication for Alexander and Thomas Glasgow

```text
John McCrackine at Carnfinton, 1834          [possible earlier generation]
|
+-- ? Mary Ann McCracken, born c1820         [wife of Thomas Glasgow]
+-- ? William McCracken, Carnfinton          [valuation-era farmer]
    +-- Mary Ann, married 1867                [candidate child]
    +-- ? Robert, married Ann Glasgow 1876    [candidate child]
    +-- James William, married 1886           [candidate child]
    +-- Elizabeth, married 1889               [candidate child; James witness]
```

James William–Elizabeth is directly reinforced by a cross-witness, while the
Alexander Marks witness may connect James to Mary Ann's Mark in-laws. If
William proves to have been Thomas's wife Mary Ann's brother, Ann Glasgow—whose
father Alexander is Thomas's possible brother or cousin—married Mary Ann's nephew. That
would be unusually specific evidence of two Glasgow households marrying within
one extended Carnfinton McCracken family. It would materially strengthen the
Alexander–Thomas kin-network case, although it would not alone prove that the
men were brothers.

## Death-informant correction — 13 September 2026

Fresh visual inspection of [the original 1888 register](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/deaths_returns/deaths_1888/06179/4766466.pdf),
entry 348, corrects the earlier statement that Nancy had no recorded residence.
The informant column reads Nancy Glasgow, her mark, daughter, present at death,
Gortahar. This supplies a concrete residence for tracing Nancy but does not
identify her with Ann or Thomas's daughter. Alexander's 16 May 1888 death remains
the best-supported probable match for [Glasgow-951](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-951);
the family and place evidence is indirect, and the death's reported age of 84
conflicts with his 1851 census age of 40.

## Source findings — Scottish death index, 23 September 2026

The ScotlandsPeople statutory deaths index lists **Ann McCracken**, female, 49, died 1898 in Calton (Glasgow), reference 644/4 70, with other surname **Glasgow** and mother's maiden surname **McCaughern**. Her maiden name, her mother's maiden name (Mary McAughin/McCaughan) and her age (4 in 1851) identify her as Alexander-951's daughter. Her brother Robert-938 had settled in Calton. The register image (6 ScotlandsPeople credits) has not been viewed. It should give the exact date, her husband's status, her parents and the informant. The live profile was updated with death year 1898 and place Calton, Glasgow, both certain. As a married McCracken living in Scotland, she is an unlikely match for the "Nancy Glasgow, daughter" who informed at the 1888 Gortahar death.
