# Findings: [Ann (Glasgow) McCracken (Glasgow-438)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-438)

Last updated: 2026-08-13

## Current conclusion

Ann is strongly identified as the four-year-old daughter of Alexander and Mary
Glasgow in Lisnagaver in 1851. On 13 October 1876 she married Robert McCracken
at Ballymoney. The register calls her a full-age spinster of Lisnagaver and
names her father as **Alexander Glasgow, labourer**. This also corrects the
profile's birthplace from Lisnagarvey/Lisburn to **Lisnagaver, County Antrim**.

Their only presently identified child, William James McCracken, was born at
Lisnagaver on 21 September 1877. He died there the next day from debility from
birth; Ann was informant for both registrations. He therefore left no
descendants. No later child, secure later household, remarriage, or death has
yet been identified for Ann or her husband.

Robert's family can now be placed in a narrower **candidate McCracken
cluster**. Griffith's Valuation lists Robert and William McCracken at
Carnfinton, the reported birth townland of Thomas Glasgow's wife Mary Ann
McCracken. Later marriages identify several children of a William McCracken in
that townland. James William and Elizabeth are the strongest reconstructed
sibling pair because Elizabeth's 1889 marriage was witnessed by James
McCracken. Mary Ann is independently reinforced: she married George Mark, son
of Alexander Mark, and an Alexander Marks witnessed James William in 1886.
Ann's husband Robert may be another sibling, but his 1876 register
describes father William as a labourer rather than a farmer and gives Robert's
residence as Lisnagaver. Treat this as a strong lead, not a proved attachment.

## Affinal implication for Alexander and Thomas Glasgow

```text
John McCrackine at Carnfinton, 1834          [possible earlier generation]
|
+-- ? Mary Ann McCracken, born c1820         [wife of Thomas Glasgow]
+-- ? William McCracken, Carnfinton          [valuation-era farmer]
    +-- Mary Ann, married 1867                [candidate child]
    +-- ? Robert, married Ann Glasgow 1876    [candidate child]
    +-- James William, married 1886           [candidate child]
    +-- Elizabeth, married 1889               [candidate child; James witness]
```

James William–Elizabeth is directly reinforced by a cross-witness, while the
Alexander Marks witness may connect James to Mary Ann's Mark in-laws. If
William proves to have been Thomas's wife Mary Ann's brother, Ann Glasgow—whose
father Alexander is Thomas's probable brother—married Mary Ann's nephew. That
would be unusually specific evidence of two Glasgow households marrying within
one extended Carnfinton McCracken family. It would materially strengthen the
Alexander–Thomas kin-network case, although it would not alone prove that the
men were brothers.
