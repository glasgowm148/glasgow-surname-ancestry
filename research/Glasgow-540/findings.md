Last updated: 2026-09-08

## Current answer

Hugh Glasgow signed his will on 29 March 1827 and it was proved on 25 April 1831. This confirms the live profile's existing rejection of the family history's 1824 death claim; it is not a correction to the profile's current estimated death date of about 1831. The will identifies five children and one granddaughter but does not name his wife.

## Effect on Adam Glasgow parentage

Adam Glasgow of Inishrush was alive in 1827 and 1831 but is absent while Hugh individually names and benefits four sons. This materially weakens Hugh as Adam's father, but is not conclusive because the will does not say `all my children` and also omits two children from the late account.

The reported 1780 family farm near Portglenone, the 1740 Hugh beside an
Inishrush-linked Ellis/McCullough collector group, and the 1772 parish Hugh
together make this family a strong match to Adam and John of Inishrush's wider
local Glasgow cluster. The account's statement that Hugh left many kin in
Ireland strengthens a collateral placement. These facts do not prove a
father-son or uncle-nephew link.

## Identity and migration clarification

- Both 1740 Tamlaght O'Crilly householders are high-confidence earlier-generation namesakes, not credible matches for this profile's Hugh under the approximate 1739 birth and independently documented 1827 survival.
- The 1772 parish subscriber is chronologically compatible with this profile,
  but cannot be distinguished from either older Hugh using the notice alone.
  Preserve it as an unassigned free-space occurrence; do not add it to this
  profile or create another person from it.
- The family history's August 1796 emigration passage concerns this Hugh's household. It should not be transferred to his father `Glasgow-548`.
