# Findings: Glasgow-548

Last updated: 2026-10-17

## Current conclusion

**Earlier Tyrone parentage review, 17 October 2026:** William Glasgow-591 is assessed as the probable father of John Glasgow-551, and John as the probable father of Hugh Glasgow-548, within the explicit family-history pedigree. These are tradition-based probable roles, not documented identifications of the Scottish candidates, the Leitrim witness or either 1740 Hugh.

Reviewed: 2026-10-17

**Parentage review, 17 October 2026:** Hugh Glasgow-540’s father Hugh Glasgow-548 and mother Margaret McCullough-1964 are now assessed as **probable**, combining the explicit descendant account with the independently identifiable Pennsylvania household. The 1740 household identities and the older Scottish ancestry remain separate questions. [Evidence and alternatives](/branches/research-irish-tamlaght/#hugh-mccullough-probable-parents).

No contemporary record found identifies the Hugh Glasgow born about 1706 in County Tyrone, his wife Margaret McCullough, or their sons. Two distinct men named Hugh Glasgow were Protestant householders in Tamlaght O'Crilly parish in 1740. Either could be the older Hugh of the family history, but the return supplies no age, occupation, wife, children, or townland and cannot distinguish them.

The second Hugh occurs in a collector sequence strongly linked to the Inishrush leasehold community, immediately before Hugh Ellis and James McCullough. That makes him a better geographic lead, not a proved Inishrush resident or a proved husband of Margaret McCullough.

The conjunction is nevertheless substantial indirect corroboration of the late
family account: an adult Hugh of the correct generation appears in the correct
parish seven years after the reported marriage, immediately beside an Ellis
from the Inishrush head-tenant family and a James McCullough. Entry 1272 is the
high-confidence candidate for this profile, while still short of a direct
identity statement because two Hugh householders were returned.

The earlier recommendation to create an unconnected profile for entry 1272 is superseded. Retain this Hugh as the leading compatible candidate and keep both 1740 occurrences separately identifiable in the records. Missing additional corroboration alone does not establish another person or justify a duplicate profile. The precise 1740 identity remains unresolved, and this parentage review makes no merge or new person.

The late account also says this Hugh's grandson John was born in 1780 on the
family farm in County Londonderry, `not far from` Portglenone. In combination
with the 1740 Ellis/McCullough cluster, the 1772 parish Hugh, and Adam and John
later at Inishrush, this makes the Hugh-548 family a high-probability match to
their extended local Glasgow cluster. It still does not state the intervening
relationship.

The traditional sons John-549 and Robert-550 are both old enough to father Adam
and John of Inishrush, but the child sequence points instead to shared parents
called Adam and Maria. The return itself supplies a cleaner structural model:
entry 1272 may represent Hugh-548, while the other 1740 Hugh may head the
collateral line that produced older Adam/Maria. This respects the late account's
explicit three-son list rather than adding an unreported son to Hugh-548. It is
still a hypothesis because neither Hugh's children are named in the return.

## Collector-order analysis

The surname continuity is useful but not conclusive:

- The second sequence contains many surnames independently documented at Inishrush in the 1734 lease or the 1831 census, including Ellis, McClelland, Hasty, Walsh, Neill, Henry, Laughlin, Murphy and McFadden.
- Henry Lennox immediately precedes the second Hugh, while 1831 Lennox households were concentrated at Moneysallin. This is evidence that the sequence can cross locality boundaries.
- The first sequence contains Kane, Quinn, O'Neill, Woods and Richey names strongly repeated at Gortmacrane in 1831.
- The 1831 comparison is 91 years later and proves surname continuity, not residence or identity in 1740.

Therefore entry 1272 is best described as **in an Inishrush-linked collector cluster**, not `of Inishrush`.

## Identity separation

- Neither 1740 householder is a credible match for the younger Hugh on WikiTree as `Glasgow-540` if his approximate 1739 birth is even broadly correct. Treating him as an adult householder in 1740 would also require exceptional longevity through his 1827 will and very late fatherhood for children born in the 1780s-1790s. This is a high-confidence generational exclusion, not an age statement in the 1740 return.
- One of the 1740 householders could be this profile's older Hugh. No record found chooses between them or verifies the McCullough marriage.
- Entry 1272 is the stronger identity candidate for this profile because of the
  McCullough adjacency, but should be created separately and unconnected unless
  a direct bridge is found. Entry 1195 remains a distinct possible root for the
  older Adam/Maria line; that placement is not proved.
- The Hugh named in 1772 cannot be assigned to either generation from that
  notice. Preserve it as an unassigned occurrence in the Tamlaght
  O'Crilly/Inishrush documentary cluster rather than creating another person.

## Do not add as fact

- Do not assign entry 1272 to Inishrush as a stated residence; the townland is explicitly not specified.
- Do not identify entry 1272 as Margaret McCullough's husband solely from adjacency to James McCullough.
- Do not state that this older Hugh emigrated in 1796. The family-history wording applies that migration to the younger Hugh's family.

## Hugh–McCullough parentage assessment — 17 October 2026

Hugh Glasgow-548 and Margaret McCullough-1964 are assessed as the probable parents of Hugh Glasgow-540, the emigrant whose Pennsylvania will was made in 1827 and proved in 1831. William Melanchthon Glasgow’s family history, p.2, explicitly names the elder Hugh and Margaret as the married parents of John, Robert and their youngest son Hugh, born in County Londonderry in 1739. Page 3 follows this younger Hugh’s household through John, whom the author calls his grandfather, and its emigration in 1796. This is specific descendant testimony about a named family, rather than a parent invented from dates or a shared surname. The younger Hugh’s recorded will independently names the same four sons John, Hugh, Alexander and Robert, and daughter Nancy, within the corresponding Pennsylvania household. That agreement supports the family history’s identification of the emigrant and the reliability of its nearer family relationships. It does not independently name his parents. The explicit family account, consistent household identity and feasible generations together warrant probable parentage rather than leaving the father and mother ungraded.

Parentage rests on one retrospective family account; later online copies and family trees are derivatives, not independent witnesses. Its author gives no Irish baptism or marriage source, and its 1824 death claim is contradicted by Hugh’s signed 1827 will and 1831 probate. The 1733 marriage, 1739 birth, older Hugh’s circa-1706 birth and Margaret’s imported vital estimates retain their separate evidence limits.
