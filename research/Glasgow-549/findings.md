# Findings: [John Glasgow (Glasgow-549), born 1735, County Londonderry, Ireland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-549)

Last updated: 2026-07-21

## Current conclusion

John is known only from William Melanchthon Glasgow's late family history, which
names him first among three sons of Hugh Glasgow and Margaret McCullough.
The account supplies no birth date, residence, wife, child or death. The current
about-1735 County Londonderry birth and about-1785 death are unsupported.

No found record directly connects this John to Adam Glasgow or John Glasgow of
Inishrush. He is chronologically capable of fathering both men, as is his
traditional brother Robert. The late family history places their brother Hugh's
family farm near Portglenone by 1780, independently consistent with the local
1740, 1772 and Inishrush evidence.

No evidence distinguishes this John from Robert-550 as the direct father. John
born 1767 used `John` in the third-son position, which already repeats his own
name; his first son `Adam` therefore points more naturally to an older Adam as
paternal grandfather than to this profile. The existence of a second local Hugh
householder in 1740 supplies a possible collateral root for that older
Adam/Maria model.

## Tree effect

John remains a possible **uncertain** direct father, but is not preferred over
Robert-550 by the current evidence. The older Adam/Maria model descending from
the other 1740 Hugh is structurally cleaner. Do not insert the existing
unidentified-father placeholder as this John's son: that would add an impossible
extra generation between a man born in the mid-1730s and Adam born about 1758.

## Record findings and identity limits — 17 October 2026

A possible identification concerns John Glasco, charged at Bow Street. John Glasco, Esq., said he was an officer on the Staff and was brought before the Bow Street magistrate charged with wounding patrolman John Smith. The reported incident occurred on Tuesday night after the play in Covent Garden; Glasco was committed. (The Times, 2 May 1799, p.3, Public Office, Bow Street; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/32820363/).) Allegation and committal are not conviction. Military status is self-reported and unconfirmed; no Irish origin or kin is stated. The identification remains uncertain.

A possible identification concerns John Glascow, London chairman. John Glascow, a chairman, said he saw a man in black running with pistols after Martha Ray was shot in Covent Garden. (Derby Mercury, provider-labelled 19 March 1779, p.2; [record](https://www.newspapers.com/image/394231375/); Old Bailey original, printed p.207; [record](https://www.dhi.ac.uk/san/ob/1770s/177904040005.gif); Old Bailey Proceedings, James Hackman record t17790404-3; event dating only; [record](https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/record/t17790404-3).) The Old Bailey indictment specifies that Martha Ray was shot on 7 April 1779 (printed p.207); that is the event date, not the indictment or newspaper publication date. The newspaper describes evidence after the shooting, not established trial testimony. Its March provider label is incorrect. The identification remains uncertain.
