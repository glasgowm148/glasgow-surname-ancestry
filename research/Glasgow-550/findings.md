# Findings: Glasgow-550

Last updated: 2026-07-21

## Current answer

The profile's asserted birth about 1737 in County Londonderry and death about 1787 are unsupported estimates. No found contemporary source identifies this Robert's residence, wife, child, or relationship to Adam Glasgow of Inishrush.

## Parentage effect

No found contemporary or near-contemporary source identifies the biographical Robert represented by `Glasgow-550`. The documentary record instead separates every located same-name man by place and date:

| Robert identity | Documented range and place | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Killwaughter farmer and leaseholder, wife Rachel Wilson | 1738-1741, County Antrim | Too old to be born about 1737; excluded. |
| St Vincent Army surgeon, brother James at Beith | 1785-1825+, West Indies/Scotland | Survived the guessed death and lacks an Irish link; excluded. |
| Robert of Loy, gentleman/freeholder | 1786-1825, Derryloran, County Tyrone | Later Tyrone cluster; no wife, child, or Adam stated. |
| Robert junior of Cookstown, gentleman | 1817, County Tyrone | Later namesake; no stated relation to Robert of Loy or Adam. |
| Robert of Coltrim | 1831, County Londonderry | Later-generation householder; no ages or kin stated. |

Robert remains chronologically possible only because his profile dates are estimates. A son's name, a broad County Londonderry label, or a derivative family tradition cannot identify him as Adam's father. No evidence found connects Robert directly to Adam, Inishrush, or a tenancy inherited by Adam.

The newly established 1767-John naming reconstruction does not improve Robert's
case. John's sons occur as Adam, James, John and Arthur, with his own name in the
traditional third-son position and Maria in the second-daughter position. If the
pattern was followed, the expected paternal grandparents were Adam and Maria,
not Robert and an unknown wife. This is indirect evidence, not an exclusion, but
it means Robert is not the leading father merely because he was a traditional
brother of Hugh-540 or because Adam's proved son was named James.

The late account's statement that Hugh-540's son John was born on the family
farm near Portglenone in 1780 strengthens the wider family's connection to the
Inishrush district. It does not place Robert there. Robert and John-549 remain
equally unproved direct-father candidates; the first-son name Adam points more
naturally to the older Adam/Maria model than to either traditional brother.

The public Y-DNA result for John born 1767 does not supply the missing bridge.
No displayed kit is documented as a strict male-line descendant of this Robert,
John-549, Hugh-548 or Hugh-540. An older WikiTree reference to a five-step match
with a “Melanchthon branch” does not name the tester or pedigree and is not
reproducible from the current public metadata.

### Suggested corrections

- Remove or explicitly label the about-1737 County Londonderry birth and about-1787 death as unsupported estimates. No cited source supplies either event.
- Remove the suggestion that the 1831 Robert of Coltrim documents this man or his descendants. The census householder was a later individual and the record states no parentage.
- Do not use the 1740 return's lack of a Robert householder to disprove this person. A child born about 1737 would not be listed as a householder.
- Do not use the 1766 religious census as a negative for Tamlaght O'Crilly: the surviving indexed corpus has no named householder return for that parish.

### Do not add as fact

- Do not attach Adam Glasgow of Inishrush as Robert's son. No found record states the relationship.
- Do not identify Robert with the Killwaughter leaseholder. That Robert had wife Rachel Wilson and held a substantial lease in 1738, so he cannot have been born about 1737.
- Do not identify Robert with the St Vincent Army surgeon, Robert of Loy, Robert junior of Cookstown, or the 1749 Adams County emigrant on name alone.
