# Findings: Glasgow-591

Last updated: 2026-08-12

## Current conclusion

No reviewed record identifies Glasgow-591's father or proves which Scottish
William became the ancestor remembered in the late Tyrone family history. The
Cavers Covenanter is now the strongest identified candidate because a man in
his immediate 1679 prisoner sequence, James Young, is documented among survivors
who later went to Ulster. That remains FAN evidence, not a migration or
father-son record for William.

Public Y-DNA results provisionally connect the William-attributed line to the
later Kilwaughter line within the broader R-FT25406 paternal cluster. The
William-attributed Big-Y tester is confirmed R-FT20271; the Robert-c.1710 and
James-1731 kits are only STR-grouped in the parent FT25406 subgroup and require
Big-Y confirmation. This supports older shared paternal ancestry, not the paper
identity of William or descent from him.

## Y-DNA identity boundary

Kit `B696189` proves that its tested direct-paternal line is R-FT20271. It does
not independently prove that the earliest man in that chain is this profile or
that the later family tradition correctly identifies William's Scottish life.
Until the tester-to-root generations are audited, public summaries should call
it the **reported William c.1655 line**, not a proved Glasgow-591 result.

## Surname-tradition correction

The literal claim that a Cameron paternal line first adopted Glasgow in 1689 is
rejected by the older shared Glasgow Y-DNA structure. A later confusion between
`Cameron` and `Cameronian` is plausible but inferential. DNA does not identify
William, prove military service or supply the missing William-to-John paper link.

## Potential-father scoring review — 10 August 2026

[John Glasgow (Glasgow-1088)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1088) is
chronologically capable of fathering William, but the comparison supplies only
a 37-year age gap, the broad word “Glasgow” in their Scottish locations, and
the common name John among William's descendants. John's documented household
moved from Glasgow to Duddingston; William's birthplace is guessed and his
Irish migration rests on later family tradition. The match should remain below
the actionable threshold unless a specific parish, migration or relationship
record joins the two men.
