## Danville deposition — 27 October 2026

The [original deposition](https://old.lva.virginia.gov/ChanceryImg/143-Pittsylvania/Box041/143_1841_001_0021.pdf), with its [attestation](https://old.lva.virginia.gov/ChanceryImg/143-Pittsylvania/Box041/143_1841_001_0022.pdf), records James Glasgow and Roderick R. Hudnall at the National Hotel in Danville on **8 April 1840**. It belongs to Pittsylvania chancery case **1841-001**, James M. Sutherlin and wife against Armistead G. Weir, written “Wier” in the deposition caption. The case’s closing year is not the deposition date.

James says that in 1836 he heard Weir say he had bought fodder from Sutherlin; he thought the quantity was two stacks. He also gives an opinion of a stack’s value. The page bears a signature reading **James Glasgow**. This is witness testimony about another man’s purchase, not evidence that James bought or sold the fodder. Hudnall’s separate answer follows; it must not be attributed to James.

The name and court locality fit this James, whose held profile cites an 1840 Pittsylvania census, but the manuscript supplies **no age, occupation, residence, spouse or parent** to establish the match. Retain Glasgow-62 as a compatible candidate. The separate Virginia-born [James M. Glasgow-193](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-193) and [James William Glasgow-2925](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2925) have held Ohio and Tennessee household accounts; those locations alone do not exclude a temporary Danville appearance. No duplicate person or new family connection is proposed.

A [notice dated 6 April 1840](https://old.lva.virginia.gov/ChanceryImg/143-Pittsylvania/Box041/143_1841_001_0034.pdf) names James Glasgow and Roderick R. Hudnall and schedules their depositions for Wednesday the 8th at the National Hotel in Danville. It corroborates the date and venue of the already inspected testimony; it is not a second May appearance and adds no family identifier.

## Pittsylvania death and parent-claim review — 23 October 2026

The inspected [USGenWeb 1860 Pittsylvania death transcription](https://www.usgenwebsites.org/vagenweb/pittsylvania/deaths/1860Deaths.htm) records James Glasgow, cooper, age 71 years 10 months 12 days, Pittsylvania birthplace, cause **“killed,”** and Susan Glasgow as informant and wife. Its parent column and exact death-date cell are blank. This is a modern transcription of county returns; the original register was not inspected. The existing 28 September 1860 date comes from the previously cited death index, FHL 2048580. Combining that date with the transcribed age yields 16 November 1788; this is a calculated birthday, not independent birth evidence, so the approximate birth field is retained.

The [marriage-bond compilation](https://www.usgenwebsites.org/vagenweb/pittsylvania/marriages/1767marriage.pdf#page=104), printed p. 101/PDF p. 104, visually checked 23 October, gives James Glasgow/Susanna High, 21 October 1818, with Freeman High bondsman and no James parent. Printed p. 83/PDF p. 86 lists Robert Glasgow as bondsman for the 22 December 1815 Eudaley/Glasgow marriage. The original review below supersedes that role: Herod Worsham was surety, while Robert Glasgow signed a separate age-and-willingness declaration. The declaration remains a local associated lead, not an established father or proved brother/sister link. The compilation is not the original bond. The 1818 original has since been inspected, as described in the 30 October source review below; the 1815 family-identification test remains open.

A [contributed Ancestry aggregation](https://www.ancestry.com/genealogy/records/james-glasgow-24-1csrc5) names Robert Glasgow 1760–1839 and Sarah Cooper 1763–1853 as parents, without an inspected supporting record. No named parent is selected from this claim. [Robert-1530](/people/glasgow-1530.html) has matching years but is a documented Glasgow weaver with Jack/Brash marriages; he is not identified by a year match. [Robert-12](/people/glasgow-12.html), the Ohio immigrant with different wives, remains unconnected to this household. [Robert-395](/people/glasgow-395.html) is specifically inconsistent: his son James was already dead in a 1837 newspaper account, whereas this cooper lived to 1860. [William-357](/people/glasgow-357.html) and his Sarah Cooper 1756–1812 are a different Currituck couple; neither can be renamed or imported to fill this gap. The 1815 bond may help identify a local family, but does not establish the contributed Robert/Sarah couple.

## Original 1815 roles and signature comparison — 30 October 2026

The original 22 December 1815 bond names Robert Eudaley and Herod Worsham as obligors; both signature entries bear marks. Robert Glasgow instead signed the separate declaration that Elizabeth was of age and willing to marry. The typed compilation’s Robert Glasgow bondsman entry is contradicted by the original bond. Neither original states that Robert was Elizabeth’s father or that Elizabeth was James’s sister. The [bond front](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-C91C-R33H-Y) is DGS **007741063**, full-film image **311** (item2 image175); the [endorsement](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-C91C-R33H-2) is image310, dated22December1815; the [separate declaration](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-C91C-R334-N) is image309 (item2 image173). The declaration says Elizabeth was “of age” and willing; it supplies no exact age or literal kin term. The preceding image308 is a different Farthing marriage and was not treated as part of this family.

The original1818 bond and1840 deposition signatures were compared visually. Both read James Glasgow; broad cursive similarities are compatible with one man but do not supply a distinctive family, trade or age identifier. This was a non-expert comparison, not handwriting authentication. Glasgow-62 remains a compatible candidate for the deponent; no identity merger or parent attachment follows. The corrected1815 roles supersede the earlier bondsman description throughout this account.
