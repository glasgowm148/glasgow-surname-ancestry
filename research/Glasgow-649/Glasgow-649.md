"Arthur Glasgow died in May 1822, aged 72 years, so he was born in about 1750. He married widow Rebeckah Anderson McNutt McCorkle, on 7 Jan 1782 in Rockbridge County, Virginia.<ref>Arthur Glasgow in the Virginia, U.S., Compiled Marriages, 1660-1800</ref>She was the widow of John McCorkle (died Jan 1781) and the daughter of John McNutt and Katherine Anderson.

According to the source below, "Arthur Glasgow was a descendant of Earl Glasgow, of Scotland," where Arthur emigrated from to our colonies. Oren Frederic Morton's, A History of Rockbridge County, Virginia, Supplement, Staunton, VA, 1920, page 304-306 has some data on Arthur and his family through Alexander McNutt Glasgow. Page 253-254, says the 3 Glasgow brothers, Arthur, Robert and Joseph came to our colonies in the late 1700's. Glasgow Township was named for the family.

In 1820 he was living in Rockbridge County, Virginia and had 9 slaves.<ref>1820 United States Federal Census for Arthur Glasgow</ref>

He died in May, 1822 and is buried in Falling spring Presbyterian Church Cemetery in Glasgow, Rockbridge County, Virginia.  The date is visible on his headstone.<ref>Arthur Glasgow in the U.S., Find a Grave Index, 1600s-Current</ref>

=== Probable Moneymore father — 17 October 2026 ===

The existing Moneymore father account, Glasgow-1024, is assessed as the probable father of Robert Glasgow-12 and, by inference through their established brotherhood, Arthur Glasgow-649 and Joseph Glasgow-1543. The catalogue description of David Faris’s 1990 genealogy explicitly identifies the Adams County emigrant Robert (1749–1839) as a son of a James resident at Moneymore. It identifies a particular father and son, rather than offering a same-name local candidate. The family letter of 6 November 1806 printed by Faris names Arthur and Robert as Joseph’s brothers; Joseph’s 1831 will independently identifies Arthur as his deceased brother and the younger Robert as Arthur’s son. These sources distinguish the two Roberts and establish the sibling group to which the father attribution can reasonably extend. Robert’s pension supports the County Derry setting and 1769 migration. The approximate generations are compatible; the circa-1722 birth estimate is not independent evidence. The positive father attribution and established family group together warrant probable parentage, while remaining below documented or certain.

The father attribution survives here through a catalogue abstract of a retrospective genealogy; the book’s underlying parentage notes and a contemporary Irish parent record have not been inspected. The letter and will corroborate the sibling group, not the father’s name independently. Glasgow-1024 is used for its record-defined Moneymore father role: the inherited dates, Mary Harkness marriage, 1770 immigration, 1804 naturalisation, older James pedigree and proposed Corvanaghan identity are separate questions. The Corvanaghan identity and older father remain very uncertain. No probability is assigned to those claims by this review. The same father is inferred across the three established paternal-surname siblings; no evidence for different fathers has been identified. Other proposed siblings, including James-3305, John and Mary, and the proposed Adam connection are not upgraded.

Three existing father links change from an uncertain saved WikiTree flag and an ungraded/unknown local assessment to a formal probable local grade. No parent is newly attached, no live flag changes and no new working-tree component is created. All mothers remain separately uncertain; James’s own parents are unchanged. This is an inference from the combined existing evidence, not a newly discovered parent record.

=== Current research ===

[[Glasgow-649|Arthur Glasgow (Glasgow-649), born about 1750, later Rockbridge County, Virginia]] is now proved to be a brother of [[Glasgow-1543|Joseph Glasgow]] and [[Glasgow-12|Robert Glasgow]]. His claimed Moneymore birth and attached parents are still not established.

David Faris prints a family letter dated 6 November 1806 from Joseph and Betty Glasgow to Arthur. Joseph addresses Arthur as his brother and refers to “brother robert.” Joseph's 1831 will independently calls a younger Robert the son of his deceased brother Arthur and Joseph's nephew. The combined evidence makes the three-way sibling relationship strong; it does not identify their parents.

The repeated name <code>Arthur</code> in John Glasgow's descendants is real but heavily confounded by John's wife, '''Mary Arthur'''. Their son Arthur can naturally have been named from his mother's surname; later <code>Arthur Glasgow</code> compound names can honour that son or the maternal surname without referring to Arthur-649.

A useful working hypothesis remains: Arthur-649 could have had an older or half-brother Adam who remained in Ireland and became the older Adam implied by John-822's naming pattern. Faris calls emigrant Robert a son of '''James of Moneymore''', while later accounts make Robert, Joseph and Arthur brothers. The resulting <code>Adam-James-John-Arthur</code> sequence could preserve John's father Adam, grandfather James, John himself and his paternal uncle Arthur. No inspected source names Adam in that emigrant family, however.

This hypothesis concerns the record-defined '''James of Moneymore''', whose own dates and spouse are not supplied by the WorldCat summary. It must not be used to validate the internally conflicted live profile Glasgow-1024 or the wholly derivative James-3305.
