# Findings: [Arthur Glasgow (Glasgow-649), born 1750, Moneymore, County Londonderry, Ireland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-649)

Last updated: 2026-08-19

## Current conclusion

[Arthur Glasgow (Glasgow-649), born about 1750, later Rockbridge County,
Virginia](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-649) is now proved to be a
brother of [Joseph Glasgow (Glasgow-1543)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1543)
and [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-12)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-12).
His claimed Moneymore birth and attached parents are still not established.

David Faris prints a family letter dated 6 November 1806 from Joseph and Betty
Glasgow to Arthur. Joseph addresses Arthur as his brother and refers to “brother
robert.” Joseph's 1831 will independently calls a younger Robert the son of his
deceased brother Arthur and Joseph's nephew. The combined evidence makes the
three-way sibling relationship strong; it does not identify their parents.

The repeated name `Arthur` in John Glasgow's descendants is real but heavily
confounded by John's wife, **Mary Arthur**. Their son Arthur can naturally have
been named from his mother's surname; later `Arthur Glasgow` compound names can
honour that son or the maternal surname without referring to Arthur-649.

A useful working hypothesis remains: Arthur-649 could have had an older or
half-brother Adam who remained in Ireland and became the older Adam implied by
John-822's naming pattern. Faris calls emigrant Robert a son of **James of
Moneymore**, while later accounts make Robert, Joseph and Arthur brothers. The
resulting `Adam-James-John-Arthur` sequence could preserve John's father Adam,
grandfather James, John himself and his paternal uncle Arthur. No inspected
source names Adam in that emigrant family, however.

This hypothesis concerns the record-defined **James of Moneymore**, whose own
dates and spouse are not supplied by the WorldCat summary. It must not be used
to validate the internally conflicted live profile Glasgow-1024 or the wholly
derivative James-3305.

## Relationship assessment

The most coherent version of the new hypothesis is:

```text
James Glasgow of Moneymore, identity and dates not established
├── ? Adam Glasgow, born by about 1740, remained in Ireland
│   = Maria
│   ├── Adam Glasgow, born about 1758
│   └── John Glasgow, born 1767
├── Robert Glasgow, born 1749, emigrated
└── ? Arthur Glasgow, born about 1750, emigrated to Virginia
```

This is chronologically possible and would let John's first son `Adam` honour
his paternal grandfather, `James` preserve the preceding paternal generation,
and `Arthur` honour a paternal uncle. The sequence is not a strict application
of the usual naming convention because a second son ordinarily represented the
maternal grandfather. The model is not yet preferable to the second-1740-Hugh
model because no source names Adam as Arthur's brother, Faris's evidence remains
uninspected, Ellen Glasgow's account instead places the family's Irish stage in
Down and Antrim, and Mary Arthur independently explains the name Arthur.
