## Evidence relevant to the proposed match

- The 1830 Richland Township census is misidentified: its John was aged 40-49,
  while this John was 63.
- The family Bible gives no place for son James's 1823 death; it does not prove
  that John was already in Ohio.
- John's family register and 1847 letter connect him personally to Inishrush.
- His age and Covenanter background fit the Drimbolg 1809/1827 John.
- The 1831 household is an exact probable reconstruction from the Bible: after
  married daughter Rose and deceased son James are removed, exactly four males
  remain. Jane is the best-supported missing female because descendant evidence
  says she married John Gray in Ireland and a two-person Presbyterian John Gray
  household appears at Boveedy in 1831 and persists in the 1833 tithe. The census
  does not name the wife, so this remains a probable identification.
- His sons occur as Adam, James and John in the traditional paternal-grandfather,
  maternal-grandfather and father positions, with father John correctly repeated
  third; Maria is the second daughter, the paternal-grandmother position.
- The 1847 letter's report about James Glasgow and Henry Barkly's daughter fits
  Adam's proved son James at Inishrush and the adjoining Tyanee network, where
  Adam's proved daughter Nancy had married John Kyle.

These points make identity with the 1831 John strong, but do not distinguish
brotherhood from cousinship with Adam. They justify submitting a merge proposal
and leaving it pending for review. Older Adam and Maria should remain candidate
parent names in Research Notes, not structured parents.
