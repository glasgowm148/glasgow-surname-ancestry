== Biography ==
John Glasgow was born in Ireland on 10 April 1767.<ref name="JohnRegister">[https://hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I368&tree=tree1 John Glasgow family register and descendant page] (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref><ref name="JohnLetter">[https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/documents/John%20Glasgow%20letter%20to%20Jane%20Glasgow%20Gray%203-20-1847.pdf John Glasgow to Jane Glasgow Gray, 20 March 1847], scan and transcript (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref> He married [[Arthur-2480|Mary Arthur]] on 21 May 1798, and their nine children were born in Ireland between 1799 and 1815.<ref name="JohnRegister" />

The family lived at '''Inishrush, County Londonderry'''. In 1847, John recalled that they had kept more bees there than they ever kept at one time after leaving Ireland.<ref name="JohnLetter" />

The first secure evidence of John in Ohio is his 1833 purchase of land in Guernsey County.<ref name="Deed1833">[http://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-89WY-TFPB Guernsey County deeds, volume H, page 457].</ref> He was recorded there in 1840.<ref name="Census1840">“'''United States Census, 1840''',” database with images, FamilySearch (https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XHRF-1B6 : 8 September 2017), John Glascow, Guernsey, Ohio, United States; citing p. 355, NARA microfilm publication M704, roll 396; FHL microfilm 20,165.</ref> By 1847, John and Mary were being cared for by their son Arthur, who left “nothing undone” to make them comfortable.<ref name="JohnLetter" /> They were living in Arthur's household in Londonderry Township in 1850.<ref name="Census1850">“'''United States Census, 1850''',” database with images, FamilySearch (https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MX3M-HP3 : 12 April 2016), John Glasgow in household of Arthur Glasgow, Londonderry, Guernsey, Ohio, United States; citing family 1538, NARA microfilm publication M432.</ref>

The Glasgows were a strict Covenanter family; family tradition states that even whistling on the Sabbath was forbidden.<ref name="JohnRegister" /> John's surviving letter reflects this outlook through repeated references to prayer, salvation and holding to Christ “for righteousness and life.”<ref name="JohnLetter" />

John died on 23 December 1851 and was buried in the Scotch Covenanter Cemetery in Guernsey County.<ref name="Grave">“Find A Grave Index,” database, FamilySearch (https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:QVKX-FB2D : 13 December 2015), John Glasgow, died 1851; burial in Scotch Covenanter Cemetery, Guernsey County, Ohio; citing {{FindAGrave|43524841}}.</ref>

== Research Notes ==
* [[Glasgow-3927|John Glasgow (bef.1813-)]] represents the John Glasco who headed house 2 at Inishrush in the 1831 census. His household contained four males and four females, described in the Torrens transcription as all Presbyterian.<ref name="Census1831">[https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/Tamlaght/1831census_3.html “Tamlaght O'Crilly Census – 1831”], entry 1019, John Glasco, Innisrush house 2; transcription by Richard John Torrens (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref> The combined identity assessment is '''94%''', based on the household reconstruction, the independent Inishrush family evidence below and the migration chronology. The census itself gives no ages, individual household names or relationships. Published transcriptions disagree about the denomination column, which remains unresolved pending inspection of the original return. Under this strongly supported identification, John emigrated between the 1831 census and his 1833 Ohio land purchase. The identity assessment is separate from the 18% father hypothesis and does not identify the parents of the record-defined householder.<ref name="Census1831" /><ref name="Deed1833" />
* The John Glasgow recorded in Richland Township, Belmont County, in 1830 was aged forty to forty-nine and was almost certainly a different man. John, born in 1767, would have been about sixty-three. This rejected identification supplies no evidence that he was in Ohio by 1830.<ref name="Ohio1830">[https://archive.org/details/populationsc18300127unit/page/n484/mode/1up 1830 United States census, Richland Township, Ohio], original census image (accessed 20 July 2026).</ref>
* John may have descended from [[Glasgow-1475|Adam Glasgow (bef.1706-)]], associated with Kilbirnie and the Presbytery of Irvine. This remains unproved.

=== Current Larne reconstruction ===

[[Glasgow-1509|James Glasgow, the older Larne ropemaker]], is the selected '''very uncertain working father, assessed at 18%'''. No mother is identified. John and [[Glasgow-3902|Adam Glasgow of Inishrush]] were nine years apart and headed the parish's two Glasgow households in 1831. John's 1847 letter reports intimate news about the local James Glasgow and Henry Barkly family, and both descendant families used James. These agreements favour a brother or close-collateral relationship and make an extension of Adam's tentative James father worth retaining. They do not independently identify James as John's father.<ref name="JohnRegister" /><ref name="Census1831" /><ref name="JohnLetter" />

James was a Belfast ropemaker taking a Larne ropewalk lease in 1760; a 1773 affidavit places the relevant Larne James's birth before October 1743. This is a real, chronologically compatible candidate, but no inspected record names John as his son or establishes a migration to Inishrush.<ref name="LarneRopewalk1760">[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-S7XZ-8 Registry of Deeds, ropewalk lease dated 10 September 1760, registered 24 November 1763], James Glasgow of Belfast, ropemaker, Gardenmore, Larne.</ref><ref name="LarneAge1773">[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-M72W-M Registry of Deeds, affidavit sworn 27 October 1773 and registered 15 November 1773], James Glasgow of Larne, gentleman, aged upwards of thirty.</ref>

The 18% assessment depends on both the unproved Adam–John brotherhood and Adam's own uncertain paternity; the two selected links are correlated hypotheses, not corroborating records. John's sons Adam–James–John and daughters beginning Rose–Maria favour an older Adam and Maria as the stronger unnamed rival. Cousinship with Adam remains possible. The naming model does not create those parents, and James's reported wife [[Shutter-46|Janet Shutter]] is not established as John's mother. Adam-3902 himself was about nine at John's birth and cannot be his father.<ref name="JohnRegister" /><ref name="AdamMonument">[https://www.oneirishrover.com/tamlaght-graveyard/ Tamlaght graveyard, Glasgow inscription], Adam died 30 June 1844 aged 86; wife Rose died 31 July 1833 aged 66.</ref>

The public Y-DNA comparison supplies context for a wider paternal network, but participant pedigrees and reported distances do not identify John's father or prove a specific relationship to Adam or [[Glasgow-951|Alexander Glasgow]].<ref name="JohnCurrent">[https://www.familytreedna.com/groups/glasgow/about Glasgow Surname Project at FamilyTreeDNA]. Project groupings and participant pedigrees provide context for a paternal-line hypothesis, not a statement of John's parentage.</ref>

=== Inishrush and Drimbolg evidence ===

A separate McKeown family Bible records John Glasgow McKeown's birth at Inishrush on 17 April 1821 and baptism by the Rev. Joseph Orr. Drimbolg Reformed Presbyterian history records a John Glasgow in 1809 and as an elder in 1827. Together, these independent records substantially strengthen the identification of this John with the 1831 Inishrush householder, although neither names Adam as a relative.<ref name="McKeownBible">[https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I1&amp;tree=tree1 John Glasgow McKeown], family-Bible transcription and descendant page.</ref><ref name="Drimbolg">[https://www.oneirishrover.com/drimbolg-reformed-presbyterian-church/ Drimbolg Reformed Presbyterian Church], congregation history and session extracts.</ref>

The Guernsey County deed is the earliest secure Ohio evidence presently located for John; it establishes neither an Irish parent nor a sibling.<ref name="Deed1833Retained">[https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-89WY-TFPB Guernsey County deeds, volume H, page 457].</ref>

=== Possible John Glasco in Irish rosters, 1798–1799 ===
John Glasco is PRESENT at the Black-Rock Association meeting 8 May 1798.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/1214061848/ The Public Register, or, Freeman's Journal, 1798-05-29, p.4].</ref>

John Glasco appears in the 24 September 1799 roster, continued beneath an earlier COUNTY OF LONDONDERRY heading.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/1214179229/ The Public Register, or, Freeman's Journal, 1799-09-24, p.2].</ref>

The two entries retain separate identities: the 1798 Black-Rock attendee is not proved to be the 1799 County Londonderry roster bearer. A shared surname and county do not establish relatives.

This may concern [[Glasgow-822]]. Possible 1767 Irish-born Londonderry connection; no roster link.
