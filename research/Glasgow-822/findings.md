# Findings: Glasgow-822

Last updated: 2026-08-09

## Current conclusion and tree action

John Glasgow (`Glasgow-822`), born 10 April 1767, is the strongest identity
candidate for the John Glasco who headed Inishrush house 2 in 1831. Because the
existing profile belongs to a separately maintained identity, create the 1831
householder as a new record-defined profile and submit a merge proposal. Leave
the proposal pending until both identities are reviewed.

Adam Glasgow (`Glasgow-3902`), born about 1758, cannot have been John's father:
he was only about nine when John was born. Brother and cousin are both viable,
but no record states either relationship. John and the record-defined duplicate
`Glasgow-3927` should not be attached beneath Adam's unidentified-father
placeholder `Glasgow-3905`.

## 1831 household reconstruction

The most probable reconstruction is now:

```text
Inishrush house 2
Males:   John Glasgow; sons Adam, John and Arthur                 = 4
Females: Mary Arthur; daughters Maria, Lavina and Margaret       = 4

Elsewhere
Rose Glasgow: already married to Alexander McKeown
Jane Glasgow: probably the unnamed wife in John Gray's 1m/1f
              Presbyterian household at Boveedy
James Glasgow: died 20 April 1823
```

This is not direct proof because the census named only household heads. It is,
however, a substantially tighter fit than the earlier unspecified missing-daughter
argument: family evidence says Jane married John Gray in Ireland, and the parish
return contains a contemporary John Gray household with exactly the required wife.
Maria has no matched Alexander Wallace household in the parish, while the only
Richard Mulholland household is a three-person Roman Catholic household at
Killygullib and is not a credible match to Lavina's later husband.

## Relationship assessment

### Direct-proof result

No inspected gravestone, family Bible, 1847 letter, census, tithe, flax list,
deed index, probate index, lease transcription or church-history extract calls
Adam and John brothers or names a shared parent. The separate McKeown Bible makes
John's identity with the Inishrush/Drimbolg man appreciably stronger; it does not
change the exact relationship from inferred to proved.

| Placement relative to Adam | Assessment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Son | Contradicted | Adam was about nine when John was born. |
| Brother | Plausible, but insufficient for attachment | Only nine years apart; separate Presbyterian households in the same townland; John fits the Drimbolg elder; patterned naming and the 1847 news support association. No record names a shared parent or uses a kin term. |
| Cousin | Plausible alternative | The records never expressly name a shared parent. |
| Unrelated namesake | Possible but weaker | Requires two contemporary Covenanter John Glasgows associated with the same Inishrush/Drimbolg setting after the false Ohio evidence is removed. |

## Shared-parent naming hypothesis only

The Bible revives, but does not prove, this working reconstruction:

```text
Older Adam Glasgow = Maria Unknown
├── Adam Glasgow (about 1758), of Inishrush
└── John Glasgow (1767), probably the 1831 Inishrush householder
```

This is stronger than simply observing that John used the names Rose and Adam,
but it remains circular if used to create the very parents it is meant to test.
His sons fall in the order `Adam - James - John`, matching the traditional
Scottish paternal-grandfather, maternal-grandfather, father sequence; his second
daughter was Maria, the conventional paternal-grandmother position. Keep the
shared parents unnamed structurally until a record supplies them, but preserve
older Adam and Maria only as search terms, not attached people.

## Relationship to Hugh Glasgow-548

William Melanchthon Glasgow's late family history says Hugh-548's youngest son
Hugh had a family farm in County Londonderry near Portglenone by 1780. That
specific locality aligns with the two 1740 Tamlaght O'Crilly Hugh households,
the 1772 parish Hugh, and John's proved Inishrush association. The cumulative
case makes membership in Hugh-548's extended local family highly probable, but
does not state the intervening parent.

The two 1740 Hugh households suggest a cleaner topology. Entry 1272, beside
Ellis and McCullough, is the stronger candidate for Hugh-548; entry 1195 could
head the collateral line that produced older Adam and Maria. If the choice is
restricted to Hugh-548's traditional sons, John-549 and Robert-550 are both
possible direct fathers, but the current evidence does not distinguish them.
Older Adam cannot be placed beneath either without compressing two generations
into 1758.
