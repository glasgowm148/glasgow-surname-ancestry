# John Glasgow direct-link audit, 21 July 2026

## Question

Find a record explicitly connecting John Glasgow, born 10 April 1767 and later
of Inishrush and Ohio, to Adam Glasgow of Inishrush or to the Hugh Glasgow
family near Portglenone.

## Completed searches

| Record group | Result |
| --- | --- |
| John and Mary Glasgow family Bible | Gives John, Mary, marriage date, children and later deaths; no parents or siblings. |
| John Glasgow's 20 March 1847 letter | Proves former Inishrush residence. The `Adam` letter is most plausibly from John's son Adam. `R. Mulhollan` was almost certainly son-in-law Richardson Mulholland; McCaivey brought the James/Barkly news. No kin term links target Adam, James or Hugh. |
| 1831 census and collateral household reconstruction | John and Adam headed the parish's only two Glasgow households, both at Inishrush. John's Bible family reconstructs house 2 almost exactly; Jane probably occupied John Gray's Boveedy household. They were recorded as houses 2 and 91, not adjacent households, and the census states no relationships. |
| Registry of Deeds Index Project, Glasgow variants and Inishrush townland index through 1850 | No indexed memorial names Adam or John as parties at Inishrush. The 1730s head leases name other tenants. Indexing is incomplete and original memorial text remains unsearched. |
| PRONI Freeholders database | A successful direct search under `Glasgow`, `Glasco`, `Glasgo`, `Glascow`, `Glassgow`, `Glaskow`, `Glascho` and `Glesgow` returned twelve Glasgow entries. None names Adam or Inishrush. The five John entries concern Drumgreenagh, Drumcon or Goston and contain no identifying kinship. |
| Public Glasgow Y-DNA project and 2016 Legacy Tree report | John's kit belongs to the broad Glasgow paternal group. No public kit has a documented strict male-line descent from target Adam or the relevant Hugh, so DNA does not state the recent connection. |
| Hiltner/McKeown source catalogue and archived media | The cited private 2018 `Descendants of John Glasgow.pdf` / `Glasgow Register Report.doc` was not publicly attached or archived. Public Bible, letter and descendant pages contain no parent statement. |
| Public Hiltner source `S17` association list | The source is attached only to John and Mary, their children, grandchildren and the spouses/families of those descendants. It exposes no parent, sibling or earlier-generation Glasgow. This is a bounded negative for the public database, not a substitute for obtaining the underlying private files. |
| Reformed Presbyterian periodicals checked to date | No obituary for John born 1767 was found. A different John Glasgow obituary states that he was born near Portglenone on 28 April 1780 and emigrated with his father in 1796; this strongly fits Hugh Glasgow's will-named son John, not the Inishrush John born 1767. |
| [Sterrett's abstract of obituaries in *The Reformed Presbyterian*, 1837-1862](https://media.wpgs.org/wpgs/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2021/06/Volume-08-Number-01-Membership-Year-1981-1982_members.pdf) and [original volume 15](https://books.google.com/books?id=1Hs0AQAAMAAJ&printsec=frontcover) | The abstract passes from November 1851 to January 1852 without a December entry and contains no John Glasgow. It includes James Galbraith of Londonderry, Ohio, in July 1850, showing that the compiler covered John's settlement. The correct original target is volume 15 (March 1851-February 1852). Its Google Books ID is `1Hs0AQAAMAAJ`, but search-within-volume results and relevant pages could not be retrieved in this audit. This is a bounded negative for the abstract only; the original volume remains unsearched. |
| Hugh Glasgow's 1827 will | Names sons John, Hugh, Alexander and Robert and daughter Nancy. Adam and John born 1767 are absent. This is adverse evidence against Hugh as their father, though not proof against collateral relationship. |
| Original 1847 John Glasgow letter image | The complete three-page manuscript was inspected against the transcript. No omitted word calls Adam, James, Hugh or another Irish Glasgow John's brother, father, uncle or cousin. |
| Guernsey County histories, 1882 and 1895 | Full text searched for Glasgow, McKeown and Mulholland. Neither volume contains a biographical account identifying John's Irish parents or siblings. |
| [Guernsey County will index, 1812-1900](https://www.sampubco.com/wills/oh/ohguernsey01.htm) | The complete published testator index contains no Glasgow entry, so John left no recorded will in county Will Book B (1841-1852). This does not exclude an intestate administration, which remains the more relevant unresolved probate series. |
| [Guernsey County probate publications](https://www.guernseycountyohiogenealogy.com/publications), including *Index to Wills and Estates 1850-1900* and *Probate Court Case Files Index* | The public SAMPUBCO testator index and the 1967 typed *Wills Index of Guernsey County, Ohio, 1812-1900* contain no Glasgow. The society confirms that a separate probate case-file index exists for the mid-1840s onward, but its entries are not publicly exposed. Therefore no will has been located, while an intestate case remains untested; no case number or kin statement can yet be claimed. |
| Harrison County historical collections and Alexander McKeown descendants | Alexander McKeown's patent and will abstract confirm his Ohio family with Rosanna Glasgow. A Page County biography says Alexander and Rose were natives of County Antrim, married there and emigrated in 1823, but does not name Rose's parents or connect John to Adam or Hugh. |
| [Arthur Glasgow's wife Jane Neill](https://hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I460&tree=tree1) and [Rev. William Kennedy McKay's papers](https://sources.nli.ie/Record/MS_UR_086186?lng=en&print=1) | Jane's 1886 obituary says she joined Rev. McKay's Presbyterian congregation at Portglenone aged eighteen, about 1833. The same minister married Adam's proved sixth daughter Nancy Glasgow in 1842. This is a concrete overlap between the religious network of John's son Arthur and Adam's daughter, but Jane was an in-law and neither notice states Glasgow kinship. PRONI `D2594` contains McKay/First Portglenone papers; the [public church inventory](https://www.billmacafee.com/churchrecords/churchrecordsnorthmidantrim.pdf) shows no surviving pre-1845 marriage or member roll that can presently test John and Adam. |
| [Complete Derry diocesan will calendar, 1612-1857](https://www.billmacafee.com/wills/derrywills.pdf) | The full surname-ordered transcription was searched for `Glasgow`, `Glasco`, `Glasgo`, `Glascow`, `Glassgow`, `Glaskow`, `Glascho` and `Glesgow`; none occurs. Adam's 1844 estate therefore has no entry in this will calendar. The result does not cover the separate administration-bond index or every surviving grant-book entry. |
| [First Kilrea baptisms, 1845-1847](https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/KilreaP1/Baptisms/1847.html) and [communicant candidates, 1825-1838](https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/KilreaP1/Communion.html) | All three annual baptism transcriptions were checked entry by entry. None records Glasgow, Barkly variants or a matching illegitimate child. The candidate list likewise has no Glasgow/Barkly, but is not a full member or discipline register. The untranscribed discipline pages of `MIC1P/87` remain unresolved. |
| [Drimbolg congregational-history extracts](https://www.oneirishrover.com/drimbolg-reformed-presbyterian-church/) | The full published page names John Glasgow at a session in 1809 and as an elder in 1827. Adam is absent from the published excerpts. This supports John's local identity but yields neither a joint appearance nor a kin term; the complete `MIC1C/15` manuscript remains unread. |
| [PRONI eCatalogue](https://apps.proni.gov.uk/eCatNI_IE/), `T1762` hierarchy and `T1762/5` | The apparently promising “Glasgow family and Presbyterian papers” collection is dated 1795-1899. Its sole Glasgow-family item is the will and codicil of William Glasgow, farmer of Oritor, County Tyrone. No item description names Adam, John or Inishrush. | Closed as an Inishrush lead; it is not a family dossier for this cluster. |
| [PHSI envelope index](https://www.nifhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/PHSI-Index.pdf) and [other-name table](https://www.nifhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/Presb_Hist_Soc_Envelopes_other_names_mentioned.pdf) | Glasgow occurs in envelope `G61` and in Anderson `A27` and Hopper `H89`. These envelopes are described as archivist correspondence with genealogical enquirers, not original church records, and no public catalogue text identifies Adam, John or Inishrush. |
| [FamilyTreeDNA Glasgow Y-DNA public table](https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview) | John's kit `N18544` is in the broad U106 Glasgow group, but no displayed kit has a documented strict male-line descent from Adam born about 1758. | Cannot test the proposed recent Adam-John relationship; it supports only wider paternal kinship. |

## Current result

No inspected record yet explicitly calls John and Adam brothers or names a
shared parent. The strongest supported placement remains close collateral kin,
probably brothers, while the direct line to the Hugh family is unproved.

## Highest-value unresolved records

1. Vintners estate leases, rentals and tenant succession for Inishrush,
   especially `D1062/1/5`, `D2094/50-51,68,75-78`, `T2825/C/19/1-13` and
   `D1201/53`.
2. Drimbolg Reformed Presbyterian session book `MIC1C/15`.
3. Guernsey County loose administration and church-session records for John.
4. The private 2018 Glasgow compilation cited as Hiltner source `S17`.
5. Full-text, not merely volunteer-indexed, Registry of Deeds memorials for
   Inishrush and its superior landlords.
6. Derry and Raphoe diocesan grant book, 1812-1851, and the separate Derry
   administration-bond index. Adam died in 1844, inside the surviving grant-book
   span; a grant may identify the administrator even though the complete will
   calendar contains no Glasgow entry.
7. PHSI Glasgow correspondence envelope `G61`; first establish its dates,
   locality, provenance and cited original sources. Do not reopen PRONI `T1762`
   unless a separate record first connects the Oritor and Inishrush families.
