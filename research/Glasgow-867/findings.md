## Current conclusion

**17 September 2026 (part 2) — corrections.** (1) The Rasharkin GLASGO memorial's "Elizabeth Ann, died 16 June 1933 aged 84" is **James Garvin Glasgow's widow Eliza Ann (Aiken)**: PRONI Will Calendar, Glasgow, Eliza Ann, of Tullynahinion, widow, d. 16 June 1933, probate Belfast 17 July 1933 to Robert Andrew Aicken, farmer, and William Shaw, solicitor (£601 5s); James Garvin Glasgow's estate (d. 14 Feb 1918, Lisnagaver) was administered 8 May 1918 by Eliza Ann Glasgow, widow (£310). The 5 September master review confirms the direct John–Samuel brotherhood and

John Glasgow of Lisnagaver directly identified his father as **John Glasgow,
farmer**, in his 1846 marriage. His brother Samuel is proved by the 1851 census.
The Rasharkin Second Presbyterian gravestone erected by John's son James Garvin
Glasgo gives John's death as 20 June 1895, aged 78 (probably an error for 1885; see the correction of 7 October 2026 below), and identifies Margaret Garvin
as James's mother. It therefore gives John an approximate birth year of 1816/17,
although the 1851 return reported his age as fifty.

The later date makes John `Glasgow-1022` a less comfortable father candidate:
he would have been about sixty-five when this John was born. That remains
possible, especially because early Irish ages are inconsistent, but the shared
name and occupation no longer make the identity the clear leading solution.
Keep the link uncertain.

Alexander `Glasgow-2385` cannot be this John's son. Alexander was already a
full-age bachelor in April 1847 and named his own father as John Glasgow,
labourer. John `Glasgow-867` had married only five months earlier and was himself
probably born about 1817. Alexander belongs to a different father-John branch.

## Effect on relationships

- Margaret Garvin is John's proved wife.
- Ann `Glasgow-1371` is John's proved daughter. Her maternity is strongly
  supported by the 1851 household but not named in her marriage.
- James Garvin `Glasgow-2406` is proved as the son of John and Margaret by the
  combined marriage and gravestone evidence.
- Samuel `Glasgow-2908` is John's proved brother.
- John `Glasgow-1022` may remain as an uncertain father, but the gravestone date
  lowers the confidence of that identification.
- Alexander `Glasgow-2385` was detached as John's son. He is a different
  adult son of a John Glasgow, labourer.

## Remaining parentage problem

The named father was a John Glasgow who worked as a farmer by 1846. The evidence
does not give his residence, wife or age. If John and Samuel shared a father, that man must have been capable
of fathering Samuel, born about 1791, and John, whose sources disagree
between about 1801 and about 1817. The Killoquin estate tenant books for 1772-74
and 1835 are the most promising source for separating the local John households.

## Original 1851 image and age conflict (24 August 2026)

The original house 34 manuscript confirms the reported ages as **John 50**,
**Margaret 30**, daughter Ann 3 and brother **Samuel 60**. Samuel's age was
misread as `6` in one electronic transcription. John and Margaret both reported
marriage in 1846, matching the civil marriage and making this the Margaret
Garvin household.

The Rasharkin Presbyterian gravestone transcription nevertheless says that
Margaret Garvin's husband John died on 20 June 1895 aged 78, implying birth
about 1816/17 rather than about 1800/01. The two ages remain irreconcilable on
present evidence. John's birth should not be narrowed from either source alone.
The reverse of the census return names no household deaths in 1841-51 and
therefore supplies no parent.

Source: [National Archives of Ireland, 1851 census, Lisnagaver house 34](https://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Rasharkin/Lisnagaver/34/).

## Recommended correction — John's death in 1885, 7 October 2026

The [original civil death register](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/deaths_returns/deaths_1885/06280/4799372.pdf) (Dervock registrar's district, entry 2; group registration ID 6910472) records **John Glasgow, married farmer, who died at Lisnagaver on 24 June 1885, aged 78**. The informant was **James Glasgow, son, of Lisnagaver**. The entry was read from the register image for Glasgow-3474 (29 August 2026) and again in the Glasgow-951 searches of 23 September 2026. The irishgenealogy.ie index of Glasgow deaths in Ballymoney registration district, 1864–1910, searched on 17 September 2026, has this John (1885, aged 78) and no John Glasgow in 1895.

This is probably the death of this John. He was a married farmer of Lisnagaver; his son James Garvin Glasgow was a farmer of Lisnagaver when he married in 1886; and the register's age, 78, is the age on the family gravestone. The gravestone transcription's "20 June 1895, aged 78" is therefore probably an error for 1885, although the stone itself has not been seen. This is an inference, graded probable: the register names no wife or parent. The 17 September 2026 live-session log reports that Glasgow-3474 was merged into this profile with the 1885 death kept, and the edit register lists that merge as done; the 21 September audit noted that it had not re-verified the identification. On this reading, statements above that John died in 1895, that he was born about 1816–17, or that the two dates remain to be resolved are superseded. A smaller age conflict remains: the register's 78 implies birth about 1806–07, the 1851 census (aged fifty) about 1800–01.
