# Findings: Glasgow-867

Last updated: 2026-08-13

## Current conclusion

John Glasgow of Lisnagaver directly identified his father as **John Glasgow,
farmer**, in his 1846 marriage. His brother Samuel is proved by the 1851 census.
The Rasharkin Second Presbyterian gravestone erected by John's son James Garvin
Glasgo adds John's death on 20 June 1895, aged 78, and identifies Margaret Garvin
as James's mother. It therefore gives John an approximate birth year of 1816/17,
although the 1851 return reported his age as fifty.

The later date makes John `Glasgow-1022` a less comfortable father candidate:
he would have been about sixty-five when this John was born. That remains
possible, especially because early Irish ages are inconsistent, but the shared
name and occupation no longer make the identity the clear leading solution.
Keep the link uncertain.

Alexander `Glasgow-2385` cannot be this John's son. Alexander was already a
full-age bachelor in April 1847 and named his own father as John Glasgow,
labourer. John `Glasgow-867` had married only five months earlier and was himself
probably born about 1817. Alexander belongs to a different father-John branch.

## Effect on relationships

- Margaret Garvin is John's proved wife.
- Ann `Glasgow-1371` is John's proved daughter. Her maternity is strongly
  supported by the 1851 household but not named in her marriage.
- James Garvin `Glasgow-2406` is proved as the son of John and Margaret by the
  combined marriage and gravestone evidence.
- Samuel `Glasgow-2908` is John's proved brother.
- John `Glasgow-1022` may remain as an uncertain father, but the gravestone date
  lowers the confidence of that identification.
- Alexander `Glasgow-2385` was detached as John's son. He is a different
  adult son of a John Glasgow, labourer.

## Remaining parentage problem

The named father was a John Glasgow who worked as a farmer by 1846. The evidence
does not give his residence, wife or age. The records now require a man capable
of fathering both Samuel, born about 1791, and John, whose sources disagree
between about 1801 and about 1817. The Killoquin estate tenant books for 1772-74
and 1835 are the most promising source for separating the local John households.
