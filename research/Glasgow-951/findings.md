## Current conclusion

[Alexander Glasgow (Glasgow-951)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-951) is the
project owner’s oldest confirmed direct paternal ancestor. The original 1851
Lisnagaver house-35 return records Alexander and Mary, both 40, and children
Robert (10), James (7) and Ann (4), all born in Antrim. Both spouses report their
first marriage in **1836**. The maintained birth estimate is c.1811, not an exact
date. The complete household and the wider record sequence are now presented
visibly on his person page from `presentation.json`.

Rasharkin entries 75, 106 and 203 document the same Alex/Alexander and Mary/Molly
McAughin/McAughern family at Gortereagh in 1837–38 and Lisnagaver by 1843. The
parents of the unnamed 1838 child are documented, separately from the missing
forename and exact dates. The two James children remain distinct. Neither the
first James’s early death nor the unnamed child’s death has an identified death
or burial record. Robert’s census and marriage ages support c.1840–41; the
imported 6 November 1840 date remains unsupported. Mary’s 1871 marriage and Ann’s
1876 marriage independently name Alexander as father.

**16 May 1888 at Gortahar remains Alexander’s probable death and the
best-supported located match.** Original entry 348 was inspected again on
13 September: Alexander Glasgow, married labourer, 84; Nancy Glasgow, daughter,
present at death, **of Gortahar**; registered 25 May. Nancy’s residence is stated.
The age conflicts with the 1851 age by about seven years; her identity with Ann
McCracken is not proved. Mary Elder’s Lisnagaver father-naming marriage, husband
Robert Elder’s succession to lot 25, the Elder family at Gortahar, and Mary
Glasgow’s 17 February 1892 death there with daughter Mary Elder form the strongest
indirect bridge. The 1892 record does not itself name the deceased husband.

[Alexander of Glenleslie (Glasgow-2385)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2385)
is a real competing labourer namesake, but his c.1816 birth is a worse age fit
and no located Gortahar/Nancy/Elder connection supports reassignment. He is a
weaker alternative, not conclusively excluded. Older Alexander `Glasgow-3904`
is a poor generational fit. No better positively evidenced claimant to the
1888 death was found in this bounded review; no exhaustive new national death
search is claimed. Retain the date with an explicit identity qualification.

The [FTDNA public Glasgow project](https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview)
was checked on 13 September 2026: a tested Alexander-descendant and a tested
[James J Glasgow (Glasgow-2738)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2738)
descendant both have **Big Y-700 / R-FT20271**. The maintained Y-111 comparison
reports genetic distance three. This confirms paternal genetic kinship between
the tested lines; their named common ancestor and exact relationship remain
unresolved. The supplied Block Tree has no exclusive named downstream branch
joining only these two lines. Private variants do not by themselves identify
Alexander’s genotype. The project’s submitted “d.1852” ancestor label is not a
death record. The public Discover tree could not be retrieved in this review.

Alexander’s parents remain unproved. Daniel and Thomas are documented brothers;
Alexander may be a brother or cousin. Older Alexander and Rosanna remain a
working tradition, not established parents. The reported Thomas-descendant
autosomal match is a separate unverified lead. Mary (28 in 1871) and Maryan

proves their identity nor justifies an absolute exclusion by itself. Tithe
Plantation acres cannot be equated directly with valuation statute acres to
infer inheritance. Candidate scores remain lead rankings, not probabilities.

The expanded synthesis now follows the 1836 field-book problem, maternal McCaughan context, children’s adult identities and the separate Lisnagaver/Kilrea/Prescott proof chains. The full maintained narrative and three sourced illustrations are in `presentation.json` and `figures/`; `profile-evidence-review-2026-09-13.md` is its readable research copy. The Michigan James remains a strong candidate, not a proved emigrant son. Mary’s surname conflict in the Elder grandchildren is preserved without inventing a Glasgow father.

The death argument explicitly depends on the independently identified Elder family. The next comparisons prioritise existing consented data, direct genotype/coverage checks and independently documented descendant branches.

#### Michigan Samuel rejected as the 1838 child

The 1838 baptism was already represented by
[Unknown Glasgow (Glasgow-3940)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3940),
attached to Alexander and Mary. It was not a new discovery. The live profile
records death before 1841, evidently as an inference rather than from a death
record.

The inference is reasonable but not conclusive. The 1851 Lisnagaver household
lists Alexander, Mary, Robert, James and Ann, with no additional person marked
resident, absent, or dead since 1841. The National Archives explains that this
census recorded household members absent on census night and family members
who had died since 1841. Nearby returns demonstrate both categories, including
children away as servants or pupils. If the 1838 child belonged to this same
family and was still alive in 1851, a 12- or 13-year-old would ordinarily be
expected either in the household or its absent section; if the child died from
1841 onward, the mortality section should ordinarily record the death.

Samuel Glasgow of Franklin/Blissfield, Michigan, therefore should **not** be
identified with `Glasgow-3940`. His compatible age and Irish birth are only
surname-level coincidence, while the family census is contrary evidence and no
American record names County Antrim or Alexander and Mary. Samuel reported
about 37 years in the United States in 1894, implying immigration around 1857,
so the same-person theory requires the unexplained omission of a living
13-year-old from every section of Alexander's 1851 return. The Pleasant View
transcription's impossible `29 February 1838` date further prevents exact-date
matching. Retain the Michigan records only as a rejected-candidate audit; do
not attach Samuel to this family. Full reasoning is in the focused
[Glasgow-3940 handoff](../Glasgow-3940/findings.md).

STR-inferred topology, not confirmed mutation direction. Only FTE32242 is a
confirmed internal child SNP. DYS712 is itself a rapidly mutating Y-STR: a
367-pair father-son study observed 17 mutations and estimated a rate of about
`46.3 x 10^-3` per meiosis. Its shared `19` state is therefore a useful
secondary clue but particularly vulnerable to recurrence or back-mutation; it
must not be treated as a clade-defining event. [Mutation-rate study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12218846/)

The downloaded raw bundle resolves the private block more precisely. Kit
`B580327` has five exported private variants: `12648194 A>G`, `14337402 T>C`,
`14339231 G>C`, `15066888 A>T` and `15973436 A>C`. Its VCF also has a sixth
lineage-specific PASS call at `26534797 C>G` (depth 25). The non-matching screen
labels that position `FT124483`, but FT124483 is the different allele `C>A` at
the same multi-allelic site. It does **not** define a shared branch among the
other matches. All six calls are PASS at depth 25-78 and are absent from every
displayed match.

The 15 numeric match-side positions lie inside the B580327 quality BED and have no derived call in its exported VCF. Coverage plus omission from a variant-only export does not by itself establish a reference genotype at every position; direct calls are required for each conclusion. Most usefully, both `959947` and `200475` list `FT6135 C>T` at
`19389846`, while B580327 has a direct PASS ancestral call there (`GT 0/0`,
34 reference-C reads, one T read, one A read, depth 40) inside the quality BED.
At `FT20271` itself B580327 has a direct PASS derived call (`GT 1/1`, 57
alternate-T reads, depth 60), also inside the quality BED. That shared
off-tree state reinforces their common `DYS19=15`, `DYS456=16`, `DYS712=19`
pattern. Because FTDNA still leaves both kits terminal at FT20271, FT6135 is a
candidate pairing signal, not yet a confirmed child branch.

The three possible FT6135 outcomes are now explicit. Direct derived-T calls in
both James M. kits would support FT6135 at or above Robert `Glasgow-12` under the supplied pedigrees and a simple inheritance model, and establish that pairwise omission was a display/export limitation. Direct ancestral-C calls in both would contradict that simple account and require recurrence, reversion, or a pedigree/tester-mapping review; they would not logically exclude Robert carrying it followed by a reversion. No-call or inadequate coverage
would leave the marker unresolved. “Not displayed” is therefore not treated as
ancestral.

The FTDNA Block Tree screenshot supplied 12 August 2026 confirms the exact
block structure. `R-FT25406` is a four-SNP block (`FT25406`, `FT16394`,
`FT18145`, `FT26594`) beneath `R-FT32941/FTA35069`. `R-FT20271` is a
three-SNP child block (`FT20271`, `FGC5690`, `FT16542`). It contains five basal
samples, including B580327, plus two samples on child `R-FTE32242`. The displayed
private-variant summaries are one basal FT25406 sample averaging 4, five basal
FT20271 samples averaging 3, and two FTE32242 samples averaging 1.

Those averages reconcile closely with the directional lists once multiallelic,
recurrent and off-tree calls are excluded: approximately `5, 1, 5, 1, 4`
tree-counted private variants across the five basal FT20271 tips, `1, 2` across
the two FTE32242 tips, and 4 on the basal FT25406 tip. These rounded summaries are compatible with the orientation analysis but do not independently validate exact counts or genotype direction. It also supplies an important warning:
`200475` and `999763` are Y-111 identical, but `999763` is FTE32242-positive
while `200475` remains basal FT20271. Zero STR distance does not eliminate a
real downstream SNP split.

The B580327 DYS export is complete across all 829 supplied STR/FTY fields. It
provides a durable reference profile but, without another tester's equivalent

The dates and origin reported for `999763` closely identify its earliest named

This is concrete kinship evidence, but neither source identifies the common man
or the cousin degree. It does not prove Robert-12, his attached father James
`Glasgow-1024`, or any member of that tree was Alexander's father or grandfather.

There is also a nearer-looking **candidate** connection to the East Antrim tree.

the same SNP-level proof as the `B580327`/`999763` comparison. Robert-1143 is
therefore a high-value possible older paternal relative, not a proved ancestor,
uncle or numbered cousin.

The inherited **22 May 1852** death date is rejected, not merely uncertain. Its
traceable context is a derivative Australian reconstruction for an Alexander
Glasgow of Windsor, New South Wales, with a different wife and child cluster.
No migration or original death record joins that man to Mary McCaughan,
Lisnagaver or Gortereagh. This older conclusion is superseded by the later

identification of Alexander-951.

The two proposed candidates now separate cleanly. James `Glasgow-1702` is
**excluded**: he was born in 1799, only about twelve years before Alexander, and

contemporary and could be a brother or cousin, but the record does not connect
the two men. Adam is a collateral lead only, not a parent conclusion.

## 24 August 2026: Lisnagaver collateral and land-succession evidence

The 1846 civil marriage of John Glasgow of Lisnagaver supplies a new indirect
route to Alexander's parentage. John was a full-age bachelor and farmer of
Lisnagaver, and his father was explicitly recorded as **John Glasgow,
farmer**. He married Margaret Garvin on 16 November 1846. The certificate is
saved as `sources/john_glasgow_margaret_garvin_marriage_1846.pdf`.

The surviving 1851 census then places three Glasgow households together at
Lisnagaver:

* house 34: John Glasgow, farmer, with Samuel Glasgow explicitly described as
  his brother;
* house 35: Alexander Glasgow, farmer, with Mary and their children;
* house 37: Thomas Glasgow, farmer.

John and Samuel are therefore proved brothers and their father was named John
on the ordinary reading of the marriage record. The census does **not** call
Alexander or Thomas their brothers, so proximity alone cannot extend that
parentage to them.

The land evidence nevertheless makes a relationship worth testing. In the
1834 tithe book Alexander Glasgow held 29 acres and Robert Glasgow held 35
acres at Lisnagaver. In the first valuation, John Glasgow's lot 24 contained
21 acres 2 roods 25 perches and Alexander Glasgow's adjoining lot 25 contained
8 acres 2 roods 10 perches. Together those holdings closely reconstruct the
earlier 29-acre Alexander holding. Thomas Glasgow's lot 26a contained 32 acres
3 roods 15 perches, closely matching Robert's earlier 35-acre holding.

A Registry of Deeds mortgage dated 8 May 1886 confirms that the Thomas branch's
farm remained a distinct block. Mary Ann Glasgow, widow, and Daniel Glasgow,
farmer, both of Lisnagaver, mortgaged a farm of 33 acres 3 roods 36 perches;
Mary Ann was stated to hold it as tenant from year to year. The deed does not
state their relationship, but its acreage materially strengthens the proposed
Robert-to-Thomas farm continuity. An 1881 judgment memorial separately says
that Thomas Glasgow of Lisnagaver, farmer, was then deceased; it supplies no
parentage.

### Parentage implications

| Model | Present assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Older Alexander Glasgow and Rosanna were parents of Alexander, Thomas and Daniel | Still plausible from the descendant account, but not proved by a contemporary record. |
| John Glasgow, farmer, was father of John, Samuel and Alexander | Newly plausible if the adjacent John and Alexander holdings represent a family division; the evidence does not yet prove that John and Alexander were brothers. |
| John Glasgow `Glasgow-1022` was that father | Possible only as a candidate. His reported age and Glenleslie household create chronology and identification problems, and no record connects him to Alexander. |
| The 1834 Alexander tenant was Alexander born about 1811 | Chronologically possible; he was about 23 and married by about 1836. If so, the later acreage division need not represent inheritance from an older Alexander. |

The father name **John** must therefore be retained as a competing hypothesis,
not substituted for the current uncertain Alexander/Rosanna attachment.

### Negative searches and remaining tests

* FamilySearch full-text searches for exact `Rosanna Glasgow` and `Rose
  Glasgow` in County Antrim returned no result.
* PRONI's pre-1858 Name Search returned no Alexander Glasgow in Antrim and no
  Glasgow result at Rasharkin in the indexed wills, 1740 householders, 1766
  census or 1775 petitions.
* PRONI's freeholder database returned no Alexander Glasgow and no Glasgow
  freeholder at Lisnagaver or Rasharkin. These are limited negative findings:
  tenant farmers need not have qualified as freeholders and the Name Search
  covers only selected surviving records.

The decisive targets remain `CR5/32/1/1/1-2`, the Second Portglenone marriage
register, and Killoquin estate rentals or tenant books that can name the
predecessor and successor for the 29-acre holding. The 1818 Kilraughts
Presbyterian subscription list at the Presbyterian Historical Society of
Ireland is an additional locality check, but a subscriber name alone would not
prove parentage.

## 24 August 2026: census sequence, probate and archive-reference audit

### Complete 1851 Lisnagaver household sequence

The surviving 1851 census pages clarify the physical ordering of the Glasgow households:

* house 34: John Glasgow, aged 50, born County Antrim, with wife Margaret, daughter Ann, and his unmarried brother Samuel Glasgow, aged 60, linen weaver, born County Londonderry;
* house 35: Alexander Glasgow, aged 40, with Mary and children Robert, James and Ann;
* house 36: John Watters, blacksmith, and family;
* house 37: Thomas Glasgow, aged 35, with Mary Ann and daughters.

John and Alexander were therefore adjacent householders. Thomas was nearby but not immediately adjacent, being separated from Alexander by the Watters household. No death reported for 1841-1851 in houses 34, 35 or 37 identifies an older Glasgow relative.

John and Samuel are expressly recorded as brothers. Their different reported birth counties suggest movement from County Londonderry to County Antrim between Samuel's approximate 1791 birth and John's approximate 1801 birth. Alexander's County Antrim birth about 1811 is compatible with membership in that family, but the census does not call him their brother.

Source: County Antrim 1851 census, Lisnagaver household returns, reproduced in the surviving census transcription and FamilySearch images.

### Thomas Glasgow's administration

The PRONI Will Calendars record that Thomas Glasgow, farmer of Lisnagaver, died there on 21 June 1870. Letters of administration were granted at Belfast on 7 September 1870 to his widow Mary Anne Glasgow. His effects were under GBP200. Thomas died intestate, and no will image or testamentary statement names parents or siblings.

This record confirms Thomas's residence, occupation, wife and death date but does not advance Alexander's parentage.

Source: [PRONI Will Calendars search](https://apps.proni.gov.uk/WillsCalendar_IE/WillsSearch.aspx), Thomas Glasgow, Lisnagaver, died 21 June 1870, administration 7 September 1870.

### Archive-reference correction

The PRONI eCatalogue shows the D3209 collection as correspondence of Sir Robert Bateson Harvey dating only from 1790 to 1792. D3209/1, dated 19 April 1790, is the sole indexed item in that collection mentioning Lisnagaver. No indexed 1835 Lisnagaver rental was found, so an alleged D3209 1835 tenant book must not be cited or treated as an existing lead without a corrected archival reference.

The older PRONI church-record guide describes the crucial early Kilraughts Reformed Presbyterian material as `CR5/32A/1`, including society lists, baptisms, marriages, deaths and removals from about 1804 to about 1831. The current lookup request uses the modern reference `CR5/32/1/1/1-2`; the older reference should be supplied as an alias in any follow-up.

### Descendant-post reassessment

The 2020 replies to the 2007 Genes Reunited post add Griffith's Valuation observations but no source for the claimed parents Alexander and Rosanna or Rosanna's reported death on 23 October 1822. The post remains a useful descendant tradition, not evidence establishing the parents.

FamilySearch Pedigree Resource File entries for [Alexander](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/2:2:3JBF-PQL) and [Rosanna](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/2:2:3JBF-PQ8), submitted in the “Dowds and Stewart” file on 18 July 2018, were inspected on 12 September 2026. Their only displayed source is an Ancestry member tree. The Rosanna entry gives 28 October 1822, conflicting with the descendant post's 23 October date. The entries establish derivative-tree provenance, not an independent historical source.

### Current parentage assessment

Two competing models remain:

1. **Older Alexander and Rosanna:** supported only by the unsourced descendant tradition concerning Thomas; compatible with the name Alexander and the 1834 Alexander Glasgow tenancy, but not independently proved.
2. **A father named John:** John Glasgow's 1846 marriage directly names his father as John Glasgow, farmer. John and Samuel are proved brothers, and John was Alexander's immediate neighbour. This model applies to Alexander only if an unproved sibling relationship between Alexander and John can be established.

The land pattern remains suggestive rather than dispositive. John and Alexander's later holdings together approximate the earlier Alexander Glasgow holding, while Thomas's holding approximates Robert Glasgow's earlier block. Tenancy subdivision or transfer can explain this without biological inheritance.

The decisive outstanding source remains the early Kilraughts Reformed Presbyterian register and society lists. No new record located in this pass names Alexander's parents.
## 24 August 2026: early marriage, baptisms and Gortereagh residence

### Marriage year established by the 1851 census

Alexander and Mary each reported that they married in **1836**. This is not merely a later tree estimate: it appears in the marriage-year field of their 1851 Lisnagaver census household.

Rasharkin Presbyterian's surviving marriage register begins in 1845, so it cannot supply their 1836 marriage. The reported year nevertheless narrows any search in another congregation or presbytery record.

### Rasharkin Presbyterian baptisms

The published transcription of the original Rasharkin Presbyterian register, PRONI `MIC1P/292`, identifies the family before the 1851 census:

* entry 75: **James Glascaw**, born 17 January 1837 and baptised 9 February 1837, son of Alex and Mary McAughin, residence Gortereagh;
* entry 106, in the 1838 sequence: an unnamed or illegible **Glascow** child of Alex and Molly McAughern, residence Gortereagh; the baptism date, birth date and child's forename were not transcribed;
* the register transcription has no entries for 1840 or 1841, the probable period of son Robert's birth;
* entry 203: **James Glascow**, born 9 October 1843 and baptised 7 January 1844, son of Alex and Molly McAughern, residence Lisnagaver.

The reuse of James for the child born in 1843 makes the first child’s earlier death probable, but does not prove it or supply a date. No burial or death record for the first James has been located. The incomplete 1838 entry is potentially important because its child's forename might preserve a grandparent's name. No free original image was found and `MIC1P/292` is absent from PRONI's public digitisation list, so an onsite inspection or ordered page copy is required; the name must not be guessed from the transcription.

Source: Richard John Torrens, [Rasharkin Presbyterian Church baptisms from 1834](https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/RasharkinP/Baptisms_1834.html), transcription from PRONI `MIC1P/292`.

### Residence sequence and the 1834 Alexander

The evidence now supports this sequence:

* 1834: an Alexander Glasgow held 29 acres at Lisnagaver in the tithe applotment;
* 1836: Alexander and Mary married;
* 1837-1838: their children were baptised from Gortereagh;
* by 1843: they were again at Lisnagaver;
* 1851 and Griffith's Valuation: Alexander was a Lisnagaver farmer.

Alexander-951 was about 23 in 1834 and was old enough to be the tithe occupier before his marriage. There is therefore no need to posit an older same-named father to explain the 1834 entry. In the absence of a distinguishing designation, the tithe entry is at least as plausibly Alexander-951 as `Glasgow-3904`.

This materially weakens the current older-Alexander parent hypothesis. Apart from the unsourced descendant post, no independent record yet establishes that a separate older Alexander existed in this family.

### Gortereagh and the McCaheran family

No Glasgow occupier appears in the 1834 Gortereagh tithe list. It does include **William and James McCaheran**, a local spelling compatible with McAughin/McAughern/McCaughan. Alexander and Mary's residence there immediately after marriage may therefore reflect Mary's family or a subtenancy rather than a Glasgow holding.

The first known son was named James, and a James McCaheran was at Gortereagh in 1834. This is a useful maternal-family hypothesis, not proof that James was Mary's father. The name could commemorate either side of the family, and the illegible 1838 child's name remains unknown.

The 1851 Lisnagaver census also contains a substantial McCaheren cluster, including widow Sarah McCaghrin, aged 70, and several younger households. None is expressly related to Mary Glasgow, so no parent should be assigned from proximity alone.

### Grave-inscription search

The Ulster Historical Foundation gravestone index returned no Glasgow inscription for Kilraughts. A county-wide Antrim search returned only a Glasgow/Shuter inscription at Inver Church of Ireland. This is limited negative evidence because the index does not cover every grave or graveyard and many families had no surviving stone.

### Revised parentage assessment

* **Older Alexander and Rosanna:** now weaker. The descendant tradition remains the only evidence, and the 1834 tithe Alexander may be Alexander-951 himself.
* **Father John:** still possible only through the unproved proposition that Alexander was brother of John and Samuel. John's 1846 marriage directly names his own father as John, but no source extends that statement to Alexander.
* **Another father, possibly James:** the first son's name and the lack of a proved older Alexander make this possible, but naming custom is not adequate evidence.

No parent can yet be assigned confidently. The highest-value unresolved records are an onsite or ordered copy of the original 1838 baptism entry, the early Kilraughts membership/death material (especially the reported Rosanna death), and the 1790 Lisnagaver rental.
## Original 1851 household-image audit (24 August 2026)

The original Lisnagaver return for house 34 records **John Glasgow, 50**,
farmer and head; **Margaret Glasgow, 30**, wife; Ann Glasgow, 3, daughter;
and **Samuel Glasgow, 60, brother**, unmarried linen weaver. The manuscript
therefore confirms that Samuel's age was 60, not the `6` found in one
electronic transcription. John and Margaret both reported that they had
married in 1846, independently matching their civil marriage.

John's census age conflicts directly with the Rasharkin Presbyterian
gravestone transcription, which identifies Margaret Garvin and gives her
husband John an age of 78 at death on 20 June 1895 (implying birth about
1816/17). Because the wife and marriage chronology identify the same couple,
the conflict cannot be resolved by assuming that the records concern two
unrelated John Glasgows. John's age is therefore unsafe as evidence that he
belonged to an older generation than Alexander. The 1846 marriage remains the
direct evidence that John's father was another **John Glasgow, farmer**.

The reverse/death tables were also inspected for Lisnagaver houses 34, 35 and
37. John's and Alexander's returns list no household member who died between
1841 and 1851. Thomas's return lists only his infant daughter Eliza. No parent
or older Glasgow relative appears. This closes a useful census route but does
not distinguish the father-John hypothesis from the descendant tradition naming
older Alexander and Rosanna.

## Gortahar identification and daughter Mary — 24 August 2026

The 14 June 1871 civil marriage of Robert Elder and Mary Glasgow directly names Mary's father as **Alexr. Glasgow, farmer**, and gives Mary's residence as Lisnagaver. Mary was aged 28. This proves an additional daughter of Alexander Glasgow of Lisnagaver and supplies the bridge to the later Gortahar death records.

Because Mary Elder's marriage identifies her father as Alexander of Lisnagaver, this death pair is now a strong match for [[Glasgow-951|Alexander Glasgow]] and Mary McCaughan. The formerly maintained 27 February date was a transcription error, corrected against original entry 234 on 12 September 2026. This fits a move away from Lisnagaver before the Gortahar death, but it does not prove kinship among the three occupiers.

Robert Glasgow of Prescott's 1849 will names brothers Daniel, Samuel, John and Thomas, the children of brother James, and sister Mary. Alexander is omitted. That weighs against making Alexander another proved sibling of Daniel and Thomas; he could instead belong to James's child-group, but no direct record yet establishes that placement.

## Larne family reconstruction and parentage reassessment — 13 September 2026

**The Larne–Kilwaughter family is a priority origin hypothesis for Alexander's paternal line.** The useful model connects an Irish branch with a branch that moved to South Carolina in the 1770s. [Robert of Tennessee](/people/glasgow-2769.html), born in South Carolina about 1790–94, is proposed as [James J's](/people/glasgow-2738.html) father. There are two questions to solve together: which older Larne household supplied the Irish branch, and which man in that branch fathered Alexander. The American household evidence was not derived from the DNA, so their agreement is meaningful. But the proposed links from James J to Robert and Archibald are the very links being tested: they cannot also be counted as an independent proof that the match descends from Archibald.

There are already **two additional historical comparison routes** in the [public project](https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview): a reported Robert-1143 line tested at Y-111, and a reported John-822 line tested at Y-67. They place the Larne and Inishrush questions within the same broader STR research network. Their public broad haplogroup labels do not yet establish either as FT20271-positive. Auditing those descent paths and obtaining resolved SNP states would test the proposed geographic connection more directly than treating all current FT20271 matches as interchangeable. John's omission of Alexander from his family Bible weakens **John as Alexander's father**, not John as a collateral relative.

The proposed older Larne tree must also be tested. James-1509’s imported and live attachment is to [William-1505](/people/glasgow-1505.html), while the reviewed catalogue also carries a competing Hugh hypothesis. A William Glasgow, merchant of Larne, witnessed the 1760 lease; his identification with William-1505 needs checking against the other adult Williams. The derivative 1737 baptism names Hugh. [Hugh-1503's](/people/glasgow-1503.html) monument names a different adult son James, born about 1747/48. Both cannot casually be treated as the same child; identifying both as surviving sons of one Hugh would need explanation. William and Hugh's 1736 joint tenancy establishes association, while the proposed connections from them and Robert-1143 to [Robert-1502](/people/glasgow-1502.html) remain unproved. This is the unsettled joining generation, rather than a reason to disregard the well-supported branches below it.

A second origin model remains useful: the two **Hugh Glasgow householders recorded in Tamlaght O'Crilly in 1740**, followed by a parish Hugh in 1772, could represent local continuity behind Adam. John's [Bible](https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I368&tree=tree1) gives sons Adam–James–John and second daughter Maria, suggesting older Adam and Maria under a conventional naming pattern. That pattern is not fully followed, and applying it to Adam-3902 depends on the unproved brotherhood. These are substantive local alternatives. They prevent treating a good Larne reconstruction as already the only surviving family.

**The research can now be narrower:** test Archibald's missing adult son in estate **647**; test the Larne household through William-3324's surviving **1815 will, D300/1/5/49**, the **1762 marriage** and earlier Belfast records; identify Alexander's immediate family in **CR5/32/1/1/1–2**; and resolve the DNA placement of the specific Larne and Inishrush comparison lines. Each addresses a particular missing connection. The [research priorities](#next-research) explain the outcomes to look for.

The original newspaper alert of **12 September 2025** was inspected directly on 13 September 2026. That makes a registration notice the specific working context, but the full image and governing columns remain unread: MyHeritage now presents a subscription paywall and the archive viewer requires sign-in. The extract cannot yet establish an adult qualification, successful registration or the man’s identity. ### The Irish–Canadian family: what the original deeds establish

The Canadian connection is now supported by an explicit transaction. In the [registered deed of **30 July 1861**, B465](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LWB8-D), **Daniel Glasgow of Kilrea, County Londonderry, watchmaker**, and **Thomas Glasgow, farmer of the County Antrim townland written Lislagaver/Lisnagaver**, sell Samuel Glasgow of Prescott two undivided sixths of the Centre Street property. Their wives are **Margaret** and **Mary Anne**, and the deed expressly derives the interests from Robert Glasgow's will. Place, occupation, wives and the already proved Daniel–Thomas brotherhood identify these men with [Daniel-3896](/people/glasgow-3896.html) and [Thomas-987](/people/glasgow-987.html). This resolves the former uncertainty about whether the will's Irish brothers were the known Kilrea and Lisnagaver men.

Robert's [will of **9 October 1849**, B12](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LWY9-S), registered in 1856, gives one sixth each to brothers **Daniel, Samuel, John and Thomas**, one sixth to sister **Mary**, and one sixth collectively to **the children of brother James**. Combined with B465, this places Daniel and Thomas in the same documented sibling group as [Samuel of Prescott-4010](/people/glasgow-4010.html) and Robert. The original does **not** call James deceased: the earlier death-before-1849 inference is withdrawn. It names no parents.

### James's family: a promising identification with an unresolved title problem

Eliza Jane and Mary are explicitly sisters and daughters of **James the Prescott carpenter**. Identifying that James with Robert's brother is a strong property-based hypothesis, because both daughters convey interests in the same two parcels to Samuel. It is not yet proved by an express kinship or inheritance clause. Each daughter's **one-ninth** interest is also recorded in the official abstract. Together they exceed the **one-sixth** collective share given to James's children in Robert's will. Earlier ownership, additional inherited interests or a record error must be examined before treating these as a simple distribution of that sixth. The fractions cannot responsibly supply a child count.

The **1868 Alexander deed has now been inspected**. [C114, 25 March 1868](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQRC-Y), DGS 008200420, image 120, pp.164–165, identifies **Alexander Glasco of Rochester, Monroe County, New York, express agent, son of James Glasco formerly of Prescott, carpenter**. Through attorney Macneil Clarke he conveys **one eighteenth** for **$166.66** to **Mary Eno Glasgow, widow of the late Samuel Glasgow**. The [accompanying C113 power of attorney](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQRK-B) was made at Rochester on **28 December 1867**; both instruments were registered on **18 August 1868**. The abstract's August date is therefore registration, not the deed's execution date.

This positively identifies a son of James the carpenter alongside Eliza Jane and Mary. It supplies **three named children**, without claiming a complete family or resolving how their title shares arose. It identifies a Rochester express agent, not a Lisnagaver farmer: linking him to Alexander-951 would require an independent wife, age, birthplace or migration bridge. No such bridge was found in this inspection, so this father statement is not assigned to Alexander-951. The earlier Prescott witness named Alexander is a relevant comparison, but the shared name does not prove he was the express agent either. **Mary Eno** is the widow's recorded name; the document does not establish Eno as a maiden surname.
