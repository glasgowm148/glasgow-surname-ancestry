Last updated: 2026-09-17

## New parentage lead for the Glasgow family: Lisnagaver birth, 3 October 1887

The original civil birth return records John Glasgow born at Lisnagaver on 3
October 1887 to James Glasgow, farmer, of Lisnagaver, and Eliza Ann Glasgow,
formerly Lappin. James was the informant and is identified as the father. This
is a new record for the associated Glasgow family and is relevant to
[[Glasgow-951|Alexander Glasgow]] because the Lisnagaver James is a strong
candidate for [[Glasgow-973|James Glasgow]], Alexander's documented son born in
1843.

The record is not proof of that identity: it gives no age or parentage for the
father, and a second James Glasgow has not been excluded. It therefore supports
a possible grandson lead for Alexander, but does not justify adding John as a
child of [[Glasgow-973|James Glasgow]] or changing any structured WikiTree
relationships.

Source: [IrishGenealogy civil birth record](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view?record_id=f66be225e5-4072024),
[original 1887 birth return](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/birth_returns/births_1887/02541/1940589.pdf),
entry 352, Dervock registrar's district, Ballymoney union, County Antrim,
accessed 17 September 2026.


## Current conclusion — Alexander's father, 17 September 2026

**Leading hypothesis: Alexander's father was John Glasgow, farmer — the man
named as father in the 1846 marriage return of [John Glasgow of Lisnagaver
(Glasgow-867)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-867). Status: plausible
(conflict-free indirect case; no record yet names Alexander's own father).**

The reasoning rests on the 14 January 1892 Clinton County probate citation for
[Nancy Glasgow of Plattsburgh (Glasgow-4070)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4070),
read as the legal document it is. A New York probate citation had to be issued
to the testator's heirs and next of kin. Nancy was unmarried, about 68 in 1889
(born c.1820–21), so her next of kin were her surviving brothers and sisters and
the children of deceased brothers and sisters; New York admitted no
representation among collaterals beyond brothers' and sisters' children. The
citation names **no sibling directly** and lists **only children of named
people**: the children of John Glasgow late of "Baladonly", of Alexander
Glasgow formerly of Rasharkin, of Robert Glasgow, of Jane (Glasgow) Dysart, of
Elizabeth (Glasgow) Anderson (the Andersons "of Gortahar"), of Ann McClelland
and of Mary Thompson. That is exactly the shape of a next-of-kin list for a
woman all of whose siblings were dead: John, Alexander, Robert, Jane, Elizabeth,
Ann and Mary are Nancy's deceased siblings, and every cited person is a niece or
nephew. Grand-nephew Robert Wallace Anderson — a legatee — is correctly absent,
which shows the list was drawn to the legal rule, not to who was remembered.

Three consequences follow for Alexander:

1. **Alexander-951 was Nancy's brother, not her father.** His identity with the
   citation's Alexander was already probable (Mary's 1871 and Ann's 1876
   marriages name Alexander of Lisnagaver as father; James, Mary and Ann are
   the three children the citation lists). Nancy's c.1820–21 birth excludes
   daughterhood. Alexander's death at **Gortahar** in 1888 and his widow's death
   there in 1892 place his last years in the townland of his sister Elizabeth's
   Anderson sons. Alexander, Jane (Dysart) and Elizabeth (Anderson) all baptised
   children in the same Rasharkin Presbyterian register in 1834–46.
2. **Alexander was the brother of John-867, whose father is on record.** The
   citation's "James G. Glasgow, Ann and Lizzie, children of John Glasgow late
   of Baladonly" match John-867's children James **Garvin** Glasgow (b. c.1852),
   Ann (b. 1848, later Bellingham, whose 1875 marriage names father John) and
   Elizabeth Ann (d. 1933 aged 84, on the family memorial). "Late of" fits
   John's 24 June 1885 death at Lisnagaver. "Baladonly" is Ballydonnelly, a
   townland in Rasharkin civil parish. John-867's 1846 marriage names his
   father **John Glasgow, farmer**; the 1851 census makes Samuel-2908 (b. c.1790,
   County Londonderry) John's brother. If Alexander and John-867 were full
   brothers, Alexander's father was that John.
3. **The older-Alexander-and-Rosanna hypothesis is now contradicted for
   Alexander-951.** It depended on Alexander being a brother of Thomas-987 and
   Daniel-3896. Thomas died in 1870 and Daniel in 1871, both leaving children
   who were alive in 1892 — Thomas's at Lisnagaver itself. Had they been
   Nancy's brothers, their children were next of kin and had to be cited. They
   are absent while cousins in New Zealand are named. Thomas and Daniel were
   therefore not Nancy's siblings, and Alexander was not their brother. The
   same test excludes [Robert of Glenleslie (Glasgow-1367)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1367),
   Alexander's current uncertain WikiTree father: none of his documented family
   (Margaret's Young children, Hugh, William, Samuel, the Doonbought Robert-1049
   whose daughters were Catherine, Margaret, Maria, Netta and Jane) appears in
   the citation.

Reconstructed sibling group of John Glasgow, farmer (order approximate):
Samuel c.1790 (Londonderry-born, d. 1875, unmarried); Robert c.1797? (the 1834
Lisnagaver tithe co-occupier, Griffith's Gortereghy occupier and possibly the
1868 Ballymoney decedent aged 71 — identity unresolved; daughters Mary Jane and
Ann not yet found); John c.1801/07 (Antrim-born, d. 1885); Jane c.1804
("Lisnagavor" in the Dysart family tradition, m. James Dysart, d. 1868);
Alexander c.1811 (Antrim-born, d. 1888); Elizabeth by c.1816 (m. James
Anderson, Drumack; sons at Gortahar); Ann (m. McClelland); Mary (m. Thompson);
Nancy c.1820–21 (d. Plattsburgh 1891). A thirty-year span implies the father
had two wives; no mother is identified for any of them. The father's 1796
Desertoghill flax-grower placeholder [Glasgow-4090](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4090)
is a record-defined stand-in for the named John, not an identified man: the
family's County Londonderry origin (Samuel's birthplace; John-867's Desertoghill
marriage and Londonderry-born daughter) makes it a reasonable lead only.

What the argument does **not** establish: no record names Alexander's father
directly; the citation cannot distinguish full from half siblings (New York
treated half-blood equally), so a shared mother with a different Glasgow father
remains a formal alternative; the newspaper text is known from an inspected
clipping transcribed on Glasgow-4070, not from a re-inspected image in this
session; and the citation's Robert is unidentified. The decisive record is
Nancy's underlying Clinton County probate petition or heir schedule, which
should state each cited person's relationship. Full analysis: *Nancy Glasgow's
next-of-kin citation and Alexander's father* below.

## Current conclusion

[Alexander Glasgow (Glasgow-951)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-951) is the
project owner’s oldest confirmed direct paternal ancestor. The original 1851
Lisnagaver house-35 return records Alexander and Mary, both 40, and children
Robert (10), James (7) and Ann (4), all born in Antrim. Both spouses report their
first marriage in **1836**. The maintained birth estimate is c.1811, not an exact
date. The complete household and the wider record sequence are now presented
visibly on his person page from `presentation.json`.

Rasharkin entries 75, 106 and 203 document the same Alex/Alexander and Mary/Molly
McAughin/McAughern family at Gortereagh in 1837–38 and Lisnagaver by 1843. The
parents of the unnamed 1838 child are documented, separately from the missing
forename and exact dates. The two James children remain distinct. Neither the
first James’s early death nor the unnamed child’s death has an identified death
or burial record. Robert’s census and marriage ages support c.1840–41; the
imported 6 November 1840 date remains unsupported. Mary’s 1871 marriage and Ann’s
1876 marriage independently name Alexander as father.

**16 May 1888 at Gortahar remains Alexander’s probable death and the
best-supported located match.** Original entry 348 was inspected again on
13 September: Alexander Glasgow, married labourer, 84; Nancy Glasgow, daughter,
present at death, **of Gortahar**; registered 25 May. Nancy’s residence is stated.
The age conflicts with the 1851 age by about seven years; her identity with Ann
McCracken is not proved. Mary Elder’s Lisnagaver father-naming marriage, husband
Robert Elder’s succession to lot 25, the Elder family at Gortahar, and Mary
Glasgow’s 17 February 1892 death there with daughter Mary Elder form the strongest
indirect bridge. The 1892 record does not itself name the deceased husband.

[Alexander of Glenleslie (Glasgow-2385)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2385)
is a real competing labourer namesake, but his c.1816 birth is a worse age fit
and no located Gortahar/Nancy/Elder connection supports reassignment. He is a
weaker alternative, not conclusively excluded. Older Alexander `Glasgow-3904`
is a poor generational fit. No better positively evidenced claimant to the
1888 death was found in this bounded review; no exhaustive new national death
search is claimed. Retain the date with an explicit identity qualification.

The [FTDNA public Glasgow project](https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview)
was checked on 13 September 2026: a tested Alexander-descendant and a tested
[James J Glasgow (Glasgow-2738)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2738)
descendant both have **Big Y-700 / R-FT20271**. The maintained Y-111 comparison
reports genetic distance three. This confirms paternal genetic kinship between
the tested lines; their named common ancestor and exact relationship remain
unresolved. The supplied Block Tree has no exclusive named downstream branch
joining only these two lines. Private variants do not by themselves identify
Alexander’s genotype. The project’s submitted “d.1852” ancestor label is not a
death record. The public Discover tree could not be retrieved in this review.

Alexander’s parents remain unproved by any direct record. Since 17 September 2026 the
leading hypothesis is the father named John Glasgow, farmer, shared with John-867 and
Samuel-2908 (see *Current conclusion — Alexander's father* above). Daniel and Thomas
are documented brothers, but Nancy's 1892 next-of-kin citation omits their children,
so Alexander is now unlikely to be their brother; older Alexander and Rosanna are a
contradicted rather than a working tradition for Alexander-951. The reported
Thomas-descendant autosomal match is a separate unverified lead. Mary (28 in 1871) and Maryan
(17 in 1851) remain possible but unproved identities; the age conflict neither
proves their identity nor justifies an absolute exclusion by itself. Tithe
Plantation acres cannot be equated directly with valuation statute acres to
infer inheritance. Candidate scores remain lead rankings, not probabilities.

The expanded synthesis now follows the 1836 field-book problem, maternal McCaughan context, children’s adult identities and the separate Lisnagaver/Kilrea/Prescott proof chains. The full maintained narrative and three sourced illustrations are in `presentation.json` and `figures/`; `profile-evidence-review-2026-09-13.md` is its readable research copy. The Michigan James remains a strong candidate, not a proved emigrant son. Mary’s surname conflict in the Elder grandchildren is preserved without inventing a Glasgow father.

The paired death ages imply Alexander and Mary were about seven and eight years older than the census chronology. This is compatible with one couple but is not independent identity proof. Ancestral FT6135 results in suitable James M descendant lines would contradict simple inheritance under the supplied pedigrees, not logically exclude an earlier carrier followed by reversion. The outstanding discriminating work is Nancy’s later identity, the later life

#### Michigan Samuel rejected as the 1838 child

The 1838 baptism was already represented by
[Unknown Glasgow (Glasgow-3940)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3940),
attached to Alexander and Mary. It was not a new discovery. The live profile
records death before 1841, evidently as an inference rather than from a death
record.

The inference is reasonable but not conclusive. The 1851 Lisnagaver household
lists Alexander, Mary, Robert, James and Ann, with no additional person marked
resident, absent, or dead since 1841. The National Archives explains that this
census recorded household members absent on census night and family members
who had died since 1841. Nearby returns demonstrate both categories, including
children away as servants or pupils. If the 1838 child belonged to this same
family and was still alive in 1851, a 12- or 13-year-old would ordinarily be
expected either in the household or its absent section; if the child died from
1841 onward, the mortality section should ordinarily record the death.

Samuel Glasgow of Franklin/Blissfield, Michigan, therefore should **not** be
identified with `Glasgow-3940`. His compatible age and Irish birth are only
surname-level coincidence, while the family census is contrary evidence and no
American record names County Antrim or Alexander and Mary. Samuel reported
about 37 years in the United States in 1894, implying immigration around 1857,
so the same-person theory requires the unexplained omission of a living
13-year-old from every section of Alexander's 1851 return. The Pleasant View
transcription's impossible `29 February 1838` date further prevents exact-date
matching. Retain the Michigan records only as a rejected-candidate audit; do
not attach Samuel to this family. Full reasoning is in the focused
[Glasgow-3940 handoff](../Glasgow-3940/findings.md).

The best no-PRONI continuation is now to search **through the sister's
children**, not only under her unknown married name. The National Archives of
Ireland 1841/1851 census-search database has a dedicated `Maiden Surname of
Mother` field. A search for mother `Glasgow`, restricted first to Rasharkin,
Kilconway and adjoining Antrim/Londonderry localities, could expose pension
applicants whose mother was an otherwise missing Glasgow sister and recover the
claimed 1841/1851 household. The live results service timed out on 13 August,
so this search remains pending rather than negative.

Scottish statutory deaths from 1855 are the other high-value route. Women are
indexed under maiden and married surnames, and an original death registration
can name both parents, including the mother's maiden surname. Search women born
in Ireland about 1790-1822 under Glasgow as either surname, then prioritise
Antrim/Rasharkin-linked census households. This route could prove both older
Alexander and Rosanna in one record; the Irish bride audit could only test the
father.

The land pattern slightly weakens any simple claim that Alexander and Thomas
merely divided one father's Lisnagaver farm. In 1834 Alexander held about 29
acres and Robert about 35. The later John-plus-Alexander holdings total about
30 acres, while Thomas held about 33 acres. This is compatible with two older
tenant blocks descending separately, although tenancy transfers need not follow
blood relationships and it does not exclude brotherhood. John belongs to a
documented father-John branch, so the cluster was not one obvious nuclear family.

A new collateral route may identify Thomas's parents without first proving
Alexander's sibling edge. Daniel Neely Glasgow, Thomas's proved son, was a

Thomas and Mary Ann; the identification remains unproved until an original US

conflicts with the state index's 1931 date.

Ancestry searches returned no matching Alexander Glasgow in the 1900, 1910,

The full STR and private-variant extract supports a provisional visual topology
inside FT20271. Alexander's `B580327` and `1002232` share `DYS714=27`. Kits
`959947`, `200475`, `999763` and `478604` share the combination `DYS19=15`,
`DYS456=16` and `DYS712=19`; within that pattern `959947` differs at
`DYS650=20`, `200475` and `999763` are identical across the displayed Y-111
panel, and `478604` differs from `999763` at the first CDY copy. This is an
STR-inferred topology, not confirmed mutation direction. Only FTE32242 is a
confirmed internal child SNP. DYS712 is itself a rapidly mutating Y-STR: a
367-pair father-son study observed 17 mutations and estimated a rate of about
`46.3 x 10^-3` per meiosis. Its shared `19` state is therefore a useful
secondary clue but particularly vulnerable to recurrence or back-mutation; it
must not be treated as a clade-defining event. [Mutation-rate study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12218846/)

The downloaded raw bundle resolves the private block more precisely. Kit
`B580327` has five exported private variants: `12648194 A>G`, `14337402 T>C`,
`14339231 G>C`, `15066888 A>T` and `15973436 A>C`. Its VCF also has a sixth
lineage-specific PASS call at `26534797 C>G` (depth 25). The non-matching screen
labels that position `FT124483`, but FT124483 is the different allele `C>A` at
the same multi-allelic site. It does **not** define a shared branch among the
other matches. All six calls are PASS at depth 25-78 and are absent from every
displayed match.

The 15 numeric match-side positions lie inside the B580327 quality BED and have no derived call in its exported VCF. Coverage plus omission from a variant-only export does not by itself establish a reference genotype at every position; direct calls are required for each conclusion. Most usefully, both `959947` and `200475` list `FT6135 C>T` at
`19389846`, while B580327 has a direct PASS ancestral call there (`GT 0/0`,
34 reference-C reads, one T read, one A read, depth 40) inside the quality BED.
At `FT20271` itself B580327 has a direct PASS derived call (`GT 1/1`, 57
alternate-T reads, depth 60), also inside the quality BED. That shared
off-tree state reinforces their common `DYS19=15`, `DYS456=16`, `DYS712=19`
pattern. Because FTDNA still leaves both kits terminal at FT20271, FT6135 is a
candidate pairing signal, not yet a confirmed child branch.

The three possible FT6135 outcomes are now explicit. Direct derived-T calls in
both James M. kits would support FT6135 at or above Robert `Glasgow-12` under the supplied pedigrees and a simple inheritance model, and establish that pairwise omission was a display/export limitation. Direct ancestral-C calls in both would contradict that simple account and require recurrence, reversion, or a pedigree/tester-mapping review; they would not logically exclude Robert carrying it followed by a reversion. No-call or inadequate coverage
would leave the marker unresolved. “Not displayed” is therefore not treated as
ancestral.

### Newly located McCaughern–Elder locality and probate evidence, 14 September 2026

Two independent public leads extend the McCaughern/Elder network around the Glasgow household, but neither proves Mary's parentage or identifies Nancy.

* The [1943 Belfast Street Directory transcription](https://www.lennonwylie.co.uk/PT194314.htm) lists **Robert McCaughern, farmer, Lisnagaver**. This is a new post-1851 occurrence of the surname in the same townland, roughly ninety years after the surviving 1851 return recorded several McCaheren/McCaughern households there. It supports long local continuity of the surname, not biological connection to Mary Glasgow.
* The contemporary [1910 Ulster Towns Directory](https://www.libraryireland.com/UlsterDirectory1910/Rasharkin.php) independently lists **John Elder, postmaster** at Rasharkin and **Robert M'Caughern, farmer, Lisnagava** among the inhabitants in the neighbourhood. It confirms that the two surnames remained present in the same local network, but it does not establish that this John Elder was Mary's son-in-law's relative.
* The [National Archives of Ireland will-calendar entry](https://www.willcalendars.nationalarchives.ie/search/cwa/details.jsp?id=1639459843) records **John Elder**, who died 30 January 1904 in County Antrim, with William John Harkness as primary executor and **Robert M'Caughern as secondary executor/beneficiary**. This is an inspected archival transcription, stronger than the earlier RootsChat quotation. The entry gives no residence or kinship; the 1904 John cannot be equated with the 1910 postmaster without a separate identity record.
* The [RootsChat discussion quoting a separate PRONI calendar entry](https://www.rootschat.com/forum/index.php?topic=502783.0) reports that **Daniel M'Caughern of Ballydonnelly**, who died 12 January 1893, had a will proved by **John Elder of Lisnagaver** and Samuel M'Caughern. The underlying 1893 calendar or will remains uninspected, so this remains a secondary-source lead. It is now a targeted route for testing whether the Elder and McCaughern households had a documented relationship.

These findings narrow the next maternal-network test: obtain the 1893 probate abstract or will, identify the 1904/1910 Elder and directory McCaughern individuals, and compare them against the 1851 Lisnagaver households. They do not justify assigning a McCaughern parent to Mary or merging any Elder in the probate records with Mary's husband Robert Elder.

The FTE32242 lines are downstream of FT20271 rather than merely similar STR
matches, so they form a genuine paternal cousin branch. On the supplied
pedigrees the best present genealogical label for that branch is **descendants
of James M. Glasgow (`Glasgow-193`)**. These son-line results conditionally place the mutation at or above James M. Localizing it below Robert-12 requires a reliable ancestral result from an independently documented collateral Robert line; the basal display alone does not supply one. Kit `254947`
remains a marker-only candidate. The common FT20271 ancestor is older
than Alexander and Robert-12, but the public results do not say how much older.
Until one of the other testers' paternal pedigrees is independently audited to
the branching generation, describe these men as **paternal genetic relatives**
or **genetic cousin lineages**, not numbered cousins.

Kit `254947` should be kept one evidential tier lower: its ancestor label closely
matches Robert `Glasgow-1143`, but the connection is based on the older Y-STR
cluster rather than a confirmed shared downstream SNP.

James `Glasgow-1025` is another possible collateral, not a located ancestor.
Kit `200475` is displayed at R-FT20271, as is Alexander's result; this does not establish shared negative calls for every downstream branch. It is associated with the
Robert `Glasgow-12` line, despite the supplied result shortening the death year
to 1836 and reporting Scotland. Kits `999763` and `478604` carry downstream
FTE32242 and converge one generation lower at James M. `Glasgow-193`, through
different sons. If those descendant pedigrees are correct and `200475` is
genuinely negative for FTE32242, the SNP marks the James-193 branch rather than
Robert-12 generally. A Big Y upgrade of
Kilwaughter kit `254947` or `B835762`, followed by private-variant comparison,
is the fastest way to learn which tested line is closest to the documented
Kilwaughter men.

This also rules out treating broad surname proximity as enough. The same public
project contains Glasgow/Glasco men on unrelated Y branches. Only the men under
FT20271 and its descendants belong to this demonstrated paternal cluster.

## 24 August 2026: Lisnagaver collateral and land-succession evidence

The 1846 civil marriage of John Glasgow of Lisnagaver supplies a new indirect
route to Alexander's parentage. John was a full-age bachelor and farmer of
Lisnagaver, and his father was explicitly recorded as **John Glasgow,
farmer**. He married Margaret Garvin on 16 November 1846. The certificate is
saved as `sources/john_glasgow_margaret_garvin_marriage_1846.pdf`.

The surviving 1851 census then places three Glasgow households together at
Lisnagaver:

* house 34: John Glasgow, farmer, with Samuel Glasgow explicitly described as
  his brother;
* house 35: Alexander Glasgow, farmer, with Mary and their children;
* house 37: Thomas Glasgow, farmer.

John and Samuel are therefore proved brothers and their father was named John
on the ordinary reading of the marriage record. The census does **not** call
Alexander or Thomas their brothers, so proximity alone cannot extend that
parentage to them.

The land evidence nevertheless makes a relationship worth testing. In the
1834 tithe book Alexander Glasgow held 29 acres and Robert Glasgow held 35
acres at Lisnagaver. In the first valuation, John Glasgow's lot 24 contained
21 acres 2 roods 25 perches and Alexander Glasgow's adjoining lot 25 contained
8 acres 2 roods 10 perches. Together those holdings closely reconstruct the
earlier 29-acre Alexander holding. Thomas Glasgow's lot 26a contained 32 acres
3 roods 15 perches, closely matching Robert's earlier 35-acre holding.

A Registry of Deeds mortgage dated 8 May 1886 confirms that the Thomas branch's
farm remained a distinct block. Mary Ann Glasgow, widow, and Daniel Glasgow,
farmer, both of Lisnagaver, mortgaged a farm of 33 acres 3 roods 36 perches;
Mary Ann was stated to hold it as tenant from year to year. The deed does not
state their relationship, but its acreage materially strengthens the proposed
Robert-to-Thomas farm continuity. An 1881 judgment memorial separately says
that Thomas Glasgow of Lisnagaver, farmer, was then deceased; it supplies no
parentage.

## 24 August 2026: census sequence, probate and archive-reference audit

### Complete 1851 Lisnagaver household sequence

The surviving 1851 census pages clarify the physical ordering of the Glasgow households:

* house 34: John Glasgow, aged 50, born County Antrim, with wife Margaret, daughter Ann, and his unmarried brother Samuel Glasgow, aged 60, linen weaver, born County Londonderry;
* house 35: Alexander Glasgow, aged 40, with Mary and children Robert, James and Ann;

John and Alexander were therefore adjacent householders. Thomas was nearby but not immediately adjacent, being separated from Alexander by the Watters household. No death reported for 1841-1851 in houses 34, 35 or 37 identifies an older Glasgow relative.

John and Samuel are expressly recorded as brothers. Their different reported birth counties suggest movement from County Londonderry to County Antrim between Samuel's approximate 1791 birth and John's approximate 1801 birth. Alexander's County Antrim birth about 1811 is compatible with membership in that family, but the census does not call him their brother.

### Residence sequence and the 1834 Alexander

The evidence now supports this sequence:

* 1834: an Alexander Glasgow held 29 acres at Lisnagaver in the tithe applotment;
* 1836: Alexander and Mary married;
* 1837-1838: their children were baptised from Gortereagh;

Alexander-951 was about 23 in 1834 and was old enough to be the tithe occupier before his marriage. There is therefore no need to posit an older same-named father to explain the 1834 entry. In the absence of a distinguishing designation, the tithe entry is at least as plausibly Alexander-951 as `Glasgow-3904`.

This materially weakens the current older-Alexander parent hypothesis. Apart from the unsourced descendant post, no independent record yet establishes that a separate older Alexander existed in this family.

### Gortereagh and the McCaheran family

No Glasgow occupier appears in the 1834 Gortereagh tithe list. It does include **William and James McCaheran**, a local spelling compatible with McAughin/McAughern/McCaughan. Alexander and Mary's residence there immediately after marriage may therefore reflect Mary's family or a subtenancy rather than a Glasgow holding.

### Revised parentage assessment

* **Older Alexander and Rosanna:** now weaker. The descendant tradition remains the only evidence, and the 1834 tithe Alexander may be Alexander-951 himself.

### Best current hypothesis for Mary McCaughan's parents, 14 September 2026

The local evidence now permits a ranked hypothesis, while still falling short of proof:

1. **Most likely working family:** the McCaheran group at **Gortereghy**, where the 1834 tithe return lists **William and James McCaheran together** (index 7490). a 21-year lease from 1 November. The named principal occupiers were Archibald
McNeill and partners, James McNeill and partners, and John Taylor and partners;
no Glasgow was named. The term ended around November 1789, making PRONI
`D3209/1` (Killoquin rent roll and arrears, 1788-1789) the best surviving
record for testing whether the Glasgow tenancy began at that renewal. The
unnamed "partners" prevent the advertisement from proving that no Glasgow was
already present as a subtenant.

## Larne family reconstruction and parentage reassessment — 13 September 2026

**The Larne–Kilwaughter family is a priority origin hypothesis for Alexander's paternal line.** The useful model connects an Irish branch with a branch that moved to South Carolina in the 1770s. [Robert of Tennessee](/people/glasgow-2769.html), born in South Carolina about 1790–94, is proposed as [James J's](/people/glasgow-2738.html) father. He is not proposed as the father of Alexander, Thomas or Daniel. Their ages therefore supply no objection to that American parentage.

There are two questions to solve together: which older Larne household supplied the Irish branch, and which man in that branch fathered Alexander. [James Glasgow of Larne (Glasgow-1509)](/people/glasgow-1509.html), reportedly born in 1737, is a concrete candidate for the earlier generation. Identifying him with [Glasgow-3905](/people/glasgow-3905.html) means testing whether he was **Adam of Inishrush's father**: 3905 represents that unidentified parental role, rather than a second man established by records. Its placeholder status neither supports nor disqualifies James.

### Where the DNA and the wider catalogue narrow the search

The [DNA evidence](#paternal-dna) is positive family evidence. Alexander's established descendant line and James J's line share **R-FT20271**, are three steps apart at Y-111 and share DYS714=27. Alongside James J's South Carolina origin and the documented Kilwaughter emigrant family, that makes the Larne connection worth pursuing ahead of an arbitrary same-surname pedigree. The American household evidence was not derived from the DNA, so their agreement is meaningful. But the proposed links from James J to Robert and Archibald are the very links being tested: they cannot also be counted as an independent proof that the match descends from Archibald.

There are already **two additional historical comparison routes** in the [public project](https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview): a reported Robert-1143 line tested at Y-111, and a reported John-822 line tested at Y-67. They place the Larne and Inishrush questions within the same broader STR research network. Their public broad haplogroup labels do not yet establish either as FT20271-positive. Auditing those descent paths and obtaining resolved SNP states would test the proposed geographic connection more directly than treating all current FT20271 matches as interchangeable. John's omission of Alexander from his family Bible weakens **John as Alexander's father**, not John as a collateral relative.

The proposed older Larne tree must also be tested. James-1509’s imported and live attachment is to [William-1505](/people/glasgow-1505.html), while the reviewed catalogue also carries a competing Hugh hypothesis. A William Glasgow, merchant of Larne, witnessed the 1760 lease; his identification with William-1505 needs checking against the other adult Williams. The derivative 1737 baptism names Hugh. [Hugh-1503's](/people/glasgow-1503.html) monument names a different adult son James, born about 1747/48. Both cannot casually be treated as the same child; identifying both as surviving sons of one Hugh would need explanation. William and Hugh's 1736 joint tenancy establishes association, while the proposed connections from them and Robert-1143 to [Robert-1502](/people/glasgow-1502.html) remain unproved. This is the unsettled joining generation, rather than a reason to disregard the well-supported branches below it.

A second origin model remains useful: the two **Hugh Glasgow householders recorded in Tamlaght O'Crilly in 1740**, followed by a parish Hugh in 1772, could represent local continuity behind Adam. John's [Bible](https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I368&tree=tree1) gives sons Adam–James–John and second daughter Maria, suggesting older Adam and Maria under a conventional naming pattern. That pattern is not fully followed, and applying it to Adam-3902 depends on the unproved brotherhood. These are substantive local alternatives. They prevent treating a good Larne reconstruction as already the only surviving family.

**The research can now be narrower:** test Archibald's missing adult son in estate **647**; test the Larne household through William-3324's surviving **1815 will, D300/1/5/49**, the **1762 marriage** and earlier Belfast records; identify Alexander's immediate family in **CR5/32/1/1/1–2**; and resolve the DNA placement of the specific Larne and Inishrush comparison lines. Each addresses a particular missing connection. The [research priorities](#next-research) explain the outcomes to look for.

### James's family: a promising identification with an unresolved title problem

Eliza Jane and Mary are explicitly sisters and daughters of **James the Prescott carpenter**. Identifying that James with Robert's brother is a strong property-based hypothesis, because both daughters convey interests in the same two parcels to Samuel. The **1868 Alexander deed has now been inspected**. [C114, 25 March 1868](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQRC-Y), DGS 008200420, image 120, pp.164–165, identifies **Alexander Glasco of Rochester, Monroe County, New York, express agent, son of James Glasco formerly of Prescott, carpenter**. Through attorney Macneil Clarke he conveys **one eighteenth** for **$166.66** to **Mary Eno Glasgow, widow of the late Samuel Glasgow**. The [accompanying C113 power of attorney](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQRK-B) was made at Rochester on **28 December 1867**; both instruments were registered on **18 August 1868**. The abstract's August date is therefore registration, not the deed's execution date.

This positively identifies a son of James the carpenter alongside Eliza Jane and Mary. It supplies **three named children**, without claiming a complete family or resolving how their title shares arose. It identifies a Rochester express agent, not a Lisnagaver farmer: linking him to Alexander-951 would require an independent wife, age, birthplace or migration bridge. No such bridge was found in this inspection, so this father statement is not assigned to Alexander-951. The earlier Prescott witness named Alexander is a relevant comparison, but the shared name does not prove he was the express agent either. **Mary Eno** is the widow's recorded name; the document does not establish Eno as a maiden surname.

### Mary Glasgow Black and the Drumoolish connection

The [original **C298 deed of 15 September 1869**](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQTY-K), registered **1 July 1870**, explicitly identifies **Mary Black, formerly Mary Glasgow, as sister of the late Robert Glasgow of Prescott**. It names her six children and conveys the family’s interest to Samuel’s widow for **$800**. This is another direct Irish bridge: two of the children reside at **Drumoolish, County Londonderry**. The deed gives a record-based family to compare with the wider Bann Valley and Inishrush evidence; it does not name Mary’s parents or make her a daughter of Adam.

### Consequences for Alexander's parentage

The Canadian records now expand **Daniel and Thomas's documented family**, which makes their brothers' and sister's records directly relevant to this enquiry. They still do not name Daniel or Thomas's parents, and they do not establish Alexander-951 as another brother. The Alexander-and-Rosanna tradition remains a hypothesis about Thomas's parents. Alexander's possible place among James's children now has named family and property records to test, but the carpenter's identity with Robert's brother and any connection between his Rochester son and Alexander-951 must each be demonstrated.

The 1861 deed also places wives **Margaret** and **Mary Anne** in the recorded family transaction. It strengthens the identification of the existing Irish couples and gives a later dated occurrence of Daniel's wife before his widowed death in 1871. It does not supply either woman's maiden name or establish the mother of every child. No new Y-DNA result was obtained: documentary linkage to the Prescott brothers expands the family that can be compared with Alexander's paternal line, without proving a genetic match or the joining generation.

The other entries demonstrate why the local surname set must remain separated into household clusters: the same register records McAughern/McAuhern families under John, Hugh and James in Ballydonnelly, Moneyleck and Gortereigh. The James-and-Nancy-Marks entry is especially relevant to the Gortereigh locality occupied by Alexander and Mary Glasgow's early children, but no relationship to Mary McCaughan is stated.

### Two Archibald McCaheren households in 1851, 14 September 2026

The 1851 return appears to contain **two distinct Archibald McCaheren households at Lisnagavor**, a material correction to the earlier working arrangement:

* one household has Archibald, reported deceased in 1851 at age 46, with Elizabeth age 48 and children Margaret 20, Catherine 18, Robert 16, John 14, Elizabeth 10 and Hugh 6;
* another has Archibald age 60, also reported deceased in 1851, with Elizabeth age 58 and children Mary 22, James 20, Elizabeth 18, Sarah 14 and Margaret 10.

The direct Rasharkin baptisms for John (1838), Elisabeth (1842) and Hugh (1845), all children of Archy and Elisabeth Kilpatrick of Lisnagaver, fit the first household's age pattern. They do not fit the second household's listed children as closely. The older household is therefore a plausible context for the proposed [[McAughin-1|Mary Jane (McAughin) Elder]], but that identity and the wife's maiden name remain unproved.

This creates a review flag for [[McAughin-2|McAughin-2]]: its current birth estimate of about 1791 and Elizabeth Kilpatrick relationship may reflect a conflation of the two Archibald households, or one or more inaccurate ages. The evidence is strong enough to separate the households in research notes, but not to justify a live WikiTree profile correction without the original census image and the 1855 Elder marriage record. ### Black branch and possible 1866 witness, 14 September 2026

The original civil return for **Robert Glasgow and Jane Graham, 5 April 1866**
(Churchtown Presbyterian meetinghouse, Tamlaght O’Crilly) names Robert’s father
as Alexander Glasgow, farmer, and records **John Black** as a witness. The
return gives no address or relationship for John Black. His identification with
the John Black in the Canadian deed research is therefore a testable possibility,
not an established fact.

The original **John Black–Martha Savage marriage, 13 March 1862**, identifies
John as a full-age bachelor, farmer of **Drumoolish**, son of **Robert Black,
farmer**; Martha was 20, of Knockaduff, daughter of William Savage. This is a
strong identity anchor for the John and Martha in the deed because the names,
townland and farming context agree. The return is page 178, entry 136, image
5491449.

The Churchtown baptism transcription records **Elizabeth Black**, 31 July
1845, daughter of **Robert Black and Mary Glasgow**, residence Drumoolish
(page 7, entry 116; cited register PRONI MIC 1P/347A/1). This names a specific
Black–Glasgow parental couple and supports the working identification of the
deed family; the original baptism image remains to be checked, and 31 July is a
baptism date, not an established birth date. The same transcription records
“Thomson Black,” 10 January 1843, child of Robert and a mother transcribed as
“Gear” and marked illegible. It may be relevant to the later Thomasina
Montgomery, but the reading must not be normalised without the original image.

The 1831 Tamlaght O’Crilly census transcription lists **Robert Black** at
Drumolish, house 57, with two males and one female, all three Presbyterians
(row 643). It is a candidate earlier household only: the occupants are unnamed
and several other Black households occur in the same townland. A December 1862
newspaper report of twin boys born to John Black’s wife at her father William
Savage’s Knockaduff home, followed by Churchtown baptisms on 5 November 1863
for Robert and William Black, children of John and Martha Savage of Drumoolish,
supports the couple’s continuity. The newspaper says the twins were born 20
December, while the baptism transcription says they were nine months old; the
date discrepancy is retained rather than harmonised.

**Assessment.** The 1866 witness is an established record fact; identifying him
as John Black, son of Robert Black and Mary Glasgow, is plausible from the
Drumoolish, congregation and chronology evidence but remains unproved. Even a
confirmed witness identity would establish contact with Alexander’s son, not
Alexander’s brotherhood or shared parentage with Mary Glasgow. The new route is
to obtain the Elizabeth and 1843 baptism images, investigate Robert Black and
Mary Glasgow’s marriage and household, and compare further Churchtown witness
occurrences. It does not justify adding older Alexander and Rosanna as
Alexander’s parents.

### Record-level identification of the Dysart couple, 14 September 2026

The underlying FamilySearch civil-registration entry can now be opened directly rather than relying on the search-result summary. The associated persons name **Jas McCaughan** as John's father and **Jas Dysart** as Sarah's father. It also shows that “next of kin” was used in this court record as a specific legal family category, not merely as a broad circle of acquaintances.

This remains negative evidence about the Walworth connection, not a parentage record for Nancy. It does not explain why Nancy called Margaret Dysart her niece or identify the Glasgow/Anderson link; those require Nancy's own underlying petition or another record naming her relatives.

Source: [FamilySearch, Clinton County Guardianship and Probate Records 1856–1863](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:33SQ-GYC4-6F3?view=fullText&keywords=Hiram%20Walworth&searchForm=simple&lang=en), image 78, pp.119–120, Hiram Walworth probate petition and order; accessed 16 September 2026. The FamilySearch transcript is OCR-derived; the named-heir wording is visible in the record transcript and should be checked against the image before quoting as a legal transcription.

### Nancy was a documented creditor of her named nephew Robert Anderson, 16 September 2026

The Clinton County real-estate/probate volume supplies a much stronger explanation for Nancy's appearance in an Anderson legal proceeding. In June 1885, Frank Shields, executor of **Robert Anderson of Beekmantown**, petitioned for authority to sell or mortgage estate property to pay debts. The citation required **Nancy Glasgow** and several other people to appear; the record then states that Nancy appeared **through Hiram Walworth as her attorney**. The decree lists a valid claim styled **“Nancy Glasgon, Note $100”** among the debts to be paid from Robert Anderson's real property. The OCR is imperfect, but the repeated identity, attorney relationship and claim amount are clear enough to establish a documented financial/legal connection.

This is the first concrete record tying Nancy directly to **Robert Anderson**, the man she later called her nephew in her 1889 will. It also explains why Nancy was named in that 1885 citation: she was a claimant/creditor in the estate proceeding, not necessarily one of Robert's heirs or next of kin. The record does not by itself prove that “nephew” was biological, nor does it identify Nancy's parents; however, it materially strengthens the Anderson connection and separates it from the Walworth question. Hiram's role as Nancy's attorney in 1885 also predates his role as executor of her will.

Source: [FamilySearch, Clinton County Real Estate and Sale Records 1849–1886](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:33SQ-GYCZ-WY5?view=fullText&keywords=Nancy%20Glasgow%2CEstate%20of%2Cof&searchForm=simple&lang=en), images 609–611, pp.516–521, Robert Anderson estate petition, citation and decree; accessed 16 September 2026. Visual inspection of image 611 confirms the handwritten list includes Nancy Glasgow's $100 note among the claims allowed against Robert Anderson's estate; the OCR's “Glasgon” is a spelling error.

### Robert Anderson's proved will identifies his household, 17 September 2026

The original Clinton County will-book entry for **Robert Anderson of Beekmantown** was inspected separately from the 1885 land-sale proceeding. The will is dated **11 August 1883** and was admitted to probate on **26 November 1883**. It leaves Robert's real and personal property to his wife **Dinah Anderson** and appoints **Frank Shields** executor. The probate proofs identify James J. Brown and Thomas Weir as witnesses and confirm that the will was admitted as valid for both real and personal property.

This is a new primary record confirming the identity and immediate household of the Robert Anderson whose estate later listed Nancy Glasgow's $100 note. It is consistent with the already documented Anderson descent through [[Glasgow-4071|Elizabeth (Glasgow) Anderson]] and her son Robert, but it does not itself prove Nancy's biological relationship to Elizabeth or to Alexander Glasgow. The will names no children or parents; the associated 1885 sale record's reference to a special guardian for an infant Robert Anderson remains the better target for testing Robert's children.

Source: [FamilySearch, Clinton County Will Books 1883–1887](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:33SQ-GYCC-1RW?view=fullText&keywords=Frank+Shields%2CRobert+Anderson&searchForm=simple&lang=en), images 97–98, pp.127–129, Robert Anderson will and probate proofs; accessed 17 September 2026. ### 1860–1880 Walworth household continuity: Nancy Glasgow/Glascoe as a long-term Plattsburgh servant

The authenticated FamilySearch search located two additional household records that materially strengthen Nancy Glasgow's identity and explain the Walworth connection. In the **1860 U.S. census**, **Nancy Glascoe**, female, age **30**, estimated birth year **1830**, born in **Ireland**, was enumerated in The Village of Plattsburgh, Clinton County, in the same household as **Hiram Walworth**, **Cornelia L. Walworth**, **Delia A. Walworth**, **Graham Walworth**, Jane Dundas and James Colligan (record **MC7C-HHK**). The indexed citation identifies the household entry as that of Hiram and Cornelia Walworth and gives page 64.

In the **1880 U.S. census**, **Nancy Glasgow**, female, age **65**, estimated birth year **1815**, born in **Ireland**, was in Plattsburgh in a Walworth household with **Delia A. Walworth**, age 70, and **Alice C. Walworth**, age 28 (record **MZZF-HDJ**). The record describes Nancy as **single**, occupation **servant**, and relationship to head **Other**; it also records both parents' birthplaces as Ireland. This is not a kinship designation. Together with the 1870 record (Nancy Glascow, age 50, Irish-born, in a Plattsburgh household with Delia Walworth), the three censuses show a persistent 1860–1880 association across spelling variants, though the reported ages vary substantially.

Assessment: the 1860 and 1880 records make the identification of the Plattsburgh Nancy substantially stronger and provide the first direct evidence that her Walworth relationship may have been domestic/employment-based rather than a stated family relationship. They also explain why Hiram Walworth appears as executor in Nancy's will. They do **not** prove that Nancy was related to Alexander-951 or to Mary and Ann; the 1880 census explicitly says only “Other,” not sister, cousin or niece. The next decisive target remains the original Clinton County probate file, especially any petition or kinship schedule, with local directories or Walworth estate papers as secondary targets.
