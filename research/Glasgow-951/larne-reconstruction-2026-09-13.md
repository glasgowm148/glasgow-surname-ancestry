# The Larne connection: a working family reconstruction

**The Larne–Kilwaughter family is a priority origin hypothesis for Alexander's paternal line.** The useful model connects an Irish branch with a branch that moved to South Carolina in the 1770s. [Robert of Tennessee](/people/glasgow-2769.html), born in South Carolina about 1790–94, is proposed as [James J's](/people/glasgow-2738.html) father. He is not proposed as the father of Alexander, Thomas or Daniel. Their ages therefore supply no objection to that American parentage.

There are two questions to solve together: which older Larne household supplied the Irish branch, and which man in that branch fathered Alexander. [James Glasgow of Larne (Glasgow-1509)](/people/glasgow-1509.html), reportedly born in 1737, is a concrete candidate for the earlier generation. Identifying him with [Glasgow-3905](/people/glasgow-3905.html) means testing whether he was **Adam of Inishrush's father**: 3905 represents that unidentified parental role, rather than a second man established by records. Its placeholder status neither supports nor disqualifies James.

### The American branch has a coherent chronology

| Connection | Evidence and present assessment |
| --- | --- |
| [Robert of Kilwaughter (1143)](/people/glasgow-1143.html) → [James (1144)](/people/glasgow-1144.html), born 1731 | Strongly supported by the congregation-register transcription and the sworn Wilson inheritance evidence. The [1738 lease](https://www.irishdeedsindex.net/mem.php?memorial=74015) independently associates Robert, wife Rachel and James junior; it does not itself call James a son. |
| James → [Archibald (2304)](/people/glasgow-2304.html), about 1769–1818 | James's [1775 will](https://web.archive.org/web/20101124184247id_/http://www.lifesweaving.com/Glasgow/Paper_JGlasgowWill.html) and the [1817 equity complaint](https://web.archive.org/web/20101124184247id_/http://www.lifesweaving.com/Glasgow/Paper_Complaint.html) name Archibald among his children. This is a record-supported connection, not merely a tree attachment. |
| Archibald → Robert of Tennessee (2769) | A substantive missing-son hypothesis. The [1800 Newberry household](https://genealogytrails.com/scar/newberry/1800nbycensusG.htm) contains one boy under ten. Archibald's monument-named son James was born about 1806 and cannot be that boy. Robert's early-1790s birth fits. |
| Robert → James J (2738), born about 1809/10 | Both report South Carolina origins; Robert's [1820 household](https://archive.org/details/populationsc18200124unit/page/n21/mode/1up) has a boy aged 10–15, and James later has a documented Giles County land trail. No located record names Robert as James's father. |

The [Kilwaughter chronology](https://web.archive.org/web/20101124184247id_/http://lifesweaving.com/Glasgow/Kilwaughter/Timeline.html) associates James's family with the 1772 Irish petition; his 1775 will places him in South Carolina. These fit the reported 1772/73 migration without proving an exact voyage. If Robert was James J's father, their South Carolina births followed by Robert's [1814 Tennessee militia service](https://scout.lib.utk.edu/repositories/2/resources/4000) fit a later move within America. The Irish collateral branch can have remained in Ireland throughout.

The age fit is stronger when calculated properly. Robert's 1820 and 1840 brackets, combined with Archibald's under-ten boy in 1800, allow **5 August 1790–7 August 1794**, if all three returns concern him and their age bands are accurate. His 1850 age of sixty requires a modest discrepancy; it favours the older end of the working range. That older end also gives a more comfortable fatherhood age for James J around 1809/10. The woman aged 16–25 in Archibald's 1800 household could have been about sixteen to twenty at an early-1790s birth if near the older end of her range. Her age is a constraint to check, not a general exclusion of the family. [Census dates: 1800](https://www.archives.gov/research/census/1800), [1820](https://www.archives.gov/research/census/1820), [1840](https://www.archives.gov/research/census/1840).

This makes Archibald a worthwhile leading named candidate for **Robert's** father. It does not make Robert the uniquely established father of **James J**. [James Glasgow (2439)](/people/glasgow-2439.html), born about 1787, and Sarah form another age-compatible Newberry household. Its 1831 estate should be compared with Archibald's. Robert's Bradshaw Creek associates and James J's district-six land trail still need joining. The [1848 deed](https://tngenweb.org/giles/deed-noah-hancock-to-james-glasgow/) proves James's residence then; its recital of an 1845 title bond proves the earlier transaction, not necessarily residence in 1845.

### James of Larne fits the missing older generation

James was a **Belfast ropemaker** when he took the [1760 Larne ropewalk lease](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-S7XZ-8). That gives the hypothesis a real adult and a property trail near Adam's estimated birth, rather than a name supplied only by a pedigree. The [1773 affidavit](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-M72W-M) says he was over thirty. It supports the older James identity, although the especially good generational fit below depends on his reported 1737 birth.

| Person to compare with James's household | James's approximate age at the reported birth | Evidence for the proposed connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Adam of Inishrush (3902)](/people/glasgow-3902.html), about 1758 | 21 | A normal father–son interval. Adam's [monument](https://www.oneirishrover.com/tamlaght-graveyard/) names a son James, providing modest naming support. The full child order is unknown; James cannot be called Adam's first son. No record yet joins Adam to the ropemaker. |
| [William (3324)](/people/glasgow-3324.html), about 1763 | 26 | The strongest existing child hypothesis: William's daughter **Jane Shutter** supplies distinctive support for descent from James and Janet Shutter. William's own household is established by the [published monument](https://archive.org/stream/ulsterjournalofa06ulstiala/ulsterjournalofa06ulstiala_djvu.txt). |
| [John of Inishrush, later Ohio (822)](/people/glasgow-822.html), 1767 | 30 | John and Adam have a substantial Inishrush family association and may be brothers. Making James their common father is an additional hypothesis; John's own naming sequence also supports an alternative older Adam. |
| [Older Alexander (3904)](/people/glasgow-3904.html), estimated about 1770 | 33 | A coherent possible brother of Adam. His own existence and family are reconstructed principally from the later Thomas-parent tradition; no record names James as his father. |
| [Younger James of Larne (3986)](/people/glasgow-3986.html), about 1773 | 36 | An already identified possible son, associated with the 1795 merchant and 1797 junior records. He supplies an alternative route to Alexander's generation without inventing another unnamed James. |
| [Robert of Glenleslie (1367)](/people/glasgow-1367.html), about 1779 | 42 | Fits chronologically as another possible child in the broader reconstruction. No record makes him James's son, and he is not securely identified with the Lisnagaver tithe occupier. |

**The 1762 marriage is the main household condition.** It postdates Adam's estimated birth by about four years. If James fathered Adam, Adam could belong to an earlier relationship, a premarital birth, or a chronology needing correction. None is established. Janet Shutter must not acquire maternity merely because she was James's later reported wife. The original marriage and earlier Belfast records could distinguish these possibilities.

Nor does James have to move to Inishrush himself. His [1768 sale](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-DDXW) transferred the ropewalk interest, but [Holmes's 1799 will](https://holmes-history.com/antrim/) still names a James Glasgow, roper, in Larne. An adult son could have moved independently. The ropemaker, the younger [tanner James (3991)](/people/glasgow-3991.html), and the younger merchant must remain distinct record groups; selling a workshop is not evidence of a family migration by itself.

### Two routes to Alexander deserve explicit comparison

| Working reconstruction | Why it fits | What would distinguish it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **James-1509 → older Alexander-3904 → Alexander-951**, with Adam-3902 a possible brother of the older Alexander | It gives ordinary generation intervals and accommodates the [tradition naming Alexander and Rosanna as Thomas's parents](https://www.genesreunited.co.uk/boards/board/general_topics/thread/465937), if younger Alexander belonged to that sibling family. It is much cleaner chronologically than making older Alexander a son of Adam and Rose. | A record linking older Alexander to the Larne family, and an independent record or family register joining younger Alexander to Thomas and Daniel. The tradition supplies a name, not the missing Larne connection. |
| **James-1509 → younger James-3986 → Alexander-951**, with Adam in a collateral branch | The younger James's estimated birth about 1773 gives an ordinary fatherhood age around 1804–11. Alexander's use of James for his sons is compatible with this route. It uses a known candidate of the appropriate generation. | Larne succession records naming James's children, or a Lisnagaver record naming Alexander's father James. If Thomas and Daniel were also made his sons, that would be an alternative to the testimony naming their father Alexander. They need not be assigned to him merely to test Alexander's own parentage. |

The first route better preserves the existing Thomas-family testimony; the second tests the documented merchant and junior occurrences provisionally identified with younger James-3986. Neither has a father statement for Alexander. These are **competing explanations worth testing**, not equal-probability trees. The local Robert and father-John possibilities remain in the [immediate-parent comparison](#parentage-context); a Larne origin could ultimately run through either of them.

The earlier Adam → older Alexander route compresses the generations: Adam born about 1758 would be twelve at the older Alexander's estimated 1770 birth, while Rose born about 1767 would be three. Moving the older Alexander's birth later and proposing an earlier mother can make Adam's paternity biologically possible, but adds conditions unsupported by records. A brother or cousin relationship is the more natural version to investigate. This chronological preference does not itself prove their kinship.

### Where the DNA and the wider catalogue narrow the search

The [DNA evidence](#paternal-dna) is positive family evidence. Alexander's established descendant line and James J's line share **R-FT20271**, are three steps apart at Y-111 and share DYS714=27. Alongside James J's South Carolina origin and the documented Kilwaughter emigrant family, that makes the Larne connection worth pursuing ahead of an arbitrary same-surname pedigree. The American household evidence was not derived from the DNA, so their agreement is meaningful. But the proposed links from James J to Robert and Archibald are the very links being tested: they cannot also be counted as an independent proof that the match descends from Archibald.

There are already **two additional historical comparison routes** in the [public project](https://www.familytreedna.com/public/Glasgow?iframe=ydna-results-overview): a reported Robert-1143 line tested at Y-111, and a reported John-822 line tested at Y-67. They place the Larne and Inishrush questions within the same broader STR research network. Their public broad haplogroup labels do not yet establish either as FT20271-positive. Auditing those descent paths and obtaining resolved SNP states would test the proposed geographic connection more directly than treating all current FT20271 matches as interchangeable. John's omission of Alexander from his family Bible weakens **John as Alexander's father**, not John as a collateral relative.

The proposed older Larne tree must also be tested. James-1509’s imported and live attachment is to [William-1505](/people/glasgow-1505.html), while the reviewed catalogue also carries a competing Hugh hypothesis. A William Glasgow, merchant of Larne, witnessed the 1760 lease; his identification with William-1505 needs checking against the other adult Williams. The derivative 1737 baptism names Hugh. [Hugh-1503's](/people/glasgow-1503.html) monument names a different adult son James, born about 1747/48. Both cannot casually be treated as the same child; identifying both as surviving sons of one Hugh would need explanation. William and Hugh's 1736 joint tenancy establishes association, while the proposed connections from them and Robert-1143 to [Robert-1502](/people/glasgow-1502.html) remain unproved. This is the unsettled joining generation, rather than a reason to disregard the well-supported branches below it.

A second origin model remains useful: the two **Hugh Glasgow householders recorded in Tamlaght O'Crilly in 1740**, followed by a parish Hugh in 1772, could represent local continuity behind Adam. John's [Bible](https://www.hiltner.com/mckeown/getperson.php?personID=I368&tree=tree1) gives sons Adam–James–John and second daughter Maria, suggesting older Adam and Maria under a conventional naming pattern. That pattern is not fully followed, and applying it to Adam-3902 depends on the unproved brotherhood. These are substantive local alternatives. They prevent treating a good Larne reconstruction as already the only surviving family.

**The research can now be narrower:** test Archibald's missing adult son in estate **647**; test the Larne household through William-3324's surviving **1815 will, D300/1/5/49**, the **1762 marriage** and earlier Belfast records; identify Alexander's immediate family in **CR5/32/1/1/1–2**; and resolve the DNA placement of the specific Larne and Inishrush comparison lines. Each addresses a particular missing connection. The [research priorities](#next-research) explain the outcomes to look for.
