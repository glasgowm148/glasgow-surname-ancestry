# Alexander Glasgow: weighing the parent hypotheses

Reviewed 13 September 2026 against the maintained record readings, candidate audit, original-correspondence assessment and DNA matrix. This is a new comparison of existing evidence, not a newly discovered parent record. Direct retrieval of the Robert death-index page and Genes Reunited thread failed in this pass; their earlier recorded inspections are reused.

## Current conclusion

The useful shortlist is three **local family explanations**, rather than ten equally promising named fathers. Older Alexander and Rosanna are the first named-couple hypothesis to investigate because a descendant account actually names them as Thomas's parents. That specificity does not establish that they are more likely than all unnamed alternatives to be Alexander-951's parents. Their present status remains **possible**, not probable.

The strongest combined result is Alexander's association with the Lisnagaver–Gortereagh family network and his independently established FT20271 paternal connection. The local records do not yet distinguish brotherhood from cousinhood; the DNA comparisons do not identify which local family supplied his father. An uncatalogued father remains an open possibility.

## Underweighted evidence and incompatible combinations

**The 1868 Robert death could weaken the father hypothesis.** The maintained official index records age 71 in early 1868, implying birth about 1796–97. If this decedent proved to be the 1834 occupier, he would have been about fourteen or fifteen at Alexander's census-implied c.1811 birth, or about seven or eight at the probable death's c.1804 birth estimate. That is an adverse paternal-age test, not support from an older generation. It does not establish the unknown occupier's age and cannot presently exclude him. [Death-index reference](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view/?record_id=cidenc-957324).

**The 1834 Alexander does not require an older father of the same name.** Alexander-951 could already have held land at about twenty-three, or about thirty on the earlier birth estimate. His later Gortereagh residence and the 1836 house-section omission do not require inherited land or co-residence with Robert. The house list is not a complete named-occupier land schedule. [Field book](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-994J-9HD6).

**The first son's name does not select Larne James.** Reuse of James makes that name meaningful within the immediate household. It does not distinguish paternal from maternal commemoration or identify a particular James. Mary/Molly's own family is unresolved, and James and William McCaheran occur in the adjoining locality. Neither those names nor the incomplete surviving child sequence establishes a grandfather. [Baptism transcription](https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/RasharkinP/Baptisms_1834.html).

**Repeated tradition is not repeated confirmation.** The 2007 post names Thomas's parents; the public 2020 reply copies it; the 2018 pedigree file cites a member tree. The private 2020 correspondent believed the parent names and separately asked whether Alexander was Thomas's brother. Her source is unknown: neither independence nor copying can be presumed. The clockmaker tradition preserves useful family context but misplaces Daniel's generation if it makes Thomas's documented brother his uncle. It cannot establish Rosanna's surname Shaw. [Tradition and source audit](../Glasgow-3904/older_pair_source_search_2026-09-13.md).

The earlier combined-father objection is withdrawn because it tested a hypothesis that was not proposed. Robert-2769 is proposed only as James J’s father, not the father of Alexander, Thomas or Daniel. Archibald’s missing boy, Robert’s age and South Carolina origin, and the later Tennessee household provide a coherent positive case for investigation. The first two links in Robert-1143 → James-1144 → Archibald-2304 → Robert-2769 → James J are record-supported; the last two remain working hypotheses. The precise Irish joining generation is still open.

**DNA cannot yet rank these named fathers.** No independently documented tested descent from a particular local father supplies the needed comparison. The shared FT20271, STR distance and matching STR features establish and describe the tested paternal connection; they do not provide independent parent identifications. The actual American hypothesis requires no transatlantic return and is compatible with an Irish collateral branch. It supplies no exact joining generation by itself. The two American parent links remain uncertain. [Dated DNA matrix](/people/glasgow-951.html#paternal-dna).

## What should happen next

For parentage, prioritise the **family-group entries in PRONI CR5/32/1/1/1–2**, spanning the relevant childhood years, and the actual document or informant behind the Thomas parent tradition. The existing PRONI enquiry is pending; its contents have not been searched to a negative result. The extraction should capture every Glasgow variant, named family members, dated admissions/removals and neighbouring entries, rather than a surname-only list.

In parallel, reconstruct the unidentified Robert's household and the record-defined father John's family without merging them into convenient profiles. Check their ages against both c.1811 and c.1804 birth scenarios. Existing consented Thomas-descendant autosomal evidence becomes discriminating only with a checked descent, amounts/segments and alternative common-ancestor analysis; Thomas has no confirmed Y-DNA comparison in the supplied evidence. A reliable paternal-line DNA disagreement could reject a same-father model; agreement in the broad cluster would still allow cousins.

Rosanna's maternity is a separate hypothesis from older Alexander's paternity. Even establishing Alexander and Thomas as paternal half-brothers would not transfer Thomas's mother. No present evidence supports selecting Catherine or inferring Mary's McCaughan relations as Alexander's own maternal family.
