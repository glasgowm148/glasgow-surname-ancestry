### The reported 1829 occurrence: an age and identity test

The original newspaper alert of **12 September 2025** was inspected directly on 13 September 2026. Its [MyHeritage result, 12021-100828509](https://www.myheritage.com/research/record-12021-100828509-/alexander-glasgow-in-united-kingdom-names-stories-in-newspapers-from-oldnewscom), does quote **“Alex. Glasgow, Lisnagaver”** from the **Belfast News-Letter, 5 June 1829**. The [British Newspaper Archive issue index](https://www.britishnewspaperarchive.com/image-viewer?issue=BL%2F0000038%2F18290605&page=0004&article=015) independently locates the Lisnagaver entry in **page 4, article 015**. Its OCR mangles Glasgow, explaining why a surname search missed the article while a townland search found it. These are two derivative extracts of one newspaper occurrence, not two independent historical records.

The archive’s damaged heading excerpt appears to concern persons applying to register, with special sessions at Ballymoney, under **County of Antrim**. That makes a registration notice the specific working context, but the full image and governing columns remain unread: MyHeritage now presents a subscription paywall and the archive viewer requires sign-in. The extract cannot yet establish an adult qualification, successful registration or the man’s identity. The earlier claim that even the alert was uninspected is superseded; the historical interpretation remains open.

The important distinction is between **an older birth date for the same man** and **a second, older man**. Even if the notice proves adult status, it would not automatically make the subject Alexander's father. Conversely, identifying the subject from the 1888 age and then using him to validate that same death identification would be circular. No birth estimate or parent ranking changes on the extract alone.

If the subject is Alexander-951, the occurrence would support a premarital Lisnagaver association before the couple's Gortereagh baptisms of 1837–38 and their documented Lisnagaver residence by 1843. That would make a temporary residence near Mary's possible family a more focused hypothesis; it would not prove a move, tenancy or inheritance.

The supplied extract also lists Robert Elder of **Gortaragh**, Arthur O'Neil of Tehorney, Henry and James Wallace of Culmore, James Tomb of Ballymaconly and James Elder of Drumcon. Their combined names and literal place spellings are useful for locating the correct column despite imperfect OCR. They must not yet be treated as neighbours, fellow freeholders or relatives. The 1829 Robert Elder cannot comfortably be the later son-in-law whose [1871 marriage](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1871/11341/8162804.pdf) gives age forty and father Nathaniel; the corresponding [1894 death](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/deaths_returns/deaths_1894/05952/4691448.pdf) gives age sixty-four. Those dates place that Robert's birth around 1830–31. A different Elder generation is therefore the appropriate comparison, and **Gortaragh** is left unnormalised pending the page.

The next test is the full image of page 4 and the heading governing this entry, with adjoining columns. The earlier PRONI freeholder-search negative does not dispose of this newspaper occurrence. The surname-search failure is now demonstrably an OCR limitation: the exact townland retrieves article 015. Neither search outcomes nor damaged heading text determine the qualification or age of the named man.

### The Irish–Canadian family: what the original deeds establish

The Canadian connection is now supported by an explicit transaction. In the [registered deed of **30 July 1861**, B465](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LWB8-D), **Daniel Glasgow of Kilrea, County Londonderry, watchmaker**, and **Thomas Glasgow, farmer of the County Antrim townland written Lislagaver/Lisnagaver**, sell Samuel Glasgow of Prescott two undivided sixths of the Centre Street property. Their wives are **Margaret** and **Mary Anne**, and the deed expressly derives the interests from Robert Glasgow's will. Place, occupation, wives and the already proved Daniel–Thomas brotherhood identify these men with [Daniel-3896](/people/glasgow-3896.html) and [Thomas-987](/people/glasgow-987.html). This resolves the former uncertainty about whether the will's Irish brothers were the known Kilrea and Lisnagaver men.

Robert's [will of **9 October 1849**, B12](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LWY9-S), registered in 1856, gives one sixth each to brothers **Daniel, Samuel, John and Thomas**, one sixth to sister **Mary**, and one sixth collectively to **the children of brother James**. Combined with B465, this places Daniel and Thomas in the same documented sibling group as [Samuel of Prescott-4010](/people/glasgow-4010.html) and Robert. The original does **not** call James deceased: the earlier death-before-1849 inference is withdrawn. It names no parents.

### James's family: a promising identification with an unresolved title problem

Eliza Jane and Mary are explicitly sisters and daughters of **James the Prescott carpenter**. Identifying that James with Robert's brother is a strong property-based hypothesis, because both daughters convey interests in the same two parcels to Samuel. It is not yet proved by an express kinship or inheritance clause. Each daughter's **one-ninth** interest is also recorded in the official abstract. Together they exceed the **one-sixth** collective share given to James's children in Robert's will. Earlier ownership, additional inherited interests or a record error must be examined before treating these as a simple distribution of that sixth. The fractions cannot responsibly supply a child count.

The **1868 Alexander deed has now been inspected**. [C114, 25 March 1868](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQRC-Y), DGS 008200420, image 120, pp.164–165, identifies **Alexander Glasco of Rochester, Monroe County, New York, express agent, son of James Glasco formerly of Prescott, carpenter**. Through attorney Macneil Clarke he conveys **one eighteenth** for **$166.66** to **Mary Eno Glasgow, widow of the late Samuel Glasgow**. The [accompanying C113 power of attorney](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQRK-B) was made at Rochester on **28 December 1867**; both instruments were registered on **18 August 1868**. The abstract's August date is therefore registration, not the deed's execution date.

This positively identifies a son of James the carpenter alongside Eliza Jane and Mary. It supplies **three named children**, without claiming a complete family or resolving how their title shares arose. It identifies a Rochester express agent, not a Lisnagaver farmer: linking him to Alexander-951 would require an independent wife, age, birthplace or migration bridge. No such bridge was found in this inspection, so this father statement is not assigned to Alexander-951. The earlier Prescott witness named Alexander is a relevant comparison, but the shared name does not prove he was the express agent either. **Mary Eno** is the widow's recorded name; the document does not establish Eno as a maiden surname.

### Mary Glasgow Black and the Drumoolish connection

The [original **C298 deed of 15 September 1869**](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSLG-LQTY-K), registered **1 July 1870**, explicitly identifies **Mary Black, formerly Mary Glasgow, as sister of the late Robert Glasgow of Prescott**. It names her six children and conveys the family’s interest to Samuel’s widow for **$800**. This is another direct Irish bridge: two of the children reside at **Drumoolish, County Londonderry**. The deed gives a record-based family to compare with the wider Bann Valley and Inishrush evidence; it does not name Mary’s parents or make her a daughter of Adam.

### Consequences for Alexander's parentage

The Canadian records now expand **Daniel and Thomas's documented family**, which makes their brothers' and sister's records directly relevant to this enquiry. They still do not name Daniel or Thomas's parents, and they do not establish Alexander-951 as another brother. The Alexander-and-Rosanna tradition remains a hypothesis about Thomas's parents. Alexander's possible place among James's children now has named family and property records to test, but the carpenter's identity with Robert's brother and any connection between his Rochester son and Alexander-951 must each be demonstrated.

The 1861 deed also places wives **Margaret** and **Mary Anne** in the recorded family transaction. It strengthens the identification of the existing Irish couples and gives a later dated occurrence of Daniel's wife before his widowed death in 1871. It does not supply either woman's maiden name or establish the mother of every child. No new Y-DNA result was obtained: documentary linkage to the Prescott brothers expands the family that can be compared with Alexander's paternal line, without proving a genetic match or the joining generation.
