# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-973)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-973)

Last updated: 2026-09-05

## Current conclusion

The Rasharkin transcription identifies this second James as born 9 October
1843 at Lisnagaver and baptised 7 January 1844, a child of Alexander and Mary.
His presence aged seven in their 1851 household strongly confirms the
relationship. An earlier James was born in 1837 and probably died before the
name was reused; the incomplete 1838 baptism entry must not be assigned a name.

The James Glasgow buried at Brookside Cemetery, Tecumseh, Michigan, is now a
**strong but unproved identity candidate** for Alexander's son. He was born in
about 1843-44, appears with the complete cemetery family in the original 1884
Michigan census, and his daughter's original 1941 death certificate says her
father James Glasgow was born in **County Antrim, Ireland**. That is a much
narrower match than the profile's former Find a Grave lead: Alexander's proved
son was born at Lisnagaver, County Antrim, on 9 October 1843.

The identity is not yet proved. No inspected American record names James's
parents, gives his exact birth date, or mentions Lisnagaver, Rasharkin,
Alexander Glasgow or Mary McCaughan. The newly assembled 1869 marriage and
1870/1880 census indexes extend the Michigan household but do not provide that
bridge. The **original 1869 marriage return**, naturalisation papers or a
passenger record remain the best prospects before merging the Michigan man
into the Irish biography as fact.

A separate James Glasgow really did occupy a small house and garden at
**Dromore, Rasharkin parish**. The original valuation-revision books carry him
at plot 18b from the 1863-79 book through the 1891-1901 book. This corrects the
earlier transcription audit, but makes him a poor match for the Tecumseh man,
who reported living in Michigan from about 1866. Valuation names can lag behind
an occupier's departure, so the evidence strongly separates the two men rather
than proving the Dromore man's identity.

Samuel Glasgow of Franklin/Blissfield is not a supported brother candidate.
The 1838 Gortereagh child was already represented by
[Glasgow-3940](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3940), and Alexander's
1851 return lists no such child as resident, absent, or dead since 1841. Samuel
has no American record naming Alexander, Mary, Gortereagh or County Antrim.

## FamilySearch source audit, 18 August 2026

The 20 attachments on [FamilySearch person MQ57-BVR](https://www.familysearch.org/tree/person/details/MQ57-BVR)
represent roughly fourteen events, not twenty independent sources. Several are
duplicate database extractions, and the two 1851 attachments are records from
the same Lisnagaver household. Attaching them to one FamilySearch tree person
does not itself prove that the Irish child and Michigan adult were the same man.

Routine records for the daughters can refine their own profiles but cannot
substitute for evidence naming James's parents, exact birthplace, or migration.

## Corrections to the live WikiTree research notes

- The proposed `29 August 1896` death is not supported by the official index.
  Michigan GENDIS and the 1 August 1896 newspaper report independently establish
  **30 July 1896** for the Tecumseh candidate. The Find a Grave date is erroneous.
- The Dromore valuation entry is real; the earlier claim that the Rasharkin
  transcription contained no Glasgow was an error. The original PRONI books
  show **James Glasgow at Dromore plot 18b**, a house and small garden under
  James Hood and later Robert Hood, in `VAL/12/B/3/13A` (1863-79), `13B`
  (1880-91) and `13C` (1891-1901). Because the same name remains on the holding
  into the 1891-1901 volume, this was probably a different local James, not the
  Tecumseh man resident in Michigan from about 1866.
- Do not yet change James's death place or attach Mary Baird and their children
  as proved relatives. Present the Tecumseh family as a well-supported candidate
  pending a parent-level or exact-birth-date link.

## Searches completed and remaining gaps

- The Tecumseh District Library's digitised cemetery index and register were
  inspected, not merely their web snippets.
- Michiganology's original death certificates for Mary and daughter Minnie were
  inspected. James's 1896 certificate is not in that digitised 1897-1952 series.
- The original 1884 Michigan state census was inspected. It reconstructs the
  same James-Mary-Minnie-Maggie-Bertha household and gives James's age,
  occupation and Irish birth, but not his parents' names or County Antrim.
- The digitised *Tecumseh Herald* run was searched for Glasgow and Baird. It
  has a gap around the crucial late-July 1896 issue. A separate search of the
  Lenawee County Family Researchers' *Michigan Messenger* compilation located
  the 1 August 1896 death report reproduced above.
- FamilySearch now identifies the 1869 James-Mary marriage and the 1870 and
  1880 US censuses. Their pasted index views establish the family but add no
  parent-level link. The original 1869 marriage return should still be checked;
  it is the only record in this batch with a realistic chance of adding an age,
  birthplace, parents or revealing witnesses.
- The next highest-value records are Michigan's 1894 state census, Lenawee
  County naturalisation files, the original 1896 death register and any coroner
  or inquest file. Search the Baird
  siblings Jackson and Samuel in the same records: a shared arrival or residence
  may reveal James's migration path.
- Do not treat Samuel Glasgow of Franklin/Blissfield as James's brother. The
  1838 Gortereagh baptism was already represented by
  [Glasgow-3940](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3940), and Alexander's
  1851 return lists no such child as resident, absent, or dead since 1841.
  Samuel has no parent or County Antrim evidence. See the
  [Glasgow-3940 handoff](../Glasgow-3940/findings.md).
- If an American record gives James's exact 9 October birthday, parents
  Alexander/Mary, Lisnagaver/Rasharkin, or a known Irish sibling, the identity
  can be upgraded to proved. County Antrim plus birth year alone is not enough.
