# Findings: Glasgow-987

Last updated: 2026-09-06

## Current conclusion

Two original records now strongly identify Thomas Glasgow of Lisnagaver as the
late brother named in Daniel Glasgow of Kilrea's 1871 will. Daniel's will names
nephew Daniel Neely Glasgow and late brother Thomas; Daniel Neely's 1888 marriage
names his father as Thomas Glasgow, farmer, and places him in Lisnagaver.

This proves a Daniel-Thomas sibling relationship but does **not** identify their
parents. Alexander Glasgow and Rosanna remain late descendant testimony only.

The possible-sister route has now been checked against every Glasgow/Glascow
bride indexed in 1845-75 in Ballymoney, Ballymena, Antrim, Magherafelt and
Coleraine. Of 23 women, five belong to already identified lines. Original
registrations for all other 18 name fathers James, Robert, William, Hugh,
Samuel, Henry or Andrew Glasgow. None names Alexander, lives at Lisnagaver or
has a witness from the known family. This weakens the hypothesis of a sister
who stayed locally and married after 1845. It does not exclude a pre-1845 bride
or an emigrant sister. The certificate table and saved originals are in
[[Glasgow-951]]'s [research handoff](../Glasgow-951/findings.md).

Thomas's son Daniel Neely adds a promising indirect route to those parents.
His daughters' original September 1889 birth returns place their father Daniel,
a labourer, in New York. An Irish-born Albany Rural Cemetery labourer who died
as Daniel `Glascow` at Troy on 12 February 1931, certificate 9941, is a strong
same-person candidate. The Troy death certificate or obituary may name Thomas
and Mary Ann, but no original US record has yet proved the identity.

Thomas belongs in the standalone **Bann Valley-Killoquin / Kilrea-Rasharkin
cluster** with proved brother Daniel and possible brother or cousin Alexander
`Glasgow-951`. This groups the documented family without claiming descent from
the Kilwaughter, Derry Main, Moneymore or Inishrush founders.

Sarah Glasgow is now directly proved as Thomas's daughter. Her original 1885
marriage to Charles Taylor names her father as Thomas Glasgow, farmer, and her
residence as Lisnagaver. Four civil births and a contemporary notice identify
their later family.

The same search found Mary Ann Glasgow's original 1914 death return at
Lisnagaver. It calls the 94-year-old widow a farmer's widow and names Sarah
Taylor as the informant, but describes Sarah as Mary Ann's **grand-daughter**.
That word conflicts with Sarah's direct marriage record and the chronology:
Thomas was born about 1815 and Mary Ann about 1820. It is therefore retained as
an unresolved relationship error or same-place namesake problem, not used to
recast Sarah as Thomas's granddaughter.

The 5 September master review confirms that Alexander must not be described as
Thomas's probable brother. Their common locality, congregation, neighbouring
holdings and paternal DNA network support close kinship but do not distinguish
brother from cousin. Daniel and Thomas's brotherhood remains proved; their
parents remain unidentified.

## Relationship conclusion

Thomas and Daniel of Kilrea were brothers at high confidence. The unusual full
name Daniel Neely Glasgow, explicit `nephew`, explicit `brother Thomas`, father
Thomas Glasgow, occupation, and Lisnagaver residence form a conflict-free two-record
proof chain.

The chain stops there. It supplies no father or mother for Thomas or Daniel.

Rose Ann's New Zealand records independently strengthen the identification of this
Thomas as the Lisnagaver/Kilrea man, but they likewise stop at Thomas and do not
identify the proposed older Alexander.

The Newberry comparison is now more concrete but remains comparative only.
Rachel Glasgow's husband is identified as Robert McCrackin (c.1771–1817), and
their stones prove children Ruth and Pulauski; probable son Cyrus administered
Robert's estate. Hiram, a grandson of Wilson Glasgow and Mary McCracken, married
Mary Ann Brandon Kennedy, and their daughter Cleora married Leland McNary
Speers. No new record links the South Carolina McCrackins to Carnfinton or names
the father of Alexander, Thomas or Daniel. The repeated McCracken marriages are
therefore a useful migration/network hypothesis, not parentage proof.

## Identity cautions

- Do not retain Alexander and Rosanna as certain parents on the strength of the
  2007 message-board account.
- Do not describe Daniel as Thomas's uncle; Daniel's original will explicitly calls
  Thomas his brother. The older family tradition apparently misstated the degree.
- Do not use the sibling proof to attach either man to Adam.
- Do not treat the 2020 correspondence as a second independent source for
  Alexander and Rosanna until Felicity's underlying family material and any
  claimed DNA segment evidence are obtained.

## Local-cluster update

Younger Alexander `Glasgow-951` is Thomas's possible brother or cousin, based on their
similar ages, farming status, tightly grouped Lisnagaver households, movements
between adjoining Gortereghy and Lisnagaver, and the late family account. His son
Robert `Glasgow-938` is the best candidate for the Griffith Gortereghy Robert.
If the brother inference is correct, Daniel and Thomas were uncles of that Robert.
Neither relationship should be stated as proved.

Thomas's wife Maryann McCracken now supplies a more specific local affinal lead.
A 1834 tithe record places John McCrackine at **Carnfinton**, Mary Ann's reported
birth townland. Griffith's Valuation later has Robert and William McCracken
there. Civil marriages identify Carnfinton children of a farmer William:
Mary Ann (1867), James William (1886), and Elizabeth (1889); James McCracken
witnessed Elizabeth's marriage. James William's marriage was witnessed by
Alexander Marks; Mary Ann's husband George Mark was a son of Alexander Mark.
This may be the same Marks in-law network. Alexander's daughter Ann married Robert
McCracken in 1876, also naming a father William, although that register calls
William a labourer and places Robert at Lisnagaver. Robert is therefore a strong
candidate—not yet a proved sibling—within the Carnfinton William household.

If valuation-era William was Mary Ann's brother and Ann's husband Robert was
his son, Alexander's daughter married the nephew of Thomas's wife. This is a
testable, unusually specific kin-network hypothesis that would strengthen the
Alexander–Thomas brother case. It remains indirect and depends on two unproved
McCracken relationships. The Griffith name **McCochrane** at Lisnagaver is
standardised as Cochrane and should not be treated as a McCracken spelling
without an explicit record.

The strongest South Carolina comparison is specifically the descendant line of
Robert `Glasgow-1143` of Kilwaughter. His son James `Glasgow-1144` had siblings
Rachel and Wilson Crossier: Rachel married Robert McCrackin, while Wilson
`Glasgow-1982` married Mary McCracken. Wilson and Mary's daughter Mariah then
married William McCracken. Thus two children of one Glasgow household and one
grandchild made three McCracken marriages across two generations. This is a
strong repeated-alliance pattern, not three unrelated Glasgow branches.

The exact relationships among Robert, Mary and William McCracken remain
unknown. Proving that they were siblings, parent and child, or close cousins
would make the Newberry pattern a much better analogue for the proposed
Carnfinton–Lisnagaver alliance. It still would not prove that the Newberry
McCrackens came from Carnfinton: the documented marriages belong to the
post-migration Newberry community, and no record yet bridges the two McCracken
groups. McCracken is also a spouse surname and cannot explain Glasgow Y-DNA.

The Marks witnesses are now a parallel free-research route. John Marks
witnessed Alexander's son Robert in 1866; Jane Marks witnessed Thomas's daughter
Anne/Nancy in 1879; Lizzie J. Marks witnessed Thomas's son Daniel Neely in 1888;
and Alexander Marks witnessed James William McCracken in 1886. Proving how
Alexander/George Mark relate to John, Jane, Lizzie and the tithe-era Gardiner
Marks could connect both Glasgow households to the same McCracken in-law family
without relying on surname repetition alone.

The older Alexander `Glasgow-3904` and Rosanna Unknown are defensible uncertain
working parents. Their dates must be earlier than the current 1790 estimates if
Daniel is their son, and no source proves Rosanna's surname was Shaw. Adam and
Rose of Inishrush should not be placed above the older Alexander: Adam is
unsupported and Rose is chronologically excluded.

The reported first child Rose Ann, born about 1841 during the gap in the public
Rasharkin baptism transcription, is compatible with the traditional maternal
name Rosanna. It is only a naming clue: Rose Ann could honour Mary Ann
McCracken's mother, whose identity is unknown. Thomas's later reported son
Alexander is potentially more useful, especially if he was the first son, but
his exact birth order and destination have not been established.

The reported son Alexander can now be bounded more tightly. Thomas's 1851
household lists only daughters, the public Rasharkin baptism transcription
through 1846 contains only daughters for Thomas and Mary Ann, and no suitable
Alexander Glasgow birth appears in the Irish civil index for 1864-1870. No
age-compatible Irish marriage or death was found in bounded civil-index
searches. If the descendant account is correct, he was most likely born after
the 1851 census and before civil registration in 1864, then emigrated.

The official South African NAAIRS index supplies the first concrete destination
lead: an Alexander Glasgow was defendant in Cape Supreme Court files in 1893
and 1898. Follow-up identifies the other parties. The 1893 plaintiff was the
estate of Gerhard Myburgh, a prominent Cape Town merchant and consul-general;
the 1898 plaintiff was Maria Lacey Welch, a repeated Cape mortgage holder later
buried at Observatory, Cape Town. This places the defendant much more strongly
in Cape Town's commercial/property orbit. It still proves neither Irish origin
nor parentage, and only the files can establish whether the two cases concern
one man. The references are KAB CSC 2/6/1/146 ref. 286 (1893) and KAB CSC
2/1/1/350 ref. 123 (1898). A [held candidate draft](../../surname-research/new-people/1893_South_Africa_Cape_Town_Alexander_Glasgow.md)
records the duplicate audit and prevents premature attachment.

The tithe/valuation acreage pattern should not be overstated as sibling proof.
Thomas's later holding is close in size to the 1834 Robert holding, while the
later John-plus-younger-Alexander land approximates the 1834 Alexander holding.
That topology is compatible with separate tenant successions and makes the
cluster less like a simple three-son division of one farm. Only the 1835 rental
and 1869 maps can test the transfers.
