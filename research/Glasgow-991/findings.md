# Findings: [James Glasgow (Glasgow-991)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-991)

## Current conclusion

Reviewed: 2026-10-15


The corrected reading of Andrew Glasgow's 1597/8 testament does **not** name a
son James. It expressly names Robert Glasgow as Andrew and Elizat Stevinsoun's
son, followed by daughters Marion, Janet and Isobel. The previous placement of
this James under [[Glasgow-3163|Andrew Glasgow]] therefore loses its only direct
premise and should remain, at most, an unsupported/uncertain cluster link while
an evidenced correction is prepared.

James is still relevant to the Mid Calder research because baptism indexes name
a father James Glasgow there in 1605, 1610 and 1616. Other indexes name a father
James at Inveresk/Musselburgh in 1609, 1614 and 1618. The records do not show
whether one mobile household or two contemporaries produced those two sequences.

The original 1605 baptism locates its James provisionally at Muirestoun, probably Murieston, and names George Glasgow as witness. George of Mureistoun in1586 remains a competing father/relative hypothesis. Andrew-3163 is now selected as the local working father, without rejecting that George alternative.

The Fisherrow tailor James and Christina Bye are compatible with this James and Christina Bie-55, whose son William has a cited 1614 Inveresk baptism. This is an identity hypothesis pending the sasines; it does not combine the Mid Calder and Inveresk sequences.

**Selected local working parent,15 October2026:** [Andrew Glasgow of Calder](/people/glasgow-3163.html), **uncertain**. Andrew’s documented rural Calder household is the preferred working anchor for James’s nearby Mid Calder family. He was alive across the estimated1575–1586 birth interval of all three Lothian fathers; Hermand John’s1577 death would require an earlier birth estimate for David. That favours this coherent provisional family without excluding the other father. Andrew was alive until1598 and the uncertain generations allow James to be an older son. This uses the actual Calder setting and compatible family sequence, not the withdrawn assertion that the testament named James. James’s Inveresk/Fisherrow identity remains a separate uncertainty. The live WikiTree parent field is separate; this is a local hypothesis, with competing explanations below.

## Identity assessment — same-name leads not connected to James, 8 September 2026

A James Glas, son of Alexander Glas, was baptised at Perth on 19 January 1575 (Old Parish Register, Perth, 387/10, p. 172, as cited from FamilySearch tree person [LZGY-NSY](https://www.familysearch.org/en/tree/person/details/LZGY-NSY); the register entry was not seen). Before 1600 the Scottish year began on 25 March, so the date may be 1575/76. No evidence carries that child into the Mid Calder or Inveresk records, and an Inveresk birth and a wife attached to James elsewhere have not been shown to belong to this profile; neither should be used for him.

Jonet Glasgow's marriage to Martin Ramsy at Mid Calder in 1624 is indexed at [FamilySearch XY9X-4PR](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XY9X-4PR). Andrew Glasgow's testament (`CC8/8/32`) was made on 6 February 1597/8 and confirmed on 10 July 1598.

Recorded in `research/Glasgow-991/Glasgow-991.md` (8 September 2026); carried into this file on 7 October 2026.

## Fisherrow tailor and Christina Bye — 15 October 2026

The 1624–1630 sasine-index text reported by the earlier sweep describes James Glasgow, tailor in Fisherrow and burgess of Musselburgh, son Alexander, and James’s spouse **Christina Bye**. This James and [Christina Bie](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Bie-55) are compatible existing candidates: their attached son [William Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3517) has a cited 10 July 1614 Inveresk baptism naming James and Christina Bie. The identity of that parish couple with the sasine couple is not proved, and the Mid Calder and Inveresk James sequences retain their existing separate assessments. [Alexander’s source table](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1148) records the index locators and uncertain column pairings. Read the 1624 and 1625 instruments before changing the father assessment.

The additional authenticated Findmypast transcript [R_686177762](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=R_686177762), inspected 15 October 2026, names William Glasgow, year 1614, Inveresk with Musselburgh, father James. It supplies neither a mother nor an exact day. It therefore does not independently verify the Christina Bie attribution in Glasgow-3517’s cited source. The maternal reading still needs its own record check.

## Printed sasine verification — 15 October 2026

The printed pages themselves were visually inspected on 15 October 2026. Page 187 calls Alexander the son of James, tailor in Fischerraw and burgess of Musselburgh, and cross-refers to Alexander’s spouse Janet Clerk. Page 188 repeats Alexander’s entry for 1625, separately cross-refers from James to his spouse Christina Bye, and places Edward in Fischerraw at the same 1624 volume/folio as James and Alexander. The dates are headed **Date of Recording**: 8 July 1624, vol.9 fol.135, and 11 July 1625, vol.10 fol.171. They are registration dates, not verified dates of execution. [Printed index pp.187–188](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015072481057&seq=194). The underlying sasines remain uninspected.

This confirms the documentary father’s name, occupation and locality independently of the earlier OCR. The identification with this profile remains a working hypothesis. The shared 1624 registration reference supplies a concrete association with [Edward Glasgow (candidate Glasgow-1557)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1557), whose tentative Corsankell ancestry makes the instrument a useful branch-joining test. It does not establish brotherhood or a parent for James.

## Murieston connection assessment — 15 October 2026

The original Mid Calder baptism of 17 February 1605 names father James Glasgow, provisionally in Muirestoun, and adult witness George Glasgow. The 1586 Calder muster list names George Glasgw in Mureistoun. Murieston is the probable common locality; the two older George occurrences may be one man, but no kinship is stated. [Maidment’s printed p.399](https://archive.org/details/analectascotica00maidgoog) and [McCall’s p.16](https://archive.org/details/historyantiquiti00mcal) were visually checked; the original OPR 694/10 p.7 was reread. The original-page facts were already recorded in September; the new result is their correlation with the 1586 holding. The residence identification is probable, and the final witness remains unread.

The witness’s residence and relationship are unstated. He might be James’s father, another relative or a neighbour. See the [complete older-George draft and compatible candidates](/research/subjects/murieston-1586-1605/#george-mureistoun) and [James’s assessment](/people/glasgow-991.html). No new father link or certainty upgrade is counted. Compare the other Mid Calder baptism pages for the same holding and witnesses, then inspect NLS MS.3724 and Beveridge’s transcript MS.3725 for George, James, heirs, leases and succession at Mureistoun. The catalogue places the 1586 land-title courts at original pp.1–15 and the chronological court sequence at pp.23–189; it records a gap between 2 May 1592 and 26 November 1594. These manuscript pages have not been read.
