== Biography ==

'''John Glasford''', also recorded as Glasfurd and Glassfuird, was a Church of Scotland minister at Kilwinning, Ayrshire. His exact birth date and birthplace have not been established. He was probably born before 1593, inferred conservatively from his admission as minister at Kilwinning in 1614; this is not a baptismal date.

=== Parentage and education ===

''Fasti Ecclesiae Scoticanae'' identifies John as a son of [[Glasfurd-11|Patrick Glasfurd]], burgess of Cupar-Fife, and [[Lucklaw-2|Margaret Lucklaw]].<ref name="Fasti">Hew Scott, ''Fasti Ecclesiae Scoticanae'', new ed., vol. III: Synod of Glasgow and Ayr (Edinburgh: Oliver and Boyd, 1920), p. 116, entry for Kilwinning; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://archive.org/stream/fastiecclesiaesc03scot/fastiecclesiaesc03scot_djvu.txt full text]. The profile formerly cited p. 119 and [https://archive.org/details/fastiecclesiaesc03scot/page/118/mode/2up?q=Glasford this scan link]; the relevant printed page is 116.</ref> A family-history summary further identifies Patrick as Patrick Glasfurd of Walltour, a Cupar notary, and Margaret as Lucklaw or Lucklay; it reports their marriage in 1571 and a Cupar birth brief for John dated 12 June 1610.<ref name="FifeHistory">Fife Family History Society, summary of Alec Glasfurd, ''The Glasfurd Family, 1550-1972'', reporting Patrick Glasfurd of Walltour, Margaret Lucklay and the 1610 birth brief; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://paperzz.com/doc/9402528/published-family-histories---fife-family-history-society published summary].</ref>

National Records of Scotland catalogue entries independently establish a Patrick Glasfurd working as a notary in Fife in 1566 and 1567. They corroborate the proposed father's existence, occupation, location and period, but do not themselves name John as his son.<ref name="PatrickNotary">National Records of Scotland, GD254/35, 8 November 1566, and GD254/190, 20 July 1567, transactions naming Patrick Glasfurd as notary; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browseDetails.aspx?reference=GD254%2F35 catalogue entry for GD254/35].</ref>

Published accounts agree that John studied at the University of St Andrews but conflict on the year. ''Fasti'' gives M.A. 1600, while the Glasfurd family-history summary gives 1610; the previously stated 1609 date has not been verified.<ref name="Fasti" /><ref name="FifeHistory" /> R. N. Smart's alphabetical register of St Andrews students and graduates for 1579-1747 and the underlying university roll should be checked before using a graduation year to estimate John's birth.<ref name="StAndrewsRegister">University of St Andrews Library, R. N. Smart, ''Alphabetical Register of Students, Graduates and Officials of the University of St Andrews, 1579-1747''; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://librarysearch.st-andrews.ac.uk/discovery/fulldisplay/alma991010756289708966/44USTA_INST%3A44USTA_INST catalogue record].</ref>

=== Ministry ===

John was admitted minister of Kilwinning in 1614.<ref name="Fasti" /> A local parish history places the end of his incumbency by 1629, while ''Fasti'' records Robert Baillie as successor in 1631. He had therefore left the charge by 1629-31.<ref name="Kilwinning">''A Short History of Kilwinning Parish'', 2nd ed., Kilwinning Heritage; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://www.kilwinningheritage.org.uk/office/resources/documents/a-short-history-of-kilwinning-parish-2nd-ed.pdf PDF].</ref>

=== Family ===

''Fasti'' names John's wife as [[Blair-16146|Jean Blair]], daughter of Robert Blair of Lochwood, and names three children: John, Jean and Margaret.<ref name="Fasti" /> No compatible WikiTree profile was located for Robert Blair of Lochwood during the duplicate search.

Their son [[Glassfuird-1|John Glassfuird]] is independently corroborated by the Edinburgh apprentice register. On 6 July 1642 he was expressly described as son of the late John Glassfuird, minister at Kilwinning, when apprenticed to James Stevenson, merchant.<ref name="Apprentice1642">Francis J. Grant, ed., ''The Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh, 1583-1666'', Scottish Record Society, part XXXV (Edinburgh, 1906), p. 71, John Glassfuird, 6 July 1642; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://archive.org/stream/registerofappren35edin/registerofappren35edin_djvu.txt full text].</ref> ''Fasti'' reports that the same John was served heir to his father in lands in Kilwinning parish on 28 February 1672, citing Ayr retour no. 577.<ref name="Fasti" />

Daughters Jean and Margaret are presently known only from the compiled ''Fasti'' entry. No compatible WikiTree profiles were found; additional dates or records are needed before they can be safely distinguished from same-name people.

=== Death ===

''Fasti'' reports that John died in December 1636.<ref name="Fasti" /> The underlying record has not yet been inspected, so the date is marked uncertain.

== Research Notes ==

=== The Edinburgh slater was a different man ===

The Edinburgh marriage register records "John Glassford, slater" marrying [[Morriel-1|Jonet Morriel]] on 15 July 1619.<ref name="EdinburghMarriage1619">Henry Paton, ed., ''The Register of Marriages for the Parish of Edinburgh, 1595-1700'', Scottish Record Society, vol. XVI (Edinburgh, 1905), p. 268, John Glassford and Janet Murriell, 15 July 1619; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://archive.org/stream/scottishrecordso16scotuoft/scottishrecordso16scotuoft_djvu.txt full text].</ref> The Kilwinning minister had held his charge since 1614, and ''Fasti'' separately names Jean Blair as his wife. The 1619 marriage therefore belongs to [[Glassfuird-2|John Glassfuird, slater]], not the minister.

The apprentice register provides a strong identification of that slater: on 28 April 1613 John Glassfuird, son of the late John Glassfuird, miller at Dalry mills, was apprenticed to Walter Murriall, slater.<ref name="Apprentice1613">Grant, ''Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh'', p. 71, John Glassfuird, 28 April 1613; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://archive.org/stream/registerofappren35edin/registerofappren35edin_djvu.txt full text].</ref> The matching trade and Murriell surname make the apprentice and the 1619 bridegroom very likely the same man.

The Edinburgh children attached to [[Morriel-1|Jonet Morriel]] are consequently assigned to the slater's household: [[Glasford-39|Margaret]], [[Glasfoorde-1|Johnne]], [[Glasfoorde-2|Alexander]], [[Glassford-312|Thomas]], [[Glasfoorde-3|Catharine]], [[Glasgow-1167|Euphame]] and [[Glassford-311|Issobell]]. The 1634 FamilySearch index for Euphame records the father as Johnne Glasgow; this spelling or indexing issue belongs within the slater-and-Morriel family and is not evidence that she was the minister's daughter.<ref name="Euphame1634">"Scotland Births and Baptisms, 1564-1950", database, FamilySearch, Euphame Glasgow, 1634, father Johnne Glasgow; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:X1Y9-V87 indexed record].</ref> The original baptism images should still be reviewed for occupation, residence and witnesses.

=== Parentage assessment ===

Patrick’s father relationship is confident from the specific parentage statement in ''Fasti'' and the matching Cupar notarial context. The reported 1610 birth brief remains a priority for contemporary corroboration; Margaret’s maternal status remains uncertain.

=== Tobacco Lord hypothesis ===

A descent from this minister to the Tobacco Lord John Glassford of Dougalston and Whitehill (1715-1783) remains unproved. No compatible WikiTree profile for the Tobacco Lord was found in the duplicate search. His documented father was James Glassford, merchant in Paisley; no record currently connects that James to [[Glassfuird-1|the minister's son John]].<ref name="TobaccoLord">Wikipedia contributors, "John Glassford", ''Wikipedia, The Free Encyclopedia''; accessed 20 August 2026. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glassford article]. This is retained only as background to the research hypothesis, not as proof of descent.</ref>

The evidence-led route is to trace [[Glassfuird-1|John Glassfuird]], apprentice in 1642 and heir in 1672, forward through his wife and children before proposing any later descent.
