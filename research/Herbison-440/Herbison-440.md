== Biography ==
{{Notables Sticker}}

'''Sir William Herbesoun''', also known as Sir William Herbert, was born in Scotland around 1520. He was a Roman priest whose life and clerical role helped strengthen his family’s standing in Glasgow during the 16th century.  

He is noted in local tradition as the brother of [[Herbertson-132|Helen Herbertson]], who married [[Hutcheson-1647|Thomas Hutcheson]] of Gairbraid. Through Helen, Sir William was the uncle of George Hutcheson and Thomas Hutcheson Jr., the brothers who founded Hutchesons’ Hospital and School, two of Glasgow’s most enduring charitable institutions.

=== Glasgow Family Connections ===
Some records also refer to William Herbesoun as the father of [[Herbison-439|Margaret Herbison]] (born around 1560). Margaret married [[Glasgow-1098|William Glasgow]], a notable figure granted remission by King James V for his involvement in a historic event in 1526. Their children, George Glasgow and Barbara Glasgow, are documented under the curatorship of Sir William Herbesoun in 1586, suggesting his role as guardian or close kin.

=== Possible Historical Links ===
The title ‘Sir’ indicates his priestly status or possible knighthood, a title not uncommon for Roman priests before the Reformation. There is mention of a Sir William Herbert with a brother named George and a mother named Margaret — names that appear repeatedly in this family, raising the possibility that the Herbesoun/Herbert families were connected branches. This link remains speculative but fits the naming patterns and local traditions of the time.

== Legacy ==
Sir William’s clerical status and connections contributed to the prosperity and social standing of his extended family in Glasgow, whose legacy lives on through Hutchesons’ Hospital, Hutchesons’ School, and place names like Hutchesontoun.
