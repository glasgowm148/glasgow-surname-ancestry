== Biography ==
'''Thomas Hutcheson''' was born around 1510–1520 and was a substantial landowner in the Gairbraid and Lambhill areas near Glasgow, Lanarkshire. He married [[Herbertson-132|Helen Herbertson]], sister of Sir William Herbesoun.

He appears frequently in local charters and rental records and is remembered for establishing the property foundations that supported his sons — who would later found the famous Hutchesons' Hospital and Grammar School.

In 1574 he was chosen by his neighbours to witness and attest the "Coutching" (division) of part of the lands of Meikle Govan called "Dumbreck Quarter," previously held in common. This shows Thomas was a man of education and repute; the local notary recorded that he and others signed the deed, while James Hill, Laird of Ibrox and Parson of Erskine, also participated.<ref>''Early Records of an Old Glasgow Family'', W.H. Hill (1902)</ref>

=== Landholdings ===
Thomas held the two merkland of Lambhill, the merkland of Nether Carmyle (known as "Hutchesounes-toune"), and properties in Provanside, Gallowgate, Drygate, and a house on the north side of Trongate near the tolbooth.

* '''15 April 1579:''' James, Archbishop of Glasgow, with consent of the Dean and Chapter, granted a charter of the two merkland of Lambhill to Thomas Hutcheson and his wife Helen Herbertson.
* '''May 1581:''' They took infeftment on this charter.
* '''11 May 1583:''' They obtained a Crown Charter of Confirmation of their title.
* '''6 January 1587:''' They secured a Novodamus from Walter, Lord Feu-Fermurer (Commendator of Blantyre), adding the merkland of Nether Carmyle (Hutchesontoun).
* '''1595–1596:''' Following Thomas’s death, their eldest son George Hutcheson expede infeftment, confirming the Lambhill and Carmyle lands to himself.<ref>[https://books.google.com/books?id=D9MMAwAAQBAJ&pg=PA78 Early Records of an Old Glasgow Family, W.H. Hill]</ref>

Thomas is styled "of Hutchesoune and Lambhill" in these records.

=== Death ===
Thomas Hutcheson Sr. died about 1594. The exact date is unverified. Hill’s title narrative separately dates George’s Crown ratification to January 1595 and his infeftment to 13 April 1596.

Hill’s hospital history reports that George took responsibility for his younger brother Thomas’s education after their father’s death.<ref>W. H. Hill, ''History of the Hospital and School in Glasgow'' (1881), [https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/43/mode/1up printed p. 43].</ref>

=== Family ===
Hill’s account of the family names two sons and three daughters. The Lambhill title inventory names Helen Herbertson as Thomas’s spouse in the charter dated 15 April 1579. A second 1587 charter gave the couple a lifetime interest and named George as their eldest son and hereditary grantee.<ref name="Hill1902Family">W. H. Hill, ''The Early Records of an Old Glasgow Family'' (1902), [https://archive.org/stream/earlyrecordsofol00hill/earlyrecordsofol00hill_djvu.txt printed pp. 52–55 and 78–79]. Hill’s printed account and title-inventory quotations, with the original deeds uninspected.</ref>

* [[Hutcheson-1648|George Hutcheson]] — eldest son, husband of Elizabeth Craig and founder of the hospital. A later burgh order separately identifies [[Hutcheson-1650|Janet Hutcheson in Holland]] as his natural daughter.<ref name="GlasgowHistory">Robert Renwick and John Lindsay, ''History of Glasgow'', vol. II, [https://electricscotland.com/history/glasgow/glasgow2_20.htm ch. XX]. Published family account and summary of the kirk-session and burgh records.</ref>
* [[Hutcheson-1649|Thomas Hutcheson]] — younger son and founder of the associated school. Hill names Marion Stewart as his wife and reports that she survived him.<ref name="Hill1902Family" /><ref>W. H. Hill, ''History of the Hospital and School in Glasgow'' (1881), [https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/49/mode/1up printed p. 49].</ref>
* [[Hutcheson-1654|Janet Hutcheson]] — eldest sister, wife of [[Duncan-27082|David Duncan]]. Hill supplies his given name from the 15 June 1642 precept.<ref>W. H. Hill, ''History of the Hospital and School in Glasgow'' (1881), [https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/8/mode/1up printed p. 8 and n. 2]. The reported precept is cited through Hill.</ref>
* [[Hutcheson-1655|Elizabeth Hutcheson]] — wife of [[Pollok-99|James Pollok]], a citizen of Glasgow.<ref name="LanarkRetours1642">''Inquisitionum ad Capellam Domini Regis Retornatarum Abbreviatio'', [https://archive.org/details/inquisitionumadc00grea/page/n498/mode/1up Lanark retours nos. 205–206], 24 May 1642. Printed abstracts of the inquests.</ref>
* [[Hutcheson-1652|Helen Hutcheson]] — youngest sister, wife of [[Hill-17647|Ninian Hill of Garioch]], whom Hill says she married in 1609.<ref name="Hill1902Family" />

The three sisters succeeded as heirs-portioners to their brother Thomas in specified lands. The two Lanark retours of 24 May 1642 name Janet and Helen in no. 206 and Elizabeth in no. 205. They name Helen and Elizabeth as the widows of Ninian Hill and James Pollok, and Janet as the widow of a Duncan whose given name is omitted. Hill separately describes the three sisters’ division of the brothers’ estate and the descent of Lambhill through the youngest sister’s family to her son [[Hill-17648|Ninian Hill of Lambhill]].<ref name="LanarkRetours1642" /><ref name="Hill1902Family" />

=== Legacy ===
Thomas’s careful property acquisition and family strategy laid the groundwork for Hutchesons’ Hospital and School, institutions that continue to benefit Glasgow today.
