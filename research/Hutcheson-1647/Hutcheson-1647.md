== Biography ==
'''Thomas Hutcheson''' was born around 1510–1520 and was a substantial landowner in the Gairbraid and Lambhill areas near Glasgow, Lanarkshire. He married [[Herbertson-132|Helen Herbertson]], sister of Sir William Herbesoun.

He appears frequently in local charters and rental records and is remembered for establishing the property foundations that supported his sons — who would later found the famous Hutchesons' Hospital and Grammar School.

In 1574 he was chosen by his neighbours to witness and attest the "Coutching" (division) of part of the lands of Meikle Govan called "Dumbreck Quarter," previously held in common. This shows Thomas was a man of education and repute; the local notary recorded that he and others signed the deed, while James Hill, Laird of Ibrox and Parson of Erskine, also participated.<ref>''Early Records of an Old Glasgow Family'', W.H. Hill (1902)</ref>

=== Landholdings ===
Thomas held the two merkland of Lambhill, the merkland of Nether Carmyle (known as "Hutchesounes-toune"), and properties in Provanside, Gallowgate, Drygate, and a house on the north side of Trongate near the tolbooth.

* '''15 April 1579:''' James, Archbishop of Glasgow, with consent of the Dean and Chapter, granted a charter of the two merkland of Lambhill to Thomas Hutcheson and his wife Helen Herbertson.
* '''May 1581:''' They took infeftment on this charter.
* '''11 May 1583:''' They obtained a Crown Charter of Confirmation of their title.
* '''6 January 1587:''' They secured a Novodamus from Walter, Lord Feu-Fermurer (Commendator of Blantyre), adding the merkland of Nether Carmyle (Hutchesontoun).
* '''1595–1596:''' Following Thomas’s death, their eldest son George Hutcheson expede infeftment, confirming the Lambhill and Carmyle lands to himself.<ref>[https://books.google.com/books?id=D9MMAwAAQBAJ&pg=PA78 Early Records of an Old Glasgow Family, W.H. Hill]</ref>

Thomas is styled "of Hutchesoune and Lambhill" in these records.

=== Death ===
Thomas Hutcheson Sr. died about 1594. This date is inferred from George Hutcheson’s confirmation and infeftment in April 1596, where Thomas is described as deceased.

Some later accounts say Thomas Jr. (the younger son) remembered his brother George acting as guardian “when I was five years old.” This is sometimes interpreted as the father dying around 1563, but land charters and sasines show Thomas Sr. was still alive at least through the 1580s. It is likely that George acted as practical guardian for his younger brother’s education and inheritance long before their father’s official death, explaining the story.<ref>https://www.theglasgowstory.com/image/?inum=TGSA02080</ref>

=== Family ===
Thomas and Helen had five known children:

* [[Hutcheson-1648|George Hutcheson (1554–1639)]] — eldest son and founder of Hutchesons' Grammar School
* [[Hutcheson-1649|Thomas Hutcheson Jr. (c. 1558–1641)]] — co-founder of Hutchesons' Hospital, unmarried
* [[Hutcheson-1652|Janet Hutcheson]] — married David Duncan
* [[Hutcheson-1654|Helen Hutcheson]] — married Ninian Hill of Garioch, Merchant Burgess in Glasgow
* [[Hutcheson-1655|Elizabeth Hutcheson]] — married James Pollok, “Cooper, Burgess in Glasgow,” grandson of “Pollok of that Ilk”

His three daughters became co-heiresses, passing Hutcheson ties into families such as Hill of Garioch, Duncan, and Pollok.

=== Legacy ===
Thomas’s careful property acquisition and family strategy laid the groundwork for Hutchesons’ Hospital and School, institutions that continue to benefit Glasgow today.

== Research Notes ==

The currently attached father [[Hutcheson-1651|John Hutcheson]] is marked uncertain. Only local tradition says John had Thomas as child; no inspected charter, baptism, testament, or other record names Thomas as John's son.

* ''Spelling:'' Early charters show the spelling “Hutchesoune.”
* ''Chronology:'' Conflicting guardianship anecdotes can be reconciled by noting that George was managing family affairs well before their father’s official death.
* ''Confirmed Sources:'' All charters and rentals match the timeline of a single Thomas Sr., not two different fathers.
