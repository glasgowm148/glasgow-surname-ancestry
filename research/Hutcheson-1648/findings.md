## Source findings — career and biography review, 13 November 2026

George Hutcheson of Lambhill was a Glasgow notary, public writer, lender and landowner whose professional business included work for the city, the University and ecclesiastical patrons. His interests combined legal practice, lending on bonds and an extensive property portfolio. The modern Oxford Dictionary of National Biography places his birth between 1550 and 1560, rather than supplying an exact birthday or proved birthplace. His father Thomas Hutcheson remains assessed as confident; no new confidence grade is proposed.

George’s charitable deed of 16 December 1639 provided property and 20,000 merks in bonds for an institution supporting poor elderly men. Thomas, his younger brother, subsequently added endowments and carried forward the associated school. Hutchesons’ Grammar School records the foundation stone on 19 March 1641 and its first pupils in 1643. George’s foundation was an almshouse in the historical sense of hospital, not a modern medical institution.

George died in December 1639. Published accounts differ between 26 and 31 December and between burial on the east and south sides of Glasgow Cathedral. Retain the existing 26 December value with its source attribution rather than silently replacing it. The original burial evidence, 1588 kirk-session entry and relevant testament have not been inspected in this batch. The older claim that he died unmarried is contradicted by later published accounts of Elizabeth Craig. The named Holland daughter Janet remains distinct from the unnamed girl in the 1588 account unless further evidence identifies them.

## Source findings — hospital and school chronology, 13 November 2026

George's deed of 16 December 1639 endowed a charitable hospital for poor older men with Trongate property and 20,000 merks. Thomas ratified the foundation in 1640 and added adjoining property and 20,200 merks for the lodging and education of twelve poor boys by his deed of 9 March 1641. Hospital here means charitable maintenance and accommodation, not a modern medical hospital. The foundation stone on 19 March 1641 and the first recorded boy's admission on 13 November 1643 are separate stages. Hill's published account says Archibald Edmiston initially attended the existing town schools; it does not show that the new foundation already taught all pupils on its own premises. Later incorporation in 1821 was not the original foundation. Sources: [Hill (1881), pp.58–63 and Appendix pp.239–252](https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/58/mode/2up), [pp.203–204](https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/203/mode/2up), and [the school's institutional history](https://www.hutchesons.org/our-school/our-history/). Hill's searchable published text was reviewed; manuscript deeds and admission minutes were not inspected. Conflicting published endowment totals are not silently harmonised; amounts are historical merks, with no modern purchasing-power conversion.


## Source findings — printed death-date evidence, 13 November 2026

Hill’s institutional history (1881), printed p.37, has now been visually checked. It reports 26 December 1639 and burial on the east side of the Cathedral, footnoting George’s Testament Testamentar. This makes 26 December the better-attributed published claim in the sources reviewed here. Henderson’s DNB (1891) gives 31 December and south-side burial without a date-specific citation. The cited testament remains uninspected, so the discrepancy is unresolved at the original-record level. Source: [Hill, printed p.37](https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/37/mode/1up).
