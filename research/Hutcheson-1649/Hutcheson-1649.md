=== Origins and education ===
Thomas Hutcheson (about 1590–1641), the younger brother of George Hutcheson, was a Glasgow lawyer and educational benefactor. He was a son of Thomas Hutcheson and Helen Herbertson. George supervised his education after their father’s death. Thomas entered the University of Glasgow in 1607 and graduated in 1610. His approximate birth follows the published epitaph’s statement that he died in his fifty-second year; it is not a recovered baptism date.<ref name="thomas-odnb">Stuart Handley, “Hutcheson, Thomas (1589/90–1641), philanthropist,” Oxford Dictionary of National Biography, Online extract, published2004/revised2009. === Legal career ===
He worked as a public writer and became register of sasines for the regality of Glasgow about 1627. He also acted for the University and was appointed a commissioner to visit it in 1640. Glasgow admitted him as a burgess on 24 April 1640 for his work establishing the hospital. As George’s heir and executor, Thomas ratified his brother’s charitable arrangements and used inherited wealth and property to enlarge the foundation.<ref name="thomas-odnb">Stuart Handley, “Hutcheson, Thomas (1589/90–1641), philanthropist,” Oxford Dictionary of National Biography, Online extract, published2004/revised2009. === Hospital and school ===
Thomas provided for a residential school serving twelve needy boys, sons of Glasgow burgesses, alongside the hospital for older men. The hospital’s institutional history records 20,200 merks for the school on 9 March 1641, a further 10,000 merks for men and boys on 3 July, and another 10,500 merks for the hospital on 14 July. These are historical merks, not modern pounds. He laid the foundation stone in the Trongate on 19 March 1641. The school’s first pupil arrived in 1643, after his death.<ref name="thomas-institution1850">The Constitution, Rules, and History of the Royal Incorporation of Hutchesons’ Hospital (1850), Printed p116 (mortification schedule and foundation-stone account). [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/the_constitution__rules__and_history_of_the_royal_incorporation_of_hutcheson_s_hospital_~_1850.pdf Source]. Published text or public extract reviewed; original manuscripts not inspected.</ref><ref name="thomas-school-history">Hutchesons’ Grammar School, Our History / Our Founders, Online history; founder section. [https://www.hutchesons.org/our-school/our-history/ Source]. Published text or public extract reviewed; original manuscripts not inspected.</ref>

=== Marriage and death ===
Hill reports that Thomas died on 1 September 1641 leaving his widow, Marion Stewart, and no issue. Hill’s printed p. 47 identifies Marion as a daughter of James Stewart of Blackhall, while describing the Blackhall attribution as an inference from the tomb arms, the witnesses to a liferent charter and other manuscripts. Hill records an earlier Mynto attribution and says the marriage contract was missing. Handley also names James Stewart of Blackhall as her father and reports that she lived until November 1670. These are published accounts, and Marion’s exact parentage remains unverified. Hill’s printed p. 50 transcribes an epitaph giving death in 1641 and age 52. Hill names Marion in the accompanying narrative. The original legal information, testament and monument remain uninspected.<ref name="thomas-hill1881-family">William H. Hill, History of the Hospital and School in Glasgow Founded by George and Thomas Hutcheson (1881), printed pp. 47 and 49–50. [https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/9542/95420646.23.pdf Digitised edition].</ref><ref name="thomas-odnb" />

On 15 October 1641 Janet, Elizabeth and Helen ratified Thomas’s hospital endowment. In the deed they describe themselves as his only lawful full sisters and heirs-portioners.<ref>William H. Hill, History of the Hospital and School in Glasgow (1881), [https://archive.org/download/historyofhospita00hutc/page/n305.jpg Appendix VI, pp. 254–255], printed transcription of the ratification.</ref>

The printed Lanark retours nos. 205–206, dated 24 May 1642, identify Elizabeth, Helen and Janet as Thomas’s sisters and heirs-portioners in the specified lands. This is evidence for that succession. Hill’s statement that Thomas left no issue remains a published conclusion and does not establish that Thomas never had a child.<ref name="thomas-retours">Inquisitionum ad Capellam Domini Regis Retornatarum Abbreviatio, [https://archive.org/details/inquisitionumadc00grea/page/n498/mode/1up Lanark nos. 205–206, 24 May 1642]. Printed abstracts; original inquests uninspected.</ref>

His support of the hospital and school endured through their trustees. Later purchases and development of trust lands must not be described as transactions personally completed by Thomas after 1641.

== Research Notes ==
The father [[Hutcheson-1647|Thomas Hutcheson]] retains his existing confident assessment. Published biographies name [[Herbertson-132|Helen Herbertson]] as mother; no maternal grade is derived from the paternal assessment. The brother is [[Hutcheson-1648|George Hutcheson]]. The printed retours identify sisters Elizabeth, Janet and Helen. The exact profile identification of [[Hutcheson-1652|Helen, wife of Ninian Hill]], remains uncertain.

The earlier 1558/circa1560 birth estimates and 35-year age-gap sentence were incompatible and are superseded by about 1590 from Hill’s age-at-death account, independently reflected in modern biography. The prior unmarried statement is superseded by the named widow.

The Glasgow City Archives catalogue describes Thomas’s grant to John Herbertson on 2 August 1641, D/TC/3/1/209. A catalogue instrument date is not by itself proof that he personally acted that day.<ref>Glasgow City Archives, [https://cityarchives.glasgowlife.org.uk/Record.aspx?id=D%2FTC%2F3%2F1%2F209&src=CalmView.catalog D/TC/3/1/209].
