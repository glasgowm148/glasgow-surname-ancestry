'''Thomas Hutcheson Jr.''' was born around 1560 near Gairbraid, Lanarkshire, Scotland, about 35 years younger than his brother [[Hutcheson-1648|George Hutcheson (1554–1639)]]. He was the youngest son of [[Hutcheson-1647|Thomas Hutcheson Sr. (c. 1510–1594)]] and [[Herbertson-132|Helen Herbertson]].

=== Education and Career ===
Thomas was reared and educated by his older brother George at the University of Glasgow. He was originally intended for the ministry but shifted to law and was appointed Registrar of Sasines for Glasgow through George’s influence. A man of culture, he maintained close friendships with the professors and contributed significantly to the College: 1,000 merks to enlarge the library and 2,000 merks to fund a bursary for the librarian.

=== Charitable Legacy ===
As George’s heir and executor, Thomas carried out his brother’s dying wish to found an almshouse for elderly men of good character. He expanded George’s original gift of 20,000 merks by adding 10,500 merks for the hospital and 20,200 merks for a new school for twelve orphan boys, sons of Glasgow burgesses.

In March 1641, Thomas laid the hospital’s foundation stone on the north side of Trongate. He directed the trust to invest the funds in the “cheappist and best haldin arrabill lands” near the burgh, ensuring the charity’s income for generations. These purchases included lands south of the Clyde, forming what became known as Hutchesontown — a testament to his vision for sustainable community support.

=== Death and Legacy ===
Thomas died unmarried and childless in 1641. His devotion to his brother’s legacy helped shape Glasgow’s civic institutions for centuries to come. Statues of George and Thomas still decorate the hospital building that bears their name.

Thomas’s investment strategy and his decision to use feu-duties to sustain the trust were key to the hospital’s survival during the civil and religious upheavals that followed.
