=== Origins and education ===
Thomas Hutcheson (about 1590–1641), the younger brother of George Hutcheson, was a Glasgow lawyer and educational benefactor. He was a son of Thomas Hutcheson and Helen Herbertson. George supervised his education after their father’s death. Thomas entered the University of Glasgow in 1607 and graduated in 1610. === Legal career ===
He worked as a public writer and became register of sasines for the regality of Glasgow about 1627. He also acted for the University and was appointed a commissioner to visit it in 1640. Glasgow admitted him as a burgess on 24 April 1640 for his work establishing the hospital. As George’s heir and executor, Thomas ratified his brother’s charitable arrangements and used inherited wealth and property to enlarge the foundation.<ref name="thomas-odnb">Stuart Handley, “Hutcheson, Thomas (1589/90–1641), philanthropist,” Oxford Dictionary of National Biography, Online extract, published2004/revised2009. === Hospital and school ===
Thomas provided for a residential school serving twelve needy boys, sons of Glasgow burgesses, alongside the hospital for older men. The hospital’s institutional history records 20,200 merks for the school on 9 March 1641, a further 10,000 merks for men and boys on 3 July, and another 10,500 merks for the hospital on 14 July. These are historical merks, not modern pounds. He laid the foundation stone in the Trongate on 19 March 1641. The school’s first pupil arrived in 1643, after his death.<ref name="thomas-institution1850">The Constitution, Rules, and History of the Royal Incorporation of Hutchesons’ Hospital (1850), Printed p116 (mortification schedule and foundation-stone account). === Marriage and death ===
Hill reports that Thomas married Marion Stewart. The verified passage does not establish her parentage or a Blackhall identification. Hill’s published history states that he died on 1 September 1641, leaving his widow but no issue. He was buried beside George at Glasgow Cathedral; the published monument text commemorates him and Marion. His support of the hospital and school endured through their trustees. Later purchases and development of trust lands must not be described as transactions personally completed by Thomas after 1641. Hill’s printed pp.49–50 have now been visually inspected. Page 49 reports death on 1 September 1641, widow Marion Stewart and no issue, describing a reported legal information referring to their marriage contract and his testament. Page 50 transcribes the epitaph with death in 1641 and age 52, interpreted by Hill as death in his fifty-second year. These are printed secondary-source statements: the original contract, information, testament and monument were not inspected. Marion is identified in Hill’s accompanying narrative, not in the quoted epitaph wording.<ref name="thomas-hill1881">William H. == Research Notes ==
The father [[Hutcheson-1647|Thomas Hutcheson]] retains his existing confident assessment. Published biographies name [[Herbertson-132|Helen Herbertson]] as mother; no maternal grade is derived from the paternal assessment. The brother is [[Hutcheson-1648|George Hutcheson]]. Reported sisters and the uncertain identification of [[Hutcheson-1652|Helen, wife of Ninian Hill]], require separate chronology review; no sibling or parent linkage is strengthened here.

The earlier 1558/circa1560 birth estimates and 35-year age-gap sentence were incompatible and are superseded by about 1590 from Hill’s age-at-death account, independently reflected in modern biography. Hill reports no issue; this is the published account, not an exhaustive proof of absence of children. The prior unmarried statement is superseded by the named widow. No spouse profile or relationship is invented.

The Glasgow City Archives catalogue describes Thomas’s grant to John Herbertson on 2 August 1641, D/TC/3/1/209. A catalogue instrument date is not by itself proof that he personally acted that day.<ref>Glasgow City Archives, [https://cityarchives.glasgowlife.org.uk/Record.aspx?id=D%2FTC%2F3%2F1%2F209&src=CalmView.catalog D/TC/3/1/209]. Catalogue metadata only; original uninspected.</ref>
