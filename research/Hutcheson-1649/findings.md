## Source findings — biography, marriage and chronology, 13 November 2026

Thomas Hutcheson (about 1590–1641), the younger brother of George Hutcheson, was a Glasgow lawyer and educational benefactor. He was a son of Thomas Hutcheson and Helen Herbertson. George supervised his education after their father’s death. Thomas entered the University of Glasgow in 1607 and graduated in 1610. His approximate birth follows the published epitaph’s statement that he died in his fifty-second year; it is not a recovered baptism date.

He worked as a public writer and became register of sasines for the regality of Glasgow about 1627. He also acted for the University and was appointed a commissioner to visit it in 1640. Glasgow admitted him as a burgess on 24 April 1640 for his work establishing the hospital. As George’s heir and executor, Thomas ratified his brother’s charitable arrangements and used inherited wealth and property to enlarge the foundation.

Thomas provided for a residential school serving twelve needy boys, sons of Glasgow burgesses, alongside the hospital for older men. The hospital’s institutional history records 20,200 merks for the school on 9 March 1641, a further 10,000 merks for men and boys on 3 July, and another 10,500 merks for the hospital on 14 July. These are historical merks, not modern pounds. He laid the foundation stone in the Trongate on 19 March 1641. The school’s first pupil arrived in 1643, after his death.

Hill reports that Thomas married Marion Stewart. The verified passage does not establish her parentage or a Blackhall identification. Hill’s published history states that he died on 1 September 1641, leaving his widow but no issue. He was buried beside George at Glasgow Cathedral; the published monument text commemorates him and Marion. His support of the hospital and school endured through their trustees. Later purchases and development of trust lands must not be described as transactions personally completed by Thomas after 1641.

Hill, [institutional history (1881)](https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/9542/95420646.23.pdf), printed pp.49–50 reports widow Marion Stewart and no issue, and transcribes the epitaph giving 1 September 1641, in his 52nd year. The printed page images have now been visually inspected; the underlying marriage contract, information, testament and monument remain uninspected. About1590 is an estimate, not an exact birth date. No Blackhall parentage is established by this passage.

## Source findings — hospital and school chronology, 13 November 2026

George's deed of 16 December 1639 endowed a charitable hospital for poor older men with Trongate property and 20,000 merks. Thomas ratified the foundation in 1640 and added adjoining property and 20,200 merks for the lodging and education of twelve poor boys by his deed of 9 March 1641. Hospital here means charitable maintenance and accommodation, not a modern medical hospital. The foundation stone on 19 March 1641 and the first recorded boy's admission on 13 November 1643 are separate stages. Hill's published account says Archibald Edmiston initially attended the existing town schools; it does not show that the new foundation already taught all pupils on its own premises. Later incorporation in 1821 was not the original foundation. Sources: [Hill (1881), pp.58–63 and Appendix pp.239–252](https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/58/mode/2up), [pp.203–204](https://archive.org/details/historyofhospita00hutc/page/203/mode/2up), and [the school's institutional history](https://www.hutchesons.org/our-school/our-history/). Hill's searchable published text was reviewed; manuscript deeds and admission minutes were not inspected. Conflicting published endowment totals are not silently harmonised; amounts are historical merks, with no modern purchasing-power conversion.


## Source findings — printed marriage and death account, 13 November 2026

Hill’s printed pp.49–50 have now been visually inspected. Page 49 reports death on 1 September 1641, widow Marion Stewart and no issue, describing a reported legal information referring to their marriage contract and his testament. Page 50 transcribes the epitaph with death in 1641 and age 52, interpreted by Hill as death in his fifty-second year. These are printed secondary-source statements: the original contract, information, testament and monument were not inspected. Marion is identified in Hill’s accompanying narrative, not in the quoted epitaph wording.
