# Findings: [Rev. Alexander Hutcheson](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Hutcheson-361)

Last updated: 18 November 2026

## Current conclusion

Rev. Alexander Hutcheson of Drumalig's wife remains unidentified. The strongest evidence for the Edinburgh groom of 8 December 1671 is the printed marriage register's description of him as a tailor, corroborated by a likely matching Edinburgh burgess with a documented apprenticeship. The identification of the groom with the minister remains unproved and is less likely than the Edinburgh tailor explanation. The separate Channelkirk entry and the Edinburgh Alison Borthwike lead remain unresolved.

The published abstract of Alexander's registered will memorial establishes that John Hutcheson was described as his only son and named executor, and identifies Beatrix Wallace and Mary Young as daughters. The abstract names five grandsons and concerns half of Drumalig. It does not give the wife, the children's mother, the daughters' birth order, their wedding dates, or the whole distribution. The will date of 25 September 1711 in the abstract conflicts with the former death bound before 5 September 1711. Retain the reported death year **1711** with day and month unresolved. [Will memorial abstract, no. 340](https://www.irishmanuscripts.ie/digital/Registry%20Of%20Deeds%20Abstracts%20Of%20Wills%20Vol%201%201708-45/Registry%20Of%20Deeds%20Abstracts%20Of%20Wills%20Vol%201%201708-45.pdf).

## Marriage records and identity

The inspected printed Edinburgh register identifies Alexander Hutcheson, **tailor**, and [Eupham Glasgow (Glasgow-3358), possible bride identity](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3358), and the edition treats 8 December 1671 as a marriage date. Charles B. Boog Watson's printed burgess roll dates the admission of Alexander Hutcheson, tailor, to 22 June 1670 and gives an apprenticeship to the late Alexander Thomsone, tailor, beginning 31 January 1655. This is a probable match for the groom. The profession and apprenticeship support a namesake distinct from the man ordained in County Down in 1657. The original Edinburgh parish manuscript remains uninspected. [Henry Paton's printed marriage register, pp. 267 and 346](https://electricscotland.com/history/records/scottishrecordso16scotuoft.pdf#page=356), [printed burgess roll, p. 265](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=275), [Eupham research account](/people/glasgow-3358.html).

The Scotland's People Channelkirk banns-and-marriages index lists Effie Glasgow and Alexander Hutcshesone on 17 September 1671 (OPR 729/10 / 230). The original and event type remain unverified. The couple may be the same as the Edinburgh couple, but the Channelkirk entry does not independently identify the groom as a tailor, the minister, or a second husband. The derivative list for Alexander Hutcheson and Alison Borthwike, Edinburgh, 2 July 1675 supplies a separate unresolved name match. The 1671 Drumalig conveyance is recorded as a purchase. Its text as reported in the existing draft names no wife or marriage settlement. [Scotland's People](https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/), [Ann Carson's derivative marriage list](https://freepages.rootsweb.com/~anncarson/genealogy/Borthwick/borthwickmarriagesoprsct.htm), [NAI Lodge/7/643](https://virtualtreasury.ie/item/NAI-Lodge-7-643).

## Children, beneficiaries and dates

P. Beryl Eustace, ed., *Registry of Deeds, Dublin: Abstracts of Wills*, vol. I, p. 144, no. 340, cites Registry of Deeds vol. 47, p. 311, memorial 30761. The published abstract gives 25 September 1711 for the will and 26 January 1725 for the memorial as printed. It expressly names [John Hutcheson](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Hutcheson-360), only son and executor, daughters Beatrix Wallace and Mary Young, and grandsons [Francis Hutcheson](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Hutcheson-359), Robert Hutcheson, John Hutcheson, William Wallace and John Young. William Wallace and John Young are described as the second sons then living of Beatrix and Mary, respectively. The earlier draft's proposed husbands, Hugh Wallace of Ballyobikin and Ravara and John Young of Listooder and Ballydian, remain leads. The abstract supplies the daughters' Wallace and Young surnames and names their sons, without giving the husbands' forenames or places. The reference to half of Drumalig and the naming of relatives do not establish every beneficiary's share. The original will, memorial image and cited Betham abstract were uninspected in this review. [Irish Manuscripts Commission edition, p. 144](https://www.irishmanuscripts.ie/digital/Registry%20Of%20Deeds%20Abstracts%20Of%20Wills%20Vol%201%201708-45/Registry%20Of%20Deeds%20Abstracts%20Of%20Wills%20Vol%201%201708-45.pdf).

W. R. Scott's 1900 study dates the will to 5 September 1711 and describes a prior transfer of half to the grandson Hans, with a life interest for the son John and entail to Francis in the other half. Keep Scott's account attributed while the memorial and associated conveyances remain uninspected. The earlier draft's January will date, inferred death of Alexander's wife and entire estate passing to Francis followed by a private division are not established by the inspected abstract. The former 5 September probate or death interpretation likewise needs checking against the cited Betham and Synod sources. [Scott's documentary study](https://archive.org/details/francishutcheso01scotgoog), [Betham abstract locator](https://familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L9LW-6Z5B?cc=4496119), [Killen's published Saintfield account](https://archive.org/details/historyofcongreg00kill/page/226/mode/2up).

## Outstanding questions

Compare memorial 30761, Betham series 1, vol. 17, pp. 121–122, and PRONI T/403 p. 43 to distinguish execution, probate and registration dates and identify the full Drumalig disposition. Identify Alexander's wife through a record that links her to the minister or his documented children.
