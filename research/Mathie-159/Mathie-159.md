== Biography ==
Jonet was probably born about 1597. The date is estimated from her marriage in 1618.


Jonet married [[Glasgow-1030|John Glasgow]] in Glasgow on 25 January 1618.<ref name="Marriage1618">FamilySearch, “Scotland Marriages, 1561–1910,” John Glasgow and Jonet Mathie, 25 January 1618, Glasgow, Lanark, Scotland; [https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:XYWK-WM3 permanent record].</ref>

== Research Notes ==
=== Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026 ===

Assess Jonet [[Mathie-159]] separately as Matthew’s uncertain working mother. Her recorded 1618 marriage and maternity in the following decade provide an actual couple and a compatible childbearing generation; her circa-1597 birth is an estimate. The Mathie associations in Stevenston are the proposed route for this household’s connection to Pyperheugh. Selecting her is a conditional couple hypothesis, not an automatic consequence of attaching John. It has very low confidence: no record calls Matthew her son, no Mathie ancestor is newly attached, and another mother remains possible. The paternal and maternal selections share the same network argument and are correlated, not two independent confirmations.

Select John [[Glasgow-1030]] as the very low-confidence working father of Matthew [[Glasgow-3538]] of Pyperheugh, Stevenston. John and Jonet Mathie married in Glasgow on 25 January 1618 and have recorded children in the following decade. A son of that household, born in the 1620s, could be an adult spouse in the 1665 testament index that names Matthew. This is a compatible generation, not a new birth estimate. The existing Mathie–Glasgow network supplies a positive social route between Glasgow and Stevenston: David Mathy and Robert Glasgow were co-witnesses in 1554, while Matthew Glasgow and John Mathie were together at Ardeer Nether in 1559–60. The later marriage joins those surname networks. The recurrence of Matthew in the same parish makes this existing household a useful selected descent hypothesis; it does not identify a father in a record.

No inspected record names Matthew’s parents or connects his Pyperheugh household directly to the Glasgow couple. The route depends on Jonet’s unproved place in the older Mathie network and on the probable identification of the 1618 husband with Ninian’s brother John represented by [[Glasgow-1030]]. John’s confident Robert [[Glasgow-1096]] father assessment remains conditional on that adult identification and is not newly upgraded. Other Stevenston fathers, collateral descent from the 1559–60 Matthew, and an unrelated same-name family remain possible. The 106-year interval rules out identity with that earlier adult; no missing generation is invented and no direct father edge to him is selected. The Saltcoats sailor John, spouse of Isobell Gray, remains a separate record-defined household; this model does not merge them. The 1619 baptismal witness Mathew Will is not Matthew Mathie or proof of a Glasgow relative. === Possible Mathie–Glasgow family connection ===

The association between the surnames can be followed for more than sixty years. On 10 May 1554 David Mathy and [[Glasgow-1096|Robert Glasgow]] were fellow Glasgow burgesses witnessing the same sasines.<ref name="Mathie1554">Robert Renwick, ed., Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow, vol. 1, protocols 183–184, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc07robegoog/page/n90/mode/2up 10 May 1554, printed p. 63].</ref> David’s son John was recorded in 1571, and may be the writer recorded in 1574 and the John Mathie admitted with the Earl of Glencairn’s burgess group in 1583.<ref name="MathieJohn">Robert Renwick, ed., Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow, vol. 6, protocol 1798, 25 October 1571, printed p. 70, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc05scogoog digitised volume]; vol. 4, protocol 1036, 4 August 1574, printed p. 19, [https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc07scogoog digitised volume]; James R. Anderson, ed., Burgesses & Guild Brethren of Glasgow, 1573–1750, [https://electricscotland.com/history/records/scottishrecordso43scotuoft.pdf entry for 8 February 1582/83].</ref>

In 1600 Katherine Mathy, daughter of John Mathy in Clerkland, married a Glasgow maltman; John Mathie senior and junior witnessed the transaction.<ref name="Clerkland1600">Robert Renwick, ed., Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow, vol. 11, protocol 3710, [https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/abstracts_of_protocols_of_the_town_clerks_of_glasgow__1900.pdf 12 November 1600].</ref> Two successive John Mathies then served the Earl of Glencairn in 1606.

Tutory retours entered on 20 January 1607 identify the deceased John Mathie of Ibert’s full brother as Matthew Mathie, burgess of Glasgow, and name John’s lawful children James, David, Agnes and Margaret.<ref name="Ibert1607">Inquisitionum ad Capellam Domini Regis Retornatarum ... Abbreviatio, vol. 2, tutory retours 100–101, 20 January 1607, [https://books.google.com/books?id=PiVDAAAAcAAJ digitised volume]; underlying register, National Records of Scotland, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browsetreeview.aspx?reference=C22 C22/3].</ref> The timing and Kilmaronock location make John of Ibert a strong candidate for the Glencairn servant killed in July 1606.

Jonet is not among the four children named in those tutory proceedings. She may have been older than the children requiring a tutor, but the younger Glencairn writer is presently the better candidate for her father or close kinsman. Her marriage to [[Glasgow-1030|John Glasgow]] may therefore continue the association first visible between Robert Glasgow and David Mathy in 1554.

The surname also appears as Mathei in the parish indexes. The older suggestion “Morriel” should be checked against the original marriage and baptism images.
