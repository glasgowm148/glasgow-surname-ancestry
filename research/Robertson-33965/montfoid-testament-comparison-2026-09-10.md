# Bessie Robertson: a testable Montfoid testament comparison

## Current conclusion

[Bessie Robertson (Robertson-33965)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Robertson-33965)
is the existing profile for the wife named in the calendar entry for John
Glasgow in Montfoid, confirmed 17 August 1610. A newly examined printed passage
has the same estate and confirmation date, but omits the testator's name.
It is a useful original-record target, not proof of Bessie's death date.

## Sources and distinctions

- Francis J. Grant, *The Commissariot Record of Glasgow: Register of Testaments,
  1547-1800*, [printed p. 426](https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso07scotuoft/page/426/mode/2up),
  Robertson, Bessie, spouse of John Glasgow in Montfoid, 17 August 1610;
  cross-reference p. 196. This calendar finding is already maintained locally.
- James Paterson, *History of the counties of Ayr and Wigtown*, Cuninghame,
  [printed pp. 53-54](https://archive.org/download/historyofcountie0003pt1pate/historyofcountie0003pt1pate.pdf),
  describes Hugh Montfoid and his wife Jean Boyd and quotes an unnamed tenant's
  testament. Lady Montfoid is creditor for rent meal; the tenant died in
  February 1606 and confirmation is given as 17 August 1610. Neither Bessie
  Robertson nor John Glasgow is named in the passage. This is a later printed
  account quoting a record, not an independently inspected original.
- The public WikiTree API identifies Robertson-33965 as the spouse attached
  to Glasgow-3296. Her biography contains the calendar link, not additional
  original testimony; her death field is empty. Profile links do not prove
  a cross-record identity.

## The discriminating test

Obtain the original of Bessie's testament and compare all of:

1. Confirmation on 17 August 1610.
2. Montfoid residence or tenancy.
3. Creditor Jeane Boyd, Lady Montfoid.
4. The distinctive debt `for ane boll of ferme meill`.
5. The opening death wording, including whether it actually says February 1606.

Agreement on the unusual creditor and rent description would be much stronger
than the shared confirmation date. Do not enter February 1606 as Bessie's death
before that comparison, and do not identify the tenant as her husband merely
because the household tenancy may have been described under his name.

## Consequences for the family reconstruction

[John Glasgow (Glasgow-3296)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3296)
currently combines three working identities: the Montfoid husband, the father
in Mid Calder baptisms, and the Quothquan man whose estate was confirmed in
1650. Those links remain uncertain. The 1650 testament's Bessie Glasgow is his
sister, not evidence that the 1610 spouse Bessie Robertson was the same woman.

If Bessie's original proves a 1606 death, reconsider any attribution to her of
children baptised later. First distinguish actual birth dates from baptism
dates and look for a named mother; do not rule on maternity from an index's
baptism date alone. An earlier death would not, by itself, exclude the husband
from later fatherhood.

No structured attachment, date or location has been changed. No new profile
is needed. No original CC9/7 volume/image reference has been inferred from
another person's neighbouring confirmation.
