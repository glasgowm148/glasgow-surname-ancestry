== Biography ==

John Spreull was born in 1616 in Glasgow.<ref name="EightCenturies">[http://sprowlgenealogy.com/ancient-sprowl-history/eight-centuries/ James Richard Sproule, Eight Centuries of the Spreull Family]</ref> He earned an MA at the University of Glasgow in 1635.<ref name="EightCenturies" /> He worked as a writer in law. The council appointed him Town Clerk of Glasgow on 21 October 1645.<ref>[https://www.british-history.ac.uk/glasgow-burgh-records/vol2/pp76-90 Extracts from the Records of the Burgh of Glasgow, vol. 2, pp. 76 to 90, British History Online]</ref> He married Catherine Merchel on 17 November 1640.<ref name="EightCenturies" />

He supported the Covenanting cause. The authorities imprisoned him on 14 September 1660 after he refused the bond on the Western Remonstrance, then released him after he subscribed it.<ref>[https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=nnc1.1000163520&view=1up&seq=163 Andrew Brown, History of Glasgow, vol. 1, p. 131, HathiTrust]</ref><ref>[https://archive.org/details/historyofsuffe01wodr/page/75 Robert Wodrow, History of the Sufferings of the Church of Scotland, vol. 1, p. 75, Internet Archive]</ref> Government banished him for nonconformity.<ref>[https://archive.org/details/historyofsufferi02wodr/page/196 Robert Wodrow, History of the Sufferings of the Church of Scotland, vol. 2, p. 196, Internet Archive]</ref> After his return the council again imprisoned him in the Edinburgh Tolbooth. The Privy Council sent him to the Bass Rock on 28 July 1683. Petitioners secured his release in old age. He died about 1690.<ref>[https://archive.org/details/historyofsufferi03wodr/page/10 Robert Wodrow, History of the Sufferings of the Church of Scotland, vol. 3, p. 10, Internet Archive]</ref><ref>[http://access.bl.uk/item/viewer/ark:/81055/vdc_000000058DB2#?c=0&m=0&s=0&cv=261&xywh=162,431,1896,1151 John Dickson, Emeralds Chased in Gold, p. 232, British Library viewer]</ref><ref>[https://archive.org/details/bassrockitscivil00mcri/page/n531 Thomas M. Crie, The Bass Rock, pp. 370 to 371, Internet Archive]</ref>

== Research Notes ==

Wikipedia gives twelve or more children for the town clerk.<ref>[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Spreul_(town_clerk) Wikipedia, John Spreul, town clerk]</ref> John William Burns states that Ann Spreull was the only child of the town clerk and that she married James Spreull, surgeon in Paisley. Burns also states that the surgeon left one daughter who married James Shortridge in 1700.<ref>[https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=nyp.33433023184579&view=2up&seq=16 John William Burns, Preface, p. vi, HathiTrust]</ref> Parish registers, testaments, and civic records can resolve the child count.

Do not confuse him with his kinsman John Spreull, the apothecary and merchant known as  [[Spruel-6|Bass John Spruel (1646-1722)]], who spent 1681 to 1687 on the Bass Rock. The shorter Bass notice above attaches to the town clerk in some nineteenth century sources.<ref>[https://archive.org/details/historyofsufferi03wodr/page/10 Robert Wodrow, History of the Sufferings of the Church of Scotland, vol. 3, p. 10, Internet Archive]</ref><ref>[https://archive.org/details/bassrockitscivil00mcri/page/n410 Thomas M. Crie, The Bass Rock, p. 251, Internet Archive]</ref>
