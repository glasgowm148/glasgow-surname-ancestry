== Biography ==

'''Robert Sprewel''' was a burgess of Glasgow. Printed county histories place him as a brother of Mr John Sprewel, the vicar of Dundonald. The vicar bought Ladymuir, Castlehill, Kingsmeadows, and Blackairne, and he died in 1555. He left the whole estate to his nephew '''[[Spreul-24|John (Spreul) Spreull (abt.1510-)]]''', the son of Robert, who is named as a burgess of Glasgow. At the Reformation this heir John held Cambuslang. <ref name="Metcalfe104">[https://archive.org/details/historyofcountyo00metc/page/104 William M Metcalfe, A history of the county of Renfrew, 1905, p. 104] full scan</ref> The Electric Scotland summary repeats the same notice and ties the later Provost of Renfrew and the Town Clerk of Glasgow to this line. <ref name="ES-nation">[https://electricscotland.com/history/nation/sprewel.htm Sprewel or Spreull, Electric Scotland]</ref>

Estimated birth year about 1490. Place not recorded. Robert appears in print because the vicar’s will notice names him as father of the heir and calls him a burgess of Glasgow. <ref name="Metcalfe104" />

== Family ==

Parents not named in the cited page. The text sets Robert and the vicar Mr John as brothers from the Cowden family. <ref name="Metcalfe104" /> Likely younger sons of [[Spreule-2|John (Spreule) Spreule of Cowdin (abt.1480-)]]

Spouse not recorded in the cited page. 

== Children ==

* '''John Sprewel''', nephew and heir of the vicar in 1555, later styled of Ladymuir, Castlehill, Kingsmeadows, and Blackairne. His grandson John served as Provost of Renfrew near the start of the reign of Charles the First. The Provost’s son Mr John Spreull became Town Clerk of Glasgow and later one of the principal clerks of Session. <ref name="Metcalfe104" /><ref name="ES-nation" />

== Research Notes ==

The currently attached father [[Spreule-2|John Spreule]] is marked uncertain. Robert's profile says he and the vicar were 'likely' younger sons of John of Cowdin; no cited source names their father.


Metcalfe prints two statements on the same page. The first names Robert as a burgess of Glasgow and father of the heir John, nephew to the vicar of Dundonald. The later sentence speaks of a vicar of Cambuslang who was succeeded by his son and heir, whose son became Provost of Renfrew. Editors can resolve this by reading the Dundonald vicar as the uncle who died in 1555, and the Cambuslang vicar as the nephew who inherited. This keeps Robert as the connector between the Cowden house and the later Renfrew and Glasgow line. <ref name="Metcalfe104" />
