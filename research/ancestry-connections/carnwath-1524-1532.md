# Carnwath and Quothquan Glasgow subjects, 1524–1532

Reviewed: 2026-10-15

## Current conclusion

The publicly readable printed court book replaces the earlier search-only lead. Four forenames occur: Robert, William, John and Thomas. John and Thomas are expressly placed at Quothquan. Their shared byname and locality support a possible local family group, with no chosen degree of kinship. This group is a plausible predecessor to James Glasgow-3910, the Quothquan weaver documented in 1607–1612 and dead by testament registration in 1624. The missing generations remain open. Robert and William are not expressly localized to Quothquan.

These are documentary subjects, not four proved new people. Consolidated biographies, creation-field limits and checked existing candidates are visible at [the public research account](/research/subjects/carnwath-1524-1532/). No named paternal edge has been added; the connectivity measure is unchanged.

## Source and dates

William Croft Dickinson (ed.), *The Court Book of the Barony of Carnwath 1523–1542*, Scottish History Society, third series 29 (1937), [NLS full scan](https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1271/7974/127179749.23.pdf). Body page n corresponds to PDF page n+129. Printed transcription inspected, not the sixteenth-century manuscript.

Dickinson warns at printed p.lxxvi (PDF81) that years may be wrong, entries are out of order, and the same court can occur in several parts of the volume. Appendix B (printed cxviii–cxxii, PDF123–127) supplies cautious reconciled dates. Do not count every indexed page as a separate event. In particular, February 1527 means 1527/28 here; John’s 15 March header has no year and is scheduled [1525/26].

## Identity and connection tests

### John Glasgow of Quothquan, 1525–1532

John of Glesquho (1524/25), John Glesquho among Quothquan tenants (1525/26), and Jhone of Glasgw in Upper Quothquan (1531/32) are treated as one working identity: the same name, locality and recurring Kello, Justice, Robeson and Thomson associates support consolidation. No record here gives his father, spouse or children. “Of Glasgow” is a personal byname in a named Quothquan tenant list; it does not itself prove that the byname was inherited.

- Glasgow-1095: Older unsourced John placeholder associated with the Stirling/Saltmarket research. Activity in 1525–1532 is chronologically compatible; no holding, relative or occupation identifies the Quothquan tenant with the burgh man.
- Glasgow-1093: William’s documented son and Glasgow burgess; adult and married by 1534. His approximate 1510 birth is inferred, so it is not a concrete exclusion from the earlier court lists.
- Glasgow-3181: Hirmanscheillis testator died November 1576. His approximate 1540 birth and uncertain attachment to the burgh son do not independently exclude an earlier adult identity; a long life and a locality bridge would need testing.
- Glasgow-3193: Documented son of James elder of Corsinkell, alive in 1571; the before-1540 birth is only an adult-status estimate. Retained as a weak chronological comparison, with no Quothquan evidence.

Follow Upper Quothquan leases, forfeiture consequences and later tenant succession into the 1607–1624 weaver James Glasgow-3910. Test John and Thomas as possible relatives without choosing a father, brother or intervening generation. Compare any named kin or property recitals with the Saltmarket family.

### Thomas Glasgow of Quothquan, 1527/28

The tenant list begins “in Quodquen” on p.64; Thomas Gleschow falls before the separate Carswell names on p.65. This supports Quothquan itself, not merely the broad barony. John and Thomas form a plausible local family group, with their degree of kinship unknown.



Read the remaining indexed Thomas entries, distinguish repeated court entries, and seek a lease or succession clause naming his family. Live searches found no pre-1600 Thomas candidate; the undated-birth Dublin 1725 and Calton 1886 men have death dates incompatible with this adult.

### Robert Glesquho before Carnwath court, 1524

The passage first abbreviates his forename as robe and then calls him Robert. It does not give his residence, age, occupation or relatives. Presence in the Carnwath court does not make him a Quothquan tenant or establish kinship to John or Thomas.

- Glasgow-1096: Glasgow burgess and fisher documented from 1551. The circa-1520 birth is an estimate, not an exclusion from 1524, although identification would lengthen his known adult career considerably. No shared relative or property has been found.
- Glasgow-4063: Holkham man died 23 March 1559/60, with no recorded age or origins. A former circa-1541 catalogue estimate cannot exclude him; no Norfolk–Carnwath bridge is established.

Seek residence or kin in the original court entry and adjacent tenancy instruments; compare both Roberts’ source-defined careers. Do not turn the litigant’s grain obligation into a stated occupation or tenancy.

### William Glesquho, court witness named in 1524

William is named with John Vyld and Robert Chanslar as witnesses to be called in a case involving Roben Ake and James Scot. The record does not identify his residence, occupation or kin. The possible 1526 remission involving William Glasgow and James Somervile is a useful comparison, not an identification.

- Glasgow-1098: The Glasgow burgess, spouse of Esbell Brade and father of an adult son in 1534, died before 16 March 1535/36. Chronology fits 1524. The live profile correctly leaves birth unknown; the stale circa-1516 estimate is not an exclusion. A possible 1526 remission gives another William to compare.
- Glasgow-3522: Later Glasgow burgess and documented son of John-1093. The circa-1540 birth is estimated, so it alone does not exclude an earlier identity. A witness in 1524 would require an unusually long career and a much earlier birth; no link supports that identification.

Compare the 1524 witness, the 1526 remission and William-1098’s property career for named associates, residence or kin. Keep William-3522 as a weak alternative until source chronology provides a concrete conflict.

## Completion of the indexed reading

John’s remaining appearances include May 1526 (day unresolved) (pp.40–41), 22 January 1526/27 (pp.51–52), and a deferred claim against Jhone Kello on 13 October 1530 (pp.115–117; Appendix B p.cxxi /PDF126 corrects the clerk’s year). The opponent is not called kin. The May heading prints 15 May whereas Appendix B p.cxix lists 26 May; the exact day remains unresolved. These records strengthen the recurring local association without proving a transfer of an individual Glasgow holding.

## Torphichen search lead resolved

The supposed “Wilyame Glasgow” in *The Knights of St John of Jerusalem in Scotland*, edited by Ian B. Cowan, P. H. R. Mackay and Alan Macquarrie (1983), is an index-boundary false hit. Printed p.249 /PDF344 has “Kamis, Wilyame” as the cross-reference for the vicar of Glamis; the next entry is Glasgow the place, with bishop, diocese, fair and toft references. The printed layout was visually checked. This is not retained as a Glasgow-surname person or counted as a new finding. [NLS original index page](https://deriv.nls.uk/dcn23/1266/1367/126613670.23.pdf#page=344). This resolves the old search-only lead, not every possible spelling in the book.
