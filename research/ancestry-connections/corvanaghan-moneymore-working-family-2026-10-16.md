# Corvanaghan and Moneymore: a conditional James family

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

Glasgow-1025 is selected as the very uncertain working father of Glasgow-2988, the 1766 Corvanaghan householder, through preferred provisional identity with Glasgow-1024 of Moneymore. The existing Corvanaghan biography already retains the Moneymore man as a compatible candidate: the places are roughly nine miles apart, the name matches and their circa 1720/1722 births are estimates rather than a real two-year conflict. The 1766 Irish occurrence can precede the compiled 1770 immigration claim associated with the Moneymore family; no post 1766 Irish occurrence has been assigned to the Corvanaghan man. This is a weak locality and chronology preference, not proof of emigration or identity. The older James is already an uncertain father in the Moneymore pedigree. Extending that attachment gives the Corvanaghan account a conditional place in the same family, without inventing an intermediate generation. It inherits two major uncertainties: adult identity and the older pedigree itself.

The older James account’s circa 1700 birth would allow an ordinary father–son interval of about 20–22 years, if the estimates are close. Those guessed years do not independently establish paternity. No contemporary record names the older James as father of either Moneymore James or the Corvanaghan householder. The current older-father attachment is a tree hypothesis, not a clause in the 1766 return, the pension record or the later family history. The older and younger James accounts might themselves be duplicates rather than father and son; if that identity is established, this additional father edge must collapse with it. Their existing Scottish ancestral attachments receive no new support. No mother is selected: the uncertain Rutherford attachment on the Moneymore account is not independently supported for the Corvanaghan man and is not copied.
