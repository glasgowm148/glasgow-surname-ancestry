# Three early working family placements

Reviewed: 2026-10-15

These are selected low-confidence father hypotheses from the existing public research. They turn previously recorded brother/cousin, remarriage and daughter/niece comparisons into a revisable family model. No new source inspection or proved parentage is claimed.

## Michael Glasgow of Stevenston

Selected father: [William Glasgow of Saltmarket](/people/glasgow-1098.html), **uncertain / possible**.

The existing Stevenston study places Michael as a probable brother or cousin of James elder of Corsankell, rather than his son. Michael was an adult witness in the Glencairn estate network in 1549; both appear in the same small estate landscape in the 1559–60 teind list. Their approximate 1525 and 1515 generations fit brothers. Select the brother option provisionally and extend James’s already uncertain father William to Michael. This uses an existing older household without inventing an intervening person. The Mathie–Glencairn network supplies a credible route between the burgh family and these estates.

**Alternatives and limits:** The brother-versus-cousin choice is unresolved, and James’s own father William is uncertain. No record calls Michael William’s son. A cousin through another burgh household, an older generation than the estimated 1525 birth, or a separate family remains possible. Brakpleuch and Dubbs may represent one Michael or two. No mother, exact birth or death is inferred.

## Jonat Glasgow, wife of David Menzeis

Selected father: [Andrew Glasgow of Corsoun, Calder](/people/glasgow-3163.html), **uncertain / possible**.

Jonat is the mother in a Mid Calder baptism in 1609. The existing comparison prefers her possible identity with Jonet Glasgow who married Martin Ramsy in the same parish in 1624, fifteen years later. That Jonet is already placed as Andrew’s testament daughter. Select Andrew as Jonat’s uncertain father through this local identity reconstruction. Her estimated 1585 generation fits Andrew’s household; the same name, parish and feasible remarriage sequence favour this model over the more distant Ninian household.

**Alternatives and limits:** This combines two uncertain identity steps: the 1609 mother with the 1624 bride, and the bride with Andrew’s testament daughter. David Menzeis’s death and the bride’s widowhood are not recorded in the material reviewed. Jonat and Jonet may be different women; retain both profiles and do not count an identity merge. Ninian Glasgow remains a competing father hypothesis. No mother is attached.

## Barbara Glasgow, wife of Johne Wilsoune

Selected father: [Ninian Glasgow](/people/glasgow-3188.html), **uncertain / possible**.

The existing branch assessment already favours Barbara as a daughter or niece of Ninian. Her 1611 South Leith marriage entry fits the Leith setting of Ninian’s possible son William and the adjoining Edinburgh craft households; Ninian’s documented son Robert was a cordiner. The estimated 1590 daughter generation fits Ninian’s estimated 1550 generation. Select daughter as the simpler working placement within this already identified household, while retaining niece as an alternative. This is a combined locality, generation and family-network inference.

**Alternatives and limits:** Neither the marriage entry nor Ninian’s testament names Barbara as his daughter. The proposed Leith brother William is himself uncertain, and shared craft geography is suggestive rather than individual proof. Barbara may instead be Ninian’s niece or belong to another local family. If she is Barbara-567, the existing William-3522 father claim becomes a rival explanation. The adult Barbara identities remain separate, the 1611 date discrepancy stays open, and no mother is assigned.

Keep the confident graph and baseline cohort unchanged. These three parent hypotheses do not merge profiles; possible duplicate identities can make profile counts exceed unique historical people. Metric gains describe the selected model and do not corroborate it.


Jonat’s earlier Ninian candidate was already counted in the working measurement, although absent from the raw tree view. Selecting Andrew replaces that counted candidate and does not earn another branch-reduction credit. Ninian is retained explicitly as an unselected alternative, rather than a second active father.
