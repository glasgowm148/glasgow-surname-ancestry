# Irvine’s 1693 father: a very uncertain Kilwinning childhood

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

John Glasgow-3375 is the selected very uncertain working father of John Glasgow-3018, treating the latter provisionally as the father named with Agnes Martine in the 1693 Irvine child entry. The route adopts the existing comparison with John Glasgow-1434, indexed at Kilwinning on 6 July 1679 as child of John and Barbara Alason. Exact forename and surname, the neighbouring Ayrshire parishes, and a specific recorded child/parent pair make this a usable local model. It is not a merge or an identification of all events currently collected under either account. If the 1679 event was an infant baptism, that child would have been only fourteen by the 1693 entry. This is a serious weakness. Baptism of an older child would allow an older father, but no evidence establishes such a baptism; neither an earlier birth nor unusually young fatherhood is asserted as fact. The current circa-1675 birth is an inherited estimate, not independent support for the match.

Barbara Allison-6866 is separately selected as the very uncertain working mother through the same proposed 1679-child identity. The indexed 1679 entry actually names Barbara Alason; this is not a mother invented from the father choice or supplied from the surname-only 1689 entry. The existing Allison profile represents that Alason/Allason household and is a compatible account. Her circa-1659 birth is also an estimate, and no date, birthplace or marriage is newly established. The mother identification and the adult John identification remain unproved. These two parent links depend on one child/adult comparison; they are not independent confirmations of the proposed family.

The Irvine source is the supplied Scotland’s People OPR index: JOHN GLASGOW, 08/10/1693, parish 595, reference 10 / 12, FR23, parents JOHN GLASGOW/AGNES MARTINE. The previous review interprets this as 8 October; the structured child field instead says 10 August 1693. Both readings remain visible, and an index from a combined births/baptisms collection does not establish a birthday. John Glasgow-1473 is retained as the child-record account. Identification with the John Glasgow younger active as skipper in 1720, the 1719 father of Grizal, or the later bursary recipient remains separate and uncertain. Neither the elder/younger wording nor the new grandparents prove those adult identities or an elder skipper’s paternity. Agnes Martine remains the mother named in the 1693 record; no new spouse or parent-field certainty is assigned to the child account.

The 1725 elder skipper, 1729 Sailors’ Poor Box treasurer, 1734 deceased shipmaster and 1740 living bailie are not newly equated with the 1693 father. The 1734 catalogue describes heirs of a late shipmaster, while the 1740 entry names a living merchant/bailie; those occurrences cannot be one man. Glasgow-1077 remains a compatible candidate for the later bailie, independently of this parent model; the recorded 1690 child is not adopted as the 1693 adult father. The new parent fields are scoped to the 1693 father proposition, not an assertion that the composite biography is one proved life. All later occurrences and original archive references remain available without an invented death date or transfer of records between profiles.

The same 1679 child remains only possibly identical with the Stevenston father of John in 1701 and Adam in 1706. The alleged two-John-sons conflict does not justify excluding the Irvine match when those identities and the survival of the children are unproved. No intervening death, second baptism or remarriage is invented to reconcile the accounts. The already selected West Kilbride John Glasgow-3161 route also uses this childhood comparison, so it provides no independent corroboration here. The retained adult accounts may be duplicates rather than distinct brothers. An older Irvine John, another local parent, or identification of the 1693 father with the older John Glasgow-3375 rather than his proposed son remain alternatives; the last option would collapse the new father edge as an identity duplicate. The older John’s possible identity with minister Glasgow-1073 and earlier ancestry keep their previous uncertainty; this is not a new proved son of the minister.

One father and one mother are selected, both very uncertain and dependent on the same 1679-child/1693-adult identity. This is one new paternal branch connection, not two independent ancestry discoveries. No original parish image, new contemporary kinship clause, certainty upgrade, identity merge or live WikiTree save is claimed. Vital fields, existing parent grades and the separate later adult identities are preserved. Connectivity counts retained profile accounts, including possible duplicates, rather than proved distinct people.
