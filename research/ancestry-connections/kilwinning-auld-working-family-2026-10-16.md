# Kilwinning: possible parents of Mary Auld’s husband John

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

Glasgow-1065 is selected as the uncertain working father of Glasgow-2467, the John recorded with Mary Auld at Kilwinning for Andrew’s baptism on 26 October 1746 and James’s on 19 August 1750. The later John’s existing biography already suggests descent from Glasgow-2463, an old parent reference with a 16 September 1683 Kilwinning date. The current captured family fields of the older household’s children point to Glasgow-1065, whose profile carries that same 1683 date and the Jean Wyllie household. This resolves the intended candidate account; it is not a new historical father statement. A specific route is the recorded Glasgow-2464, baptised at Kilwinning on 19 December 1703 to John Glasgow and Jean Wylie, residence Chappell. Treat that child provisionally as the later Mary Auld husband. If baptised in infancy, he would have been about 42 and 46 at the two known children’s baptisms, compatible ages for a father. The later profile’s before-1728 estimate permits this earlier birth. Exact name, parish, an already recorded descent suggestion and a documented child with named parents give a testable family model. No record yet establishes that the child and adult were the same man.

Wyllie-1044 is separately selected as the uncertain working mother through the same possible 1703-child identity. The 1703 index actually names Jean Wylie; she is not supplied merely because she is the selected father’s wife. Her existing profile also cites the 1712 Margaret and 1715 Matthew entries in the same household. The circa-1682 birth is an estimate, and the records cited here do not supply a marriage date or prove her age. Her identification as mother of the Mary Auld husband remains dependent on that adult’s identification with the 1703 child. The two parent fields therefore represent one conditional family connection, not two independent confirmations.

The existing child-record citations are Kilwinning OPR 599, 10/131 for John in 1703, 20/93 for Andrew in 1746, and 20/106 for James in 1750. The retained Findmypast transcript SCOT/OPR/BAP/3625663 names the 1703 parents and residence Chappell. No original register image has been newly inspected. The baptism does not establish an exact birthday. Glasgow-2468 and Glasgow-2469 retain their existing father and Mary Auld retains her existing spouse and maternal links; no new certainty is assigned to them. The 1703 child and later father remain separate profile accounts, possibly duplicates rather than two proved brothers. No merge is performed, and no vital field changes to 1703 follow from this model.

The older John’s profile also represents a Kilwinning baptism on 16 September 1683. If those child and later-father identities are correct, he was about twenty at the 1703 baptism. This is compatible, but not independent proof that the 1683 child became Jean Wyllie’s husband. The competing Jean Gardiner and Jonet Young mother names in the 1683 indexes remain unresolved, as do the older account’s existing ancestral qualifications. A later son of the Wyllie household, a grandson through a different child, or another local Glasgow father remains possible for Glasgow-2467. The already recorded descent suggestion does not specify a generation. The 1703-child route is selected because it provides a named child in the right parish with feasible later fatherhood, without inventing an intermediate ancestor; it is preferred only weakly.

The nearby Stevenston Glasgow-4170 and Mary Auld-2246 household, documented for Margaret in 1717 and Euphan in 1718, is an alternative family to compare, not an established identity with the Kilwinning John and Mary Auld-1724. Matching the mother’s name does not change the father’s recorded forename or prove that the women were the same person. No new half-sibling, remarriage or widowhood is asserted. Missing corroboration does not exclude either childhood or household comparison. Another Kilwinning John could have fathered Andrew and James. The present model should be tested against the original 1703, 1746 and 1750 residences, witnesses and any marriage entry.
