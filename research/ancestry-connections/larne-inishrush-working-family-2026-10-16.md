# Larne and Inishrush: James’s possible sons Adam and Alexander

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

Glasgow-1509, the older Larne ropemaker, is selected as the uncertain working father of Adam of Inishrush (Glasgow-3902). This adopts the named-family comparison already tested in September. Adam’s gravestone age implies birth about 1758; James’s reported 1737 birth would give an ordinary age-21 generation. The original 1773 affidavit independently places the Larne James’s birth before October 1743, although it does not prove the reported 1737 year. Adam’s proved son James (Glasgow-3903) supplies limited positive naming support. James is a documented adult with a trade and property trail, making him a more useful provisional father than the recordless Glasgow-3905 placeholder. No record names Adam as his child, and the naming argument is weak rather than corroboration of a parent clause.

The same James is selected, with very low confidence, as father of the reconstructed older Alexander (Glasgow-3904). This adopts the existing possible-brother route for Adam and Alexander. On the reported 1737 and estimated 1770 years, James would be about 33 at Alexander’s birth. Making Adam, born about 1758, Alexander’s father instead produces a twelve-year interval. Alexander’s circa 1770 birth is only an estimate, so that calculation does not exclude an Adam father: Alexander could have been born later. However, fatherhood of Daniel by about 1795/96 places Alexander in a generation born by roughly 1775–1778, leaving two short successive generations in the Adam-to-Alexander model. Adam’s wife Unknown-760890, born about 1766/67 from her monument age, cannot be Alexander’s mother on that chronology. The sibling route is therefore preferred provisionally without inventing an earlier wife. Chronological economy does not prove brotherhood or a shared father; neither selected edge is independent support for the other.

A 1760 lease identifies James as a Belfast ropemaker taking a ropewalk at Gardenmore, Larne; the 1768 assignment identifies the same property. This is a real occupational and locality sequence, distinct from the contemporary Larne tanner. Adam is documented much later at Inishrush, and the monument does not state his birthplace. The estimated Inishrush birth cannot exclude a family from elsewhere, but no Belfast birth or Larne-to-Inishrush migration is newly assigned. The 1773 Larne gentleman’s age excludes the younger Glasgow-3991 account; the identity assessment and separation of the two trades retain their previous grades. The reported James/Shutter-46 marriage in 1762 is later than Adam’s birth. It cannot establish Shutter-46 as Adam’s mother. An earlier union, premarital birth or inaccurate reported date would be needed if this father model is right; no such event or person is invented. Shutter-46 is not selected as older Alexander’s mother either: the marriage report and the son identity are independently unproved.

The two local 1740 Tamlaght Hugh households, including Glasgow-3931, and the older Adam-and-Maria naming model remain alternatives for Adam. A different unrecorded father remains possible. Neither Larne property succession nor the Inishrush household totals connect these generations. The 1831 return does not identify its unnamed occupants, and the later plot-29 succession is not evidence of James the ropemaker’s paternity. The shared Glasgow-3905 field is a recently created research placeholder, not evidence that Adam, older Alexander and Glasgow-1367 were brothers. It is not identified or merged with James. No father is extended to Glasgow-1367, and no unnamed extra generation is added.

Older Alexander is himself reconstructed chiefly from late descendant testimony naming Thomas’s parents as Alexander and Rosanna. The 2007 account and its copied 2020 restatement are one source tradition, not independent witnesses. Daniel and Thomas are proved brothers, but their father’s name is not established by a contemporary parent record. The new James father therefore depends on that reconstructed older Alexander account. Younger Glasgow-951 remains a separate, unresolved possible brother or cousin of Thomas and Daniel; the record-defined Glasgow-4090 remains his leading current father comparison. This batch does not select older Alexander as younger Alexander’s father or place that younger family beneath James. No Rosanna surname or October 1822 death date is newly established. James’s own Glasgow-1505 and Glasgow-1499 ancestry alternatives and existing children retain their earlier assessments.
