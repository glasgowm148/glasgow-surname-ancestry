# Leitrim and Tyrone: a very uncertain John-to-John connection

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

Glasgow-551, the John said in the later family history to have found a home in County Tyrone, is selected as a very uncertain working father of Glasgow-3960, gentleman of Gortigillagh, County Leitrim, recorded in 1728. This develops the comparison already recorded in both case files. Keeping their current estimated generations separate gives about 1681 for the older John and about 1705 for the witness: a possible 24-year father–son interval. The proposed connection gives the Leitrim household a testable place alongside the early Irish family tradition. Its preference over an identity match depends on retaining those two estimated generations; neither birth year is independent evidence for paternity. The same uncommon surname and an early Irish setting support investigating kinship, but do not determine its degree. There is no named-father clause or documented Tyrone–Leitrim migration, and no place of birth is inferred from this link.

The first dated occurrence used here is 30 May 1728, registered 11 July: Registry of Deeds memorial 38213, volume 57, page 235. The existing derivative abstract names John Glasgow, gentleman of Gortigillagh, County Leitrim, witnessing a transaction between James Johnston of Cashellmenon, County Donegal, and Robert Johnston of Aghadunvane, County Leitrim, with Bartley McGowan and John Gilhuly. No original has been newly inspected. “1707” is an older inferred birth boundary from assuming an adult witness aged at least 21; it is not a 1707 record. The structured about-1705 birth and County Leitrim birthplace remain estimates, not documentary dates or proof of origin. The notes favour Gortaggle in Annaduff, supported by the near-contemporary Gortogill/Cortogill forms; the older biography favours Gortnasillagh in Rossinver. Both locality assessments remain visible. This parent model uses County Leitrim only and does not resolve the townland to manufacture geographic proximity.

Identity between the two Johns is a live alternative, already raised by the earlier review: the witness could have been much older than his present estimate. If that identity is established, this provisional father edge must be withdrawn as a duplicate-person construction, rather than retained as two generations. A brother, uncle, more distant relative, or another Glasgow family is also possible. The older John’s own descent from Glasgow-591 comes from late family tradition and receives no new support here. His roughly 1681 birth was calculated from his son Hugh’s estimated generation; the broader 1656–1688 range in the profile is not a measured age. No wife or mother is supplied for either John.
