# Libberton and Uphall: selected uncertain parent families

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

Select Johne Glasgow-3093, indexed at Mid Calder in 1648 as son of Jamis Glasgow, as the very low-confidence working father of Robert Glasgow-4168, father at Libberton in 1718–1726. The preferred local route runs through Johne’s existing uncertain James-3332 father identity and that James’s possible identification with the Lindsaylands weaver indexed in 1680. Lindsaylands and Quothquan/Libberton supply the adjacent Lanarkshire family setting; Mid Calder is a separate parish and no migration is proved. A Johne born around 1648 could father Robert in the 1680s–1690s, allowing ordinary adult fatherhood at the 1718 baptism. This generation is preferred over assigning Robert directly to the older weaver, who was dead by 1680 if that identity is correct. It is a specific family hypothesis using recorded men, not a father clause in Robert’s records.

Johne’s only reviewed record is the 1648 child entry; no later occupation, residence, spouse or death identifies him with Libberton. The Jamis-to-Glasgow-3332 identity and the latter’s Lindsaylands identity are both uncertain. Its inherited circa 1634 birth would make him only about fourteen at Johne’s 1648 event if literal; that unsourced estimate must not establish or exclude parentage. The 1648 source names Jamis, not John-1084. The known parish association therefore belongs to a conditional family chain, not to an independently followed migration. James-3332 himself remains an alternative father if Robert was born before 1680 and fathered the later children at an older age. The son James apprenticed in Edinburgh in 1690, explicitly son of deceased James the Lindsaylands weaver, is a genuine alternative intermediate father or uncle; his existing held draft is retained, and no unrecorded generation is invented. The 1624 Quothquan weaver is a separate death account; its early death makes direct paternity of a 1718–1726 father much less plausible. Robert’s merged Gibson/Watson household remains a working combined account, not proof of remarriage or identity between the two women. No mother is selected for Robert, and no vital date is changed to fit the route.

Select John Glasgow-1105 and Agnes Eastone-3 as the uncertain working parents of Thomas Glasgow-1226, the father with Catherine Clarkson/Classon at Uphall from 1717 and Livingston by 1730. This selects possible identity with their named Linlithgow child Thomas Glasgow-1242 of 1675, while retaining both profile accounts. That child would be about 42 at the 1717 Margaret entry and about 60 at the 1735 Hellen entry: later fatherhood, but chronologically possible. The existing Glasgow-1242 account also represents a Thomas/Jean Duncan household at Uphall in 1704. Recurring Thomas name, the same Uphall parish and a plausible succession of households provide a more specific route than the removed Kilbirnie-minister parents. The 1675 parent clause belongs directly to the child account; transferring it to the Clarkson adult depends wholly on the uncertain identity.

Agnes’s maternity is assessed separately: the 1675 FamilySearch parent index explicitly identifies Agnes Eastone in Thomas Glesgow’s entry, while John is named as father in the paired child citation. This is a positive maternal source for that child, not merely John’s spouse field. Agnes’s attachment to the Clarkson adult remains uncertain because the child/adult identity is uncertain. Eastone/Daston readings, her estimated birth and John’s own child/adult identification remain qualified. These two new parent links share one identity premise and are correlated, not independent confirmations of the adult’s ancestry.

The 1675 child, the Duncan adult and the Clarkson adult may represent one, two or three men. There is no inspected age, occupation, marriage, widowhood or probate bridge. The 1701 Duncan marriage is reported family information, not a newly inspected register. No death of Jean, remarriage to Catherine or overlap resolution is invented. The differing Philip 1711 dates in the Thomas research compilation and the Glasgow-1242 biography, and the omission of a mother in the latter’s indexed child, do not establish a continuous Duncan household or certain maternity. They are not two independent confirmations. Catherine’s indexed surname variants remain as recorded. The before 1700 boundary for the Clarkson adult and the 2/21 February 1675 date discrepancy in the child account remain unchanged. The 1666 St Cuthbert’s Thomas-1311, child of Alexander and Issobell Anderson, remains a compatible identity alternative, albeit with fatherhood at about 51–69 in 1717–1735. His possible identification with the Edinburgh writer whose testament was indexed in 1691 is unproved and cannot be used to exclude him. The retained 1766 grave link is an unverified namesake comparison, not an established death for the Clarkson adult. The 1663 Thomas-3091 and an otherwise unrecorded local Thomas also remain possible. No identity merge or live attachment is performed.

Two selected uncertain fathers and one separately assessed uncertain mother, using existing parish, testament and apprenticeship reviews. No new original inspection, certainty upgrade, identity merge, spouse change or vital-date alteration. Robert’s new mother remains unknown. The Thomas parent pair depends on possible child/adult identity; profile-based connectivity retains potentially duplicate accounts and is not a count of proved distinct ancestors.
