## Scope and result

## Findings

### 1. The probate argument does not exclude Thomas and Daniel

**Confirmed reasoning error; high impact.** The 17 September Alexander findings infer that Thomas was not Nancy's sibling from omitted children, then conclude he could not be Alexander's brother. Even if the first proposition were established, the second does not follow: Alexander could share different parents with Nancy and Thomas. Probable siblinghood with John also does not prove a shared father.

The omission argument is applied inconsistently. The same account excuses Alexander's omitted son Robert-938 through possible informant ignorance while ruling out ignorance for Thomas's son Daniel Neely merely because he lived somewhere in New York. Robert's alleged survival to 1902 needs original-record verification; this is an unresolved test of the notice's completeness, not a proved counterexample. No evidence establishes what the informant knew.

The [23 January 1892 notice](https://fultonhistory.com/Newspaper%2024/Plattsburg%20NY%20Republican/Plattsburg%20NY%20Republican%201889-1892/Plattsburg%20NY%20Republican%201889-1892%20-%201042.pdf) concerns real and personal property. The cited pre-1898 restriction on representation in personalty cannot determine all kinship in that mixed-property proceeding. [Matter of De Voe, 107 A.D. 245 (1905)](https://law.justia.com/cases/new-york/appellate-division/1905/107-ad-245.html) expressly distinguishes those property rules; it is a later explanation, not Nancy's decree.

The notice does not name Elizabeth Anderson as a parent. Her identification comes from other records. Multiple printings are copies of one notice, not independent kinship testimony. A roughly thirty-year span of estimated births also does not establish two wives of one father.

**Retained conclusion:** Nancy's explicit niece/nephew terms and the Irish household records support a probable sibling network. John-867's father John is a conditional candidate for Alexander's father; his identity with the Desertoghill flax grower is another unproved step. Obtain the petition, heir schedule, amendments, service papers and decree; compare an expected-versus-cited relative list after checking identities and survival.

### 2. The supposedly exhausted probate route contains overlooked records

**Confirmed contradiction and collection-scope error; high impact.** The 18 September note says the PRONI Will Calendar was searched “pre-1858 and later,” gives universal zeros for Lisnagaver/Larne and treats the older John as intestate. [PRONI's calendar](https://www.proni.gov.uk/will-calendars) starts in 1858; earlier indexes are in the separate [Name Search](https://www.proni.gov.uk/name-search).

The project already contains Thomas Glasgow of Lisnagaver's administration, granted 7 September 1870 to widow Mary Anne, and Larne probate-index entries for William (1783), James (1803), William (1816), and Jane (1844–46). The maintained Larne reconstruction already identifies William-3324's surviving 1815 will, **D300/1/5/49**. These are existing leads overlooked by the blanket conclusion, not newly discovered wills in this audit. Failure to locate an unidentified John's grant proves neither intestacy nor absence of useful probate evidence.

### 3. The Antrim flax-list negative uses the wrong geographical scope

**Confirmed source-coverage error; high impact.** The [companion PDF](https://www.billmacafee.com/1796flaxgrowerslist/1796flaxgrowersantrim.pdf) is headed *Flaxgrowers' List for the Parishes in North Antrim*. The [compiler describes selected northern parishes](https://www.billmacafee.com/18centurydatabases.htm), not all County Antrim or Larne/Kilwaughter. No complete county-wide search was documented to support the broader negative.

The [Desertoghill entry, p.49, no.2070](https://www.billmacafee.com/1796flaxgrowerslist/1796flaxgrowersderry.pdf#page=49), is real: John Glasgow, two wheels. Those wheels represent a premium for flax acreage, not an inventory establishing household size, wealth or exclusive occupation. It cannot distinguish him genealogically from maritime/rope-making relatives. The entry supplies no townland, age or kin.

### 4. The DNA branch age does not date the particular family split

**Confirmed overinterpretation; high impact.** A reported c.1600 TMRCA for FT20271 was used to place the Larne/Lisnagaver split before 1669 and reject an eighteenth-century connection. A whole-branch estimate is not necessarily the common-ancestor date for a subset of descendants. [FamilyTreeDNA's explanation](https://help.familytreedna.com/hc/en-us/articles/6463932665871-Group-Time-Tree) also distinguishes estimated dates and their ranges.

The maintained matrix says Alexander's and James J's tested descendant lines share FT20271; James J's route through Robert and Archibald is unproved, while the separately reported Larne comparison lines lack resolved FT20271 calls. Therefore the argument cannot assume an established Larne tester pedigree and then use the same hypothesis as proof. The c.1600 live estimate itself was not reverified; no private DNA data were republished or reanalysed.

**Reopened question:** a closer joining generation remains possible. Audit the exact descent paths and compare existing permitted SNP evidence before inferring a historical split or recommending another paid test.

### 5. John of Larne's missing baptism is not a proved exclusion

**Confirmed overstatement.** Lack of John in Hugh's monument and the derivative child list supplies no positive son identification, but neither source is established as exhaustive. Withdrawing a naming-pattern attachment is reasonable; declaring John “not a son” or the original register the “single source” that could answer the question is not.

John-4179's c.1745–48 birth is estimated from a 1769 marriage. The proposed son born c.1760–70 is unsupported, mostly predates that marriage, and gives the estimated father an age of roughly 12–25. Resolve actual entries, rather than insert a joining generation. MIC/1B/6 remains a priority alongside succession, wills and deeds.

### 6. The Irvine merge needs original-register review; a birth-month conflict was missed

**Confirmed unresolved record conflict; not proof of two distinct people.** Live Glasgow-4118 redirects to Glasgow-3478. The batch calls them the same John, but the saved records describe a **10 October 1736** baptism and a **5 October 1738** baptism, both with Robert Glasgow and Mary Borland. Same-name successive children are plausible; an index error must also be tested.

The live biography already preserves the distinction and suggests an earlier child who died young, so the earlier source was not silently lost. However, it states a **4 October 1738** birth while the saved [Findmypast 1738 transcript](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FOPR%2FBAP%2F3574782&tab=this) gives **4 September**. The [1736 transcript](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=SCOT%2FOPR%2FBAP%2F3574593&tab=this) is a different entry. Both originals are needed before another merge, split, or exact-date correction. A complete draft now exposes the conflict.

The known-wrong 4163→1139 merge and subsequent Sarah reparenting were already acknowledged in the batch ledger. Preserve that action history; do not count it as a validated duplicate resolution.

### 8. A broken source field blocked local regeneration

**Confirmed provenance error.** The Desertoghill flax row in `www/map/data/records.csv` had descriptive prose in `source_url`. The builder rejected it as a non-external URL. Restored the checked PDF/page URL and corrected the same row's unsupported Larne-absence claim. The affected relationship claims also lacked explicit source links, allowing an unrelated Mary Glasgow death entry to be selected as their citation. The maintained rows and public evidence now point to the inspected probate notice. These were provenance faults discovered during integration, not new historical conclusions.
