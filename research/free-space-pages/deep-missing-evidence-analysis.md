# Deep evidence audit of the saved free-space pages

Completed **29 August 2026** against all 71 saved `.wiki` pages, the local
evidence capture and a live WikiTree person search. The machine audit ranked
219 passages; each ranked passage and each record-defined unlinked person was
then reviewed manually.

## Current conclusion

Most apparently strong passages had already reached the relevant live profile.
The genuinely missed material falls into two groups:

1. the detailed 1851 Glenleslie return can materially improve two sparse
   profiles and exposes two false same-name links; and
2. the medieval and early-modern pages contain several independently
   record-defined people. Pre-1500 subjects belong on individual free-space
   pages linked from the consolidated early-bearers page, not on person profiles.

The audit found no new evidence justifying removal of a populated relationship
or location. Speculative parentage narratives, AI/OCR testament readings and
same-name conflations were excluded.

### Medieval update — 6 September 2026

unverified report, and unsourced FamilySearch pedigree GWZP-L6B is quarantined
for an impossible displayed father-child chronology. The [full integration

certainty grades.

is George Glasgow's 40-year-old wife in household 20; the 34-year-old visitor

The same Glenleslie table linked its deceased William Alexander to

Irish labourer who died in 1846. The saved table now retains the named William
and all his evidence without the false profile link. The Irish man has a
[HOLD handoff](../Glasgow-1022/1846_Ireland_Glenleslie_William_Alexander.md)

The same Irvine page repeats baptism details for

already in their structured fields and the page supplies only `IGI`/`VR`

## Important exclusions

- Pages 001 and 041 contain partial or conflicting AI/OCR testament readings.
  Do not transfer names or relationships until the original images receive a
  reliable diplomatic transcription.
- Page 029's preferred parentage for John-1022 is a hypothesis, not a record.
  The census proves household relationships only.
- Page 033 mixes same-name men and parish records; do not transfer identities
  from it without matching the original register entry.
- Page 062 and parts of pages 026, 039, 042, 070 and 071 add historical or
  kinship claims beyond what the cited record says. Drafts use the consolidated
  claim-by-claim audit on page 005 and retain only record-supported facts.
- The 1506 alias-Smith purchaser is not proved to be the 1527–1553 Saltmarket
  owner. The latter is independently profile-worthy; the alias remains a
  research note.
- Sir Allan Smith is not proved to be John alias Smith's father or relative;
  Robert Watson to Agnes is the transaction's only explicit descent.
- FamilySearch GWZP-L6B has no attached historical sources and displays a
  child born ten years before the stated father. Do not use it as independent
  support for Master John to William.
- A locative `de Glasgu` occurrence does not by itself prove a hereditary
  surname, descent from another bearer, residence at the event date, or family
  connection to later Glasgow lines.
