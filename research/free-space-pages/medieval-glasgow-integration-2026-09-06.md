# Medieval Glasgow evidence integration — 6 September 2026

## Current conclusion

The new material adds two distinct 1283 associated persons and improves three source trails, but it does not join the established medieval Glasgow bearers into a pedigree. No pre-1500 person profile should be created. The two Alexanders belong on separate free-space research pages because the companion acts distinguish their fathers by different offices.

## Top ten findings

1. **Certain:** the 1283 receipt directly names Alexander as son of the late Richard, messenger of Glasgow. This is a proved father–son relationship, but not proved Glasgow-surname transmission.
2. **Certain:** the companion agreement names a different Alexander as son of Richard, former constable of Glasgow, and as a neighbouring landholder.
3. **High:** the two 1283 Alexanders must remain separate working identities; the abbreviated description “Alexander son of Richard, Glasgow, 1283” is ambiguous.
4. **Moderate:** Richard the messenger's son is the strongest local candidate yet found for the 1289/90 escheator because he was an active Glasgow property-holder only six or seven years earlier. No identifying bridge exists.
5. **High:** the older Dublin Alexander remains a possible escheator identity, but the fifty-year interval and absence of shared property, office or associates now make him the weaker of the two specific candidates.
6. **Certain:** the 1506 catalogue description proves Robert Watson as father of Agnes, his daughter and heir. It does not prove a relationship between Sir Allan Smith and John de Glasgow alias Smith.
7. **High:** the four-shilling High Street annual rent and adjoining Smith property provide a specific succession trail; the complete BL/204 instrument and later conveyances are higher-value targets than surname-only searching.
8. **High:** Gilbert's printed *Historic and Municipal Documents of Ireland*, pp. 112–123, makes the Dublin Free Citizens roll accessible online. The search produced no secure new Glasgow identification, but damaged text, OCR and uncertain completeness make that only a limited negative.
9. **Certain at calendar level:** Bain II no. 409 supplies the old receipt locator Chapter House (Scots Documents), Box 94, No. 5, together with the date, parties, amount, mutilated state and absence of a seal. The current TNA reference and original reading remain unresolved.
10. **High:** David de Glasgu is an unverified report, not a probable bearer. FamilySearch GWZP-L6B is also quarantined: it has no attached sources and displays a child born ten years before the displayed father.

Additional relationship clauses were preserved without forcing them into the Glasgow pedigree. The companion 1283 agreement says the exchanged property had formerly belonged to the deceased Cristiana Briane. Hugh de Glasgw's deed calls Peter de Castro the son of the deceased Alexander de Castro; that is a de Castro relationship, not evidence about Hugh or either Alexander son of Richard.

## Research framework

Descent and surname transmission are separate questions. A reliable instrument may prove a father–child relationship even when the child uses another byname. Conversely, two people called Glasgow do not become relatives because their dates fit. One reliable instrument explicitly naming both a Glasgow-designated father and a Glasgow-designated son can demonstrate the relationship and name attribution in that act; a second independent document is not an artificial prerequisite, though wider identity claims still need corroboration.
