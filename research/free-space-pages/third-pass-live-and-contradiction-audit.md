## Newly found substantive contradictions

### John de Glasgu, 1259

Several older pages still merged or genealogically connected:

* John de Glasgu, Bishop Gamelin's chaplain in 1259;
* an earlier John of Huntingdon, official of Glasgow;
* John of Glasgow in the Holyrood community in 1299; and
* Master John, chancellor of Dunkeld from 1377.

The records do not support those joins. The 1259 act does not call John “Master”, name his parents or make him Gamelin's brother. The Holyrood record is forty years later and has no repeated office, patron, associate or property. The Dunkeld chancellor belongs to a separate later career.

Evidence-led local replacements are now staged for:

* page 039 — John de Glasgu, Gamelin's chaplain;
* page 042 — Master John de Glasgow, chancellor of Dunkeld;
* page 032 — John of Huntingdon, official of Glasgow;
* page 024 — Glasgow surname origins.

Targeted corrections are also staged in pages 004, 019, 025, 026 and 062 so the rejected relationships do not survive elsewhere.

### Roger de Duffeld and R-FT32941

The page's “1190 Poll Tax” citation is a repeated secondary error. Modern surname scholarship cites a 1190 Yorkshire **Pipe Roll**. The citation proves a name occurrence but does not make Roger a landholder, administrator, Norman retainer, resident of Duffield Castle or ancestor of a Glasgow bearer.

No ancient DNA or documentary male line assigns Roger, John of Huntingdon, John de Glasgu or Adam de Glasgu to R-FT32941. Local evidence-led replacements are staged for pages 050 and 059. Page 012 now begins with a prominent evidence warning and points to the corrected medieval assessment; its retained older narrative still needs a later full editorial compression.
