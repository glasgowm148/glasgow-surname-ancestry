## Record-by-record analysis

### 1. Robert Glasgow, shipmaster, 1666

Argyll Estates Archives catalogue `GB 3452 ARG-04-03-67-78`, p. 149,
describes a receipt by Robert Glasgow, master of the *John of Portrush*, for
transporting barrels of coal for the earl of Argyll. The record defines an
adult occupational identity. It gives no residence or family, and the vessel
name does not prove residence at Portrush.

Reviewed profiles `Glasgow-1078`, `Glasgow-3970`, `Glasgow-3938`,
`Glasgow-1149` and `Glasgow-3357` have no vessel, occupation, family, signature
or exact-event bridge. `Glasgow-2867` was a different shipmaster almost ninety
years later. These are weak name/context candidates and should not prevent
creation. Use birth about 1640, uncertain; birthplace unknown; death after
1666, uncertain; shipmaster; no relationships.

Source: [Argyll catalogue](https://www.argyll-papers.com/downloads/campbell-family-dukes-of-argyll-papers.pdf),
`ARG-04-03-67-78`. The 1688 Ayr notarial occurrence, NRS `GD86/741`, remains an
unproved possible continuation.

### 2. Thomas Glasgow, court bills, 1684–1701

All three entries are a high-confidence match to `Glasgow-3556`. The 1684
Exchequer image describes the opposing Bryson/Brison as “of Raphoe.” The 1692
Chancery image names Abraham Creighton, Thomas's repeated 1692–1695 deed
associate. The 1701 Exchequer image names Thomas Glasgow, Esq., as plaintiff
against John Moffett, William Humphrys, Archibald Sinclair and Margaret
Sinclair. Rare name, title, locality and associate provide independent identity
bridges and extend Thomas's life boundary to after 15 May 1701.

`Glasgow-3943` is supported only by a 1725 Dublin burial and is a weak name-only
candidate. `Glasgow-3964` was a Dominican friar in Rome and Italy through this
period and is incompatible. Sources: [1684 Exchequer image](https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F007635147%2F00191&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001584141),
[1692 Chancery image](https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F007635177%2F00507&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FCHANCERY%2F0000106395),
and [1701 Exchequer image](https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F007635145%2F00152&parentid=IRE%2FCOURT%2FEXCHEQUER%2F0001527519).

### 3. Nathaniel Glasgow, 1707

FMP/Dobson `US/GPC/02394306` is Nathaniel's 1707 M.A. graduation at the
University of Glasgow and describes him as `Scoto-Hibernus`. That event is
already present on `Glasgow-1136`. “Scots-Irish” is the derivative source
title, not a United States residence; the Americas/United States label is a
collection grouping. Source: [Findmypast transcript](https://www.findmypast.co.uk/transcript?id=US%2FGPC%2F02394306&tab=this).

### 4. John Glasgow, Antigua appointment, 1707/08

Fothergill's exact entry reads “Glasgow, John, Antegoa, March 10, 1707-1708.—
Money Book, 19-200.” The introduction places it among Church of England
ministers and schoolmasters awarded £20 after appointment by the Bishop of
London to a colonial cure, while warning that some recipients did not proceed.
The record proves an Anglican appointment and bounty, not voyage or residence.

`Glasgow-1073` was a Church of Scotland Presbyterian minister continuously at
Kilbirnie from 1688 to death in 1721; his son Samuel was baptised there on 9
March 1707. He is strongly excluded. Create the record-defined appointee with
birth about 1680, uncertain; birthplace unknown; death after 10 March 1707/08,
uncertain; and no migration claim or relationships. Source: [Fothergill full
text](https://archive.org/stream/listofemigrantmi00fothuoft/listofemigrantmi00fothuoft_djvu.txt),
FMP `US/EMIGRANTMINISTERS/0394`.

### 5. Robert Glasgow, Ballykeel lease, 1738

The image reads “Glasgow, Robt., Ballykeel, Co. Ant. Lease. Hill Docts. 1738.
(O.D.).” It is a lease index, not migration evidence. Ballykeel is in
Kilwaughter parish. `Glasgow-1143` was a Kilwaughter farmer and elder, married
Rachel Wilson in 1729, had local children in 1731, 1733 and 1738, and already
has a 1738 Agnew lease in his chronology. This combined exact place, date and
property context is a high-confidence match. Source: [Findmypast image](https://search.findmypast.co.uk/record?id=US%2FGPC%2FIMAGES%2F9780806308548-002%2F0315&parentid=US%2FGPC%2F00345374),
FMP `US/GPC/00345374`.

### 6. Hugh Glasgow, householder entry 1272, 1740

Two separate Hugh Glasgow households occur in Tamlaght O'Crilly: entries 1195
and 1272. Entry 1272 immediately precedes Hugh Ellis and James McCullough. The
late family history for `Glasgow-548` says he moved from Tyrone to Londonderry,
worked for a McCullough and married Margaret McCullough. This is substantive
locality/FAN evidence and makes `Glasgow-548` the leading candidate, but the
return gives no age, wife, occupation or townland and the late account contains
known errors. Assigning it to `Glasgow-548` would overstate the evidence. The
record nevertheless proves that entry 1272 was a separate household from entry
1195, now represented by `Glasgow-3931`. Create entry 1272 as a record-defined
unconnected person, while recording `Glasgow-548` as a possible future merge
candidate. Do not merge them unless a later record supplies the missing
identity bridge.

Sources: PRONI `T808/15258`; [collector-order transcription](https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/books/Kilrea/Kilrea07.html);
[Macafee surname transcription](https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/1740religiousreturnsulstername.pdf);
William Melanchthon Glasgow, [*History of the Glasgow Family*](https://www.wikitree.com/photo.php/0/0a/Glasgow-590.pdf),
pp. 2–3.

### 7. Hugh Glasgow, declaration, 1772

The declaration names Hugh only among inhabitants of Tamlaght O'Crilly parish.
It supplies no age, occupation, household, townland or relative. The two 1740
adult Hughs could have survived; `Glasgow-540` is a plausible younger-generation
candidate; and `Glasgow-548`, `Glasgow-3931`, and the record-defined entry-1272
Hugh remain possible. The notice therefore does not independently define
another person. Preserve it as an unassigned occurrence in the early-Ireland
free-space documentary cluster; do not create, map, merge or attach it. Source:
*Belfast News-Letter*, 14 April 1772, declaration dated 6 April;
[transcription](https://arborealis.ca/records/newspapers/kilrea-news/1770-1779/#17720414).

### 8. Thomas Glasco, Dublin watchmaker, 1779–1783

Watson's directory lists “Thomas Glasco, watchmaker, 20 Ormond Quay, Dublin” in
1783. Under its heading for 19 July 1779, a marriage notice reports Thomas
Glasco of Christchurch Lane, watchmaker, marrying Miss Hynes of Ormond Quay.
The following August heading belongs to Christopher Harvey's death. The
marriage and directory entries almost certainly concern the same man: they
combine rare name, occupation and an address transition to the wife's street.

It still does not choose among `Glasco-143`, whose estimated 1766 birth and
reported 1785 Mary King marriage lack substantive record support;
`Glasco-144`, securely the 1773 Margaret Duffy groom but with no evidence of her
death or a Hynes remarriage; and `Glasco-173`, securely Sarah's father with wife
Catherine in 1760 but with no later trade or address bridge. The records cited
on those profiles also do not prove the attached parent-child chain
`Glasco-173` → `Glasco-144` → `Glasco-143`. The Philip Glasco watchmakers in
Christchurch Lane add family/trade context but not kinship. Preserve the
1779–1783 tradesman as a free-space documentary cluster rather than inventing a
serial marriage or fourth profile. Sources: [Watson's 1783 directory](https://www.swilson.info/wat1783tr.php?brw=sur&brwkey=Glasco)
and [1779 newspaper extraction](https://eddiesextracts.com/extracts1700/snl1773_99.html).
