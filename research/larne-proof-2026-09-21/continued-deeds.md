### Elizabeth Glasgow and Jane Stewart's estate, 1823

Memorial **548257**, p. 522, assignment dated **8 November 1823**, registered **17 May 1826**: [image 359](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJ7-9935-Y). Elizabeth Glasgow of Larne, widow, is **administratrix of Jane Stewart late of Curran, County Antrim, deceased**. Roger Brown Curry, a Royal Navy lieutenant, is the assignee. The security is a south-side Newtown Larne tenement and garden, 21 feet frontage by 189 feet deep, beside the lane to Larne Quay and James Mitchell's property. Witnesses are William Walsh McNeill, attorney, and James Glasgow, writing clerk, both of Larne.

Strong candidate: Elizabeth Stewart-72380, widow of William-3324; **not proved**, since neither her late husband nor any relationship to Jane is stated. An administratrix need not be a daughter or sister. This is a separate title from the north-side James–Anne–Jane chain. Retrieve Jane's administration and the earlier mortgage; identify the writing clerk independently.

The original printed monument illustration/transcription in *Ulster Journal of Archaeology*, second series 6 (1900), [p. 47](https://archive.org/details/ulsterjournalofa06ulstiala/page/47/mode/1up), was inspected: Elizabeth died **17 September 1832, aged 60**, giving birth about **1772**, and William died **5 October 1816, aged 53**. The inscription establishes the spouses and their daughters, but not Elizabeth's maiden surname or parents. Stewart-72380 and Glasgow-3324 were saved live and verified; the uncertain estate identification is stated explicitly.

### Mary Glasgow–John McGavock marriage settlement, 1843

Indented marriage settlement **10 February 1843**, registered **18 February 1843**; [opening, image 499](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-FQ3Q) and [continuation, image 500](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-F3BZ), both original images inspected. The complete book/memorial reference remains to be checked; do not infer it solely from the following entry numbered 159.

Parties: **John McGavock of Larne, innkeeper**; **Mary Glasgow of Larne, spinster**; trustees **James Smyth of Larne, merchant**, and **Henry Magee of Curran, gentleman**. The marriage is intended, not certified as solemnized on the deed date. Mary conveys an undivided half share of tenements on both north and south sides of Newtown, together with a field and adjoining houses north of Pound Road. The tenements are held under Antrim family leases/renewals; the field is conveyed forever.

An endorsed condition says that if **£250** is not paid to John McGavock by the trustees or executors of **Hugh Glasgow, deceased**, the uses declared of the premises formerly held by Jane Bell and those in the lane to Pound Road are void. **This establishes a settlement/estate connection, not that Hugh was Mary's father.** The will's date, Hugh's residence, and a father/daughter description are not supplied. Witnesses: William Walsh McNeill, solicitor, and John McCalmont, his clerk; McCalmont's affidavit at Larne, **14 February 1843**.

**Leading relationship hypothesis, low confidence:** the Hugh whose estate was to provide £250 in the marriage settlement was the father of **Mary, the intended bride of John McGavock**. A deceased father's provision for a daughter's marriage is a coherent explanation of the recorded estate payment; it is an inference from the transaction, not a stated relationship or an independently established custom applicable to this deed. An uncle, sibling, grandfather or other testator remains possible. The endorsement establishes death by February 1843; it supplies no birth, will or death year.
