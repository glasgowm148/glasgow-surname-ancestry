## Documentary audit

- **Belfast free commoner, not automatically burgess:** Young, *Town Book of the Corporation of Belfast* (1892), printed p.271, contains James's mariner admission, already quoted in Glasgow-3172/research_brief.md as 21 January 1669/70: `James Glasco Marriner Admitted and sworne a ffree Comoner`, fee ten shillings. [Volume](https://archive.org/details/townbookofcorpor00belf), [independent scan OCR](https://archive.org/download/townbookcorpora00irelgoog/townbookcorpora00irelgoog_djvu.txt). This audit recovered the actual OCR occurrence as **James Glosco**, near the end of p.271. It contains no kin or apprenticeship/master clause. Compare neighbouring admissions expressly saying `Served his time`: James's entry does not offer that origin route. The exact day is inherited from the prior image reading; this pass's attempted image endpoint returned the wrong page, so did not newly verify day or fee visually. A free-commoner admission is not proof he held the narrower governing burgess office.
- **Ships, not birthplace:** the existing detailed brief supplies PRONI MIC19/2 letters of 29 October 1667 (*Blessing* of Saltcoats, James master, Belfast butter for Dublin/Rouen) and 8 November 1679 (*James of Belfast*, James master, butter/tallow for Havre). The ship's port must not become James's birthplace or fatherland. [PRONI catalogue](https://apps.proni.gov.uk/eCatNI_IE/SearchPage.aspx). These exact letters were not newly recovered as originals in this pass.
- **1686 extract:** [NLI MS_UR_012684](https://sources.nli.ie/Record/MS_UR_012684), PRONI T/828/15, remains the precise surviving-copy target. Web open returned internal error; direct HTTP returned 403. Searches did not expose a mirror, text or image. Therefore this is access failure, not evidence that no extract survives.

[Holmes research's Macartney-letter extracts](https://holmes-history.com/belfast/) identify MIC19/1–2 as business letters and give examples of ship/port identification, but contain no Glasgow kin. They suggest a concrete check of Scottish customs counterpart records, not a familial connection: the *James of Belfast* was mastered by **James Holmes** at the Clyde on 19 May 1682 (reported NRS E72/19/21). Ship continuity does not identify a common master or establish inheritance. Inspect that original alongside the 1679 Glasgow letter to determine whether a sale/master change occurred; do not merge James Holmes and James Glasgow. This is an additional, accessible archival direction beyond merely repeating T/828/15.

The decisive reading request is still narrow: T/828/15's complete Glasgow extract, including heading, date qualification, every beneficiary, executor, witness and property clause; then the Macartney letters naming ships and counterpart customs documents.
