The [20 December 1764 Ballykeel lease](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-33Q6-L), memorial 159661, explicitly names **“Jas Glasgow son of the sd Robt”** on its [continuation](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSH3-33QZ-Y). Robert of Ballykeel/Kilwaughter leased Hugh Shannon half the lower farm, about 31½ acres. Both original images were inspected. This directly strengthens [Robert-1143](/people/glasgow-1143.html) → [James-1144](/people/glasgow-1144.html); Robert's father and the connection to the 1653/1669 householders remain unproved. The third life is Elinor Jones otherwise Agnew, the landlord's daughter.

**Memorial 63221 ([FamilySearch image 542](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSN1-FXR2)).** The original lease dated **10 March 1736**, registered 10 April 1738, names William and Hugh Glasgow of Larne as joint lessees of two north-side Newtown tenements. The named lives are George II, Frederick Prince of Wales and Prince William Augustus, not relatives; witnesses include James Stenhouse and William Harrison. This corrects the former 12 March date. It states no kinship or parentage.

**Memorial 159181 ([FamilySearch images 257–258](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJM-MJX4)).** The deed poll dated 4 June 1757, manuscript pp.473–474, refers to a head lease granted by William and Hugh Glasgow of Larne. It independently confirms the joint-lessor association by 1757; the head-lease date, heirs and kinship remain unstated.

The next decisive steps are the Agnew tenant comparisons and the named lives/recitals in the **unexamined 1751 and 1767 endorsements** to the 1736 lease. William's 1815 copy will and Elizabeth's 1824 renewal remain unread. The Belfast captain's 1686 will date does not itself prove death; the 1669 Kilwaughter householder's continuity into 1726 also needs testing.

### Two assignments checked — 21 September 2026

The [ropewalk assignment, memorial 173104, p. 510](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-DDXW), calls its witness James Glasgow **Tanner**. The separate [messuage assignment, memorial 173108, opening, image 259](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-DDD6), calls its witness James Glasgow **Farmer** on [p. 513](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-DDDL). Both are dated 28 March and registered 2 November 1768; both repeat the occupation in the affidavit and name John Houston as another witness. The first concerns a 31-year ropewalk lease at £1 14s 1½d yearly; the second a 60-year messuage lease at £5 2s 6d. The tanner evidence remains valid. The farmer’s identity and whether the witnesses were the same man remain unresolved; no kinship is stated.

The [Larne family monument transcription, p. 47](https://archive.org/details/ulsterjournalofa06ulstiala/page/47/mode/1up), records [Elizabeth, William Glasgow’s wife](/people/stewart-72380.html), dying 17 September 1832 aged 60, supporting birth about 1772. ### A new will target: Mary’s marriage settlement, 1843

An [original marriage settlement dated 10 February 1843](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-FQ3Q), registered 18 February, names John McGavock of Larne, innkeeper, and Mary Glasgow of Larne, spinster. She conveys half shares of north- and south-side Newtown tenements and property north of Pound Road. The [continuation](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJW-F3BZ) makes part of the settlement conditional on £250 being paid by the trustees or executors of **Hugh Glasgow, deceased**. Both original images were inspected.

This is a **documented estate connection with unresolved identities**. It does not call Hugh Mary’s father, give the will’s date, or establish that the intended marriage occurred that day. Mary has not been identified with William’s daughter Mary-3989, and the testator has not been assigned to either earlier Hugh. Hugh’s will and the earlier leases behind Mary’s half shares could reveal the missing succession. Trace these alongside Elizabeth’s 1824 renewal and the separate Anne–Jane renewal of 1832; shared streets alone do not establish descent.

The [original 1851 household return](https://nationalarchives.ie/collections/search-the-census/view-pdf/?doc=007246521_00320&c19=true) lists John McGavock of Larne Cross Street, merchant and innkeeper, as a widower married in 1824, 1827 and 1843. Its [death table](https://nationalarchives.ie/collections/search-the-census/view-pdf/?doc=007246521_00321&c19=true) lists wife Mary, aged 37, dying of jaundice in autumn **1843**. This is a strong candidate household, but no maiden name is supplied. William’s daughter Mary is commemorated as dying on 18 October **1844**, also aged 37. The conflicting year prevents a secure identification; inspect the marriage and burial registers and trace the property before joining these records.

**Provisional placement, low confidence:** Hugh is a possible father of Mary, the intended bride, with the estate payment representing a provision for her marriage. This is the leading relationship hypothesis from the transaction, not its explicit wording. It provisionally places him in a later Larne generation around [William-3324](/people/glasgow-3324.html). Identifying him with [Hugh-1499](/people/glasgow-1499.html), who died in 1767, would require a trust or estate continuing about 76 years; the memorial does not establish that history. If the bride proves to be William’s daughter Mary-3989, her monument establishes William as father and Hugh would instead be a collateral relative, possibly an uncle. Hugh’s own parentage remains unproved; the comparative working ranking below identifies the preferred candidates.

[Hugh-3931](/people/glasgow-3931.html), an adult householder at Tamlaght O’Crilly in 1740, is a **possible father of the testator, with low confidence**. The generations can fit, but the [1740 list](https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/books/Kilrea/Kilrea07.html) gives no children and names another Hugh. No inspected record distinguishes the proposed father from those alternatives. The settlement gives no residence for the deceased Hugh: Larne is the bride’s residence and the property’s location, so Tamlaght must not be treated as a geographic exclusion. Hugh-3931 → the testator → Mary is a testable chain of two uncertain links, not established descent.

### Preferred father candidates for the later Hugh

For an explicitly uncertain placement, **[James Glasgow-3991](/people/glasgow-3991.html) ranks first**, **[John Glasgow-4179](/people/glasgow-4179.html) second**, and **[Hugh Glasgow-3931](/people/glasgow-3931.html) remains an alternative**. James and John belong to a suitable generation for a son Hugh who could father the 1843 bride. James is explicitly a son of elder Hugh in the [Larne monument](https://archive.org/details/ulsterjournalofa06ulstiala/page/47/mode/1up); John's connection to elder Hugh is already uncertain. A son called Hugh and granddaughter called Mary would be compatible with the names in that family, but naming patterns do not establish either relationship. The reported Mary sister comes from the derivative congregational chronology and remains conditional for John.

**Preferred working chain:** elder Hugh-1499 → documented son James-3991 → possible son Hugh, deceased by February 1843 → possible daughter Mary, McGavock's intended bride. James's Larne household and property context make this a more specific model to test; no inspected record yet names his son Hugh or matches the bride's property to his. Both new links have low confidence. John's model remains viable, but using his assumed father to support another attachment must not turn the first assumption into evidence. The explicitly provisional placement has now been applied live, as described below; no new kinship evidence was discovered merely by creating the profiles.


### New profiles and uncertain links — 21 September 2026

The current working chain is [James Glasgow-3991](/people/glasgow-3991.html) → **uncertain son [Hugh Glasgow-4180](/people/glasgow-4180.html)** → **uncertain daughter [Mary Glasgow-4181](/people/glasgow-4181.html)**. Mary's probable identification with the census wife supplies the estimated birth about 1806, uncertain death in 1843 and married name McGavock. She may still be [Mary-3989](/people/glasgow-3989.html), whose monument gives death **18 October 1844 aged 37** and names [William-3324](/people/glasgow-3324.html) as father. A confirmed identity would require withdrawing the new Hugh-father hypothesis and retaining William's documented fatherhood. The marriage/burial records, original stone, Hugh's will and title succession remain the discriminating evidence.

### A 1733 Connor index is not the 1740 Ballyboggy tenant — 21 September 2026

The [PRONI Name Search](https://apps.proni.gov.uk/ProniNames_IE/SearchPage.aspx) detail for John Glasgow of Kilroot in **1733** is a Connor Diocesan administration-bond index. The original is lost; it gives no administrator, relative, occupation or exact death date. It cannot be assigned to the personally active 1740 Ballyboggy co-lessee John-3959, and does not prove John-3990 as his father. John-3990 remains an uncertain father hypothesis only.
