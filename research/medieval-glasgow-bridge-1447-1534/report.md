# Documentary bridge investigation: Glasgow surname, 1447–1534

Research completed 30 August 2026. This is an evidence-led handoff, not a
pedigree. Place references, locative descriptions and hereditary-surname claims
are kept distinct.

Short citation used below: Joseph Robertson, ed., *Liber Collegii Nostre
Domine: Registrum Ecclesie B.V. Marie et S. Anne infra Muros Civitatis
Glasguensis* (Maitland Club, Glasgow, 1846). Robertson printed the collegiate
register from the better of two authenticated contemporary copies then held in
Glasgow city archives.

## 1. Executive finding

The search found a material **property bridge**, but not yet a proved paternal
bridge.

An older John Glasgow held a tenement on the west side of the street from
Glasgow's market cross to the South Port on 2 May 1527. It lay **between John
Rankin on the south and James Young on the north** and owed a four-shilling
annual rent to James Houston's foundation. A newly recovered instrument dated
**20 February 1528/29** places **William Glasgow immediately north** of the
adjoining Henryson/Houston parcel, with Andrew Craig immediately south. The
edition's contents explicitly dual-date the act `20 Februarii 1528-9`. That
parcel is demonstrably the one later held by Rankin: Houston resigned it to
John Rankin in 1535 with the same William-north, Craig-south and Our-Lady-west
boundaries. This reduces the unlocated John-to-William transmission to about
**twenty-one and a half months**.

In February 1534/35 Rankin's same parcel was bounded by **William Glasgow on
the north** and Andrew Craig on the south. In March 1535/36 James Young's
adjoining property was bounded by **the late William Glasgow on the south** and
Robert Cochran on the north. A St Andrew prebend rental in Houston's foundation,
confirmed 3 June 1542 and transumed 8 January 1548/49, then charged the same
four-shilling annual upon `the tenement of the late William Glasgow`, between
the late John Crownar north and John Rankin south. Finally, in 1564 John
Glasgow entered the fore tenement as heir of **William Glasgow, his father**;
it still lay between the late John Rankin on the south and Robert Young on the
north.

The chain is therefore:

`James/Robert Young / late John Crownar | John Glasgow (1527) -> William Glasgow (by 20 Feb 1528/29; late by 1536) -> William's son John | Henryson/Houston -> John Rankin | Andrew Craig`

The unusually short interval, exact southern boundary, and continued identical
four-shilling institutional burden make the old John's and William's title a
**strong property succession**, materially stronger than surname coincidence.
It does **not** say that the older John was William's father. Sale, assignment,
tenancy, or another non-genealogical transmission remain possible. The exact
John-to-William instrument and any kinship formula have not been found.

The 16 March 1535/36 record also materially corrects William's chronology: he
was then explicitly `q.` (late), so he died between 17 February 1534/35, when
his lands were described without `q.`, and 16 March 1535/36. The commonly
repeated death date about 1543 is incompatible with this evidence.

Continued reconstruction materially strengthens the **rear-parcel** part of
the chain. On 13 May 1563 John Glasgow senior and Jonet Spreull conveyed to
their son John a tenement bounded by Robert Young north, the late John Rankin
south, and St Mary's collegiate-church land west. In 1564 the same block was
split into a fore tenement inherited from William and a back tenement subject
to the liferent of Eufame Craig, widow of an unnamed Glasgow. This is strong
circumstantial evidence that the 1534 family settlement concerned the rear
component of the Rankin–Young parcel. The best current reconstruction makes
Eufame the older John's widow: a widow's liferent created while John held the
whole property could survive the rapid transfer of the fee to William and
remain layered over the rear parcel in 1564. This also avoids requiring Esbell
Brade to die and William to remarry within about fourteen months. The printed
blank in Eufame's husband's forename remains potentially decisive.

No inspected record connects the 1527 John to the burgess John de Glasgu of
1447/1455. No shared person, property, office, or instrument connects the
Stirling witness of 1475/1479–80 to either Glasgow man. The chronology permits
identity, but the evidence does not establish it.

Collateral Brade/Brady reconstruction has, however, produced two certain
pre-1550 pedigrees and a much stronger context for Esbell Brade. Stirling
protocol abstracts directly call Richard Brady a son of John Brady in 1471 and
Gilbert Brady the same John's grandson and heir in 1477–82; Gilbert and
Isabella were spouses in 1478. More importantly for Glasgow, five Rutherglen
instruments of 1538/39–1543 preserve a direct four-generation chain: James
Hamilton; his daughter and heir Mariota, widow of John Brady; their son John
Brady, weaver and burgess, with wife Isabella Oswald; and John and Isabella's
son and heir Malcolm Brady. The same South Street property passed through this
household. This does not prove Esbell's parents, but it replaces a generic
surname theory with a documented contemporary family immediately beside
Glasgow.

### Post-gap relationship audit - 30 August 2026

The complete published Glasgow protocol sequence from 1547 through 1586 was
searched for every William Glasgow occurrence and relevant kinship formula.
Protocol 847 (24 August 1565) directly calls William `his son` when John Glasgow
senior conveyed him a burgage tenement. Mychael Lyndesay appears both with
William in 1564 and in that 1565 conveyance. In 1586 Stephen Glasgow witnessed
George entering as heir of the late William and then served as George's
curator; protocol 2189 independently proves Stephen was another son of the late
John and Jonet Spreull. No competing living adult William appears in the
series. This combination is sufficient to identify the record son confidently
with [William Glasgow (Glasgow-3522)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3522),
born by 9 June 1550. His father link to
[John Glasgow (Glasgow-1093)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1093) was
saved as confident on WikiTree and read back successfully. Jonet remains an
uncertain mother because the 1565 act calls her John's spouse but not
explicitly William's mother.

### Second confident relationship: James elder's son John

The Glencairn estate record [NRS GD39/5/59](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD39%2F5%2F59),
9 February 1571, describes Corsinkell as occupied by “James Glasgow, elder
James Glasgow and John Glasgow, his sons, Christian Campbell.” The grammar
directly identifies James and John as sons of James elder. The place and the
earlier elder/younger pairing identify the first son with
[[Glasgow-3921|James Glasgow younger]].

The previously attached [[Glasgow-3193|Kilwinning John]] remains only a
possible identity match: the 1559-60 teind material places one or two Johns at
Burrowland and Dalgarven, but no reviewed deed carries either holding into
Corsinkell. A bounded official NRS catalogue search for exact-name entries in
1500-1600 Glencairn and Eglinton material returned no such bridge.

To avoid turning a direct relationship into an unproved identity claim, a
separate record-defined profile, [[Glasgow-4061|John Glasgow of Corsinkell]],
was created on 30 August 2026. James elder is attached confidently and James
younger is therefore a proved brother. Glasgow-3193 remains attached to James
elder uncertain and is explicitly documented as a possible merge only if a
future property bridge is found.

The same corpus exposes a new but not yet conclusive Archibald lead. Archibald
Glasgow's 1551 fore-and-back tenement lay immediately east of Thomas Bargill;
from 1554/55 through 1557 the living John Glasgow is the eastern boundary of
Bargill's corresponding back title. With Robert witnessing Archibald's 1551
purchase, this materially strengthens a close family/property-network case,
but the omitted transfer mechanism prevents Archibald's parentage from being
upgraded.

Continued collateral work has now proved two additional parent-child links to
people documented before 1550. Protocol 795 (9 June 1564) directly calls
Thomas Mure heir of his mother, the late Jonet Bradye. The half-acre he
inherited at Kinclaith is strongly identifiable with the half-acre granted in
1528 to Jonet Brady, widow of Edward Scott: both have the same area and eastern
boundary, John Woddrop. Protocol 2960 (24 January 1586/87) directly calls smith
James Bradie heir of his father, the late Glasgow citizen Ninian Bradie. The
rare lay Ninian is independently documented at Rutherglen in 1542 and Glasgow
in 1564–71. These do not yet connect Esbell Brade to either household, but they
replace two isolated early bearers with reconstructed families and inherited
property.

## 2. Best new leads, ranked

1. **Find the John-to-William title instrument in the now-narrow interval.**
   John held the burdened parcel on 2 May 1527 and William was the adjoining
   northern owner by the instrument dated 20 February 1528/29. Search that
   approximately 21.5-month interval for a sale, resignation, heir service,
   marriage provision, or liferent involving Glasgow, Rankin, Young, Houston,
   Henryson/Makandoy or Craig.
2. **Inspect GCA B/10/1/3, especially protocols 748, 805 and 809–810.** Protocol
   809, at manuscript fol. 54, leaves Eufame Craig's deceased Glasgow husband's
   forename blank. A high-resolution original image may recover a faint name;
   the surrounding deeds show the back-tenement fee and liferent separately.
3. **Inspect NRS NP1/5 around protocols 1196, 1201 and 1233.** This is the exact
   Michael Fleming register, 1530–1537. Protocol 1233 (folio 87 in the printed
   abstract) supplies William's death terminus; protocols 1196 and 1201 may
   retain marginal title notes omitted from print.
4. **Obtain Blackhouse charter BL204 / GUA16403 (28 April 1506).** The catalogue
   alone says Sir Allan Smith and `John de Glasgow alias Smith` acquired a
   four-shilling ground annual. The full Latin, witnesses, notary, seal and
   endorsement may identify John's relationship to the Smith family or his
   later property.
5. **Inspect the original St Andrew prebend rental and foundation instruments.**
   The printed register (p. 36, reportedly fol. xij b) charges four shillings
   on the late William Glasgow's Crownar–Rankin tenement; the foundation was
   confirmed 3 June 1542 and transumed 8 January 1548/49. Compare the original
   with NLS Adv.MS.9A.1.12 (1813 transcript; microfilm Mf.Sec.MSS.156) and the
   5 January 1548 sasine that repeats the 1527 John wording and boundaries.
6. **Trace the Young–Crownar title, not merely the surnames.** Elizabeth/Besse
   Crownar was widow of James Young and mother of David; another 1555 entry
   calls Robert Young her eldest son. Robert later held the exact northern
   parcel. Search for the instrument moving James's/new-church title to Robert
   and for any relationship between Besse and the late John Crownar.
7. **Trace John Rankin and Helen Leis to Bartholomew Rankin.** Their deeds of
   1535 and 1553 flank the Glasgow parcel. A fuller Rankin title bundle may name
   the adjoining Glasgow owner at intermediate dates.
8. **Read James Darow's protocol book B66/1/1/1 throughout.** The printed
   Stirling texts give only two witness appearances. Search the manuscript for
   Glasgw/Glesgw variants, heirs, wife, occupation, departures, and every member
   of the 1475/1480 witness circle.
9. **Resolve the 1455 charter date discrepancy.** The scholarly transcript
   reads 10 April 1455; the 1712 Blackhouse inventory reportedly dates BL82 /
   GUA12430 to 19 April. An image of the original is required.
10. **Trace the proved Rutherglen Brady household in GCA collection RU.** The
    archive confirms that its Royal Burgh of Rutherglen holdings include
    charters from 1542–1617, but they are not online and the new catalogue did
    not index Brady. Follow the South Street tenement of John Brady, Isabella
    Oswald and Malcolm Brady from the five 1538/39–1543 instruments, looking
    for Esbell, siblings, marriage provisions and Malcolm's heirs.

## 3. Newly recovered or newly significant records

### 3.1 John de Glasgu, Glasgow, 1447

- **Date:** 21 June 1447.
- **Text and reference:** *Liber Collegii Nostre Domine*, Munimenta Fratrum,
  instrument 26, p. 168; Blackhouse BL68 / University of Glasgow Archives
  GUA12418.
- **Record:** Alexander Sawar, burgess of Glasgow, with his wife's consent, sold
  Friar John of Govan, prior of the Glasgow Dominicans, a 3s 6d annual rent from
  Sawar's rood on the west side of Walkergate. It lay between John Ade (north)
  and William Stevenson (south).
- **Relevant wording:** `presentibus ... Johanne de Glasgu ... burgensibus`.
  The grammar places John among the named burgesses.
- **Other witnesses:** Thomas Wyntir, bailie; John Red; John Ade; William
  Oglach; William Stephani; William Jackson; Robert Pettigrew, sergeant.
- **Notary:** Henry Michael, clerk of Glasgow diocese and public notary.
- **Provenance:** scholarly Latin transcription inspected; official catalogue
  entry inspected; no original image located online.
- **URLs:** [printed volume](https://archive.org/details/libercollegiino00annegoog),
  [official Blackhouse catalogue PDF](https://www.gla.ac.uk/media/Media_223124_smxx.pdf).

This is a surname/byname occurrence in a civic list, not merely the place-name
Glasgow. The act does not say that John owned the subject property.

### 3.2 John de Glasgu, Glasgow, 1455

- **Date:** transcript: 10 April 1455; Blackhouse catalogue/inventory: 19 April
  1455 (unresolved).
- **Text and reference:** *Liber Collegii Nostre Domine*, appendix instrument
  XXV, pp. 262–263; BL82 / GUA12430.
- **Record:** John Stenson, burgess, sold precentor Master David Cadzow a 3s
  annual from a west-side Fullers Street tenement, between the late Andrew
  Raburn (north) and Thomas Wan (south). Mariota Dunn, widow of the late William
  Kerswell, burgess, sold Cadzow a 2s annual from a north-side St Thenew Street
  tenement between Matthew Stewart (west) and William Goldsmith (east).
- **Wording:** `presentibus Johanne Macmulan balliuo / Dauid Hyne Andrea Brady
  Johanne de Glasgu et Roberto Fausyde sergiando`.
- **Notary:** Henry Michael, Glasgow-diocese clerk and public notary.
- **Provenance/URLs:** Latin transcription and catalogue inspected, no original
  image located: [printed volume](https://archive.org/details/libercollegiino00annegoog),
  [catalogue](https://www.gla.ac.uk/media/Media_223124_smxx.pdf).

The repeated notary supports one Glasgow civic milieu, but the two transactions
do not prove that the 1447 and 1455 witness owned either parcel.

### 3.3 John Glasgw, Stirling, 1475

- **Date/reference:** 11 April 1475; *Charters and Other Documents Relating to
  the Royal Burgh of Stirling* (1884), Appendix I, item 5, p. 185.
- **Record:** Robert Gelis senior, son and heir of the late Robert Gelis,
  burgess, and John Gelis younger, apparent heir of Robert senior, sold Sir John
  Hastings, canon of Dunblane, a 6s annual from Master John Spalding's tenement
  on the north side of Stirling High Street, between John Kirkwood west and the
  late Robert Haket east.
- **Witnesses:** John Lyntoun, `John Glasgw`, Thomas and John Forsithson,
  Nicholas and Michael Taylor, William Benny, John Auldcorn, Alexander Brade,
  Alexander Kirkwood, and Sir James Darow, chaplain/notary.
- **Status:** John's own status is not stated.
- **Provenance/URL:** scholarly English calendar inspected; manuscript image not
  inspected: [printed volume](https://archive.org/details/chartersotherdoc00stir).

### 3.4 John Glasgw, Stirling, 1479/80

- **Date/reference:** 2 March 1479/80; same volume, Appendix II, item 13,
  p. 208.
- **Record:** Adam Cosour resigned annual rents of 20s and two of 10s for the
  St Anne and Virgin Mary altars. Properties included a tenement between Gilbert
  Brady west and the late William Murray of Touchadam east; Duncan Thomson's
  tenement between Richard Smithson and the friars' vennel; and Trone land on
  the west side of High Street leading to the port.
- **Witnesses:** Thomas Stewart, Andrew Morys, `John Glasgw`, and others. Sir
  Andrew Bully, chaplain, received sasine.
- **Provenance/URL:** printed abstract, not the Latin manuscript:
  [printed volume](https://archive.org/details/chartersotherdoc00stir).

### 3.5 John de Glasgow alias Smith, 1506

- **Date/reference:** 28 April 1506; Blackhouse BL204, old reference GUA16403.
- **Catalogue abstract:** Agnes, daughter and heir of Robert Watson, sold a 4s
  ground annual from an east-side High Street tenement on the road from the
  cathedral to the market cross. The subject lay between the tenements of Sir
  John Finnyson, vicar of Eastwood, and Sir Allan Smith on the north. The
  acquirers were Sir Allan Smith and `John de Glasgow alias Smith`.
- **Provenance:** official catalogue entry only; no image or full transcription
  located online. The official Flickr set is a selection, not a complete
  digitisation.
- **URLs:** [catalogue](https://www.gla.ac.uk/media/Media_223124_smxx.pdf),
  [collection guide](https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/archivespecialcollections/discover/university/features/blackhouse/),
  [selected images](https://www.flickr.com/photos/uofglibrary/sets/72157637150636673/).

The alias should separate identities, not be silently discarded. Cuthbert
Simon's protocol 137 (31 May 1505, pp. 102–104, fol. 35) names William Smith's
eldest son Andrew and younger children Patrick, James, Allan and Mariota; it
does not name John. Allan Smith appears as a priestly witness in 1513. This
makes Sir Allan plausibly the son named in 1505, but does not establish how
alias John belonged to the family. The inspected source was [*Liber
Protocollorum M. Cuthberti Simonis*, vol. II](https://archive.org/details/liberprotocollor02bain).

### 3.6 The older John's exact tenement, 1527

- **Date/reference:** 2 May 1527; *Liber Collegii Nostre Domine*, pp. 85–86.
- **Record:** Andrew Loppe resigned to Master James Houston, subdean of Glasgow,
  a 4s annual rent from `tenemento Johannis Glasgu`.
- **Location:** west side of the common street from the market cross to the
  South Port, between John Rankin south and James Young north.
- **Wording:** `Andreas Loppe ... vnum annuumredditum quatuor solidorum ... de
  tenemento Johannis Glasgu ... inter terras Johannis Rankyn ex australi et
  terras Jacobi Young ex boreali`.
- **Bailie/notary/witnesses:** John Gayo, bailie; John Black, apostolic notary,
  MA, priest of Glasgow diocese; Robert Anderson, William Adam, John Craig and
  John Good.
- **Provenance/URL:** Latin scholarly transcription inspected:
  [printed volume](https://archive.org/details/libercollegiino00annegoog).

### 3.7 William already holds the northern parcel, 1528/29

- **Date/reference:** 20 February 1528/29; *Liber Collegii Nostre Domine*,
  pp. 90–91. The instrument states 1528, while the edition's contents
  explicitly supply the dual date `20 Februarii 1528-9` (fol. xxxi b, p. 90).
- **Record:** George Burrell, one of Glasgow's bailies, went to a tenement with
  a small garden and a large rear garden/orchard. Malcolm Henryson alias
  Makandoy, brother and lawful nearest heir of the late David Henryson alias
  Makandoy, was entered as heir and immediately sold/resigned it to Master
  James Houston, subdean.
- **Fore boundaries:** Andrew Craig south and `Willelmi Glasgu` north.
- **Rear boundaries:** lands of the New Church of the BVM and St Anne west,
  Thomas Adam, tailor, north, and Jonet Nutishill south.
- **Wording:** `inter tenementa ... Andree Craig ex australi et Willelmi
  Glasgu ex boreali partibus`; `Malcolmus Henrici alias Makandoy frater et
  legitimus et propinquior heres quondam Dauid Henrici alias Makandoy`.
- **Witnesses/notary:** Sir Richard Otterburn, George Rob, Finlay Makculloch,
  Thomas Fleming, priests, and Henry Makandoy alias Henryson; John Moresoun,
  priest of Glasgow diocese and apostolic notary.
- **Provenance/URLs:** scholarly Latin transcription and original-volume scan
  visually inspected: [volume](https://archive.org/details/libercollegiino00annegoog),
  [p. 90 image](https://archive.org/download/libercollegiino00annegoog/page/n195_w1600.jpg),
  [p. 91 image](https://archive.org/download/libercollegiino00annegoog/page/n196_w1600.jpg).

The Henryson/Houston subject is the later Rankin parcel: both descriptions
place it between William Glasgow north and Andrew Craig south, with collegiate-
church land to the west. William was therefore established on the target
northern parcel by this date, not first in 1534/35.

### 3.8 William at the same parcel, 1534/35

- **Date/reference:** 17 February 1534/35, 3 p.m.; Michael Fleming appendix,
  protocol 1201, pp. 73–74.
- **Record:** Master James Houston resigned to John Rankin and Helen Leis a
  waste tenement and yard west of the Cross-to-Nether-Barresyet street.
- **Boundaries:** `lands of Wylyem Glasgow on the north`, Andrew Craig south,
  Our Lady land west.
- **Burden:** 2 merks annual to Our Lady Kirk founded by Houston and 4s to the
  Blackfriars.
- **Sasine/witnesses:** Patrick Lyon, bailie; Sir Andrew Walker and Sir James
  Schaw, chaplains; William Adam, Patrick Anderson, John Craig, John Croket and
  John Walker, sergeant.
- **Provenance/URL:** printed Scots abstract inspected:
  [Town Clerks protocols, vol. IV](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc08robegoog).

### 3.9 William expressly deceased, 1535/36

- **Date/reference:** 16 March 1535/36, 2–4 p.m.; protocol 1233, pp. 86–87,
  fol. 87.
- **Record:** Patrick Lyon, bailie, gave Robert Smith sasine as heir of several
  explicitly stated Smith relatives in a long property and annual-rent holding.
  One item was `xx s. of anwell of James Yong place, betuix the lands of q.
  Wylzem Glasgw on the sowtht, and the landis of Robert Cochren on the northt`.
- **Witnesses:** George Burrell, bailie; David Lindsay; Archibald Herbertson;
  Sir William Herbertson, chaplain; Archibald Robison; John Walker; John
  Waulderston; William Gardiner.
- **Provenance/URL:** printed abstract inspected:
  [Town Clerks protocols, vol. IV](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc08robegoog).

Here `q.` is the edition's abbreviation for *quondam*, “late/deceased.” William
was therefore dead by this act.

### 3.10 The institutional rent identifies William's tenement, 1542/1548

- **Document dates/reference:** James Houston's foundation/dotation was
  confirmed at Glasgow on 3 June 1542 and judicially transumed on 8 January
  1548/49; *Liber Collegii Nostre Domine*, St Andrew Apostle prebend, p. 36
  (contents table: fol. xij b).
- **Wording:** `Item quatuor solidi annui redditus de tenemento quondam
  Wilelmi Glasgu jacente in eadem via ex parte occidentali inter tenementa
  quondam Johannis Crovnar ex boreali et tenementum Johannis Rankyn ex
  australi`.
- **Translation:** “Item, a four-shilling annual rent from the tenement of the
  late William Glasgow, lying on the west side of the same street, between the
  tenement of the late John Crownar on the north and John Rankin's tenement on
  the south.”
- **Provenance/URLs:** scholarly Latin transcription and scan visually
  inspected: [volume](https://archive.org/details/libercollegiino00annegoog),
  [p. 36 image](https://archive.org/download/libercollegiino00annegoog/page/n141_w1600.jpg).
  An independent later transcript of one mid-sixteenth-century notarial copy is
  catalogued at the National Library of Scotland as
  [Adv.MS.9A.1.12](https://manuscripts.nls.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/12930),
  with microfilm **Mf.Sec.MSS.156**; it was located but not inspected.

This is direct evidence that the 1534 Crownar–Rankin subject was William's
tenement, and it preserves the same four-shilling annual that was charged on
John Glasgow's Rankin–Young tenement in 1527. The equation of the two annuals
is **STRONG**, not DIRECT, because the entry does not recite the 1527 conveyance.

### 3.11 The old John's name retained in 1548 and 1553

- **5 January 1548:** a fresh foundation sasine for James Houston's collegiate
  church included `tenementum Johannis Glasgu`, west side Cross-to-South-Port,
  owing the same 4s annual, between John Rankin south and former James Young
  north. The immediately following item describes Rankin's tenement between
  John Glasgow north and Andrew Craig south. Robert Watson acted as procurator;
  John Hall was bailie. *Liber Collegii*, pp. 108, 114–115.
- **21 October 1553:** Hegait protocol 167 records Rankin's resignation to his
  son Bartholomew and Janet Swan, reserving liferent; the Rankin property lay
  between the lands of `q. John Glasgow` north and Andrew Craig south.
- **Provenance:** printed transcripts/abstracts, not original images.

The 1548 form could reproduce the foundation's 1527 title description, but the
1553 boundary independently says that an adjoining John Glasgow was deceased.

### 3.12 William's son enters the parcel, 1564

- **28 October 1564, protocol 805, p. 57:** David Lindsay, bailie, gave John
  Glesgw sasine `as heir of William Glesgw, his father` of a fore tenement
  between the late John Rankin south, Robert Young north, a back tenement of
  `[blank] Glesgw` west, and the street east.
- **16 November 1564, protocol 810, p. 59:** John Glesgw senior sold the same
  fore tenement to William Craig for £40; the boundaries were unchanged and
  the western back tenement belonged to John.
- **Provenance/URL:** printed abstracts inspected:
  [Town Clerks protocols, vol. III](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc06scogoog/page/n69/mode/2up).

The father-son relationship is DIRECT. The inherited parcel's identity with
the 1527/1535 parcel is STRONG from its two lateral boundaries.

### 3.13 William's 1526 remission

- **Date/reference:** 2 July 1526; *Registrum Secreti Sigilli Regum Scotorum*,
  vol. I, entry **3412**, p. 516 (manuscript references vi.8, vii.7).
- **Wording/context:** a `Preceptum Remissionis` names James Sandilands of
  Calder, Robert Bruce of Auchinbowie, Richard Graham and numerous others,
  including `Willelmi Glasgw`, for the premeditated killing of the late James
  Somerville and associated offences, subject to customary exceptions.
- **Provenance/URL:** scholarly Latin calendar inspected:
  [printed volume](https://archive.org/details/registrumsecret00scotgoog). The
  surviving sealed remission is catalogued as NRS **GD119/193**; its fuller
  description includes Walter Forestar, James Auld, Alexander Broune, Robert
  Grahame and George Tennent among accomplices omitted from the shorter printed
  entry, and requires assythment to Somerville's kin. [Official NRS
  record](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browseDetails.aspx?reference=GD119%2F193).

The entry gives William no residence, occupation, wife or relative. Its date
falls within the 20 June–3 July 1526 Sandilands settlement of the Calder barony
(NRS GD119/194–195), confirming the remission's Calder-retinue context but not
William's residence. None of the distinctive co-remission names occurs in
William's small 1530–36 Glasgow circle. Identity with the Glasgow property
holder remains POSSIBLE.

### 3.14 The rear tenement and Eufame Craig, 1563–64

- **13 May 1563; protocol 748, printed p. 40:** John Glesgw senior resigned to
  `John Glesgw, junior, his son` a tenement between Robert Young north, the late
  John Rankin south and St Mary's collegiate-church land west. John senior and
  Jonet Spreull, survivor, reserved five merks yearly. Archibald Lyon gave
  sasine; Robert Young, David Landes, James Craig, Robert Tennend and Robert
  Burn witnessed.
- **16 November 1564; protocol 809, printed pp. 58–59, manuscript fol. 54:**
  `Eufame Craige, the relict of wmquhill {blank} Glesgw`, holding conjunct fee,
  frank tenement and liferent of the whole back tenement between late John
  Rankin south, Robert Young north and John Glasgow's fore tenement east, sold
  her liferent to John Glasgow elder for £40. David Lindsay, Gilbert Craig,
  William Young and James Anderson witnessed.
- **Same day; protocol 810:** John elder sold the adjoining fore tenement to
  William Craig for £40; the back tenement remained west of it.
- **5 February 1565/66; protocol 860, pp. 77–78:** William Craig sold that fore
  tenement to his son Archibald Craig and Archibald's wife Margaret Smith. Its
  boundaries remained late John Rankin south, Robert Young north, John
  Glasgow's back tenement west, and the street east.
- **Repository/reference:** the official catalogue identifies the manuscript
  as Glasgow City Archives **B/10/1/3 (GCA B10/1/3)**, William Heygate's protocol
  book, 15 September 1562–11 July 1576. [Official catalogue
  record](https://cityarchives.glasgowlife.org.uk/Record.aspx?src=CalmView.Catalog&id=B%2f10%2f1%2f3).
  The inspected texts are the record-society abstracts, not manuscript images:
  [protocol 748](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc06scogoog/page/n52/mode/2up)
  and [protocols 809–810](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc06scogoog/page/n71/mode/2up).

The sequence separates **fee** from **liferent**. John senior could convey the
rear fee to his son in 1563 while Eufame's pre-existing liferent continued; in
1564 he bought that burden. The most economical explanation is that Eufame was
the older John Glasgow's widow and her reserved interest survived the passage
of the fee from John to William. The coordinated Craig transactions support
this: James Craig witnessed the 1563 rear-fee conveyance, Gilbert Craig
witnessed Eufame's sale, and William Craig bought the adjoining fore tenement
from John on the same day before passing it to his son Archibald. This is a
strong circumstantial reconstruction rather than a named marriage. Eufame as
William's later widow is still possible, but would require Esbell's death and
William's remarriage between 31 December 1534 and 16 March 1535/36.

### 3.15 Young–Crownar reconstruction

- **9 November 1512; Cuthbert Simon protocol 596, vol. II, printed pp. 458–59:**
  `Johanne Coronare` witnessed sasine of a ten-shilling annual from John
  Slater's Stockwell property. Masters John Spreull and John MacGour, priests,
  also witnessed. John Coronare is chronologically capable of being the late
  John Crownar whose land bounded the Glasgow back tenement in 1534, but the
  record states no identity or relationship.
- **13 February 1554/55; Hegait protocol 207, printed p. 70:** Elizabeth
  Crownear, widow of James Young, conveyed two St Thenew-croft rigs to `David
  Young, her son`, reserving liferent. Robert Young was the northern neighbour
  and a witness.
- **12 April 1555; diocesan Rental Book, printed p. 160:** George Young entered
  a 6s 3d land in Meikle Govan by consent of `Robert Zowng, his eldest brother`;
  `Besse Crownare, his mother`, retained enjoyment for life.
- **3 September 1562; protocol 1440:** Robert Young, citizen, and Margaret
  Arbuckle are expressly spouses. This controls a potentially misleading 1571
  pronoun: Margaret was mother of Robert's sons, not of Robert.
- **8 September 1571; protocol 1788, printed pp. 66–67:** Robert Young, baker
  and citizen, conveyed to his elder son James a tenement formerly belonging to
  the new church, on the west Cross-to-South-Port street, between **John
  Glasgow south** and **John Cochran north**. This is the same orientation as
  James Young's 1536 place between late William Glasgow and Robert Cochran.

Elizabeth/Besse is very likely one woman, and Robert and George are very likely
additional sons of her marriage to James Young, but the two records do not
repeat the husband's name. Grade: **STRONG CIRCUMSTANTIAL**. The exact
James-to-Robert property transmission is still absent. The Crownar evidence
also explains how the 1534 rear boundary could differ from the fore boundary,
but it does not prove that late John Crownar was Besse's father or relative.

The Michael Fleming manuscript itself is now precisely identified as **NRS
NP1/5, Notarial protocol book of Michael Fleming, 1530–1537; “Lanark, Register
of Glasgow”; open, on-site**. [Official NRS catalogue
record](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=NP1%2f5).

### 3.16 Two additional pre-1550 Brady family reconstructions

**Jonet Bradye and Thomas Mure.** On 2 April 1528 Jonet Brady, widow of the
late Edward Scott, citizen of Glasgow, received a half-acre in Kynclaith
between Robert Smith west and John Woddrop east (*Liber Collegii*, pp. 89–90).
On 9 June 1564 Thomas Mure was served heir of the late `Jonet Bradye, his
mother`, in a half-acre in Kinclaith between William Hall west and the late
John Woddrop east. The inherited parcel, rare name and unchanged eastern
boundary make the woman's identity **STRONG CIRCUMSTANTIAL**; the mother-son
relationship is **DIRECT**. Renwick, *Abstracts*, vol. III, protocol 795,
[p. 54](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc06scogoog/page/n67/mode/2up).

On 7 March 1531/32 Andrew Muir and Janet Bradye jointly acquired a
four-shilling annualrent for the longest liver (vol. IV, protocol 1115). The
abstract does not call them spouses. Thomas later occupied Andrew Mure's
tenement (vol. II, protocol 552), so Andrew as Jonet's second husband and
Thomas's father is a **strong circumstantial** reconstruction, not a direct
kinship statement. Protocol 796 directly names Thomas's spouse Marion
Crawford; protocol 1818 (9 January 1571/72) directly calls Thomas brother of
Jonet Mure, without saying whether they were full siblings.

**Ninian and James Bradie.** On 24 January 1586/87 James Bradie, smith, entered
a Garngad half-acre `as heir of q. Ninian Bradie, citizen, his father` and
immediately transferred it to James Rob in Dalsholm. This is **DIRECT**
father-son and inheritance evidence. The unusual lay Ninian is documented at
Rutherglen in 1542 and Glasgow in 1564 and 1570–71, a **STRONG** identity chain
anchoring the father before 1550. Sir Ninian Brady/Brydin, chaplain and notary
at Selkirk, is kept separate. Renwick, *Abstracts*, vol. IX, protocol 2960,
[p. 125](https://archive.org/details/abstractsprotoc01scogoog/page/n136/mode/2up).

The 1534 back tenement is now **strongly, though circumstantially**, identified
as the western rear component. The same John and Jonet who received a back
tenement from William and Esbell conveyed a tenement in this exact block to
their son in 1563; the 1564 instruments then distinguish the rear from the
inherited fore title. The apparently different boundaries are explicable by
depth: James Young's widow was Elizabeth/Besse Crownar, while Rankin's western
boundary was St Mary's/Our Lady land. No one instrument explicitly equates the
1534 and 1563 subjects, so the conclusion is not DIRECT.

No property held or witnessed by the 1447/1455 John has been found. In both
acts he is a witness rather than a stated owner, so those parcels cannot yet be
traced as “his” land.

## 5. Stirling analysis

The Stirling John appears twice across five years in Sir James Darow's notarial
milieu. The records place him among urban property and altar-endowment actors,
but neither calls him burgess, merchant, owner, clerk, or relative. The 1475
act has a large witness group; only John Glasgw himself recurs in the 1480 act.

Chronology does not rule out identity with the 1447/1455 Glasgow burgess: an
adult in 1447 could still witness in 1480. The 33-year span makes identity
possible, not extraordinary. Against identification, there is no shared
property, office, notary, witness, or explicit movement between the burghs.
James Darow is confined to the Stirling records; Henry Michael to the Glasgow
acts.

Alexander Brade witnesses the 1475 Stirling charter and Gilbert Brady owns an
adjoining property in 1480. This demonstrates a Brade/Brady surname network in
Stirling, but neither man is related in the documents to Esbell Brade. It is not
a bridge.

Assessment:

- same man as Glasgow's 1447/1455 John: **POSSIBLE, presently unsupported**;
- his son or relative: **UNSUPPORTED**;
- same property/social network: **UNSUPPORTED**;
- unrelated bearer: also possible; not provable from the two acts.

## 6. William Glasgow's origins before 1534

The evidence now establishes:

- a possible 1526 occurrence in a large royal remission;
- William holding the target northern parcel by 20 February 1528/29;
- an unqualified Glasgow witness occurrence on 28 February 1530/31;
- husband of Esbell Brade and father of John by 31 December 1534;
- holder of the exact Rankin–Young parcel on 17 February 1534/35;
- dead by 16 March 1535/36;
- his son John later entered the fore tenement as his heir.

The 1534 settlement reserves the profits to William and Esbell for life. This
is deliberate intergenerational property planning, but it does not state how
William acquired the property or name his parents. William is never assigned a
trade or civic office in the inspected entries. “Burgess” should not be added
without a direct record.

The best origin clue is now the older John attached to the same exact parcel
only about twenty-one and a half months earlier. The same four-shilling collegiate rent
is later explicitly charged on late William's Crownar–Rankin tenement. This is
strong succession evidence, but a relationship formula is still absent.

## 8. Negative findings

- Full-text and index searches of Cuthbert Simon's published protocols,
  1499–1513, found no unambiguous personal Glasgow surname bearer. Hits were
  overwhelmingly the city, diocese, cathedral, or offices. The search did find
  John Coronare with Master John Spreull in protocol 596, but neither is stated
  to be related to the later Glasgow family.
- Variant searches in [*Register of the Great Seal*, vol. III
  (1513–1546)](https://archive.org/details/registrummagnisi03scot) found
  no unambiguous William or John with Glasgow as surname; occurrences concerned
  the city/diocese or people with independent surnames. OCR limitations mean
  this is not a manuscript-level negative.
- *Register of the Privy Seal*, vol. I (1488–1529), produced the 1526 William
  candidate but no relationship or Glasgow residence.
- The current NRS catalogue was searched for exact `William Glasgow` and the
  forms `Glasgw`, `Glasgu` and `Glesgw`, 1500–1540. Its only exact personal hit
  was GD119/193, the same 2 July 1526 Sandilands/Calder remission; no second
  record located William independently in Calder or elsewhere.
- Complete OCR and index searches of *Exchequer Rolls*, vols. XV–XVI
  (1523–1536), found no unambiguous Glasgow surname bearer. All retained hits
  referred to the city, cathedral, diocese, an office, or a place.
- An authenticated Scotland's People Virtual Volumes reference search for NRS
  NP1/195 returned no result. The Cuthbert Simons protocol book, 1511–1547,
  therefore remains an on-site record rather than an online image source.
- The official Blackhouse catalogue, items dated approximately 1400–1540, gave
  BL68, BL82 and BL204 but no obvious catalogue-level instrument transferring
  the Rankin–Young parcel from John to William. The catalogue is not a full-text
  index of every name in each parchment.
- The printed Stirling charter volume's index and target pages produced only
  the 1475 and 1479/80 John occurrences and no heir/widow/title continuation.
- Printed Glasgow protocols after 1547 were searched by the stable Rankin,
  Young and Craig boundaries. They supplied the 1553 and 1564 links, but no
  explicit statement that the older John was William's father.
- Full-text and index searches across the published Glasgow protocol volumes
  found Eufame Craig only in protocol 809. No second entry supplies her
  deceased Glasgow husband's forename or states a relationship to William,
  Gilbert Craig or William Craig.
- The complete published index to the Glasgow Commissariot testaments,
  1547–1800, contains no testament for the target John, William, Esbell or
  Eufame, and no Glasgow-surname testament close to 1547–70. It also contains
  no Crownar entry. This is a true index-level negative, but William died before
  the series begins.
