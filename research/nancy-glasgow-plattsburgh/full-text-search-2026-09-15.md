## James G. Glasgow review: comparison with Nancy's probate citation

The 81 FamilySearch full-text hits for “James G Glasgow” are not 81 separate
people. Most of the usable hits form a repeated Jo Daviess County, Illinois
land-record cluster: James G. Glasgow appears in deeds from the late 1860s
through the 1890s, in Guilford Township, sometimes with Sarah Glasgow or
“Sarah Glasgow his wife.” The 1867–68 deed transcript also places James G.
Glasgow in a 160-acre transaction in Jo Daviess County. Other hits are
scattered OCR/index references in Tennessee, Kentucky, Ohio, New York,
Missouri, Nova Scotia, and Ireland and do not, by themselves, establish a
second identity.

The Jo Daviess man has a WikiTree profile: [Glasgow-3128](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3128),
James G Glasgow. The profile gives him a birth in 1833, emigration to New
York in 1851, settlement at Galena/Jo Daviess in 1858, marriage to Sarah
Mayow in 1864, and a son William H. Glasgow. His linked father is
[Glasgow-3129](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3129), James Glasgow,
born about 1810 in Ireland; the profile names that father as the son of
James Glasgow and Peggy Slowne and husband of Jane Ramsey. Thus the
Illinois FamilySearch cluster and Glasgow-3128 are a strong same-person
identification, although the WikiTree profile itself has conflicting birth
details (6 November versus 11 June 1833) and needs better primary sourcing.

This does **not** tie him to Nancy's notice. The notice explicitly identifies
its James G. Glasgow as a child of **John Glasgow**, late of Baladonly,
County Antrim, Ireland. The identified Illinois James G. is documented on
WikiTree as the son of **James Glasgow**, not John, and the targeted
FamilySearch searches for James G. Glasgow with Antrim and for John Glasgow
with Baladonly returned no records. On present evidence, Glasgow-3128 is a
different James G. Glasgow and should not be attached to Nancy's probate
family.

Status: the James G. Glasgow named in Nancy's probate citation remains
unidentified. The best next lead is a record that names the citation man's
father or the Baladonly/Antrim connection, rather than additional duplicate
Jo Daviess deeds.

### Additional Antrim check

The local Antrim material does produce one superficially plausible John–James
line, but it is not a match yet. [Glasgow-3474](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3474),
John Glasgow of Lisnagaver, County Antrim (about 1807–1885), has a proved son
[Glasgow-4043](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-4043), James Glasgow,
estimated born about 1830. The father's death record names “James Glasgow of
Lisnagaver” as informant. However, this James has no middle initial, no
American or Plattsburgh record, and the father's locality is Lisnagaver, not
Baladonly. It is therefore only a collateral lead, not an identification of
the probate notice's James G.

A second local family, [Glasgow-867](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-867),
John Glasgow of Rasharkin/Lisnagaver, has children Ann and James Garvin
Glasgow, but that James was born about 1852 and is not the James G. named in
the 1892 citation. These local records reinforce that the probate notice is
likely referring to an as-yet-unconnected Antrim Glasgow household, rather
than the already identified Illinois man.
