## Adam D. Glasgow: education, teaching and Belfast ministry

- [Belfast News-Letter, 29 June 1830, p.3](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390569859/):
  the **Royal Belfast Academical Institution, School Department** prize list
  names **Adam D. Glasgow (Broughshane)** in trigonometry and astronomy, equal
  with Frederick Clarke; abbreviated entries also place him in first-class
  geometry and algebra. The heading and passage were inspected separately in
  the same column. The adjacent Belfast Academy report is a different notice.
- [Belfast News-Letter, 6 May 1831, p.3](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390545285/):
  **Belfast College**, report dated **30 April 1831**, names Adam D. Glasgow,
  Broughshane, for mathematics in the public examinations and jointly with
  Frederick Clarke in the junior mathematical course and additional reading.
  It also lists **James Glasgow, Institution**, with Robert Finlay in voluntary
  mathematical examinations covering spherical trigonometry, conic sections,
  and differential and integral calculus.
- [Belfast News-Letter, 10 January 1837, p.2](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390542489/):
  Adam D. Glasgow resigned as **assistant in the Mathematical and Mercantile
  School of the Royal Belfast Institution**. On Thursday **22 December 1836**
  (the report says 22nd of the previous month), his mathematical class gave
  him a copy of Donnegan's Greek Lexicon, commending his conduct and teaching.
  This is a resignation/presentation, not an appointment.
- [Belfast News-Letter, 17 January 1840, p.2](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390553900/):
  **Rev. Adam D. Glasgow** received a unanimous call to the Presbyterian church
  in **Berry Street, Belfast**, connected with the General Synod of Ulster.
  This is a call; the report does not give an ordination date.
- [Belfast News-Letter, 10 March 1840, p.2](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390555182/):
  a Berry Street church soiree on **Thursday 5 March** presented a pulpit dress
  to its newly ordained minister, **Rev. Adam D. Glasgow**. The address thanks
  him for visits to the congregation. 5 March is the presentation date,
  not an established ordination date.

### Identity assessment

**Glasgow-1781, Adam Dickie Glasgow (1815–1863), is a strong compatible
candidate.** His live profile was checked on 4 October 2026: Ballymena birth,
Presbyterian ministry, and an earlier Belfast period agree with these notices.
His reported birth would make him about 15 in 1830 and 21 at resignation.
The repeated exact forename, middle initial and institutional setting support
continuity between the education and teaching notices; continuity into the
ministerial notices is a supported inference, not a stated parentage clause.
Neither Dickie in full nor parents, spouse, age or later migration appear in
these inspected passages. Keep the attribution qualified pending college and
ordination records. The Inishrush Adams remain separate comparison subjects;
different locations or occupations alone do not exclude a namesake.

**James McClure Glasgow-1780 (1805–1890) is a strong compatible candidate for
the 1831 James.** His live Education section expressly names the Royal Belfast
Academical Institution and mathematical/natural-philosophy distinction; it was
missing from the bounded local inventory. That existing biography claim was
checked, but its underlying education source was not newly read. The newspaper
adds a specific dated examination lead. His already documented sibling link
to Adam strengthens the family comparison, but co-occurrence in the prize list
does not itself prove that these two newspaper men were brothers.

Other locally checked Adam comparisons: Glasgow-3373, born 7 July 1804, remains
a chronologically compatible namesake without an institution/ministry bridge;
Glasgow-1622, born before 1794 and recorded as a farmer in his son's 1851
marriage, has no such bridge; Glasgow-3902, c.1758–1844, is the Inishrush
landholder. They are not silently removed merely for absent corroboration.
No parent, mother, sibling or spouse certainty is upgraded by this batch.

## William Glasgow: Cookstown and the new Claggan church

[Belfast News-Letter, 10 November 1846, p.4](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390561508/)
reports the opening of **Claggan Presbyterian Church on Thursday 15 October**.
**Wm. Glasgow** appears among the collectors labelled **Cookstown**, and again
in the Cookstown donor group included in the **10 shillings each** list.
The Cookstown locality comes from the shared list punctuation, not from the
separate Inishrush entry. Inishrush is attached to donor James M'Caw.

This supplies a contemporary Cookstown church/community record, but no age,
trade or family. It remains unassigned between the Cookstown William accounts.
It is compatible with the later retired merchant/uncle comparison
**Glasgow-3814**, and with adult namesakes **Glasgow-585** and **Glasgow-1737**;
no exclusive identity or uncle relationship follows. Reuse the existing
Cookstown held account and link the younger mill-partner account rather than
create a new person from this single donation. Presbyterians attending or
collecting at Claggan are not thereby proved resident there.
