### cookstown-robert-inn-academy-1836-1840

Robert Glasgow was the named Cookstown contact for letting the King’s Arms Inn in October 1836. A 1840 Cookstown Academy advertisement names Robert Glasgow as chairman of its superintending committee and an applicant contact for a teacher.

The notices name roles, not his age or family. The 1836 contact is not explicitly described as innkeeper or owner. The Academy is not described as a Sunday school. Consolidated as a same-name Cookstown research account; identity between the notices, the mill partner, Robert-2656 and Robert-3941 remains unproved. Neither notice identifies the already-deceased father of the surgeon.

[Belfast News-Letter, 18 October 1836, p. 3, King’s Arms Inn letting](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390551780/); [Belfast News-Letter, 30 October 1840, p. 2, Cookstown Academy](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390563103/)

### cookstown-benjamin-school-donor-1850

Master Benjamin Glasgow of Cookstown is listed for one shilling towards support of scholars in the agricultural school in June 1850.

A donor list, not proof that Benjamin attended the school. “Master” supplies no exact age. Benjamin-2059 (reported born about 1835) and Benjamin-2566 (about 1840) are compatible young catalogue namesakes; assignment to either, or to the Drapersfield Benjamin, is unresolved. The surgeon who died in 1833 is excluded.

[Belfast News-Letter, 28 June 1850, p. 2, agricultural-school scholars’ support](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390559842/)

### samuel-glasgow-moneymore-tenant-right-1845

An 1845 tenant-right discussion quotes Samuel Glasgow, described as an intelligent farmer examined at Moneymore, advocating secure tenure and protection against rent charged on improvements. He described seeing reclamation on Sir Thomas Staples’s land in his neighbourhood.

Moneymore is where he was examined, not an explicit home townland. The date of his examination, age and relatives are unstated. Cookstown farmers Samuel-2054 and Samuel-1698 are compatible; Lisnagaver Samuel-2908 is a weaker regional comparison, without a locality or occupation bridge. No connection to Lisnagaver is established.

[Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser, 19 March 1845, p. 1, Ulster tenant-right discussion](https://www.newspapers.com/image/1050103241/)

### james-glasgow-dublin-exchequer-1820

James Glasgow was plaintiff against Maxwell Lepper and Clotworthy Dobbin in a Court of Exchequer report headed Dublin, 22 June 1820. The defendants’ motion to rescind the contempt process was refused with costs and liberty for the plaintiff to continue process.

The report gives a procedural decision, not final judgment on the dispute, and no age, occupation, residence or relatives for James. George Frederick Moulds is named as plaintiff’s agent. Identity with the later Belfast tobacco seller or any candidate is unproved.

[The Irishman, 30 June 1820, p. 2, Court of Exchequer, Dublin, 22 June](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61258711/)

### james-glasgow-belfast-tobacco-1822-1823

James Glasgow offered leaf tobacco in three Belfast advertisements in 1822–23. The August 1822 notice directs buyers to him at James M’Master’s; the May 1823 notice locates samples at M’Master’s North Street premises.

Repeated name, trade and contact support a consolidated seller account. M’Master’s premises are a commercial contact, not a proved home or owned property. No age, family or link to William Glasgow the grocer is stated. Chronologically compatible regional James profiles remain leads; no profile is selected.

[The Irishman, 15 March 1822, p. 2, tobacco advertisement dated 9 March](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61259036/); [The Irishman, 23 August 1822, p. 3, tobacco advertisement dated 20 August](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61260054/); [The Irishman, 30 May 1823, p. 3, tobacco advertisement dated 27 May](https://www.newspapers.com/image/61261904/)
