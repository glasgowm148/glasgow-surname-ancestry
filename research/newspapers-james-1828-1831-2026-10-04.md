## Verified records

### Lease

An auction notice describes the large shop and stores at No.32 Rosemary Street, Belfast, as formerly occupied by Mr James Glasgow. The lease interest was offered for sale on 1 April 1828; the notice dated 21 March gives 34 years unexpired from 1 November next and £30 late Irish currency yearly rent.

Former occupancy is explicit; ownership, trade and residence are not. The surname is certain, but identity with the 1822–23 tobacco seller, William the grocer or another James remains unproved.

[Belfast News-Letter, 1 April 1828, p.3, auction of shop and stores, No.32 Rosemary Street](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390544933/)

### College

The 2 May 1828 Belfast Institution College Department awards list Jas. Glasgow, Broughshane, for proficiency in the junior mathematical class, and James Glasgow for daily examinations and exercises in Logic and Belles Lettres. The 1 May 1830 Belfast College report awards James Glasgow, Institution, senior-course voluntary mathematics and the premium on the third book of Locke’s Essay.

The shared name and institution support a student sequence, with the two 1828 forms probably one student. Glasgow-1780 is a strong compatible candidate; none of these passages gives age, full middle name, parents or spouse. No sibling relationship is established by the notices.

[Belfast News-Letter, 9 May 1828, p.3, Belfast Institution College Department, awards on 2 May](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390546969/) · [Belfast News-Letter, 7 May 1830, p.2, Belfast College report dated 1 May](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390568607/)

### Jury

A separate report lists James Glasgow on the petty jury for Friday 1 August 1828. He is a juror, not one of the accused in the following riot-and-assault report.

The name is James Glasgow, not the automated index’s combined William James Glasgow. No residence, occupation or kin is stated. This remains an unassigned legal occurrence; identity with the 1820 Exchequer plaintiff is unproved.

[Belfast News-Letter, 5 August 1828, p.4, petty jury, Friday 1 August](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390549197/)

### Larne

James Glasgow of Larne is no.16 among persons applying to register freeholds at the Carrickfergus General Quarter Sessions on 13 January 1831. His December 1830 entry describes houses and land in Upper Glenarm, townland of Larne, with a yearly value of £10.

This is an application published on 24 December 1830, not proof that registration succeeded. Glasgow-3986 is the strongest compatible local candidate, but the notice gives no age, occupation, relatives or bridge to the 1795 merchant or 1797 junior. It does not connect Larne to Lisnagaver.

[Belfast News-Letter, 24 December 1830, p.3, County Antrim freehold-registration applications](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390574399/)

### Juvenile

James Glasgow and Walter Moffat are named secretaries announcing the Belfast Juvenile Society’s sixth annual meeting at Rev. Dr Cooke’s May Street meeting-house on Monday 15 November 1830. In March 1831 Rev. Walter Moffatt and Mr James Glasgow attended a Knockbracken meeting as the Society’s deputation; Rev. Thomas Houston chaired it.

The repeated society and associates support one society account. The same Walter Moffat appears in the 1828 Institution report, making James-1780 a compatible student/ministry-network candidate; this does not prove that the society secretary is the college student. The newspaper does not call James the president, chair or minister.

[Belfast News-Letter, 12 November 1830, p.2, Belfast Juvenile Society sixth annual meeting announcement](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390573239/) · [Belfast News-Letter, 4 March 1831, p.4, Belfast Juvenile Society, Knockbracken meeting](https://www.newspapers.com/image/390543442/)

## Exclusion verified on original

Glasgow is his city, not his surname. The notice is dated Glasgow, 4 March, but the newspaper date is 7 March.
