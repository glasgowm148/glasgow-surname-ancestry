The 16 August 1831 declaration lists Robert Glasgow and John Glasgow among freeholders of the barony printed Loughinshollen (modern Loughinsholin), County Londonderry, supporting Sir Robert Bateson. They are co-signatories; no kinship, occupation, age or individual townland is given. Castledawson and 4 July 1831 refer to the opposing meeting discussed in the declaration, not their homes or their signing date.

## Cookstown merchant, 1848–1850

John Glasgow, explicitly called a merchant at Cookstown, appears among John Cassell coffee agents on 30 September 1848, and again in February 1849. A separate 2 February 1849 advertisement appoints him there as agent for the Russian Company’s Caravan Tea and Arabian Coffee. This extends the inspected Cookstown agency sequence to 1848–1850.

Glasgow-2058 is compatible by name, Cookstown district and the merchant occupation independently recorded in Benjamin-2059’s 1856 marriage. None of these advertisements names relatives, age or street address. Repeated agent lists are advertisements for one trading role, not independent family proofs. John’s parents remain unknown. Glasgow-1968 remains a weaker comparison: his family histories give migration to Michigan in 1844 or 1848; the Drummeen tithe and 1850/1851 probate leads are not securely assigned to him, and probate year is not a death date.

## Dundrum collection list, 1840

John Glasgow occurs in the Loyal National Repeal Association subscription report, under “Per Mr. Edward Byrne, Dundrum,” published on 19 October 1840. This is a personal surname occurrence in a named collection list.

The list supplies no age, occupation, exact address, relatives or individual subscription amount. Dundrum identifies the collection context; it is not an explicit statement of John’s residence.
