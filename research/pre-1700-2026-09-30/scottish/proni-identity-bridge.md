## Hamill identity and estate context

- **D1825/A/2**, subseries, [1628]–1814: its Clondahorky/Portin Island title summary places Hugh Hamill of Dublin in a 1673 acquisition from Sir Hans Hamilton, then identifies his 1674 recovery, 1675 mortgage, the **William Dutton of Greenforte → Archibald Glasgow of Ramelton bond in 1675/6**, and Hamill's 1682 conveyance of redemption rights to Michael Sampson and William Wray. The catalogue does **not** say the bond itself names Hamill or gives Archibald a right in the whole title group. This is archival/estate context rather than an express personal association.
- **D1939/18/8/3**, 22 June 1678: Richard Hansard of Lifford sold the Lifford estate for £3,452 to **Hugh Hamill of Strabane**.
- **D1939/18/8/4**, 21 December 1680: the catalogue includes an extended deed transcription calling **Hugh Hamill of Lifford, County Donegal, esquire**, granting a Ballindrait tenement and associated land to John Wigtoune of Raphoe. This joins Hamill's Lifford/Ballindrait landholding world; it contains no Glasgow.
- **D1939/18/8/2**, 1678–1698, lists Hamill's Lifford estate incumbrances. **D1939/25/2/2**, 6 October 1685, names Hamill of Strabane and the Creighton/John Hamilton/John Johnston debt group. **D1939/18/7/2A**, 13 March 1700, includes a schedule of Creighton's payments for Hamill secured only by deeds of 5–6 October 1685. Those dates and associates identify a continuous Lifford debt history.
- The 1673 Dublin Hamill and 1678–1700 Strabane/Lifford Hamill are plausible occurrences of one regional estate actor. The descriptions supply no explicit Dublin-to-Strabane identity statement. **D1618/2/17**, 25 March 1682, separately shows Hamill of Strabane in Castlederg mortgage dealings. **D1939/23/2/1**, 9 August 1680, places him in Abraham Creightoun younger's marriage settlement; it names no Glasgow or Archibald household relative.

This supports a **regional creditor/estate connection**, with two cautions: identification of the earlier Hamill and the precise significance of Archibald's separately catalogued bond. It is not yet the distinctive inherited estate or named family link sought in this task.
