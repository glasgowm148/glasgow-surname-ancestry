# Independent review: Betham MS 225 p. 90 — 30 September 2026

## Recommendation

**The abstract directly proves that Archibald Glasgow of Rathmalton, County Donegal, clerk, had an eldest son Thomas. Its attribution to the Irish cleric represented by Glasgow-1101 is confident. Attribution of that son to the Lifford/Raphoe man Glasgow-3556 is strongly probable, but the saved evidence does not yet close that identity gap sufficiently for my recommendation to mark the latter's father certain.** This is a material new father record, not merely another surname occurrence. Preserve the father attachment, strengthen its account, and distinguish direct kinship from the inferred identity.

**Supplement after fresh PRONI investigation:** see `proni-identity-bridge.md`. A newly inspected c.1700 estate-paper transcription, D1939/21/3, identifies Thomas Glascoe as purchaser of part of Hugh Hamill's Lifford lordship and explains his Creighton litigation. Archibald's 1675/6 bond is catalogued in another Hamill estate title group. This adds financial/estate context beyond the initial name-county-chronology argument described below. The catalogue does not state that Hamill was a party to Archibald's actual bond, and no title or family clause transmits an interest from Archibald to Thomas; my conservative recommendation remains strongly probable. The main researcher may weigh a confident inference, but must describe that inference accurately rather than presenting Betham as expressly identifying the Lifford man.

## Source actually inspected

Viewed the saved full image `research/pre-1700-2026-09-30/betham/ms225-image097.jpg`, NLI Genealogical Office MS 225, Old Series 3, handwritten p. 90, viewer image 97. The bottom pedigree names:

- `Archibald Glasgow / of Rathmalton in / Co Donegal Clk`;
- `will dat — April / 1673`;
- wife `Elizabeth / McCauley` (surname reading should retain the abstractor's transcription qualification);
- `Thomas Glasgow / eldest son`;
- Margaret, `only daur`, joined to Thomas Cairns (husband's surname should remain qualified until separately checked).

The image is Betham's pre-destruction genealogical abstract, not the complete will. It contains no visible Thomas residence, age, occupation, wife, property, executor designation or probate date. April 1673 is the will date, not Thomas's birth date or Archibald's death date. The separately indexed 1677 probate remains distinct.

## Strongest case for the proposed father

The testator's unusual full name, exact Ramelton/Rathmalton location and `clerk` designation identify the already researched Irish Archibald much more specifically than a same-name county match. His eldest son could readily be the Protestant esquire documented around Raphoe from 1684, at Lifford in 1689 and in the Ballindrait/Shannon property sequence through 1701. The dates follow naturally after a father's 1673 will and 1677 probate. The known Thomas has no securely established birth year that conflicts with the abstract; his `before about 1671` bound must not be treated as birth in 1670. A married daughter in 1673 also fits a mature household whose son subsequently appears in substantial transactions. No saved source positively identifies another contemporary Thomas of Donegal as a better match.

This evidence now resolves the previously missing *degree* if the son is Glasgow-3556: he is a son, not merely a possible nephew, brother or unspecified relative. It does not independently demonstrate that identification. The Creighton association strongly joins the Lifford/Raphoe man's later records to each other; neither Creighton nor a matching estate appears in this abstract. Archibald's William Dutton bond likewise supplies no bridge to Thomas in the saved material.

## Counteralternative and why the remaining gap matters

To make the proposed identification wrong, **Archibald's named son Thomas must be a different Thomas from the later Lifford/Raphoe esquire**. He could have died before 1684, lived elsewhere, or remained unrecorded, while another Thomas Glasgow became the Donegal legal actor. That alternative is not positively documented and should not be elevated into an equal competing pedigree. Nevertheless the abstract offers no discriminating identifier with which to test it; uncommon surname, compatible generation and county proximity are currently the whole bridge between the two Thomas record clusters. There are already several seventeenth-century bearers, so the surname is uncommon rather than demonstrated unique.

Thomas Glasgow-1103, baptised in Scotland in 1612, is **not a strong direct rival**: he would be about 72 in 1684 and 89 in 1701, and no saved Irish record identifies him. An undocumented son/grandson of that Thomas is only a hypothesis; it is not grounds by itself to reject Glasgow-3556. My reservation is the limited identification supplied by the newly inspected source, not a rule requiring a second literal father clause or an invented Scottish-descendant alternative.

A deed recital naming the Lifford Thomas as Archibald's son/heir, Thomas's own probate naming Elizabeth McCauley or the Cairns sister, or another sufficiently distinctive shared household/estate identifier would close the gap. One would suffice; all are not required.

## Scope and integration

The Irish father-son finding is independent of the uncertain identification of Archibald with the 1614 Glasgow child. Do not upgrade Archibald's George Glasgow-1099 father link from this abstract. It also gives no Thomas-to-Nathaniel descent.

Supersede the statements in both profiles that no inspected record names Archibald's wife/child or a Thomas son. Publish the newly proved household and the strong but qualified match to Glasgow-3556 in both maintained findings and their person pages, the Donegal family synthesis and affected open questions. Keep the original birth and later-life qualifications visible. This review made no live or site edits and created no browser session.
