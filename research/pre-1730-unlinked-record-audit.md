# Pre-1730 unlinked-record audit

The initial map contained fourteen pre-1730 associations marked `Unassigned
occurrence`. This audit separates record-backed matches, geographic clusters,
unproved reconstructions and profile leads whose Irish premise fails.

| Entry | Result | Cluster / profile assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alleged David Glasgow, Londonderry, 1696 | Withdrawn; no substantiated record or profile | The 16 April 1696 Corporation resolution and its 226 signatories are real, but accessible searches under Glasgow/Glasco/Glascho/Glascow produced no positive David. PRONI's digitised minute-book scans were not inspected page by page, so this is withdrawal of an unsupported lead rather than proof no Glasgow signed. Do not create David. |
| Glasgow among the 1689 Derry defenders/supporters | No exposed positive result | The Ulster Historical Foundation `Fighters of Derry` database is an index to W. R. Young rather than the primary source. Public searches exposed no usable Glasgow result. This is unresolved, not a negative proof; the digitised Londonderry freemen records are the next bounded city source. |
| Thomas Glasgow, active 1692–1695 | Record-backed match to `Glasgow-3556` | PRONI D1939 deeds name Thomas at Ballindroight/Ballindrait and, in 1694, as `of Shannon` in Raphoe. The map plots both localities; Shannon uses the modern Clonleigh locality without guessing between Shannon Lower and Middle. Early Donegal record cluster; no kinship to Killybegs James proved. |
| James Glasgow, Killybegs account dated 1728 | Record-backed match to `Glasgow-3001` | Early Donegal record cluster. The parliamentary table proves the name and Killybegs context, but 1728 is an accounting date rather than a death date. |
| Archibald Glasgow, Donegal, 1661–1675/76 | Candidate match to `Glasgow-1101` | Early Donegal record cluster. The April 1661 appointment names Clondavaddog, Tullyfern and Aughnish individually; PRONI `D1825/A/2` describes the 1675/76 bond obligee as `of Ramelton`. The map now shows all four places. No record yet bridges this Irish actor to the Glasgow-baptised M.A. |
| Nathaniel Glasgow M.A., active 1707–1743 | Corrected match to `Glasgow-1136` | The 1707 degree roll calls him `Scoto-Hibernus`; Sherling calls him a native of Keady, County Donegal. The original published Synod minutes record his installation at `Golen` by Monaghan Presbytery on 5 April 1732 and his death in April 1743. Modern Golan townland, Donagh parish, is the probable spelling match, but that modernization remains qualified. |
| Captain James Glasgow, Belfast, 1667–1686 | Record-backed match to `Glasgow-3172` | Distinct Belfast maritime record. PRONI `MIC19/2` names the shipmaster and `T/828/15` is a Belfast will extract. Nothing inspected links him to Kilwaughter. |
| Patrick Glasco, died 1754 aged 60 | Record-backed match to `Glasgow-3920` | Distinct Kilclone, County Meath family. The memorial explicitly makes Patrick father of James, Richard and William. |
| Charles Glasgow, baptised 1686, and James Glasgow, born c.1719 | Profiles `Glasgow-2917` and `Glasgow-2916`, but relationship unproved | Kept together only as the Cross/Derry reconstruction. The baptism proves a Scottish Charles; it does not prove migration to Cross or descent to James. No record joins this pair to Moneymore. |
| Unnamed Glasgow at Cavankilgreen, 1725 | No profile match | Locality cluster with [William Glasgow (Glasgow-3962)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3962), farmer, who appears at the same townland in 1746. Same-person identity is plausible but unproved. |
| John Glasgow, gentleman of Gortigillagh, County Leitrim, 1728 | No profile match; locality resolved | Standalone Leitrim deed actor. Logainm records modern Gortaggle, Annaduff parish, as `Gortogill` or `Cortogill` in a 1715 Registry deed. That near-contemporary spelling makes Gortaggle the strong match for the 1728 `Gortigillagh`; it supplies no family bridge to a Tyrone, Derry or Antrim profile. |
| George Glasgow, Dublin burial dated 22 December 1676 | Record-backed match to `Glasgow-3171`; family unplaced | IrishGenealogy record `DU-CI-BU-127347`, p. 74, identifies the Church of Ireland parochial area as St John, Dublin. The map now plots the historic St John church area at Fishamble Street; the register does not establish George's residence or relatives. |
| Janet Glasgow Woodside, claimed Irish birth c.1678 | Irish claim unsupported | No source connects her to Ireland or to John Glasgow of Cavers; do not place her in an Irish family cluster. |
| Rev. James Patrick Glasgow, claimed Irish birth c.1680 | Irish claim contradicted | Maryland ministry is documented, while the biographical source for his son calls him a native of Scotland. No Irish cluster assignment. |
| James Glasgow, baptised Kilbirnie 17 March 1700 (`Glasgow-1140`) | Scottish identity confirmed; claimed Irish evidence disproved | The profile mistakes Viscount Garnock's brother, James Crawfurd, for Robert Glasgow's brother. Moreover, the Ireland lines added to the 1720 letter and the alleged 1725 remittance were exposed as forgeries. Neither document concerns James Glasgow. |

## Localities still genuinely unresolved

After checking the profile text and available supporting material, no smaller
Irish locality is stated for Janet Glasgow Woodside, Rev. James Patrick
Glasgow, William Glasgow (`Glasgow-591`), William Walter Glasgow
(`Glasgow-1026`) or John Glasgow (`Glasgow-551`). Charles Glasgow's claimed
`Cross, County Londonderry` has no record securely identifying which Cross was
meant. These remain broad or rejected pins rather than guessed townlands.

The word *cluster* in the map now distinguishes an explicit family from a
record-only geographic grouping. Sharing a cluster colour does not assert
kinship where the record does not state it.
