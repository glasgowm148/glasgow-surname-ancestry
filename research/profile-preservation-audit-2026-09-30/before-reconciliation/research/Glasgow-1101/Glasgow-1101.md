== Biography ==

'''Archibald Glasgow''' was baptised on '''19 June 1614''' at Glasgow High Kirk. He was recorded as the lawful son of [[Glasgow-1099|George Glasgow]] and [[Stewart-65226|Margaret Steuart]]. William Howie, Michael Broune and Andrew Mylne were named in the baptismal entry.<ref>Glasgow High Kirk Baptisms 1609–1777, vol. 1/1329.</ref>

He graduated '''Master of Arts''' from the University of Glasgow in '''1635'''.<ref>University of Glasgow, University Story, “Archibaldus Glasgua,” graduate, Master of Arts, 1635. https://universitystory.gla.ac.uk/people?search=&surname=Glas&forename=</ref>

== Probable Identification in County Donegal ==

A series of Irish records concerns a clergyman named '''Archibald Glasgow''' who was active in County Donegal between approximately 1641 and 1677.

The records form a geographically and chronologically coherent clerical career and probably concern one man. The uncommon name, chronology and clerical background also create a strong circumstantial case that this was the Archibald Glasgow baptised in Glasgow in 1614 and educated at the University of Glasgow.

No inspected Irish record, however, explicitly identifies the Donegal clergyman as:

* the son of George Glasgow and Margaret Steuart;
* a native of Glasgow;
* the man baptised at Glasgow High Kirk in 1614; or
* the University of Glasgow graduate of 1635.

The identification should therefore be treated as probable rather than conclusively proved.

Records concerning the Irish Archibald include:

* An '''Archibald Glasgow''' serving as rector of '''Killea, County Donegal''', in or before 1641, with later restoration in 1661.<ref>Ulster Plantation and Migration project, person record for Archibald Glasgow. https://ulster-settlers.maynoothuniversity.ie/people/32084</ref>
* A clergyman of that name associated with '''Faughanvale''' in 1654 and '''Taboyne''' in 1660.<ref>Ulster Plantation and Migration project, person record for Archibald Glasgow. https://ulster-settlers.maynoothuniversity.ie/people/32084</ref>
* An Archibald Glasgow possibly mentioned in a 1657 disciplinary record concerning drunkenness.<ref>Virtual Record Treasury of Ireland. https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/IMC-AH-15-1944-MacLysaght</ref>
* A cleric named '''Archibald Glasgow''' appointed rector and vicar of '''Clondevadock, Tullyfern and Aughnish''' in County Donegal in April 1661.<ref>James Seaton Reid, ''A History of the Presbyterian Church in Ireland'', 1853, p. 278. https://en.geneanet.org/archives/ouvrages?action=detail&book_type=livre&livre_id=6279357&page=278</ref>
* An '''Archibald Glasgow''' appearing in a County Donegal Hearth Money Roll dated 1665.<ref>Virtual Record Treasury of Ireland, TCD MS 6232. https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/TCD-MS-6232</ref>
* '''Archibald Glasgow of Ramelton, County Donegal''', named as obligee in a bond from William Dutton of Greenforte dated 1675/76.<ref>Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, D1825/A/2, bond from William Dutton of Greenforte to Archibald Glasgow of Ramelton, 1675/76.</ref>
* '''Archibald Glasgow of Rathmalton, County Donegal, clerk''', whose prerogative will was proved in '''1677'''.<ref>Sir Arthur Vicars, ''Index to the Prerogative Wills of Ireland, 1536–1810'', entry for Archibald Glasgow, clerk, Rathmalton, County Donegal, 1677. https://archive.org/details/indextoprerogati00vica</ref>
* A separate probate-index transcription records '''Archibald Glascoe of Rathmatton, County Donegal''', in '''1679'''.<ref>Irish probate index transcription supplied July 2026, entry for Archibald Glascoe, Rathmatton, County Donegal, 1679.</ref>

“Rathmalton” was an historical spelling associated with Rathmelton or Ramelton. “Rathmatton” in the 1679 transcription is probably a spelling or transcription variant of the same place. These names should not be confused with Rathmullan.

The Archibald named in the 1675/76 Ramelton bond and the Rathmalton clergyman whose will was proved in 1677 were almost certainly the same person. The uncommon name, occupation, place and close dates strongly support that conclusion.

The 1679 entry probably represents a duplicate index entry, a later probate action or a transcription of the same estate rather than a second local Archibald Glasgow. The two-year discrepancy has not been resolved and should not be silently merged into a single probate date.

If the Donegal clergyman was the man baptised in Glasgow in 1614, he died before probate was granted in 1677, aged approximately 62 or 63. Ramelton was his recorded residence, but it has not been established as his place of death.

== Possible Connection to Thomas Glasgow ==

Matching the explicitly named eldest son to [[Glasgow-3556|Thomas of
Lifford, Ballindrait and Shannon]] is strongly probable. The uncommon name,
compatible chronology and Donegal location support the match, but the
abstract gives the son no residence, age, wife or estate. Retain the existing
father field as uncertain pending a distinctive identity connection.

Thomas's Creighton dealings form a coherent later record cluster. A newly
inspected PRONI catalogue transcription of a c.1700 Lifford estate paper
specifically describes his purchase of a Lifford interest from Hugh Hamill
and subsequent litigation; it does not name his father or establish an
inherited Ramelton estate.<ref>Public Record Office of Northern Ireland,
D1939/21/3, c.1700 Lifford estate paper; [https://apps.proni.gov.uk/eCatNI_IE/SearchPage.aspx

Archibald's Dutton bond is grouped among Hugh Hamill/Portin Island title
instruments in PRONI D1825/A/2. The description does not name Hamill as a party
to that bond. It strengthens the regional financial context but supplies no
father clause or property succession. The full D1939/18/8/1, /18/1/3,
/18/3/25, /18/7/1 and /18/11/16 deeds may contain identifying recitals omitted

== Other Proposed Relationships ==

A possible relationship with [[Glasgow-992|William Glasgow (1614–)]], who married Bessie Howie, has previously been suggested because the surname Howie appears in Archibald’s baptismal entry.

The presence of the same surname is insufficient to establish that Archibald and William were brothers or cousins. William Howie was named in Archibald’s baptismal entry, but the record does not state that he was a relative.

A connection with [[Glasgow-1097|Archibald Glasgow (c.1533–)]], a burgess and property owner in Glasgow, has also been proposed. The suggested great-uncle and grand-nephew relationship depends upon several reconstructed parent-child links that have not been established from contemporary records.

These relationships should remain research hypotheses.

== Research Conclusion ==

The Donegal records from 1641 to 1677 probably document the career of one clergyman, culminating in the 1675/76 Ramelton bond and the probate of the will of Archibald Glasgow of Rathmalton in 1677.

The 1679 entry for Archibald Glascoe of Rathmatton is probably connected with the same estate, but its precise nature remains unresolved.

Identity with the Archibald Glasgow baptised in Glasgow in 1614 and awarded an M.A. in 1635 is a strong chronological and occupational fit. It remains circumstantial because no inspected Irish record names his Scottish parents, birthplace or university.

Betham now names wife Elizabeth, eldest son Thomas and only daughter Margaret. The son-to-Lifford identity remains strongly probable; no descent to Nathaniel has been proved.
