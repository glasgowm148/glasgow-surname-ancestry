Last updated: 2026-10-07

## Current conclusion

A probate index records John Glasgow at Killroot, normally Kilroot, in 1733. This
is the strongest concrete candidate for the younger John implied by `John Glasgow,
sen.` at Kilwaughter about 1700 because it supplies a suitable later-generation
name in east Antrim. It does not state age, family or a Kilwaughter relationship.

Do not merge this occurrence with John `Glasgow-3913` or John `Glasgow-3959`.
The exact index image must first show whether 1733 is a will date, probate,
administration or administration bond. A death-related grant would exclude
identity with the Ballyboggy lessee alive in November 1740.

## Source findings — 1733 entry identified as an administration bond, 21 September 2026

The PRONI Name Search, Connor Diocesan Administration Bonds, indexes John Glasgow, Kilroot, 1733 as an **administration bond**. The occupation, notes, reference and other descriptive fields are blank, and PRONI states that the original bonds do not survive. This settles the document type queried above. An administration bond is a death-related grant. By inference, if the index names the deceased, this John was dead by 1733 and is unlikely to be the Ballyboggy lessee alive in November 1740, as the conclusion above anticipated; the index names no administrator, relative or exact date. See `research/Glasgow-3913/findings.md` for the full assessment.
