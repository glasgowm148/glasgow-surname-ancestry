# Alexander, son of Richard the messenger of Glasgow — 1283

## Current conclusion

The 1283 Paisley-register receipt defines a distinct associated person and directly proves him as son of the late Richard, messenger of Glasgow. It does not prove that either man bore Glasgow as a hereditary surname. Pre-1500: maintain the [individual free-space draft](../../surname-research/free-space-pages/Alexander_son_of_Richard_messenger_of_Glasgow.md); do not create a person profile.

## Source findings

* *Registrum Monasterii de Passelet*, pp. 383–384; [DEEDS 06190338](https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/06190338/). Alexander was principal in a burgage exchange and acknowledged receipt of five marks from Gilbert of the bishop's chamber.

## Identity and relationship assessment

Richard → Alexander is **certain from the text**. Identity with the 1289/90 escheator is **possible**, supported by the Glasgow property setting and six-to-seven-year interval but lacking a shared office, property, associate or explicit alias. Keep him separate from the contemporaneous son of Richard the former constable.

## Recommended next records

Trace the exchanged burgage, its referenced companion instrument, Richard the messenger, Gilbert of the bishop's chamber and later administrative occurrences of Alexander.
