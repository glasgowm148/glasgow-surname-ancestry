# Alexander de Glasgow, royal escheator — 1289/90

## Current conclusion

Alexander de Glasgow is securely recorded as a royal escheator; his parentage is unknown. Alexander, son of Richard the messenger of Glasgow, is now the leading local identity candidate, while Alexander son of Robert in Dublin remains a weaker alternative. Neither record should be merged with the official.

## Source findings

* Bain, *Calendar of Documents Relating to Scotland*, II, no. 409, p. 100, [Internet Archive text](https://archive.org/stream/calendarofdocume02edin/calendarofdocume02edin_djvu.txt), gives the old locator Chapter House (Scots Documents), Box 94, No. 5 and cross-references Stevenson I, p. 125. The 7 March 1289/90 receipt records Thomas de Clenhil, knight, receiving £6 13s. 4d. from Sir Alexander Balliol through Alexander, escheator south of the Forth, for a fee due at Pentecost 1288. Bain described the item as mutilated and without a seal.
* The separate *Exchequer Rolls of Scotland*, I, p. 47, reads `Alexandri de Glasgow` and supports the name independently of the damaged receipt.

## Identity and relationship assessment

The official's name and office are **high confidence**. Identity with Richard the messenger's son is **possible/moderate**; identity with the 1238/39 Dublin Alexander is **possible but lower confidence**. No father, spouse or child is established.

## Recommended next records

Ask TNA to convert the old Chapter House locator to a current reference using the date, recipient, payer, amount and old box number, then inspect the original name, endorsements and any additional identifying detail. Resolving `Glasgu` versus Bain's `Glasfrith (?)` would settle inclusion in the bearer register, not parentage by itself.
