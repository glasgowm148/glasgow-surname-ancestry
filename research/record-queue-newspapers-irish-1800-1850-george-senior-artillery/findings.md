# General George Glasgow

## Current conclusion

General George Glasgow is the preferred possible father of Edward George Glasgow, the 1823 groom of Emily Caroline Medlicott and the father in the Bermondsey/Dover household: 55% subjective confidence in the complete identification. A compiled family explicitly lists Edward George at Quebec in 1794 alongside George Mark in 1795, as children of George Glasgow and Magdalen Green. The unusual combination of the two sons’ full names, Quebec chronology and Green spouse identifies the general’s family more specifically than London locality alone. George Mark’s independently held 1851 obituary calls him a son of the late General Glasgow of the Royal Artillery. Edward’s military marriage context is compatible with this family. This replaces the selected Samuel Glasgow hypothesis, assessed at 10% on 6 November from broad place and estimated age alone; no source then named Samuel as Edward’s father.

Possible, not proved: the compiled tree cites no original baptism, and its Edward has not been independently identified as the adult husband of Emily Caroline. The family compilation and online member trees are not independent corroboration. Its dates may be baptisms rather than births. The general’s biography names Margaret Green whereas the compilation says Magdalen; its 1801 marriage follows several listed children, and the March 1794 Quebec entry precedes the known October 1794 posting. These remain questions, not exclusions or invented explanations. The 1841 index’s age 41 and the separate 1843 burial’s age 47 differ from the compiled 1794 date. General George’s original will is not yet read. No mother, birth/death field, merge or identity with Glasgow-3501 is selected. The 1843 death account stays separate with its own existing assessment; it is not a second newly linked son.
