These accounts keep roles and compatible candidates visible without assuming that each role is a separate new person. ## Current conclusion

The original 1856 register confirms Benjamin-2059 as John-2058’s merchant son. The Drapersfield Benjamin’s current July 1870 action follows Benjamin-2059’s February death and strongly separates them. Full viewer review of the 22 July 1858 report supersedes the prior ambiguous reading: Benjamin testified that he was the son of the late Robert Glasgow, had managed his father’s mill since 1850, and that Robert died in 1856. The co-plaintiff William remains a qualified brother inference from counsel’s reference to the late Robert’s sons; Benjamin’s evidence says “witness’s uncle, Mr. Glasgow, sen.”; the William identification is separate case context, with the side of the family unstated. Mothers remain unknown. Robert-2656 is a strong compatible father candidate (not proved as the mill Robert); Robert-3941 remains compatible. John-2058’s parents remain unresolved.

## 1800–1850 extension: distinguish the surgeon family

Benjamin Glasgow, surgeon, formerly of Vera Cruz, Mexico, died at New Orleans on 26 November last, according to a May 1834 notice. He was son of the late Robert Glasgow of Cookstown, County Tyrone. A Dublin notice repeats the surgeon, New Orleans and father details. 1833 is inferred from “last” in the 1834 publication; no age or mother is stated. Both notices concern one death, not independent witnesses. Glasgow-3412 is a strong compatible surgery-graduate candidate, not a proved identity. The father was dead by 20 May 1834, distinct from the mill father who died in 1856. Robert-3941 remains compatible by chronology only. No existing-profile father confidence is upgraded. [Connected sourced family draft](/research/subjects/newspapers-irish-1800-1850/#benjamin-surgeon).

4 October 2026 correction: the 1834 surgeon notice makes Glasgow-3412 a strong candidate for a Benjamin who died in 1833, rather than a surviving mill partner. If that surgeon identification is correct, the 1858/1870 mill activity is a concrete death-date conflict. Keep the surgeon and mill documentary accounts separate; Glasgow-3412’s surgeon identity itself remains qualified.

Neither notice identifies the already-deceased father of the surgeon.

Master Benjamin Glasgow of Cookstown is listed for one shilling towards support of scholars in the agricultural school in June 1850. A donor list, not proof that Benjamin attended the school. “Master” supplies no exact age. The surgeon who died in 1833 is excluded.
