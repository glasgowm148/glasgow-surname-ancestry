# WikiTree Research

Verify each claimed identity, relationship, date, and place against the underlying record. Distinguish documented facts, evidence-based inferences, and hypotheses.

- For a documented sibling pair sharing the paternal surname, a proved father of one sibling can support a confident inferred father for the other when chronology fits and no concrete alternative parentage or conflicting evidence is known. Do not keep that father link uncertain solely because the record omits “full” before “sister” or “brother”. Explain the combined evidence as an inference; assess maternity separately and do not turn an unsupported half-sibling possibility into an active competing hypothesis.

- When a user directs an audit to be additive, prioritize newly found records and sourced conclusions. Preserve existing profile text, citations, structured dates and family links; add a clearly qualified research note for conflicts. Do not turn the audit into removals, detachment or citation pruning unless the user later asks for a specific change.

- Keep the transcription index compact: short display names, record year with a concise supported place beneath it, populated kin fields only, and a link to the full reading. Put detailed dates, court/image references, source-availability notes and interpretation on case pages, not preview cards. Place the illustrated-reading guide after the catalogue, and do not add an index-wide original-pages availability panel. Maintain display labels separately from full research titles and evidence.

- Coloured teaching-font overlays must share the base word's text origin, padding, font metrics and wrapping. Check actual glyph registration with colour groups enabled on desktop and mobile; overlapping outer boxes do not demonstrate aligned lettering.
- Teaching-font colours attach to explicit character/glyph mappings, never horizontal-width masks of the whole word. Manuscript region masks remain separate and must not be represented as literal glyph tracing; keep neutral and coloured typography consistent.

- When a supplementary manuscript concerns a distinct estate but its family connection is untested, classify it as associated research material, not a rejected match or an unrelated family. Keep record boundaries separate from genealogical conclusions. Synchronize revised classifications and accepted names across case pages, family-summary cards, audit labels and downloadable data; correcting explanatory prose alone does not retire a stale status.

## Cost-aware research batches

- When asked to conduct actual research, perform readily accessible surname-variant, parent-only and relevant record-index searches before handing them back as suggested next steps. Identify searches not performed and concrete access gaps explicitly; an ordinary web-search miss is not a search of the underlying record collection.
- In research replies and handoffs, give the actual search terms and collection scope instead of vague phrases such as "broader searches". For archive searches, explain whether a hit identifies a person, a certificate or a whole volume, and provide enough detail to repeat unfinished searches.
- Follow the project's model routing: delegate file inventories, literal index extraction, formatting, citation-field comparisons and routine synchronization checks to `gpt-5.6-luna` when delegation saves work. Keep identity judgments and substantive drafting with the model appropriate to their complexity.
- Separate mechanical work from research judgments rather than assigning an entire mixed batch to a more expensive model. Reuse verified captures, request concise handoffs, and avoid duplicate investigations or broad reviews after focused checks pass.

## Preserve attached people and locations

- Never clear, detach, blank, or remove an attached person or structured location unless evidence supports a specific replacement and that replacement is applied in the same edit.
- Before every proposed or live structured-data edit, compare the existing and intended values. If a populated person or location would become empty, abort that part of the edit unless the sourced replacement person/profile or replacement location is ready to be entered immediately.
- A weak, uncertain, conflicting, broad, or apparently incorrect attachment/location is not sufficient reason to leave the field empty. Preserve it, mark it uncertain where WikiTree permits, and explain the conflict in Research Notes while investigating the evidenced replacement.
- This rule applies to live edits, WT+ fixes, paste-ready drafts, chronology corrections, and recommendations in `findings.md`. If no evidenced replacement has been established, make no destructive structured-data change.

## Identify and link every person

- Treat documentary rows as evidence about people, not as profile identities. Before creating or queueing anything, cluster compatible records into the fewest defensible people and search whether each cluster fits an existing WikiTree profile.
- In a duplicate check, identify the person the existing profile actually represents. A different person discussed in its hypothesis or research notes is a candidate identification, not automatically the same profile subject; compare the underlying records before merging.
- A historical record's lack of a WikiTree or other modern profile identifier is not an evidential limitation. Assess identity from the recorded name, dates, named relatives, locality, chronology and competing candidates; keep any real identity gap separate from missing database metadata.
- Separate identification of a recorded child from identification of the named adults across later households and from inferred death boundaries. A matching exact birth date must not acquire generic historical doubt because its presentation mapping is incomplete. Keep a complete multi-record argument in one canonical section, with working references elsewhere rather than duplicate proof tables.
- Do not let uncertainty about a documented child's later-life or migration identity downgrade a record-proved parent-child relationship. Assess and publish those two identity scopes separately. When repeated records establish spouses under literal given-name or surname variants, assess the spouse relationship independently and display the recorded forms beside the normalized profile name.
- A superseded accepted pedigree attachment and a still-open relationship hypothesis are separate assertions. Give the current hypothesis its own stable claim reference, retain the old claim and its provenance as history, and use the same scoped resolution in HTML, dossier relationships/claims and family-network exports. Never globally replace superseded statuses or transfer a possible father's spouse into an active mother hypothesis.
- Resolve approved record assessments before rendering or exporting: compare default dossier fields, claims, reciprocal relationships, embedded technical JSON and network exports, not only presentation fields. Keep identity qualifications beside resolved vital facts and preserve imported alternatives separately. Formatting overrides are not research decisions; missing publication mappings must not silently leave contradictory current assessments.
- Keep a record's date precision separate from confidence in identifying its subject. An exact register date can belong to a strongly supported or uncertain profile match. Resolve that match from the combined family evidence; do not require a marriage record as a blanket condition or downgrade it merely because an assessment was not mapped into the page.
- Prefer adding a compatible fact to an existing profile. Create a new profile only when the evidence defines a distinct person and a completed duplicate audit finds no compatible profile; if identity is ambiguous or candidate profiles remain, keep one consolidated evidence handoff on **HOLD**. Never create one profile or draft per fact.
- Treat automated same-name candidate scores as leads, not as surviving compatible profiles. When a manual review has explicitly excluded every returned candidate, documented each exclusion, and produced a complete sourced draft for a distinct person, mark the draft **READY TO CREATE**; a later audit must not restore **HOLD** solely from the same unreviewed name/place score.
- In a batch duplicate audit, compare exact dates, places, named relatives, occupations and residences before counting proposed people. Consolidate compatible repeated records into one person/HOLD, and document any index-date or spelling conflict instead of creating parallel identities from it.
- For death-index batches, search in two passes before calling a row unmatched: (1) the exact death date across compatible names; (2) the recorded forename and variants against existing profiles with a compatible age-derived birth range and locality, including profiles whose death date is blank or only a year. Respect `before`/`after` birth qualifiers, and compare documented birth, residence and death places with the registration district without treating that district as an exact residence. Review the full candidate profile before proposing a new person; an absent death date alone never rules it out.
- Before committing a new death-index profile, inspect WikiTree's creation-screen suggestions as a final duplicate check. Hold any record with a decent surviving candidate; create it only when the suggested profiles have a concrete conflicting date, later-life record, identity or locality history.
- Before creating an Unknown parent placeholder, search the named same-family parent or spouse first, using the children, household locality, dates and surname variants. Prefer the compatible named profile and document the evidence; use an Unknown placeholder only after that duplicate check finds no defensible fit.
- A civil-index profile is not finished when its cited mother or spouse is left unconnected. After the duplicate check, connect an existing compatible relative or create one sourced Unknown relative with the recorded surname, then attach all children supported as siblings. Audit newly created profiles for missing family links before closing a batch; reuse one relative profile wherever the records are compatible and split only for a concrete conflict.
- When the user supplies birth and marriage indexes with a death batch, cross-check all three before finalizing profiles. Use matching birth dates, registration districts and mothers' maiden surnames to identify children and sibling groups; check the marriage table for compatible parents and later-life contradictions. Apply supported dates and family links to live profiles and the public audit, rather than leaving the crosswalk as a list of unused leads.
- When the user asks to account for every entry in a combined civil-record batch, track each registration number to the live profile where its event is cited. Attach compatible birth, marriage and death entries to an existing person; create a new person only after a duplicate check finds no decent fit or finds a concrete conflict. A candidate link, local note or uncited crosswalk row does not complete the entry.
- For death-index creation batches, distinguish adults from children. When an adult woman's only surname evidence is the death index, record the index surname as current surname and use Unknown for LNAB unless other evidence supports it. For an infant or child with no plausible marriage, use the recorded surname as LNAB unless contrary evidence appears; do not add a married-surname caveat to a child's biography. A same-name, same-district birth shortly before a child's death can support an uncertain exact birth date when the age also agrees; cite both indexes and explain the identification briefly.
- When an index supplies only a registration district, use the clean place form (for example, `Belfast, Ireland`) in a structured location field and mark it uncertain. Say `Belfast registration district` only in the citation or narrative; do not put `registration district` in the location field. Write the profile biography around the recorded date, age, district, and any specific birth or marriage lead; avoid repeating generic audit or creation-process prose on live profiles.
- For live WikiTree batch searches, use the documented `searchPerson` action with the project's `appId` and pace requests. If an API response reports an unknown action or limit, first check the action, parameters, application ID and request rate; retry a valid small query before declaring the duplicate check blocked. Record a completed search and remaining identity uncertainty separately.
- When a death batch comes with birth or marriage indexes, cross-reference all supplied events before finalizing identity decisions or editing profiles. Cluster compatible entries into people, resolve profile candidates from dates, relatives and locality, and make one consolidated live edit per managed person. A single matching index row in an incomplete supplied list is a lead, not proof of identity.
- Search WikiTree before reporting on each named historical person, using name variants, dates, places, relatives, the local One Tree export when relevant, and the live WikiTree API.

Give each distinct pre-1500 record bearer an individual WikiTree free-space page instead, and link that page from the consolidated early-bearers free-space page. If the individual page does not yet exist, prepare a free-space-page draft outside `surname-research/new-people/`; if it exists, improve and cross-link it. Pre-1500 people remain documentary subjects, not profile-creation tasks.

Keep the attachment intact, maintain the sourced biography on the canonical individual free-space page, and link that page prominently from managed relatives and project pages so readers reach the current account first.
- If a matching profile is established, make the person's name clickable every time the person is introduced: use `[Display Name (WikiTree-ID)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/WikiTree-ID)` in ordinary responses and `[[WikiTree-ID|Display Name]]` in paste-ready WikiTree markup.
- Public individual catalogue pages must expose confirmed WikiTree destinations prominently beside the page heading, with an explicit WikiTree action and identifier. Do not bury the only link in capture metadata or source prose; keep candidate matches distinct and use canonical individual free-space destinations for applicable pre-1500 subjects.
- Keep the catalogue One-Tree view free of repeated research-status and confidence tags. Use parent-link row colours with a shared legend and accessible status text; give names, life details and spouses a clear reading order, with research notes and alternative parent links in each person's menu.

Show evidence, alternatives and next research actions only when supported by the case file, not invented confidence scores or generic resolutions. Date the Latest research feed from maintained research entries, not filesystem timestamps or rebuild dates, and preserve the release history separately.
- For a substantive individual-page review, compare the live WikiTree biography with the maintained evidence and present a coherent life account with visible census-household and other record tables. Keep record wording, source provenance, identity assessment and unresolved conflicts distinct; do not hide the principal evidence inside technical archives or substitute parent-candidate scores for the biography. Where an established descendant Y-DNA result is relevant, state the known named SNP and historical comparison lines, distinguish confirmed documentary ancestry from the genetic connection, and explain the specific unresolved relationship. Audit the wider maintained case for evidence needed to explain the person's life, family, places and changing circumstances. Connect records into explicit arguments: explain identity continuity, source independence, conflicts, plausible alternatives and what would distinguish them. Bring consequential existing findings into the narrative without waiting for the user to enumerate them; distinguish available evidence from research still required, and do not invent personal experiences to fill gaps. Never leave a named person unlinked without the applicable recommendation or HOLD reason.
- If a possible profile match is not proved, link it explicitly as a possible candidate, explain the conflict or missing evidence, and do not treat it as that person.
- When several record subjects lack profiles, list each one and recommend a separate profile only where the evidence distinguishes separate people.

## Prepare WikiTree material

- When a live profile update adds a record that names a spouse or other relative, find or create that person's profile in the same task. Use LNAB `Unknown` when the maiden name is unrecorded, and state any relationship the record implies but does not state in that profile's Research Notes. Don't leave a named relative as plain text in the biography.
- Link every person mentioned in a biography who has a WikiTree profile, not only Glasgows: spouses, children, siblings and informants. Before linking a candidate identity, check it against known facts; for example, a man who emigrated or died elsewhere cannot be a later local occupier.
- When a record gives an exact date or place, set that structured field's status to `certain` in the same edit. Check every date and place field the record supports, not only the one you were correcting.

- Cite original images or primary records where available; identify transcriptions as such.
- Use logical, evidence-led speculation constructively when it helps explain a family or property pattern. Label the proposed relationship or reconstruction clearly once, give the concrete reasons for it, and write the surrounding prose naturally; do not smother a sensible hypothesis in repetitive disclaimers. Reserve categorical language for documentary proof.
- Assess the actual proposed multi-generation pedigree against the wider catalogue before ranking parents. Separate immediate parentage, collateral kinship, duplicate identity and the upstream joining generation; evidence against one edge must not reject the others. Do not replace a collateral hypothesis with an invented common-father scenario. Compare record-supported branches, migration, age ranges and DNA together, explaining which connections the combined evidence favours and what would distinguish the remaining models.
- Save provenance at discovery time, not during profile drafting: whenever a mapped record is added or materially researched, populate `source_title`, the original external `source_url`, `source_type`, and `source_status` in `www/map/data/records.csv` before moving on. Never rely on browser history, chat, a local capture, or the public catalogue to reconstruct the URL later.
- Verify the saved URL resolves to the claimed record. Prefer an item/image URL; when the archive exposes only session-bound results, save its stable public search/catalogue URL plus the collection, sub-index, reference, date, place and access limitation in the title/status. If no external source can be recovered, mark the work **HOLD** and do not generate paste-ready profile text.
- Treat `glasgow.phenotype.dev`, relative repository paths and local `sources/` files as internal research aids only. They must never populate a public citation or `source_url` field.
- Provide paste-ready native WikiTree syntax, not Markdown, and defensible creation fields.
- Write every paste-ready replacement as a complete, polished profile—not a patch note appended to the old biography. Use the durable `Current conclusion` as the narrative basis, preserve useful sourced material from the live profile, remove obsolete or misleading text, and explain the evidence naturally.
- Never remove an existing source or citation from the captured live WikiTree biography when preparing a replacement. Integrate it naturally into the narrative, Research Notes, or Sources even when it is derivative, weak, or supports a rejected claim. Qualify what it proves. Exact duplicate occurrences of the same citation may be consolidated while keeping one complete, accessible citation and every distinct record. Do not mention “the earlier profile,” “retained sources,” or the editing process in the biography. A replacement may improve, supplement, or supersede a citation, but every distinct source must remain identifiable and accessible.
- Never overwrite live biography research with a shorter, condensed or reorganised replacement. Before saving a full replacement, compare it with the live biography and confirm every existing sourced statement is preserved; a queue flag such as `preserve_captured_detail: false` or a "concise controlling draft" is not licence to drop researched narrative. If the replacement would reduce the live text, do not save it: apply the new material additively (append or integrate the new section, research note or correction) while leaving the existing account intact, or reconcile the two first. When in doubt, prefer an additive edit and keep the full prior text.
- When a biography corrects a vital fact, apply the same correction to WikiTree's structured fields in the same task — birth/death date and location values plus their certainty statuses, names, and parent/spouse relationships. Never leave a corrected date or place only in the prose while the data fields still hold the old value.
- Never paste editorial or process notes into a profile: no HTML comments such as `<!-- REVIEWED FULL REPLACEMENT ... -->`, no "the profile previously used X", "the structured date should therefore be", "the existing source is retained as a finding aid", or explanations of a baptism-vs-birth or index-vs-field edit. A profile records the person and the evidence, not the edit. Research Notes are only for genuine unresolved research, identity conflicts and source limitations; keep a replaced citation in the text or Sources without narrating that it was retained.
- Write in the voice of a human profile editor. For a corrected relationship, prefer prose such as `[[Glasgow-123|Robert Glasgow]] was previously attached as his father. However, ...` followed by the evidence and outcome. Never put editor commands or headings such as `Required profile change`, `Add`, `Remove`, `Detach`, `Replace`, `Revise`, or `Evidence-led project update` inside the copied biography.
- A replacement profile must normally contain the appropriate templates, `== Biography ==`, a prose life narrative with inline `<ref>` citations, `== Research Notes ==` for identity conflicts, uncertain relationships and rejected claims, and `== Sources ==` with `<references />`. Use named references when a source supports more than one statement. Do not dump bare URLs or an AI work queue into the biography.
- Plausible working relationships may remain attached with an explicit uncertain status. Do not present them as proved. Replace an attachment only when positive evidence establishes the specific replacement; never detach it merely because the current placement is weak or contradicted if that would leave no evidenced replacement.

## Maintain findings and catalogue person pages

- Manuscript boxes must enclose complete letterforms, including ascenders, descenders, abbreviation strokes and trailing flourishes, with a small visible margin. Never fit only the word's body or mechanically expand every box by the same percentage. Where lines overlap, allow contextual strokes and explain them; do not truncate the target word to avoid overlap. Keep numbers and controls outside the handwriting and show one selected outline at a time when full-stroke boxes overlap.
- For illustrated testament readings, provide magnified, unaltered word samples and image-specific letterform explanations that a secretary-hand novice can compare with the source. Separate literal reading, expanded/modern form, observed strokes, same-clerk comparisons and unresolved alternatives. Do not manufacture visual features or use an expected legal formula as proof of unreadable letters. Organise the transcription index by record-keeping court area and ascending record year, showing the year prominently and distinguishing it from death dates.
- A letter guide must identify its word, letter position and exact source-pixel region, with a wider passage locator and an original-only default. Align any coloured path to visible ink, not to an expected letter shape; do not trace overlapping or unreadable strokes as certain. Withhold unaligned guides rather than presenting decorative curves as evidence. Prefer source-specific comparisons from supplied examples and the same document; label teaching alphabets separately and never use them as proof of the reading.
- When the user supplies a saved full-text/machine transcription of a record, continue with it in the same task: assess and record its content, clearly labelled as a transcription with its provenance and OCR-error risks. Do not block on obtaining the original image, and do not spawn image-verification agents when no supplied image path exists. Schedule a source-image visual check as a follow-up, and treat any image-only conclusion as unverified until that check.
- Use Astra (`gpt-6-astra`) at `xhigh` for manuscript vision, letterform alignment and visual review of transcription walkthroughs. Make important kin names independently inspectable through numbered source-crop reading steps, comparisons and explicit alternatives, not generic alphabet descriptions. Reproduce requested transcription UI problems in a real browser and inspect the rebuilt desktop/mobile views and reading controls before handing back; a successful local build alone does not satisfy the visual check.
- Letterform teaching must pair the actual manuscript with toggleable, clearly labelled coloured stroke guides and sourced comparative letterforms. Keep original pixels unchanged, locate traces on the exact displayed image, preserve full selected strokes and explain untraced intersections or damage. Trace only visible diagnostic ink; never reconstruct missing strokes, imply a proven pen-stroke order, or present a guide as the original ink.
