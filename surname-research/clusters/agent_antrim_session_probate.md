# East Antrim: Kilwaughter session and probate evidence

Research scope: the 1669 Kilwaughter hearth-roll entries, the Larne/Kilwaughter congregational record for 1699-1701, and the James Glasgow will indexed in 1727.

## Result

The public evidence proves an early **Kilwaughter Glasgow cluster**, but not a pedigree within it.

- In 1669, **James Glascho** and **Jo Glascho** were separate taxable householders in the Kilwaughter Demesne. **Elizabeth Glascho** was separately at Glenarm Town.
- In 1699-1700, **John Glasgow senior**, **Robert Glasgow**, and **James Glasgow** held responsible congregational roles. No entry calls any one of them another's father, son, brother, heir, or other relative.
- A surviving 1721-1769 session-book list names **James Glasgow** as a session member and marks him **deceased in 1726**. In 1722 he was one of six commissioners to the Presbytery meeting at Larne.
- PRONI's index confirms a **James Glasgow of Kilwater** in **Connor Diocesan Wills, 1727**. PRONI explicitly states that the original document does not exist and that no further information survived. The entry has no occupation, parish, county, notes, description, or PRONI document reference. It therefore cannot identify a wife, child, executor, beneficiary, or other kin.

The best-supported reconstruction is that the James active in 1700, the session member active in 1721-22 and deceased in 1726, and the 1727 Kilwater testator are probably the same man. This remains a high-quality circumstantial identification, not an explicitly linked record chain. The 1669 James may also be that man, but the 31-year gap makes that a weaker hypothesis.

## Source chain and record survival

### 1. 1669 Hearth Money Roll transcript

W. Macafee's surname-sorted transcript records the following on PDF page 36:

| Record | Name as recorded | Barony | Parish | Townland |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 5348 | James Glascho | Glenarm Upper | Kilwaughter | Demesne, unnamed |
| 5347 | Jo Glascho | Glenarm Upper | Kilwaughter | Demesne, unnamed |
| 5730 | Elizabeth Glascho | Glenarm Lower | Tickmacrevan | Glenarm Town |

Source: [W. Macafee, *Hearth Money Rolls (1669) ... Co. Antrim*, p. 36](https://www.billmacafee.com/1660shearthmoneyrolls/1669hearthmoneyrollsantrim.pdf).

This source proves co-residence of James and Jo within the same Demesne grouping, not a relationship. It does not expand `Jo` to John, although John is the usual interpretation.

### 2. Porter's transcript of the lost 1699-1701 book

Classon Porter printed the early material in *The Christian Unitarian*. His source statement is unusually useful:

- In 1863 he said the original could not be found, but a transcript had been made or obtained by Rev. Samuel Eccles for Rev. D. Davison. Porter obtained that transcript and printed copious extracts from it. He described it as covering 1699-1701.
- In his concluding 1864 inventory, Porter described the surviving item as a folio transcript of part of an old session book, said he still did not know the original's location, feared it was lost, and stated that he had printed all material parts of the transcript.

Sources:

- [Classon Porter, "Congregational Memoirs: Larne and Kilwaughter," *The Christian Unitarian* 2 (1863), pp. 409-414](https://archive.org/details/christianunitari0002unse)
- [Classon Porter, continuation, *The Christian Unitarian* 3 (1864), pp. 15-19 and 379-380](https://archive.org/details/christianunitari0003unse)

The published transcript supplies these Glasgow facts:

| Date | Person | Place or district | Explicit role or action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 Mar 1699 | John Glasgow, senior | Parish of Larne; attended Presbytery at Ballymenoch | One of four men appointed to present the congregation's supplication to expedite William Ogilvie's trials. The inserted Presbytery minute independently lists John among the four men who appeared from Larne. |
| Apr 1699 | John Glasgow, senior | Larne/Kilwaughter meeting house | One of five heads of families asked to decide the rebuilt meeting house's dimensions and timber requirements. |
| 17 May 1699 | John Glasgow, senior | Kilwaughter | With Patrick Agnew, acted as bondsman for GBP8 of the minister's stipend plus proportional firing, ploughing, and harrowing. |
| 17 May 1699 | Robert Glasgow | South Side | With James Callwell, acted as bondsman for GBP10 plus turf, ploughing, and other duties. |
| 26 May 1699 | Robert Glasgow | Presbytery not specified | One of four appointed to produce the stipend bonds to Presbytery and petition for Ogilvie's ordination to be expedited. |
| 27 Feb 1700 | John Glasgow | Kilwaughter | With Robert White, chosen to inspect the minister's land for its division into ploughing and harrowing shares. With Patrick Agnew, also appointed to determine how the house should be repaired, the cost, and how funds should be raised. |
| 27 Feb 1700 | Robert Glasgow | South Side of the water | With Callwell and Robinson, chosen to inspect the land. With Callwell, also appointed to determine the house repair and finance. |
| 29 May 1700 | James Glasgow | Kilwaughter | Appointed district overseer of the office-house work, including provision of materials and agreement with workmen. |

Important limits:

- `senior` distinguishes John from a younger contemporary John but does not state that the younger man was his son.
- The 1699-1700 entries call these men heads of families, representatives, bondsmen, appointees, or overseers. They do **not** explicitly call John, Robert, or James elders in those entries.
- The three surnames appearing in the same congregational transcript do not establish a relationship between the men.

### 3. Surviving 1721-1769 session book

Porter's 1864 continuation transcribes the first-page list headed "The Names of the Session" from the book beginning in 1721. It includes:

- **James Glasgow**, name erased, marked **deceased 1726**.
- **Robert Glasgow**, name erased, marked **turned out**.
- **Hugh Glasgow**, name erased, marked **dead 8 July 1767**.

Porter then prints the relevant entries:

- **3 June 1722:** Presbytery met at Larne. James Glasgow was one of six named commissioners. The minute separately says Brice Blair attended as elder, so James's role in this particular entry should be described as commissioner, not Presbytery elder.
- **17 May 1747:** Robert Glasgow, formerly a member of the Session, was accused of fornication and refused public satisfaction. This explains the first-page annotation "turned out."

Source: [Porter, *The Christian Unitarian* 3 (1864), pp. 196-198 and later 1747 discussion](https://archive.org/details/christianunitari0003unse).

PRONI's official guide confirms that the Larne and Kilwaughter Non-Subscribing Presbyterian material at `MIC1B/6` contains baptisms from 1720, marriages from 1721, session minutes from 1720-48, discipline cases from 1721-49, and poor accounts from 1720-57. It does **not** list an original 1699-1701 volume. Source: [PRONI, *Guide to Church Records*, pp. 289-290](https://www.proni.gov.uk/files/proni/2026-01/PRONI%20Guide%20to%20Church%20Records.pdf).

This explains the evidence chain: the 1721 onward material survives in the later book; the 1699-1701 material survives publicly through Porter's nineteenth-century printing of a transcript whose original was already missing.

## Identity assessment

| Proposed continuity | Assessment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Jo Glascho, 1669 -> John Glasgow senior, 1699-1700 | Plausible, not proved | Same uncommon surname and exact parish/demesne district; 30-year interval; no age, signature, occupation, or kin link. |
| James Glascho, 1669 -> James Glasgow, 1700 | Plausible, not proved | Same uncommon name and exact parish; 31-year interval. A 1669 householder could still be active in 1700, but the records do not identify him. |
| James Glasgow, 1700 -> session member/commissioner, 1721-22 | Strongly plausible, not proved | Same uncommon name, congregation, and pattern of responsible congregational service; 21-year interval; no explicit backward identifier. |
| Session member James, deceased 1726 -> Connor will entry, Kilwater, 1727 | Very strong circumstantial identification | Same name and exact locality, with probate indexing immediately after the recorded death. No will survives to close the chain. |
| James, 1669 -> James, deceased 1726 | Plausible but lower confidence | It requires one man to remain locally visible across 57 years. Biologically possible, but there is no age or relationship evidence. |
| Robert, 1699-1700 -> session member expelled 1747 | Unresolved | The first man was already an adult in 1699. The later man could instead be the younger Robert who married in 1729. The session list spans 1721-69 and does not date Robert's admission. |

## Consequence for the root cluster

Treat the following as one geographic and congregational **cluster**, not as a proved family:

- 1669: Jo and James at Kilwaughter Demesne.
- 1699-1700: John senior at Kilwaughter, Robert at South Side, James at Kilwaughter.
- 1721-26: session member James, deceased 1726.
- 1727: James of Kilwater in the Connor will index.

Do not derive a father-son chain from the sequence of forenames. No located record names the parents, spouse, children, siblings, executor, beneficiary, or heir of the James who died in 1726. The will index cannot be pushed further because PRONI expressly records that the document and any fuller information do not survive.

The archived Donna Jean Glasgow timeline is useful as a finding aid, but it labels its proposed identities as belief or probability and explicitly says that, for labeling purposes, it treats the 1669 and 1726 James entries as the same person. That is a research convention, not record proof.
