## Results

### [George Glasgow (Glasgow-3171), birth unknown, buried Dublin in 1676](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3171)

The stale IrishGenealogy link has been replaced by the current official
record:

- [IrishGenealogy record `DU-CI-BU-127347`](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/view?record_id=3aacb9f330-62336)
- [Original St John register image, page 74](https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/church/d-328-1-2-074.pdf)

The original image reads: **22 December 1676, George Glasgow, buried in the
church**. The index identifies the denomination/area as Dublin Church of
Ireland and the parish as St John. It supplies no age, address, occupation,
relative or entry number.

Consequences:

- The record proves a **burial**, not a death on that date. Death was on or
  before 22 December 1676.
- The event place can be stated as St John, Dublin; birthplace and residence
  remain unknown.
- The profile's estimated birth in 1600 has no support and should be removed.
- Burial inside the church may suggest means or standing, but cannot identify
  a family or occupation without a fee schedule, vestry record or grave entry.
- Nothing in this record connects George to Belfast, rural Ulster, or the
  eighteenth-century Dublin watchmakers.

### [James Glasgow (Glasgow-3172), shipmaster active in 1679, Belfast-associated](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3172)

The public description for PRONI `MIC19/2`, *Business letters of George
McCartney, Merchant*, is dated 1672-1681 and contains two entries on 8 November
1679:

- George Macartney told Peter Cossart that he had freighted the *James of
  Belfast*, James Glasgow master, to carry butter and tallow to Havre de Grace.
- Macartney told Sir Josua Allen that he had freighted James Glasgow's ship for
  the same cargo.

This directly proves James was master of a Belfast-designated vessel in 1679
and places him in George Macartney's Belfast-to-France mercantile network. It
does not name James's wife, children, parents, birthplace or home address.
"Captain" is a reasonable modern description of a shipmaster, but the record
itself calls him **master**. The second letter's shorthand reference to his
ship should not be treated as proof that he owned it.

Relevant catalogue copies:

- PRONI eCatalogue, reference `MIC19/2`: [catalogue search](https://apps.proni.gov.uk/eCatNI_IE/SearchPage.aspx)
- [National Library of Ireland catalogue, MS 13,521-22](https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/MS_UR_044709): microfilm `n.4343-4, p.4014-5`, described as a 1944 transcript of two outgoing-letter volumes whose originals are at the Linen Hall Library.

### Claims not yet verified

  result is titled *Business Letters of George McCartney, Merchant ...* and is
  dated about 1660-1667. The result exposes no digital record. The inherited
  claim that James was master of the *Blessing of Saltcoats* on 29 October
  1667 remains unverified until the full catalogue description or transcript
  is inspected.
- The inherited statement that PRONI `T/828/15` is a 1686 Belfast will extract
  for James Glasgow was not independently recovered during this audit. It must
  not yet be cited for a terminal date or family. Order or inspect the extract
  and transcribe every person, place, occupation, witness and executor.
- The inspected evidence therefore proves one James in 1679. It does not yet
  prove that the alleged 1667 shipmaster, the 1679 master and the alleged 1686
  testator were one person.

## Maritime context

T. C. Smout's published study shows that northern Ayrshire, especially
Saltcoats, maintained heavy trade with the Ulster ports, including Belfast.
That makes a Saltcoats/Belfast voyage historically ordinary and does not by
itself establish Scottish birth or kinship for a vessel's master.

- T. C. Smout, [*The Overseas Trade of Ayrshire, 1660-1707*](https://aanhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/overseas-trade-of-ayrshire.pdf), especially printed pp. 56-57 and the discussion of Saltcoats-Ulster traffic.
