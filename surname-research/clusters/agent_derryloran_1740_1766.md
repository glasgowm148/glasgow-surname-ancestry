# Derryloran Glasgow household audit, 1740-1799

Last updated: **17 July 2026**

## Conclusion

The surviving records do not support one pedigree called a
`Derryloran-Moneymore branch`. They support three Derryloran townland clusters,
one later record-backed nuclear family, and a separate adjacent Kildress
occurrence set:

```text
Derryloran parish, 1740 (townlands not recorded)
  James + James + William + William
    |  aggregate continuity is plausible; exact identities are not proved
    v
Derryloran parish, 1766
  Drumearn:    James + James + John       [working extended-family cluster]
  Gortein:     Samuel                     [separate root]
  Killycurry:  William                    [separate root]

Derryloran parish register, 1799
  William + Mary
    -> James, baptized 21 Aug 1799         [proved nuclear family]

Adjacent Kildress parish, 1766
  Whitehill:   Samuel Glasgoe
  Corvanaghan: James Glasgow              [separate occurrence set]
```

No inspected record states a relationship between the 1740 householders, ties
any one of them to a named 1766 householder, or connects the Derryloran names to
the profile-based Moneymore pedigree. The smallest defensible topology is
therefore **not one family tree**. It is the clusters above, with possible links
recorded as hypotheses.

## Exact 1740 occurrences

[Macafee's surname-sorted transcription of the 1740 Protestant-householder
return](https://www.billmacafee.com/1740returns/1740religiousreturnsulstername.pdf),
PDF p. 119, contains four separate Derryloran entries. The return gives the
parish, but no townland, age, spouse, child or relationship.

| Record | Original surname | Forename | What it proves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14391, list no. 128 | `Glasgo` | `Jas` | One James household in Derryloran |
| 14393, list no. 130 | `Glasgo` | `Jas` | A second James household in Derryloran |
| 14466, list no. 203 | `Glascy` | `Wm` | One William/possible variant-surname household in Derryloran |
| 14571, list no. 308 | `Glasgow` | `Wm` | A second William Glasgow household in Derryloran |

The standardized index treats `Glascy` as Glasgow, but its different original
spelling should remain visible. It is reasonable to search both `Glasgow` and
`Glassey` descendants; it is not reasonable to merge them solely from this
index normalization.

## Exact 1766 occurrences

The [National Archives of Ireland original Derryloran return,
`IHP/1/665`](https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/NAI-IHP-1-665) is preserved as
four images. The [RCB Library manuscript `MS 23`](https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/RCBL-MS-23)
is a complete typed transcript of that original. Its [transcript p. 162](https://by2022-prod.adaptcentre.ie/loris/RCBL_23_162.JPG/full/full/0/default.jpg)
and [p. 163](https://by2022-prod.adaptcentre.ie/loris/RCBL_23_163.JPG/full/full/0/default.jpg)
give these exact entries:

| Townland heading | Entry | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drumearn | `Jas. Glasgo` | Definite Glasgow occurrence 1 |
| Drumearn | `Jas. Glasgo` | Definite Glasgow occurrence 2 |
| Drumearn | `Jn. Glasgo` | Definite Glasgow occurrence 3 |
| Gortein | `Sam Glasgo` | Definite Glasgow occurrence 4 |
| Killycurry | `Wm Glasgo` | Definite Glasgow occurrence 5 |
| Killkeehan | `Sam Glassey` | Search collateral; do not merge with Glasgow |
| Grange Lands | `Wm Glassey` | Search collateral; do not merge with Glasgow |

This is decisive for transcription, but not for pedigree. There are **five**
definite Glasgow householders in 1766, not six. There is **no James Glasgow at
Killycurry** in either the complete RCBL transcript or the original return.

## What can and cannot bridge 1740 to 1766

The two James heads in both returns make continuity at parish level plausible.
One or both 1740 Jameses could have survived to 1766, but the 1740 return lacks
townlands and the 1766 return lacks ages. Repetition of the same number and name
is not identity evidence.

Only one definite William appears in 1766. He could be either 1740 William, a
son or other relative of either man, or a later arrival. The disappearance of
the other William can equally be explained by death, migration or failure to
appear in this return.

John at Drumearn is likely close collateral of the two Jameses because three
rare-surname households occur together in one small townland. The record does
not say whether he was a brother, son, nephew or cousin. The best evidence grade
is **plausible close family; exact relationship uncertain**.

Samuel at Gortein and William at Killycurry should remain separate documentary
roots. Their shared parish is a useful search umbrella, not a proved
relationship. The 1740-to-1766 interval is 26 years, so a same-person and a
next-generation explanation are both chronologically possible.

## The 1799 nuclear family

The RCB Library's [published transcription of the Derryloran parish register,
p. 3](https://www.churchofireland.org/cmsfiles/pdf/AboutUs/library/AngRecord/DerryloranVol1.pdf#page=11)
records:

> James GLASGOW, parents William/Mary, baptized 21 Aug 1799.

This directly proves a **William Glasgow + Mary Unknown -> James Glasgow**
nuclear family in Derryloran. No abode, occupation or birth date is supplied.

[James Glasgow (about 1799), Glasgow-1702, County Tyrone; later
Ballynasollus](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1702) is a strong identity
candidate because his 1851 civil marriage names his father as William, farmer,
and his 1882 death age estimates birth about 1799. This is still a candidate,
not a proved identity: the adult records do not name his mother and the baptism
does not give an abode.

The currently attached parents of Glasgow-1702 are not record-backed as drawn:

- [William Glasgow (1760), Glasgow-2915, claimed Scotland then Cookstown](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2915)
  is sourced only to a MyHeritage tree. The St Luran register proves that the
  baptized James's father was William, but does not prove that this profile is
  that William or that he was born in Scotland.
- Margaret Mitchell is sourced only to an online tree. If Glasgow-1702 is the
  baptized James, the register instead names his mother as **Mary**.

The 1766 Killycurry William could biologically be the 1799 father, but he would
have been at least about 51 in 1799 even if only 18 in 1766. A son or younger
collateral named William is at least as plausible. No direct edge should be
drawn.

## Separate Kildress evidence

The [original 1766 Kildress return, `IHP/1/667`](https://www.virtualtreasury.ie/item/NAI-IHP-1-667)
and the [RCBL complete transcript, p. 176](https://by2022-prod.adaptcentre.ie/loris/RCBL_23_176.JPG/full/full/0/default.jpg)
record `Sam Glasgoe` at Whitehill and `James Glasgow` at Corvanaghan. This is an
adjacent-parish research set, not an extension of Drumearn. `Corvanaghan` in
Kildress must not be confused with `Gortnavreeghan` in Templeport, County Cavan.

## Negative and coverage evidence

No Glasgow/Glasgo entry appears in the public [1796 County Tyrone flax-growers
list](https://failteromhat.com/flax/tyrone.htm). This list records qualifying
flax-bounty recipients, not all households, so absence must not be treated as
death, migration or extinction of a cluster.

[RootsIreland's Derryloran source guide](https://www.rootsireland.ie/derry-genealogy/derryloran-civil-parish/)
shows why the gap is difficult: the main surviving parish substitutes are the
1740, 1766 and 1796 lists, while Derryloran Church of Ireland baptisms begin in
1797. The 1799 baptism is therefore a genuine new bridge into the later local
families, but it cannot reconstruct the missing relationships by itself.

## Recommended proof targets

1. Search the St Luran original register and all later entries for additional
   children of William and Mary, an abode, occupation, burial or marriage.
2. Trace the 1799 James through Killycurragh and Ballynasollus land and church
   records to test the Glasgow-1702 identity.
3. Obtain the original 1759 testament/will-calendar item cited by Glasgow-1025;
   residence, beneficiaries and executors could separate the two Drumearn
   Jameses.
4. Search Cookstown Presbyterian and estate material by **townland plus
   forename**, not surname alone: Drumearn J/J/John, Gortein Samuel and
   Killycurry William.
5. Treat the reported 1775 Cookstown Dissenter-petition James as unverified
   until the PRONI name-search entry or image is captured. Even when verified,
   `Cookstown` alone will not identify which Drumearn James.
