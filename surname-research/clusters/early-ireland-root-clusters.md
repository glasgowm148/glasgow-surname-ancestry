## Bann Valley and Lisnagaver context

The new collateral evidence expands the locality network but does not place
Alexander's parents:

- John Glasgow and Mary Arthur's daughter Maria was living in Rasharkin parish
  by 1840–1842 through her marriage to Alexander Wallace. This proves movement
  from the documented Inishrush family into the same parish as the Lisnagaver
  households, but no Wallace–Lisnagaver witness, lease or probate link has yet
  been found.
- The 1825 Dunaghy tithe material shows Robert Glasco at Dunbught No. 2 and two
  James Glasgows, distinguished as elder/unsuffixed and junior, at Ballybogey.
  These clusters predate Alexander and Robert at Lisnagaver in 1834 and are
  viable alternative origins for the Lisnagaver households.
- A 1768 Belfast News-Letter estate advertisement offered three Lisnagaver farms
  on new twenty-one-year terms beginning 1 November 1768. It names no Glasgow,
  but the accepted proposals, leases and first post-letting rent rolls are a
  bounded route to the preceding occupiers and any lives named in leases.

The resulting working sequence is geographical, not genealogical:

```text
Inishrush family -> Maria Glasgow/Wallace at Rasharkin by 1840 (proved movement)
                 -> relationship to Lisnagaver Glasgows unproved

Dunaghy 1825: Dunbught Robert + Ballybogey James and James junior
                 -> possible source population for Lisnagaver, unproved

Lisnagaver 1768: three-farm re-letting
Lisnagaver 1834: Alexander + Robert Glasgow
Lisnagaver 1851: John/Samuel + Alexander + Thomas households
```

Source for the letting: *Belfast News-Letter*, 27 July 1768, estate notice
reproduced in [the collected historical volume](https://dn790009.ca.archive.org/0/items/cu31924028119737/cu31924028119737.pdf).

## Immediate profile consequences

- [John Glasgow (Glasgow-3913), born before about 1651, residence Kilwaughter Demesne](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3913)
  now represents the 1669 `Jo Glascho`. He remains unconnected. [John Glasgow
  (Glasgow-1498), born about 1650, Cavers, Roxburghshire](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1498)
  is a different profile unless a migration bridge is found.
- [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1502), active 1699-1700, County Antrim](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1502)
  conflates the older congregational actor with [Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1143),
  born about 1710, Kilwaughter](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1143),
  alive in 1772.
- [Janet Glasgow Woodside (Glasgow-2249), claimed born 1678, Ireland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-2249)
  has no source for Irish birth or her Cavers father attachment.
- [Reverend Patrick Glasgow (Glasgow-635), birth claimed about 1680, Ireland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-635)
  has documented Maryland activity only from 1736; the birth date and Irish
  classification are unsupported, while a major biography calls him Scottish.
- [James Glasgow (Glasgow-3001), active 1728, Killybegs](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3001)
  belongs in the eighteenth century because 1728 is an account date, not a
  proved death date.
- [Samuel Glasgow (Glasgow-1492), born about 1726, Moneymore-labelled](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1492)
  can cite the 1766 Gorteen householder only as a possible identity. The
  Whitehill Samuel belongs to Kildress and is not evidence of a second residence.
- [James Glasgow (Glasgow-1702), born about 1799, County Tyrone; later Ballynasollus](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1702)
  is a strong candidate for James baptised at St Luran on 21 Aug 1799 to
  William and Mary. His later marriage independently names father William;
  the identity still needs an abode or mother bridge.
- [Philip Glasgow (Glasgow-1241), baptised 1711, Uphall/Linlithgowshire, Scotland](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1241)
  may be the elder Dublin watchmaker, but the Scottish baptism-to-Dublin bridge
  is not proved. Philip junior's 1770 death belongs to a separate man.

The current seven-profile seventeenth-century Irish index therefore contains
only four defensible Irish witnesses after audit, and even those represent
regional occurrences rather than four linked progenitors.
