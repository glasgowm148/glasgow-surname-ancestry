# Early Scotland: working descendants of Glasgow-1098

Last updated: **17 October 2026**

This map adopts the project hypothesis that the early Scottish Glasgow men are
branches of [William Glasgow (Glasgow-1098), about 1500, Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1098).
It grades the missing links rather than treating each unproved sixteenth-century
connection as a separate root.

## Newly proved burgh descendant

The direct burgh line now extends one generation farther:

```text
William Glasgow (Glasgow-1098)
  -> John Glasgow (Glasgow-1093), proved son in 1534
    -> William Glasgow (Glasgow-3522), direct 1565 son record; profile match highly probable
      -> George Glasgow (Glasgow-1099), proved son and heir in 1586
        -> Jonet Glasgow (Glasgow-3061), proved lawful daughter in 1624
```

John Stewart's burgess admission on 25 November 1624 describes his wife Jonat as
the lawful daughter of George Glasgow, merchant, burgess and guild brother. The
same husband, their first known Glasgow baptism in March 1625, and George-1099's
matching civic identity resolve the edge to the existing profiles. The record
does not name Jonet's mother. [Complete findings](../../research/Glasgow-3061/findings.md).

## Robert the fisher's second proved son

Two records now reconstruct more of
[Robert Glasgow's (Glasgow-1096), about 1520, Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1096)
household:

```text
Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-1096), fisher and burgess
  -> John Glasgow, alive 10 January 1613; no WikiTree ID; probably first son
  -> Ninian Glasgow (Glasgow-3188), expressly second son in 1596
```

The 1596 burgess roll calls Ninian the second son of Robert. Ninian's 1613
testament independently leaves a gown to "Johne Glasgw his brother." The
combined evidence establishes another son John and makes him the leading
candidate for Robert's otherwise unnamed elder/first son. It does not resolve
him safely to [John Glasgow (Glasgow-1030), about 1575, Glasgow](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1030)
or [John Glasgow (Glasgow-1561), about 1579, Linlithgowshire](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1561).
[Complete findings](../../research/Glasgow-1096/findings.md).

## Selected Ayrshire route — 17 October 2026

Prefer John Glasgow-3193 of the Corsankell/Kilwinning family as Hugh Glasgow-1603’s uncertain working father. This materializes the leading Ayrshire placement already argued in the maintained five-generation map. The 1571 Corsinkell deed expressly names John as James elder’s son; the earlier Kilwinning tenant appearances are attributed to him as probable. Hugh’s surviving profile account describes an Ayrshire progenitor, his son’s account is centred on Kilwinning, and the following households lie in Kilwinning, Ayr and Kilbirnie. These successive local settings favour the documented nearby family over the existing Mid Calder/Inveresk father attachment, which depends on an online tree and has no movement, household or property bridge. A John-to-Hugh-to-John sequence is compatible supporting context, not a naming rule. Father is selected as a revisable one-generation model, rather than leaving the previously preferred local route only in prose.

Hugh remains a tree-derived generation: no inspected contemporary record securely identifies him, names his father or establishes his own c.1600 birth. John’s before-1540 boundary means a birth for Hugh near 1600 would make him an older father, around 60 or more; a grandson or collateral position may fit better. Neither date is changed to fit. An earlier Hugh birth is possible but is not adopted as a new estimate, and no missing person is invented. The 1571 John is documented as a son, while his additional Kilwinning identity is only probable. John’s brother James Glasgow-3921, another local Glasgow family, an omitted generation and the former James Glasgow-991 placement remain alternatives. This is stronger locality reasoning, not a probable or proved father assessment. The 1677 deed-index Hugh, preacher of the gospel, remains an unresolved identity comparison and supplies no evidence for this father choice.

The existing 137-profile pre-1850 working child side is rerouted from James of Mid Calder to John of Corsankell; descendants retain every earlier identity and relationship qualification. No mother, spouse, merge, new person or changed vital field is selected. Hugh-to-John-1604 and the minister’s separate father and duplicate questions are unchanged. Shared Y-DNA can motivate the extended-family model but cannot identify this father. Both proposed fathers already belong to the same working component: cohort membership, working component and pair coverage, supported connectivity and confidently marked connectivity remain unchanged. This is a better-supported placement within the working tree, not a newly connected component or confidence upgrade. No new source inspection or live WikiTree save is claimed.

## Best placement of Rev. John Glasgow M.A.

[Rev. John Glasgow M.A. (Glasgow-1073), about 1653, Kilbirnie, Ayrshire](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1073)
fits the **Corsankell-Stevenston-Kilwinning** branch best. The leading existing-
profile topology is:

```text
William Glasgow (Glasgow-1098), about 1500, Glasgow
  ? James Glasgow elder (Glasgow-1168), about 1515, Corsankell
    = record-proved sons James-3921 and John-3193
    ? John Glasgow (Glasgow-3193), born before 1542, Corsankell/Kilwinning
      ? Hugh Glasgow (Glasgow-1603), estimated about 1600, Ayrshire
        ? John Glasgow (Glasgow-1604), estimated about 1626, Kilwinning
          ? Rev. John Glasgow M.A. (Glasgow-1073), about 1653, Kilbirnie
```

This is five parent-child steps below Glasgow-1098. The first, third, fourth and
fifth steps remain unproved; the 1571 Corsankell father-son edge is direct. The
chain is nevertheless the best present *placement* because it follows one compact Ayrshire corridor. Exact generation spacing remains open; the old estimates do not prove a father rather than grandfather.

Do not cite the existing Ancestry links on Glasgow-1603 or Glasgow-1604 as proof.
They make those two profiles useful placeholders only. The earlier attachment of Glasgow-1603 to the Midlothian [James Glasgow (Glasgow-991)](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-991)
is retained as an unselected uncertain alternative. John-3193 is now the selected uncertain working father, matching this map’s preferred local topology. Hugh’s biography itself calls him an Ayrshire progenitor. Glasgow-1604's
birth, death, father and three sons are likewise tree-derived.

[John Glasgow (Glasgow-3145), about 1626, Scotland/Ayrshire](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3145)
was merged into Glasgow-1604 on 27 September 2026. Its former exact same estimated birth
year and its former attachment directly to Midlothian James-991 were symptoms of the same imported-tree conflation. The former duplicate supplies no independent generation.

## Possible duplicate that could close the last gap

[John Glasgow (Glasgow-3375), about 1650, Kilwinning](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3375)
may be an ordinary local collateral, but he is also a serious duplicate candidate
for Rev. John-1073:

- both are John Glasgow, born about 1650-53 in the same Kilwinning-Kilbirnie area;
- Rev. John matriculated by 1672 and graduated in 1674, so a 1679 marriage is
  chronologically normal;
- John and Barbara Alason had children recorded at Kilwinning in 1679 and January
  1689; Rev. John's documented marriage to Jean Cunningham was in January 1690;
- ordination at nearby Kilbirnie in August 1688 does not exclude a Kilwinning
  baptism five months later.

The case is not yet strong enough for a merge. Paterson and *Fasti* mention Jean
Cunningham and Margaret Scott but not Barbara, and the indexed Kilwinning entries
do not call John a minister or M.A. Inspect the full 7 June/6 July 1679 and 31
January/1 February 1689 pages. If either calls him `Mr John Glasgow`, minister, or
schoolmaster, merge Glasgow-3375 into Glasgow-1073 and retain Glasgow-1604 only as
an uncertain father. If the entries identify a trade or residence incompatible
with the minister, keep the men as probable cousins or brothers.

## What the university record adds

The university's own register lists `Johannes Glasgua` among the third class in
1672 and `Joannes Glasgouensis` among the first order of M.A. graduates on 16 July
1674. The same 1672 list explicitly labels Irish, Scots-Irish and English students,
but gives John no such label. It does not name his father or home, but it makes a
Scottish upbringing more economical than the former Kilwaughter-son placement.

Sources:

- *Munimenta Alme Universitatis Glasguensis*, vol. 3, printed pp. 124-125
  (1672 matriculation) and p. 40 (1674 graduation), [searchable register](https://archive.org/stream/munimentaalmeuni03mait/munimentaalmeuni03mait_djvu.txt).
- Hew Scott, *Fasti Ecclesiae Scoticanae*, vol. 3, [p. 103](https://electricscotland.com/bible/fastiecclesiaesc03scot.pdf).
- James Paterson, *History of the County of Ayr*, vol. 2,
  [pp. 252-253](https://archive.org/details/historyofcountyo02pateuoft/page/252/mode/2up).

## Gaps ranked by value

1. **Identity test: Glasgow-1073 versus Glasgow-3375.** Full Kilwinning OPR pages
   could close the last generation immediately.
2. **Father test: Glasgow-1604.** Find any original record for a John Glasgow of
   the right generation who died at Kilwinning about 1664; the present date is
   not sourced. Resolve same-year Glasgow-3145 as a probable duplicate.
3. **Middle-generation test.** Obtain the 1610 Montfoid, 1632 Brekpleughe and
   1634 Brigend testaments rather than their indexes; heirs, debts and cautioners
   may connect Corsankell to Kilwinning.
4. **Minister's origin test.** Search Irvine Presbytery CH2/197 from 1687 and
   Kilbirnie kirk session CH2/208 from 1688 for his call, testimonial, trials and
   ordination. These are contemporary records most likely to name a former parish
   or relative.
5. **Root bridge.** Search Glencairn/Cunningham estate papers for the predecessor
   of James-1168 at Corsankell. This tests the working Glasgow-1098 to Glasgow-1168
   edge.

## Working conclusion

John M.A. should no longer be described as a separate Ayrshire root. Place him in
the Glasgow-1098 descendant project under the Corsankell branch. The immediate
father remains unresolved, but Glasgow-1604 is the best existing-profile
candidate and Glasgow-3375 is the most important duplicate test. Robert-1078's
Kilwaughter branch remains a possible remote collateral branch, not the best
immediate parent line.
