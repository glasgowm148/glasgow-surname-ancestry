'''Alexander, son of the late Richard, messenger of Glasgow''', was a principal in a Glasgow burgage-property exchange in 1283. In the associated receipt he acknowledged receiving five marks from Gilbert, a man of the bishop's chamber. The Latin identifies him as `Alexander filius quondam Ricardi nuntii de Glasgw`.<ref name="Paisley338">''Registrum Monasterii de Passelet'', pp. 383–384; [https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/06190338/ DEEDS 06190338], receipt and associated burgage exchange, 1283.</ref>

The wording directly proves that Richard was Alexander's father and had died by 1283. It does not establish that either man bore Glasgow as a hereditary surname: `nuntii de Glasgw` may describe Richard's office or location, “messenger of Glasgow”.

== Research Notes ==
This Alexander must remain separate from [[Space:Alexander_son_of_Richard_constable_of_Glasgow|Alexander, son of Richard the former constable of Glasgow]], who is named as an adjoining property-holder in the companion agreement.<ref>''Registrum Monasterii de Passelet'', pp. 384–385; [https://deeds.utsc.utoronto.ca/charters/06190340/ DEEDS 06190340].</ref>

He is a better chronological and geographical candidate for the escheator than [[Space:Alexander_son_of_Robert_de_Glascu|Alexander, son of Robert de Glascu]], admitted to the Dublin guild in 1238/39. That comparison changes the priority of the identity search, not the relationship evidence. A recurrence under the messenger patronymic, the exchanged burgage, an administrative office or a shared associate is needed before merging records.
