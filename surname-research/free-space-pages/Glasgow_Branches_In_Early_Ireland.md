= Glasgow Branches and Clusters in Early Ireland 🇮🇪 =
''An evidence-graded overview of Glasgow surname occurrences, family clusters and reconstructed lineages in Ireland from the seventeenth to nineteenth centuries.''

This page concentrates on Counties Antrim, Londonderry/Derry, Tyrone and Donegal, with later branches in Cork and overseas. A dated Irish record proves a person or household at a place and time. It does not by itself prove identity with a same-name Scottish baptism, kinship between nearby householders, or descent from a later WikiTree profile.

Project-level Y-DNA may support broad paternal affinity among tested Glasgow lines. It does not identify a named seventeenth- or eighteenth-century father, distinguish one Irish locality cluster from another, or replace a documentary chain.

== 🌱 Documentary Roots and Reconstructed Lineages ==

The surviving evidence first establishes dated '''occurrences'''. It does not by itself prove that a same-name Scottish baptism is the Irish person, that adjacent householders were relatives, or that a later family descends from a particular householder. This page therefore separates documentary Irish roots from reconstructed lineages.

=== Evidence labels used on this page ===
* '''Documented occurrence''' — a record proves a name, date and place.
* '''Probable repeated identity''' — several records likely concern one person, but the identity chain is indirect.
* '''Working cluster''' — people share a tight locality and period; kinship remains a research hypothesis.
* '''Proved relationship''' — an original record states the relationship and both people are resolved.
* '''Reconstructed lineage''' — family tradition, inherited trees, naming patterns or Y-DNA proposes descent but does not prove each edge.

==== B0. Tyrone–Melanchthon tradition ====

[[Glasgow-591|William Glasgow (c.1656)]] is a family-tradition ancestor. William Melanchthon Glasgow's family history says an ancestor William came from Scotland and remained in northern Ireland after the 1689 campaign. It does not identify his parents, Scottish parish, military unit or contemporary Irish residence. The tradition is useful as a research lead, not as a documentary root.

==== B1. Hugh / Pennsylvania descendant tree ====

The descendant tree associated with [[Glasgow-548|Hugh Glasgow]] may represent a substantial later family. The current evidence does not establish that Hugh was a son of [[Glasgow-591|William]], identify him securely with either Tamlaght O'Crilly householder, or reconcile the 1740 Irish return with any claimed earlier emigration date.

[[Glasgow-3896|Daniel Glasgow (born about 1795/96)]], of Kilrea, called Thomas his late brother and Daniel Neely Glasgow his nephew in his original 1871 will.<ref name="DanielWill">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, Will Calendars, original registered will of Daniel Glasgow of Kilrea, proved 1871. The original wording identifies Thomas as Daniel's late brother and Daniel Neely Glasgow as his nephew.</ref> Daniel Neely's civil marriage on 28 June 1888 names his father as Thomas Glasgow, farmer, and gives Lisnagaver as the groom's residence.<ref name="DanielNeelyMarriage">[https://www.irishgenealogy.ie/files/civil/marriage_returns/marriages_1888/10763/5925382.pdf Civil marriage of Daniel Neely Glasgow and Mary Jane Boyd], 28 June 1888, accessed 17 July 2026.</ref> Together these records prove that Daniel and [[Glasgow-987|Thomas Glasgow (born about 1815)]] were brothers.

[[Glasgow-951|Alexander Glasgow (born about 1811)]] is not explicitly called their brother. His placement is cumulative: Alexander and Thomas were County Antrim-born Presbyterian farmers of similar ages; their families moved from adjoining Killoquin townlands into Lisnagaver at nearly the same time; and their households appear in the same small 1851 enumeration cluster.<ref name="Lisnagaver1851">National Archives of Ireland, 1851 Census of Ireland, Rasharkin, Lisnagaver, [https://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Rasharkin/Lisnagaver/35/ Alexander Glasgow household 35] and [https://www.census.nationalarchives.ie/pages/1851/Antrim/Kilconway/Rasharkin/Lisnagaver/37/ Thomas Glasgow household 37], accessed 17 July 2026.</ref><ref name="RasharkinBaptisms">Richard John Torrens and Barbara Braswell, [https://genealogy.torrens.org/BannValley/church/RasharkinP/Baptisms_1834.html Rasharkin Presbyterian baptisms, 1834–1846], recording Alexander's family at Gortereagh and Lisnagaver and Thomas's at Moneyleck and Lisnagaver, accessed 17 July 2026.</ref> This is a defensible working sibling placement, not a proved edge.

The 1851 census still records Alexander farming at Lisnagaver. His proved son [[Glasgow-938|Robert Glasgow (born about 1840)]] was the generation that later migrated to Glasgow, Scotland. Robert's 1866 civil marriage names his father as Alexander Glasgow, farmer, and links Lisnagaver with Gortereghy.<ref name="RobertMarriage">[https://civilrecords.irishgenealogy.ie/churchrecords/images/marriage_returns/marriages_1866/11531/8240963.pdf Civil marriage of Robert Glasgow and Jane Graham], 5 April 1866, accessed 17 July 2026.</ref>

A Griffith's Valuation transcript lists Alexander, John and Thomas at Lisnagaver and Daniel and Robert at Gortereghy.<ref name="GriffithRasharkin">[https://www.failteromhat.com/griffiths/antrim/rasharkin.htm Griffith's Valuation transcript for Rasharkin], accessed 17 July 2026.</ref> These names support a locality cluster; they do not establish relationships or exact plot adjacency.

A descendant account published in 2007 names an older Alexander and Rosanna as Thomas's parents.<ref name="GenesReunited">[https://www.genesreunited.co.uk/boards/board/general_topics/thread/465937 Genes Reunited descendant account], 25 January 2007, accessed 17 July 2026.</ref> It preserves several later facts about Thomas's family, so the couple is useful as uncertain working parents. It supplies no contemporary record and does not establish '''Shaw''' as Rosanna's surname. If Daniel was their son, the older Alexander must have been born by about 1778 and Rosanna before about 1780.

Adam and Rose of Inishrush are not placed above the older Alexander. No source identifies Adam as his father, while Rose, born about 1767, is chronologically excluded as mother of a man born by about 1778. Adam, John and Samuel in the wider Bann Valley material remain possible collateral relatives only.

== 📖 Historical and Geographic Context ==

=== Seventeenth-century settlement ===

The [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_of_Ulster Plantation of Ulster], the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1641_Irish_Rebellion 1641 rebellion], Restoration-era religious pressure and the [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamite_War_in_Ireland Williamite War] form the broader setting for Scottish settlement in Ulster. They explain why migration is historically plausible; they do not identify a particular Irish Glasgow with a Scottish baptism or prove a family relationship.

The 1653 Larne deposition and 1669 hearth-money return establish early east-Antrim occurrences. The 1692–1695 Raphoe deeds and the Belfast maritime material show that the surname also appeared in distinct Donegal and Belfast settings. These records should be treated as separate locality clusters unless evidence connects them.

=== Eighteenth-century household clusters ===

The 1740 Protestant-householders return provides stronger contemporary anchors for Tamlaght O'Crilly and Derryloran than the inherited founder trees currently attached to those areas. Repeated forenames within one parish are useful for cluster formation, but they do not distinguish fathers, sons, brothers or unrelated same-name householders.

=== Presbyterian records and local movement ===

Many later Antrim families appear in Presbyterian congregational and baptismal material. Such records are particularly useful for tracing household movement between nearby townlands, as with Gortereagh, Moneyleck and Lisnagaver in the Bann Valley–Killoquin cluster. Shared congregation, occupation and locality strengthen a hypothesis but do not substitute for an explicit relationship statement.

=== Migration beyond Ireland ===

Documented or plausible outward movements include the Kilwaughter/South Carolina branch, later Pennsylvania descendant trees, the Cork/New Zealand family and Robert Glasgow's migration from County Antrim to Glasgow, Scotland, after 1866. Each migration must be proved within its own family chain; a destination or surname match does not merge the source cluster with another branch.

== 🧬 DNA Evidence and Limits ==

Y-DNA evidence should be described at the level it actually supports.

* Matches among documented descendants may support continuity within a tested paternal line.
* Similarity between project participants may indicate a broader shared paternal ancestor.
* A project-level match does not identify which Irish cluster supplied the common ancestor.
* It does not prove that [[Glasgow-591|William]], [[Glasgow-1081|James]], [[Glasgow-1026|William Walter]], [[Glasgow-3904|older Alexander]] or another named profile was that ancestor.
* No supplied result proves that [[Glasgow-951|Alexander]] or [[Glasgow-938|Robert]] descends from the Kilwaughter householders.
* Branch-level claims require a documented tester-to-ancestor chain and sufficient Y-DNA resolution to distinguish the proposed branches.

* The strongest early evidence supports regional occurrence clusters, not a single founding pedigree.
* Robert at Larne, Elizabeth at Glenarm and James and [[Glasgow-3913|John]] at Kilwaughter are documentary east-Antrim occurrences; their relationships and exact Scottish identities are unproved.
* The Belfast maritime and Raphoe–Killybegs occurrences remain separate from East Antrim unless identity or relationship bridges are found.
* The Tamlaght O'Crilly and Derryloran households in 1740 are firmer documentary roots than the traditional William, Cross and Moneymore founder trees.
* [[Glasgow-2917|Charles]] and [[Glasgow-1026|William Walter]] have Scottish records, but their displayed Irish migrations and descendant chains remain reconstructed.
* The Kilwaughter/South Carolina nuclear family is secure from [[Glasgow-1143|Robert]] downward; connection to the seventeenth-century East Antrim occurrences is unproved.
* [[Glasgow-3896|Daniel]] and [[Glasgow-987|Thomas]] are proved brothers. [[Glasgow-951|Alexander]] is their probable brother, not a proved sibling.
* [[Glasgow-3904|Older Alexander]] and Rosanna Unknown are useful working parents based on late descendant testimony. Rosanna's surname is not established as Shaw.
* [[Glasgow-938|Robert]], the proved son of [[Glasgow-951|Alexander]], was the generation that migrated from County Antrim to Glasgow, Scotland.
* Adam and Rose of Inishrush are not proved parents of the older Alexander; Rose is chronologically excluded as his mother.
* The seven profiles indexed to seventeenth-century Ireland are not seven proved Irish founder lines.
* Shared project-level Y-DNA does not identify a named early father or assign a later family to Kilwaughter, Cross, Moneymore or Bann Valley.
* Deep ancestry trees including [[Glasgow-992]] and [[Glasgow-991]] remain speculative without contemporary evidence.
* The Cameron origin theory remains unsupported.
