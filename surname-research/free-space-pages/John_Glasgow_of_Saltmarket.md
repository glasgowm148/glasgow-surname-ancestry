'''John Glasgow''' held a tenement in Glasgow's Saltmarket. On 2 May 1527 a four-shilling annual rent payable from his tenement was transferred to Master James Houston, subdean of Glasgow.<ref>[https://archive.org/details/libercollegiino00annegoog ''Liber Collegii Nostre Domine''], act dated 2 May 1527.</ref>

By 20 February 1528/29 William Glasgow occupied the same northern title-position beside the Henryson/Houston parcel, with Andrew Craig south. The adjoining parcel passed to John Rankin in 1534/35 without those boundaries changing, and a 1542 rental charged the same four-shilling annual upon the tenement of the late William Glasgow between late John Crownar north and Rankin south.<ref>[https://archive.org/download/libercollegiino00annegoog/page/n195_w1600.jpg ''Liber Collegii Nostre Domine'', pp. 90–91], 20 February 1528/29; [https://archive.org/download/libercollegiino00annegoog/page/n141_w1600.jpg St Andrew prebend rental, p. 36].</ref> This places the John-to-William title succession within about twenty-one and a half months.

An inventory dated 5 January 1548 repeated the same four-shilling annual, street position and adjoining owners—John Rankyn to the south and the former land of James Young to the north—identifying the same property title.<ref>[https://archive.org/details/libercollegiino00annegoog ''Liber Collegii Nostre Domine''], property inventory dated 5 January 1548.</ref>

On 21 October 1553 John Rankyn's adjoining property was bounded on the north by the lands of `q. John Glasgow`, where `q.` means the late John Glasgow. John had therefore died before that date.<ref>Robert Renwick, ed., ''Abstracts of Protocols of the Town Clerks of Glasgow'', act dated 21 October 1553.</ref>

== Research Notes ==
John of Glasgow alias Smith and Sir Allan Smith jointly acquired a four-shilling annual rent from a High Street tenement on 28 April 1506.<ref>Glasgow University Archives, Blackhouse Charters, BL/204, formerly GUA16403, instrument dated 28 April 1506; [https://yourscottisharchives.com/catalogues/565b7ef3-fc87-3f6f-aaac-149925faa39d?component-id=7cdbf375-dc6d-3de3-b53a-86376e2106da Your Scottish Archives catalogue description].</ref> The seller was Agnes, daughter and heir of Robert Watson, and Sir Allan's property formed the northern boundary. The catalogue's legacy wording includes the malformed `Watsonm`, which requires comparison with the instrument. The only explicit descent in the description is Robert Watson to Agnes. It does not call John a smith or armourer and does not state that Sir Allan was his father or relative.

The name, city and property interests make John alias Smith a candidate for the Saltmarket owner, but no record supplies the alias in both clusters. Do not present the identity as proved. The precise four-shilling rent and tenement boundaries should be followed through later conveyances for a successor, widow or heir. The complete BL/204 instrument remains unread and may clarify the terms of joint acquisition, survivorship, further associates or an omitted relationship.

Eufame Craig, widow of an unnamed Glasgow, still held a liferent in the rear of this property block in 1564. The most likely reconstruction is that she was the older John's widow and that her reserved liferent survived the rapid transfer of the fee to William. The coordinated Craig witnesses and same-day Craig purchase support this interpretation. William as her husband is less likely because he was married to Esbell Brade in December 1534 and died by March 1535/36.

This man cannot be [[Glasgow-1093|John Glasgow]], husband of Jonet Spreull, who remained alive in 1565.
