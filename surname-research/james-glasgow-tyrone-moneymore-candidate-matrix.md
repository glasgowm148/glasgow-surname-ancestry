# James Glasgow: Tyrone-Moneymore candidate matrix

Last updated: 20 August 2026

## Current result

The records do not describe one long-lived James. They require at least eight
local men or estates. One duplicate has been resolved: `Glasgow-3137` was
merged into `Glasgow-2988` on 20 August 2026 because both represented the
single 1766 Corvanaghan householder. The Corvanaghan record was preserved while
the attached children and former birth estimate remain explicitly uncertain.

No contemporary record yet proves which James was the father of Robert,
Arthur and Joseph. Faris's "James Glasgow of Moneymore" remains the only
located statement naming their father. The new evidence narrows the archival
route and removes several false matches.

The pre-1858 probate originals do not survive. The PRONI Name Search explicitly
states that no further information survives for these index entries.

## Effect on Glasgow-1024

The 1759 record no longer supports `Glasgow-1025` as "James of Drumearn": the
index says Drumcarn. Chronology allows the 1759 testator to be either the
reported father of the emigrant siblings or an older James, but name, date and
district cannot choose between generations.

The alleged wife Mary Harkness remains unproved. A real later relationship
strengthens the local family-network context: William Glasgow Harkness of St
Louis was described in 1892 as the son of **James Harkness of Oritor** and
**[[Glasgow-4026|Margaret Glasgow of Moneymore]]**. This is positive evidence that Harkness and
Glasgow families in the Cookstown-Moneymore district intermarried. Repeated
marriages between neighbouring families were common, so the record modestly
increases the plausibility of another Glasgow-Harkness marriage. It does not
identify Mary Harkness or directly prove that she married the James represented
by `Glasgow-1024`.

## Highest-value surviving sources

1. **PRONI T1959/1-8**: Glasgow, Harkness and Rutherford genealogical statements, including an unsigned account by William Glasgow of Oritor and letters from Mary Harkness of St Louis.
2. **PRONI D1413/3a-b**: 1933 Megaw notes on the Glasgow family of Cookstown and associated local families.
3. **PRONI T1762**: Glasgow family and Presbyterian papers, including a copy will of William Glasgow of Oritor, about 1827.
4. **Faris, pp. 14-19 and notes**: provenance of "James Glasgow of Moneymore" and the emigrant sibling names.
5. **PRONI D3632**: Drapers' Company Moneymore rentals and leases, about 1750-1775.

These collections can identify wives, children, successors and exact
townlands. The destroyed probate indexes cannot.

## Migrant sibling reconstruction — 20 August 2026

David Faris's catalogue summary says Robert Glasgow emigrated in 1769 with three brothers and one sister. Robert, Arthur and Joseph are independently identified as brothers, leaving one additional brother to identify.

A later Adams County family-history article explicitly identifies John Glasgow of Peters Township, Pennsylvania, who died in 1779, as their brother and says that the three surviving brothers Joseph, Robert and Arthur subsequently moved to Virginia. This is secondary evidence, but it matches the sibling count exactly. Joseph Glasgow also witnessed John's 1779 will, providing a contemporary association.

John's original will is indexed in Cumberland County Will Book C, page 161. Until the will and probate packet are inspected, the relationship remains a strong hypothesis rather than proved kinship.

Sources:

- [Faris catalogue summary](https://search.worldcat.org/title/22731567)
- [Adams County family-history account](https://www.peoplesdefender.com/2024/03/29/our-ancestors-of-adams-county-3/)

- [Pennsylvania Archives Cumberland County record guide](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/phmc/documents/archives/research-online/documents/Cumberland.pdf)

This substantially weakens the unsupported claim that Glasgow-3305 was an additional brother who remained in Ireland.

## Cookstown Mitchell branch — 20 August 2026

The 1879 *History of Hillsdale County, Michigan*, page 263, describes a distinct, traceable family:

- James Glasgow married a Mitchell.
- They moved from Scotland to northern Ireland about 1750 and settled near Cookstown.
- Their son John farmed near Cookstown and had seven children.
- John's sons William and John later settled in Hillsdale County, Michigan.

The catalogue lineage matches the narrative: [[Glasgow-1970|James]] and [[Mitchell-34480|his Mitchell wife]] -> [[Glasgow-1968|John]] -> [[Glasgow-1969|William]] and [[Glasgow-1967|John Jr.]].

[Direct page image and text](https://archive.org/details/bad0928.0001.001.umich.edu/page/263/mode/1up)

This family belongs in the Moneymore investigation because it establishes another James of the correct generation near Cookstown. It does not presently identify him with Glasgow-1024 or Glasgow-2988. The later Cookstown son and grandchildren make a simple merger with the emigrant father chronologically strained, while the 1766 Corvanaghan James remains a possible occurrence of either Glasgow-1970 or another local man.
