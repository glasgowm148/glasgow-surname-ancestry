== Biography ==

'''Robert Glasgow''' was probably born about 1670. His age and birthplace are
unknown; the estimate is based on his adult service in 1694. North Leith is
usable only as an uncertain birth location inferred from the household record
that places him there.

On 16 July 1694 Robert gave a receipt in Alexander Ainslie's name for 34
shillings sterling, the price of two dozen mutchkin bottles and ten dozen
chopin bottles supplied for the Earl of Breadalbane.<ref name="Breadalbane">National Records of Scotland, Breadalbane Muniments,
GD112/15/59, item 24, receipt by Robert Glasgow in Alexander Ainslie's name,
16 July 1694;
[https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD112%2F15%2F59
catalogue description].</ref>

On 3 October 1694 Robert was listed among four servants in Ainslie's household
at North Leith. Ainslie was described as clerk to the glass manufactory.<ref name="PollTax">National Records of Scotland, Scottish Exchequer poll tax,
North Leith parish, E70/4/10, image 11, 3 October 1694;
[https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/virtual-volumes/volume-images/volume_data-E70-4-10/REX01666?image_number=11
original image], Scotland's People Virtual Volumes.</ref>

The same employer, business, name and short interval make the receipt writer
and household servant almost certainly one man. The receipt shows that Robert
acted in a trusted business capacity, but neither original record calls him a
glassmaker.

== Research Notes ==

A modern history calls Robert a glassmaker and dates the poll-tax appearance
to 1695.<ref>Jill Turnbull,
[https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/fe29343e-a341-412e-85bd-96afca6214e5/content
“The Scottish Glass Industry, 1610–1750”] (PhD thesis, University of
Edinburgh, 1999), pp. 171, 202, 324, 350.</ref> The original record is dated 3
October 1694 and calls him Ainslie's servant. His participation in the bottle
transaction should not be expanded into a skilled occupational title without
further evidence.
