## Biography draft

On 3 October 1694 Robert Glasgow was listed among four servants in the North Leith household of Alexander Ainslie, who was described as clerk to the glass manufactory.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Scottish Exchequer poll tax, North Leith parish, E70/4/10, image 11, 3 October 1694; [https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/virtual-volumes/volume-images/volume_data-E70-4-10/REX01666?image_number=11 original image], Scotland's People Virtual Volumes.</ref>

On 16 July 1694 a Robert Glasgow gave a receipt in Ainslie's name for 34 shillings sterling, the price of two dozen mutchkins and ten dozen chopin bottles supplied for the Earl of Breadalbane.<ref>National Records of Scotland, Breadalbane Muniments, GD112/15/59, item 24, 16 July 1694, [https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/details.aspx?reference=GD112%2F15%2F59 catalogue description].</ref> The shared employer, name and year make the receipt writer almost certainly the servant taxed in October. It establishes a functional and probably trusted role in the glassworks business, though not the skilled occupation of glassmaker.

A modern study describes Robert as a glassmaker and dates the tax to 1695.<ref>Jill Turnbull, “The Scottish Glass Industry, 1610–1750” (PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh, 1999), pp. 171, 202, 324 and 350, [https://era.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/fe29343e-a341-412e-85bd-96afca6214e5/content PDF].</ref> The original tax record calls him Ainslie's servant and is dated 1694; the receipt proves his participation in the business but still does not explicitly call him a glassmaker.

## Duplicate audit

No inspected WikiTree profile contains this 1694 North Leith identity. The
strongest new chronological candidate is [Glasgow-3011](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3011), baptised at Glencorse in 1676: he was seventeen in 1694 and is the sole exact-name baptism returned for that parish from 1640 through 1690. A same-name man married at Glencorse in 1704. Neither event links him to Ainslie or the glassworks.

Older possible candidates include [Glasgow-1078](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1078), [Glasgow-1149](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1149), [Glasgow-3938](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3938) and [Glasgow-3970](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3970), but none has a record linking him to Ainslie or the Leith glassworks. The 1688 Ayr notarial servant and 1666 *John of Portrush* shipmaster are likewise possible earlier occurrences, not proved continuations.

Exact Scotland's People OPR searches found no Glasgow/Glasgo/Glascow/Glaskow/Glassgow baptism in the Leith parishes from 1694 to 1730, and no exact Robert Glasgow marriage from 1694 to 1710 or burial from 1694 to 1730. Register survival and spelling limit the negative result.
