== Biography ==
William Glasgow was master of the brig ''William'' in 1792. A Belfast notice dated 18 April advertised the vessel ''For Rotterdam and back to Belfast'', naming William Glasgow as master and stating that she would sail in eight days; freight enquiries were directed to Narcissus Batt.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/72014483/ Northern Star, 21 April 1792, p.1].</ref> Later notices advertised voyages from Belfast to Oporto and Bordeaux.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/72015499/ Northern Star, 28 July 1792, p.1].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/72015521/ Northern Star, 28 July 1792, p.5].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/72017846/ Northern Star, 12 December 1792, p.1].</ref> A notice dated 25 July 1792 also reports the same Oporto/Belfast service.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/72015527/ Northern Star, 1 August 1792, p.1].</ref> January 1796 notices report the ''William'' as arrived from Petersburg with clean hemp, twelve head of flax and codilla for M'Crackens & Co.; the forename is unstated.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/61256880/ Northern Star, 7 January 1796, p.3].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/61256896/ Northern Star, 11 January 1796, p.1].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/61256932/ Northern Star, 14 January 1796, p.1].</ref> Continuity with the 1792 master is probable, not proved.
