## Paste-ready biography

'''James Glasgow''' was a tithe occupier at Ballybogy, Dunaghy parish, County Antrim, in 1825. The original Tithe Applotment Book lists him separately from '''James Glasgow junior'''. The unsuffixed James was assessed 8 shillings; the younger James was assessed 10 shillings and 11 pence.<ref name="BallybogyTithe1825">Public Record Office of Northern Ireland, ''Tithe applotment for the parish of Dunaghy'', 1825, PRONI FIN/5/A/128, manuscript p. 25, Ballybogy entries for James Glasgow and James Glasgow junior; indexed transcription: W. Macafee, [https://www.billmacafee.com/182030stithe/tithenorthantrimrevisionorder.pdf ''Data from the Tithe Applotment Books covering North Antrim, arranged in document order''], entries 6001–6002.</ref>

The monetary figures are tithe assessments, not acreages. The two separate entries establish two contemporary men but do not prove that they were father and son. The suffix ''junior'' could distinguish the younger of two same-name men without stating their relationship.

In the manuscript sequence, the two Glasgows appear among Alexander and Wier occupiers. This documents a shared townland tenant community; it does not establish kinship among the families or descent from the John Glasgow and James Wier named in a Ballyboggy lease in 1740.
