== Biography ==
At an entertainment for Robert L. Rogers on retiring as teacher of elocution, his class met at the Royal Temperance Hotel, Waring Street, Belfast, and during the session in Rev. Dr. Cooke’s Church. On Thursday 8 April 1841, the Rev. A. J. Glasgow spoke on elocution’s importance for candidates for the sacred ministry. Identity unresolved. The printed name is Rev. A. J. Glasgow, with no expanded forename, age, congregation, kin or birthplace. == Research Notes ==
The newspaper establishes an adult clergyman called “Rev. A. J. Glasgow” speaking in Belfast on 8 April 1841. It supplies no forename expansion, age, congregation, family, birthplace or residence. Glasgow-1781 is possible but materially conflicted by J here versus Adam D. in other checked records. Glasgow-1780 is unlikely because his family is documented in Bombay, with a daughter born there 23 days before the speech. No candidate is identified; no family link is proposed.

Printed initial is J, visually distinguished from D in the same article. Do not silently expand initials or assign Adam Dickie Glasgow.

Glasgow-1780 is materially disfavoured by the documented Bombay presence and child’s birth in March 1841.

[[Glasgow-1781]]: Strong Belfast/Institution and ministerial context fits Adam Dickie Glasgow, but this notice prints J while his other checked notices say Adam D.; no source links the speaker to Berry Street. Possible only, not a profile match.
[[Glasgow-1780]]: Strongly disfavoured: James McClure Glasgow was in Bombay; his daughter was born there on 16 March 1841, only 23 days before this Belfast speech on 8 April. No evidence of an extraordinary return journey; the printed initials also do not match James.

Check Belfast Presbyterian and ministerial records for an A. J. Glasgow, including Berry Street attendance or speaking records; seek a contemporary record giving the speaker’s full name or congregation before assigning any profile.
