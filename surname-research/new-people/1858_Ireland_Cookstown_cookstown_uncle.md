## Duplicate check

- William-1675 died in Ohio in 1855 and cannot be the living 1858 witness or 1856 recipient.
- William-1969’s captured biography places him at Auburn and then Michigan from 1842; it does not fit a continuing Cookstown merchant life.
- An uncle shared by two plaintiffs does not prove that the plaintiffs were brothers or had the same father.
- 1842 hospital, 1854 rescue and 1861 sale notices do not state an age or family. They remain unassigned between same-name Cookstown men rather than being forced into this biography.

- [Glasgow-3814](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-3814): Inspected 1883 death entry identifies a bachelor aged 87, retired merchant, at Loy, Cookstown. Occupation, locality and chronology make him a strong retirement candidate and a compatible uncle candidate; no kinship bridge yet.
- [Glasgow-585](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-585): Montober William (c.1804–1898) is old enough for the uncle role. His farmer household is known, but there is no inspected mill or merchant connection; no concrete kinship conflict has been established.

== Biography ==
William Glasgow gave evidence in the Cookstown mill dispute of Benjamin and William Glasgow against John Sloan in July 1858. He was described as uncle of the plaintiffs, distinguishing him from his same-name nephew who was a plaintiff.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/390574459/ Belfast News-Letter, 22 July 1858, p.3].</ref> A Cookstown merchant named William Glasgow had retired on 1 January 1856 and was presented with a silver ewer and cake basket by commercial friends in Belfast; his identity with the witness remains unproved.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/390544967/ Belfast News-Letter, 4 January 1856, p.1].</ref>
