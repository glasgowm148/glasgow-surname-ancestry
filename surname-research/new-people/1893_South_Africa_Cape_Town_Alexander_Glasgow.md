== Biography ==

'''Alexander Glasgow''' was named as a defendant in a Cape Supreme Court motion in 1893. The executors of the late Cape Town merchant Gerhard Myburgh brought the motion.<ref name="MyburghMotion">National Archives and Records Service of South Africa, NAAIRS index, Cape Town Archives Repository (`KAB`), Cape Supreme Court (`CSC`) 2/6/1/146, reference 286, part 1, 1893, “Motion. Executors of estate late Gerhard Myburgh versus Alexander Glasgow”; searched through the [https://www.nationalarchives.gov.za/node/737 NARSSA guidance and NAAIRS access page], 13 August 2026.</ref>

Five years later, Maria Welch brought an illiquid action against a same-named Alexander Glasgow for recovery of money.<ref name="WelchAction">National Archives and Records Service of South Africa, NAAIRS index, Cape Town Archives Repository (`KAB`), Cape Supreme Court (`CSC`) 2/1/1/350, reference 123, part 1, 1898, “Illiquid case. Maria Welch versus Alexander Glasgow. Action for the recovery of money”; searched through the [https://www.nationalarchives.gov.za/node/737 NARSSA guidance and NAAIRS access page], 13 August 2026.</ref>

The exact name and same court make one recurring defendant probable, but the index does not prove that the two files concern the same man. It gives no age, birthplace, residence, occupation, parent, spouse or child. Maria Welch can be identified as Maria Lacey Welch, a Cape mortgage holder later buried at Observatory, Cape Town; Gerhard Myburgh was a prominent Cape Town merchant. Those associates place the defendant in a Cape Town commercial or property context without proving his residence.<ref name="MyburghMotion" /><ref name="WelchAction" />

== Research Notes ==

The live WikiTree `searchPerson` results for Alexander Glasgow were audited on 13 August 2026. No result stated a South African event or supplied a bridge to the Cape files. Chronologically possible profiles had documented lives elsewhere or no identifying South African evidence.

A documented Australian namesake, born in Queensland on 19 November 1878,
first arrived at Cape Town with the 1st Queensland Mounted Infantry on
13 December 1899. His age and arrival date exclude him from both court files.<ref>Virtual War Memorial Australia, [https://vwma.org.au/explore/people/783892 “Alexander Glasgow”], citing the Australian War Memorial Boer War nominal roll and National Archives of Australia service dossier; accessed 13 August 2026.</ref>

A 1943 Transvaal estate-index entry for Eliza Glasgow says that her spouse Alexander Glasgow predeceased her (`TAB MHG 913/43`). Do not combine him with the Cape defendant unless Eliza's death notice or the court files supply matching identity details.

Thomas Glasgow of Lisnagaver reportedly had a son Alexander who emigrated to South Africa or the United States and was probably born about 1851-1863. The Cape defendant is the strongest destination candidate yet found, but exact name and country are insufficient to attach a father. Keep this profile unconnected even if the two case files prove one identity, unless an original record supplies an Irish origin or family bridge.
