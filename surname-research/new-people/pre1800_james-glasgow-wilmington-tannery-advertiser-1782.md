# James Glasgow, Wilmington tannery advertiser, 1782–1785

HOLD — documented record; profile assignment qualified

[[Category:Glasgow Name Study]]

== Biography ==
James Glasgow, at Christiana Bridge in Newcastle county, advertises for private sale the former Charles Whitlock tanyard in the borough of Wilmington; notice dated 5 August 1782. Sixteen issues preserve the 5 August 1782 former Charles Whitlock property advertisement; three 1785 issues carry a different notice, explicitly naming Delaware and Thomas Glenn. The records share advertiser name and Christiana Bridge address, supporting a provisional cluster; same plot, ownership, occupation and completed sale remain unproved. The signed Wilmington/Christiana Bridge advertiser remains unidentified. Two Cecil County chronological/regional James profiles are compatible, without a property or individual bridge. James Glasgow, living at Christiana Bridge, signs a notice dated 12 March 1785 offering a complete tanyard in the borough of Wilmington, State of Delaware, then in the tenure of Thomas Glenn. Three issues reprint this separate 1785 notice. The printed Delaware designation and Thomas Glenn occupancy are explicit. Advertiser is not necessarily owner or tanner; no completed sale or identity of this plot with the former Whitlock property in 1782 is proved.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/1034008744/ The Pennsylvania Journal, or, Weekly Advertiser, 1785-03-23, p.2].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/1034008777/ The Pennsylvania Journal, or, Weekly Advertiser, 1785-03-30, p.1].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/1034008853/ The Pennsylvania Journal, or, Weekly Advertiser, 1785-04-09, p.4].</ref>

== Research Notes ==
The signed Wilmington/Christiana Bridge advertiser remains unidentified. Two Cecil County chronological/regional James profiles are compatible, without a property or individual bridge. The North Carolina secretary is another adult namesake but has a different documentary region; do not merge him solely because Wilmington is also a North Carolina place. The 1785 notices create tension with Glasgow-2868’s estimated c. 1784 death, which is not a certain exclusion; inspect that estimate before assigning the later advertiser. No continuity of property ownership is proved.

Approved bounded QMD queries for Christiana/Glasgow and tannery/Glasgow returned no relevant reviewed identity note. Catalogue James profiles with Cecil/Delaware places were checked; this is not an exhaustive external duplicate search.

Glasgow-2868 has exact 1720 birth and estimated c. 1784 Cecil Maryland death; Glasgow-3290 has uncertain 1758 Cecil Maryland birth/1789 death. Neither has an inspected Christiana Bridge or tanyard source.

Missing property or occupation fields do not exclude either candidate. Advertiser need not be the owner or a tanner. The future possession date 1 November is an offered sale term, not completed transfer.

Sixteen 1782 occurrences are one reading; three 1785 occurrences are another. Thomas Glenn and Delaware are printed in 1785; do not insert Glenn into the Whitlock notice or treat the plots as certainly identical.

[[Glasgow-2868]]: Adult chronology and later Cecil Maryland regional context are compatible with nearby New Castle, without a Wilmington/Christiana Bridge or property bridge.
[[Glasgow-3290]]: Uncertain 1758 Cecil Maryland birth permits an adult advertiser in 1782; no tanyard, Whitlock or Christiana Bridge identity bridge.
[[Glasgow-634]]: Adult chronology fits, but its North Carolina office context is a different regional lead. No connection to the Delaware tannery; missing corroboration alone is not an exclusion.

Inspect Wilmington/New Castle deeds, tannery title and Charles Whitlock estate records, Christiana Bridge tax lists, and the candidates’ own family evidence for an individual bridge.
