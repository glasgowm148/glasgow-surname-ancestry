# Robert Glasgow, master of John and Archibald, 1754

HOLD — documented surname; identity unresolved

[[Category:Glasgow Name Study]]

== Biography ==
Robert Glasgow, master of the snow John and Archibald, of Glasgow, is reported arriving in distress during a Virginia-to-Glasgow passage. One distress report preserved in three issues, not three masters. Later reprints give St John’s, Antigua, 15 February. The passage describes gale damage, leakage and lightning; Glasgow is also the vessel’s designation/port, not the master’s birthplace. The preceding Swanton vessel’s provisions and urine story in the Pennsylvania page belongs to a different vessel. No family or link to the 1751 defendant established.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/39398244/ The Pennsylvania Gazette, 1754-03-19, p.2], original passage visually inspected 5 October 2026.</ref>

== Research Notes ==
Glasgow-2474 is the leading possible role candidate because prior research reports a 1738 Irvine shipmaster. That original was not re-inspected here, and no record bridges Betty and Grizell to John and Archibald. Identity with the 1751 defendant remains unproved.

Three issues preserve one distress report, with every issue clipping retained.

Glasgow-1132, 1143, 2425, 1445, 2867 and 550 remain weaker possible comparisons. Young estimated ages are weak fits, not precise exclusions.

Port Glasgow and vessel designation do not establish Robert’s origin.

[[Glasgow-2474]]: Best role-aligned candidate among those checked: age 38 and the 1738 shipmaster record is relevant prior maritime evidence. No direct bridge to Snow John and Archibald. A Findmypast baptism transcript records him at Irvine. Public research findings associate the adult Robert Glasgow-3479 with Irvine family entries and report an Irvine Burgh Catalogue entry (4 Aug 1738) naming Robert Glasgow master of the Irvine ship Betty and Grizell; the profile merge preserves that caveat. The earlier record was not re-inspected in this batch.
[[Glasgow-2473]]: Chronologically possible (at least 51); no corroborating maritime evidence. Public source summary cites a Findmypast baptism transcript dated 7 Jan 1703 at Edinburgh; maintained vital preserves the before qualifier. Profile findings link the baptism to Edinburgh parents, but no relevant maritime or London criminal record was identified. The earlier record was not re-inspected in this batch.
[[Glasgow-3457]]: Chronologically possible, about 30; no direct maritime linkage. Public dossier has a FamilySearch baptism citation for David Glasgow in 1744 and unverified tree spouse/child links; no occupational finding for Robert. The earlier record was not re-inspected in this batch.

Inspect original ship registers, port entries and the 1738 Irvine Burgh Catalogue item; compare signatures, vessel ownership and family identifiers.
