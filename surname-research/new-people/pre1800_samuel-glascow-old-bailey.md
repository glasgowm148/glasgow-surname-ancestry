== Biography ==
Samuel Glascow was tried at the Old Bailey on 6 May 1761 for stealing a bay-coloured gelding valued at £10, property of William Hayward, serjeant at law, on 24 April. The trial records a Guilty verdict and death sentence.<ref>[https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/record/t17610506-1 Old Bailey Proceedings, t17610506-1, 6 May 1761], original transcript inspected 5 October 2026.</ref> The Derby Mercury on 22 May reports a respite during His Majesty’s pleasure, describing the owner as Mr Serjeant Heywood. The adjacent unnatural-crime statement concerns Thomas Andrews, not Samuel.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/394296948/ The Derby Mercury, 1761-05-22, p.3], original passage visually inspected 5 October 2026; issue metadata supplied by Newspapers.com.</ref>

In his defence Samuel said he had worked nine years in a Holbourn shop with John Steed and Thomas Bartlet, spent five years abroad, returned to England twelve months earlier, and lived nine months at High Wickham. He described buying tools and hair to set up business there. Witness Henry Ward attributed to him a statement that his unnamed mother lived in South-street. These are attributed statements, not independently verified residence or kinship records. No birth date or origin is supplied by the inspected trial.

Associated Old Bailey catalogue metadata lists a conditional pardon dated 6 May 1761, TNA SP 44/87 f.68; the archival document and its conditions have not been inspected.<ref>[https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/record/ar_9141_29053 Old Bailey associated-record catalogue, ar_9141_29053], inspected 5 October 2026.</ref>

The [https://www.newspapers.com/image/34424820/ Public Advertiser, 22 May 1761, p.3] says the report of seven Newgate malefactors was made to the King at St James’s “Yesterday”, and reports Samuel’s respite. A separate page-1 masthead is Friday, 22 May 1761<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/34424813/ The Public Advertiser, 22 May 1761, p.1], printed masthead original visually inspected 5 October 2026; separate saved excerpt identifies Friday, 22 May 1761.</ref>, supporting 21 May as the reported royal-review day. This does not verify military commutation or pardon conditions. [https://www.newspapers.com/image/1212389396/ Berrow’s Worcester Journal, 14 May 1761, p.1] reports Samuel among two capital convictions; its manslaughter defendant is William Smith, not Samuel. [https://www.newspapers.com/image/34424864/ Public Advertiser, 26 May 1761, p.4] advertises printed trial proceedings listing Samuel’s horse-stealing case; it is not a new trial. All three original passages were inspected 5 October 2026.
