== Biography ==
The Derby Mercury reports Samuel’s respite after the gelding-theft case. The unnatural-crime description concerns Thomas Andrews, not Samuel. Respite does not establish execution or military commutation.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/394296948/ The Derby Mercury, 1761-05-22, p.3].</ref>

The original Old Bailey trial records the gelding indictment, Guilty verdict and death sentence; an associated catalogue entry describes a conditional pardon. The trial, newspaper respite and pardon metadata are different records. Pardon conditions and later military service have not been verified.<ref>[https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/record/t17610506-1 Old Bailey Proceedings, t17610506-1, 6 May 1761].</ref><ref>[https://www.oldbaileyonline.org/record/ar_9141_29053 Old Bailey associated-record catalogue, ar_9141_29053].</ref>

Samuel Glascow is one of two prisoners reported capitally convicted at the Old Bailey; his charge is stealing a bay gelding belonging to Mr Serjeant Haywood. The manslaughter verdict belongs to William Smith, a separate defendant. The relative trial-day statement has no saved dateline; no 13 May trial date is inferred. This is a newspaper report of the case, not a separate trial or proof of ancestral identity.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/1212389396/ Berrow’s Worcester Journal, 1761-05-14, p.1].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/394296692/ The Derby Mercury, 1761-05-08, p.2].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/396510014/ Jackson's Oxford Journal, 1761-05-09, p.2].</ref>

The complete Public Advertiser report of 22 May says the Newgate report was made to the King “Yesterday”, supporting 21 May as the reported royal-review day, and reports Samuel’s respite. The complete paragraph says “Yesterday” the report of seven Newgate malefactors was made to the King at St James’s. A separate original page-1 masthead reads Friday, 22 May 1761, supporting 21 May as the reported royal-review day. Respite is reported, not proof of pardon conditions, military service or ancestral identity. Thomas Andrews’s offence is not Samuel’s.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/34424820/ The Public Advertiser, 1761-05-22, p.3].</ref><ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/34424813/ The Public Advertiser, 22 May 1761, p.1, printed issue masthead].</ref>

A one-shilling advertisement for printed Old Bailey trial proceedings lists Samuel Glascow’s horse-stealing case among its contents. This is a publication notice, not another trial, conviction or respite. The other defendants’ crimes are separate. The case association does not identify an Ulster or Pennsylvania family.<ref>[https://www.newspapers.com/image/34424864/ The Public Advertiser, 1761-05-26, p.4].</ref>
