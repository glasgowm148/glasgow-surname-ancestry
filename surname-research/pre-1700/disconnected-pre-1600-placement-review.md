## The Ninian-Leith-Edinburgh craft model

Ninian's 1613 testament directly names son Robert. William occurs, but his
formerly asserted sonship is not securely readable; the later Leith mealmaker
is only a possible identity. That William's son Archibald was apprenticed
to an Edinburgh cordiner in 1639. Against that documented family background:

- Margaret Glasgow married a cordiner in Edinburgh in 1611;
- Barbara Glasgow married a cordiner there in 1616;
- Isobel Glasgow married a tailor there in 1619; and
- another Barbara Glasgow had a South Leith marriage/proclamation entry in 1611.

The repeated trade and Leith/Edinburgh geography makes Ninian's household or
its immediate collateral circle relevant. The corrected Andrew-of-Corsoun
testament, however, independently names daughters Jonet and Isobel, so the Mid
Calder Jonet and Edinburgh Isobel must also be compared against that household.
None of the records carries a spouse or later residence from Andrew's will into
these profiles, so the exact identities and parent edges remain hypotheses.

## Structural recommendation

Use uncertain edges only for the strongest single-parent choices: John-1095 to
William-1098 (already retained), William-1098 to Elizabeth-3915 (already
retained), James-1168 to Matthew-3908, Ninian-3188/Jonet Meik to Jonat-3262,
and James-991 to Jonet-3183. Keep Michael as an unstructured brother/cousin
hypothesis, Robert-3919 as an identity/merge candidate, and the three adult
Barbara profiles unmerged pending the original 1611 entries. Do not connect
Nancy, Margaret Glasko or George Glascoe merely to eliminate an island.
