## Glasgow burgh family and Edinburgh craft-marriage cluster, 1586-1633

The following conclusions have been checked against the published records:

- Glasgow protocols 2903 and 2916 directly prove William Glasgow and Margaret
  Herbesoun as parents of George Glasgow; see
  `../../research/Glasgow-1099/findings.md`.
- Protocol 3492, not 3453, records George Glasgow's 13 November 1598 marriage

## Wester Duddingston to Edinburgh: James, son of Robert, 1641-49

Francis J. Grant, ed., *Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh,
1583-1666* (Scottish Record Society, 1906), printed p. 71,
[public scan](https://archive.org/details/scottishrecordso28scotuoft/page/71/mode/2up);
local complete copy:
`sources/grant_register_edinburgh_apprentices_1583_1666.pdf`.

On **16 June 1641** James Glasgow, explicitly the son of Robert Glasgow,
servant to William Crichtoun in Wester Duddingston, was apprenticed to William
Sclaitter, weaver. Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., *Roll of Edinburgh Burgesses
and Guild-Brethren, 1406-1700*, printed p. 208,
[PDF p. 218](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/roll_of_edinburgh_burgesses_1406_to_1700.pdf#page=218),
records James Glasgow, weaver, becoming burgess on **20 June 1649** after
apprenticeship to the deceased William Sklaitter and repeats the exact date
16 June 1641.

The shared name, master, trade and exact apprenticeship date conclusively join
the records. The adult Edinburgh weaver's father was Robert of Wester
Duddingston, not Alexander Glasgow of Inveresk. The profiles have now been
separated correctly: [[Glasgow-1150]] represents the Edinburgh weaver, while
[[Glasgow-3923]] represents Alexander Glasgow and Jannet Clerk's son baptised
at Inveresk in 1626. No further profile or merge is required. [[Glasgow-1156]],
buried at Duddingston in 1647, is the leading profile match for the named
father Robert, but that identity is not unique from the apprenticeship wording
alone and remains uncertain.

The next apprentice volume independently confirms one child. On **24 January
1672**, Robert Glasgow was apprenticed to merchant Robert Meane and explicitly
called the son of James Glasgow, weaver. The child's 5 May 1655 Edinburgh
baptism names James Glasgow and Agnes Duncan, making him the existing
[[Glasgow-1374]] at the conventional apprenticeship age of sixteen. Source:
Charles B. Boog Watson, ed., *Register of Edinburgh Apprentices, 1666-1700*
(1929), printed p. 37,
[PDF p. 47](https://www.tradeshouselibrary.org/uploads/4/7/7/2/47723681/edinburgh_apprentices_~_1666_to_1700_~_1929.pdf#page=47);
local complete copy:
`sources/watson_register_edinburgh_apprentices_1666_1700.pdf`.

## Cunninghame teind lists, 1559–60

Margaret H. B. Sanderson, *The People of Sixteenth Century Ayrshire*,
*Ayrshire Collections*, volume 14, number 8 (Ayrshire Archaeological and
Natural History Society, 1987), appendix “The Cunninghame Teind Lists of
1559–60,” pp. 327–345. [Publisher's PDF](https://aanhs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/people-of-ayrshire.pdf);
local complete copy: `sources/sanderson_people_of_sixteenth_century_ayrshire_1987.pdf`.
Sanderson cites the Scottish Record Office, Register of Acts and Decreets,
volume 22, folios 102v–167v, as the underlying record.

### What the date and list mean

The appendix expressly dates the teind lists to **1559–60**. This is not a birth,
marriage, or death year. It places named landholders in the teind litigation/list
period. Sanderson explains that:

- the named defenders were tenants holding land and owing crop teinds;
- the lists include people in arrears rather than every inhabitant;
- they generally represent heads of households;
- a tenant can appear more than once because one person could hold multiple lands;
- the list cannot establish on which holding a family actually lived; and
- “and” denotes a joint holding, not necessarily marriage or kinship.

The published list is a modern transcription. The underlying Register of Acts
and Decreets images have not yet been reviewed.

### Mathie–Glasgow network follow-up, 30 August 2026

The Ardeer pairing is part of a broader record trail. David Mathy and Robert
Glasgow were fellow burgesses and co-witnesses in Glasgow on 10 May 1554.
David's documented son John is a plausible match for a later writer who entered
the Glasgow burgess roll with the Earl of Glencairn's group in 1583. John
Mathie senior and junior were together at a Clerkland daughter's marriage in
1600; an older Glencairn servant John was killed in July 1606 and a second John
wrote an estate bond that September. Tutory retours entered in January 1607
directly name the deceased John Mathie of Ibert's brother Matthew, a Glasgow
burgess, and John's lawful children James, David, Agnes and Margaret. The date,
Kilmaronock location and Glencairn lordship make John of Ibert a strong
candidate for the servant killed in July. This creates a credible Mathie
corridor between Robert Glasgow's city circle and the Stevenston/Corsankell
estate cluster. Jonet Mathie's 1618 marriage to John Glasgow may continue that
association, but she is not among the four children named in the tutory
retours and her father remains unknown. See the
[focused analysis](../../research/Glasgow-3908/mathie-glencairn-bridge-1554-1618.md).

## Matthew Glasgow: 1559–60 versus Glasgow-3538

The current `glasgow-one-tree.xlsx` row for Glasgow-3538 reads “Matthew,”
`1650-00-00`, and “Stevenson, Scotland.” The live WikiTree capture is more precise:
the date status is **before 1650**, not a birth in 1650. The profile's only cited
record is not a birth record.

Francis J. Grant, ed., *The Commissariot Record of Glasgow: Register of
Testaments, 1547–1800* (Scottish Record Society; Edinburgh: James Skinner & Co.,
1901), p. 110, indexes **Jonet Cowan, spouse to Mathew Glasgow, in Pyperheugh,
parish of Stevinstoune**, dated **24 April 1665**. [Public PDF](https://electricscotland.com/history/records/scottishrecordso07scotuoft.pdf);
local copy: `sources/grant_commissariot_of_glasgow_testaments_1547_1800_1901.pdf`.

Conclusions:

- The profile's “24 April 1664” is a one-year error; the index says 1665.
- The entry concerns Jonet Cowan's testament index entry and names Matthew as her
  spouse. It is not Matthew's birth, marriage, or death record.
- “Stevenson” should be normalised to **Stevenston, Ayrshire, Scotland**; the
  historical entry uses *Stevinstoune*.
- The Ardeer Nether Matthew of 1559–60 and Glasgow-3538 at Pyperheugh in 1665
  **are not the same man**: the first was already a tenant/head of household about
  106 years before the second record.
- Their uncommon shared name and presence in the same parish are significant
  evidence of Glasgow-family continuity around Stevenston. The older Matthew
  could be an ancestor or collateral relative, but no reviewed record proves a
  relationship.

### Additional pre-1550 Brady relationships

- Stirling protocol abstracts now prove that Richard Brady, clerk, was a son
  of John Brady in 1471, while Gilbert Brady was the same John's grandson and
  heir in 1477–82. Gilbert and Isabella were expressly spouses in 1478. These
  are certain pre-1500 relationships, although Gilbert's intervening parent is
  unnamed and no suitable WikiTree profiles were found.
- Gilbert Brady's Berkhous Croft (1523) was held by John Brady of Easter
  Kennet in 1531; both men also held Easter Kennet property on either side of
  Gilbert's death before April 1527. This makes John a strong probable son or
  close heir, not a proved child. Esbell Brade's membership in this family is
  a useful but unproved network hypothesis.
- *Liber Collegii Nostre Domine*, pp. 124–128, proves a four-generation
  Rutherglen chain in 1538/39–1543: James Hamilton; his daughter and heir
  Mariota, widow of John Brady; their son John Brady, weaver and burgess,
  with wife Isabella Oswald; and John and Isabella's son and heir Malcolm.
  The same South Street holding passed through the family. This is the
  strongest documented Brady household near Esbell Brade, but no record yet
  makes her part of it.
