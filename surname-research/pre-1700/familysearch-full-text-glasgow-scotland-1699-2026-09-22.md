# FamilySearch full-text scan: Glasgow in Scotland to 1699

**Searched:** 22–23 September 2026; printed pages 195–196 rechecked on 22–23 September. **Status:** six new pre-1700 source-level Glasgow surname occurrences are identified in FamilySearch results: Agnes Glasgow, spouse of John Logan/Logane, in a 1601 sasine index; Margaret in Byris of Kilwinning, indexed in 1636; John Glasgow in Rashie, Kilbride, indexed in 1643; an Edinburgh apprentice John Glasgow in 1648; James Glasgow in Didup in a 1674 inquest extract; and Hugh Glasgow, preacher of the gospel, in a 1677 deed index. These are source entries, not necessarily six distinct people: Agnes is a probable but unconfirmed match to Glasgow-3178, and Margaret may be Glasgow-1100. John (1643) and Hugh are cited on possible profile matches with explicit caveats; John (1648) and James remain unresolved. The underlying sasine, testament, apprenticeship register and deed have not been examined.

## Search and source

FamilySearch Full-Text Search was run with name `Glasgow`, place `Scotland`, and an end year of `1699`. The filtered results page reported 394 results across four pages. The broad name search returns many place-name hits because `Glasgow` frequently means the city. The query is available [here](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/full-text/results?count=100&q.anyDate.to=1699&q.anyPlace=Scotland&q.fullName=Glasgow&c.recordPlace2=on&f.recordPlace1=9%2CScotland&c.recordPlace1=on&f.recordPlace0=9).

Result-card headings and snippets were reviewed across the four pages; selected accessible hits were opened and compared with the image/transcript. This was a focused review, not an image-by-image examination of all 394 results. The printed Glasgow run on images 111–112 (pages 195–196) was then read directly and compared with entries already recorded in the project.

A separate earlier-cutoff query returned 103 results and surfaced FamilySearch item `#007830795`, a scan of the 1599–1609 Ayrshire sasine index. Its actual entry is dated 1601, beyond the query cutoff; the result reflects a broad item date range rather than a precise event-date match. The second results page contained restricted 1864 probate and 1943–44 death-index items, not further accessible pre-1700 records. Read the image-level date before treating search filters as exact.

A broader [follow-up search](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/full-text/results?count=100&q.anyDate.to=1699&q.fullName=Glasgow) for the name `Glasgow` with the same end year but no place restriction returned 989 results across ten pages. The will-index item below and the Hunterston papers entry were opened from that result set.

The broad result pages 6–10 were reviewed selectively. Page 6's Scottish Notes and Queries hit did not establish a pre-1700 surname bearer; page 7's Dumbarton roll hit reads “John Barr, messenger from Glasgow,” where Glasgow is the place; page 8's accessible Dumfriesshire land-record image had no indexed names or readable transcript, and other plausible early cards were restricted to a FamilySearch Center. On page 9, an accessible Glasgow wills index image 290 produced city-of-Glasgow location hits, not a verified surname occurrence; the 1675–1681 probate result was restricted. Page 10 yielded no further usable Scottish pre-1700 candidate. This was not an image-by-image review of all 989 results.

On 23 September, place-filtered follow-up searches returned 17 Ireland results and 58 England results for Glasgow with the same 1699 cutoff; United States, Wales and Canada filters returned none. Targeted early cards included Ireland court records dated 1693–1700, a London church book dated 1699–1709, Lincolnshire probate for 1680–1699, England muster rolls dated 1686–1703/1699, and a Berwick guild book beginning in 1697. The opened image links displayed FamilySearch Center/affiliate-library access restrictions, so they supplied no image-verified surname occurrence. An accessible [1875 Irish deed-transcript image](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSK3-33XZ-V?view=fullText&keywords=Glasgow%2CIreland&searchForm=advanced&lang=en) names James Glasgow in a Glenarm property description, but the surrounding memorial dates to 1873–1875; the Full-Text result's extracted 1699 year is a false early lead. These filters add no new verified pre-1700 person and are not exhaustive searches of all result images.

## New dated surname occurrences

In FamilySearch item [*Edinburgh. Apprenticeship Records 1583–1666*](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSM7-53ZS-V?view=fullText&keywords=Glasgow&searchForm=advanced&lang=en&groupId=M9H7-2TF), item `#007764594`, Item 3, image 101, the scan reproduces Francis J. Grant, ed., *Register of Apprentices of the City of Edinburgh, 1583–1666* (Edinburgh: Scottish Record Society, 1906), printed p. 75. Under the explicit **Glasgow** heading, the entry reads: “John, son to late Thomas G., maltman, burgess of Glasgow, with William Blackwood, merchant, 12 Jan. 1648.” The heading establishes John's surname; the father's surname is abbreviated in the line. The date is the apprenticeship-entry date, not a birth or death date. The register transcription has been checked, but the original manuscript register was not examined. Before this update, no `John Glasgow + Edinburgh + 1648` occurrence was in the curated record catalogue; it is now indexed as `record-john-glasgow-edinburgh-1648`. Glasgow-1604, Glasgow-3145 and Glasgow-3610 remain chronological candidate profiles; none has an identity bridge to Thomas G., the maltman and burgess. The source and duplicate audit are preserved in the [HOLD dossier](../new-people/1648_Scotland_Edinburgh_John_Glasgow.md) and [public record findings](../../research/record-john-glasgow-edinburgh-1648/findings.md). No separate WikiTree profile has been created because that could duplicate an existing John.

In FamilySearch’s item [*Glasgow. Wills 1547–1800*](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CS8K-D98V?view=fullText&keywords=Glasgow&searchForm=advanced&lang=en&groupId=M97N-2NT), item `#007960708`, image 112 (printed index page 196), an explicit “Glasgow, Janet ...” heading is followed by ditto-marked entries. The newly identified entry reads “John, in Rashie [FamilySearch AI transcript: Rashlic], par. of Kilbride,” dated **17 April 1643**. The surname comes from the printed heading and ditto marks. The date is the testament registration/index date, not a stated death date. The source was added to Glasgow-1030 as a possible match only; the original testament and John’s identity have not been checked.

The corrected line-to-date sequence is corroborated by entries already in the project: John, sailor in Saltcoats, is indexed 11 April 1637 (NRS `CC9/7/27`); the next entry is John in Rashie at 17 April 1643; and John in Bog of Steivinstoun is dated 6 November 1685 (NRS `CC9/7/47`, already listed in the maintained index confirmations). The previous version of this report attached 17 April 1643 to the Bog entry; that reading was a row-alignment error and is superseded. The 1643 Rashie entry has no exact person/place/date row in `www/data/records.csv`. A separate raw Findmypast audit row, `SCOT/WILLS/057445`, lists a generic John Glasgow in 1643 but has not been validated; it may be the same testament, so this is not claimed as a second underlying event.

The same page 196 run includes the previously catalogued Ninian Glasgow entry dated 8 May 1613. Page 195 contains known entries including Geillis (11 May 1603), Katherine (9 May 1605), Agnes (4 March 1628) and James in Brekpleughe (21 February 1632), all already represented in maintained research/profile sources. An earlier apparent Allan lead on printed page 193 was a false positive: the surname heading there is **Gilmour**, and the ditto marks place Allan under Gilmour, not Glasgow.

Also on FamilySearch item `#007960708`, image 112 (printed p. 196), the row under the explicit Glasgow heading reads **“Margaret, in Byris of Kilwinning” — 8 November 1636**. The full page was visually checked against the complete scan of Grant's printed index, which confirms the row/date alignment: [PDF printed p. 196](https://www.electricscotland.com/history/records/scottishrecordso07scotuoft.pdf#page=206). The adjacent **Mary**, spouse of James Montgomerie, sailor in Saltcoats, is dated **24 October 1677**; 8 November 1636 belongs to Margaret. The 9 May 1605 date belongs to Katherine, spouse of Thomas Weir. A separate proposed Archibald Glasgow batch was also rejected after full-page inspection: those Archibalds fall under the **Glen** heading, not Glasgow. Thus p. 196 contributes one new Glasgow surname occurrence, Margaret; Mary is already represented by Glasgow-3539.

Hew-1066 and Glasgow-3109 also remain possible identities; no new profile is justified until the deed's residence, parties or kin can be checked. In FamilySearch item [*Linlithgow. Legal Records 1546–1564*](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSM7-J962-6?view=fullText&keywords=Glasgow&searchForm=advanced&lang=en&groupId=M9QY-96Q), item `#007764588`, Item 2, image 105/120, the scan is actually M. S. Shaw, ed., *Some Family Papers of the Hunters of Hunterston* (Edinburgh: Scottish Record Society, 1925), p. 62, entry 89. The official [Scottish Record Society catalogue](https://www.scottishrecordsociety.org.uk/publications/old-series/) lists this as Old Series no. 58, edited by M. S. Shaw and published in 1925. Entry 89, “Service as heir to Mr. Henry Hunter,” is an extract retour of General Service of Robert Hunter of Hunterstoun as heir to Henry Hunter, dated at Irvine **11 June 1674**. The inquest list includes **“James Glasgow in Didup.”** This is a new, concrete record-level occurrence absent from the curated record catalogue. The FamilySearch title is a cataloguing mismatch; it is not a 1546–1564 Linlithgow event. The printed extract gives James no age, occupation, kin or vital date. Didup is associated with Stevenston, Ayrshire in later directory evidence, but this does not make it a birth or death place.

The occurrence is recorded in the [HOLD draft and duplicate audit](../new-people/1674_Scotland_Didup_James_Glasgow.md). Possible existing profiles include Glasgow-3971 (born 1645 at Glasgow), Glasgow-3969 (later associated with Beith), Glasgow-3107 (Polwarth marriage in 1663), Glasgow-3172 (Belfast shipmaster) and Glasgow-1496 (estimated born about 1650 at Campbeltown). None has a Didup/Stevenston link; Glasgow-3105's estimated 1670 birth is too late for an adult inquest member. No profile assignment or new-profile recommendation is made until a distinguishing record is found.

## Earlier sasine-index occurrence

FamilySearch item [*Ayrshire. Land Records 1599–1609*](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CS3Q-47SZ-P?view=fullText&keywords=GLASGOW&searchForm=advanced&lang=en&groupId=M9QN-ZXH), item `#007830795`, Item 1, image 74/120, scans the Scottish Record Office's *Index to Secretary's Register of Sasines for Sheriffdom of Ayr and Bailiaries of Kyle, Carrick and Cunningham, 1599–1609* (Edinburgh: H.M. Stationery Office, 1932), p. 128. Under the printed heading **LOGAN (LOGANE)**, the index reads “Agnes Glasgow, spouse of John, in Milneburne, Stenstoun” [place spelling in the image may read **Steinstoun**]. The aligned columns give date of recording **29 June 1601**, vol. 1, fol. 94, Ayr. The heading supports John Logan/Logane, although the entry body gives only “John.”

This is a new index occurrence absent from the curated record catalogue and a probable match to Agnes (Glasgow) Logan, whose 4 March 1628 testament names John Logan, trumpmaker, in Stevenston ([Glasgow-3178 findings](../../research/Glasgow-3178/findings.md)). The shared spouse and locality make the match strong, but the 1601 underlying sasine has not been examined. The National Records of Scotland catalogues the source as [RS11/1](https://catalogue.nrscotland.gov.uk/nrsonlinecatalogue/browsetreeview.aspx?reference=RS11), covering 3 December 1599–19 July 1602. Its [sasine guide](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/learning-and-events/research-guides/sasines/?section=searching-the-general-and-particular-registers-before-1781) says index entries provide a registration date for locating the full document. No online scan of RS11/1, fol. 94 was located; confirm there before treating this as Agnes-3178's source.

Scotland’s People’s [wills and testaments guide](https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk/guides/wills-and-testaments) explains that the will-index date is when the testament was recorded and that the index omits the deceased’s death date; testaments were made after death. John in Rashie is not identified with Glasgow-4061, whose separate 1571 source supports only that he was alive on 9 February 1571. Neither FamilySearch entry supplies a death date for Glasgow-4061.

## Unresolved surname leads

FamilySearch's item [*Cairn of Lochwinyoch Matters: Lochwinnoch. Family Histories 1827–1837*](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CSJ9-2YWB?view=fullText&keywords=Glasgow&searchForm=advanced&lang=en), image 191 of 342 (printed pages 186–187), has an AI-generated transcript of a handwritten genealogical query. The transcript names “George Glasgow of the Bog” in the parish OCR reads as “Stevenson,” and “George Glasgow of the Bay” in the parish OCR reads as “Kelvening.” It asks whether these men were related to Mr Glasson, minister of Kilbirnie, or came from another family. The image looks consistent with those names and place references, but the exact readings need paleographic checking.

The note is retrospective and gives neither George an event date or parentage. Its nearby, imperfectly recognized account of the minister's ordination and marriages does not date the two Georges. Treat them as **possible late-seventeenth-century local bearers, not established pre-1700 records or identified people**.

## Database comparison

- The 1693 Robert Glasgow mentioned later on the same manuscript page is already represented by [Glasgow-1075](https://www.wikitree.com/wiki/Glasgow-1075) and the Kilbirnie baptism record `SCOT/OPR/BAP/3585750`; it is not new.
- The database has George Glasgow-1058, baptized at Kilwinning in 1687, and an unsourced profile-only George Glasgow-3120, stated born about 1660 at Kilwinning. Glasgow-3120 could be the “Bay” George, but the manuscript supplies no age or identity bridge. Do not equate them without corroboration.
- The 1677 Hugh entry is a new exact index occurrence. Glasgow-1603 is chronologically compatible, but the match is unresolved; Hew-1066 and Glasgow-3109 also remain candidates. The source is attached to Glasgow-1603 only as a possible match, not as a proved life event.
- The 1674 James Glasgow in Didup is a new record-level occurrence from a printed retour extract, not a proved identity. Its exact place/date/person combination is absent from the curated catalogue. Several James profiles remain possible; see the HOLD draft. Do not infer vital dates or create a duplicate profile from this entry alone.
- No existing evidence-register entry identifies a George Glasgow of the Bog at Stevenston. This remains a source-level lead, not a confirmed new profile.

## False positives and limits

The apparent “Isobell Glasgow” hit in *Argyll. Land Records 1617–1780* (for example [image 183](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CS39-Q94B-T?view=fullText&keywords=Glasgow&searchForm=advanced&lang=en)) is not a Glasgow surname occurrence: the original register places `Glasgow` with a different entry, while Isobell is described as a Kirkintilloch burgess's daughter and John Morson's spouse. Another Argyll hit ([image 425](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-CS39-Q9HF-X?view=fullText&keywords=Glasgow&searchForm=advanced&lang=en)) also uses Glasgow as a place. The apparent Allan entry on printed page 193 of the will index is likewise not a Glasgow surname: the printed heading is **Gilmour**. Similar city-name matches appeared in Glasgow deeds, land records and wills.

Some Scottish results, including vestry-minute and land-record images, were marked viewable only at a FamilySearch Center or affiliate library and could not be examined in this session. The search therefore does not establish that these are the only early mentions.

## Conclusion and next evidence

Six new pre-1700 surname occurrences are established in this scan: Agnes Glasgow, spouse of John Logan/Logane, in the 1601 Ayrshire sasine index; Margaret in Byris of Kilwinning, indexed in 1636; John Glasgow in Rashie, parish of Kilbride, indexed 17 April 1643; apprentice John Glasgow, indexed 12 January 1648; James Glasgow in Didup, named in a 1674 retour extract; and Hugh Glasgow, preacher of the gospel, grantee in a deed indexed 8 March 1677, Durie Office vol. 42, p. 148 (`RD3/42`). They are record occurrences, not necessarily six distinct people. The 1648 apprenticeship now has a separate public HOLD record dossier; no WikiTree identity has been established. Agnes may be Glasgow-3178; Margaret may be Glasgow-1100. The 1643 John, Hugh and James also remain unresolved or possible profile matches, as detailed above. A raw Findmypast John Glasgow 1643 candidate may be the same testament, so it is not counted separately. The John in Bog of Steivinstoun is a separate, already recorded 6 November 1685 entry and is not Glasgow-4061. None of the record/index dates is treated here as a death date. Locate NRS `RS11/1`, fol. 94, `CC9/7/28` and `RD3/42`, p. 148; seek the 1674 retour/Hunterston papers and the original apprenticeship register or Glasgow burgess/maltmen records for distinguishing identities. The two Georges in the Lochwinnoch note remain undated leads and need contemporary corroboration.
