About this research

We research people named Glasgow and their families, beginning in Scotland and Ulster and following their descendants elsewhere. The aim is to establish family relationships from historical records and to identify the ancestors we have yet to trace.

What does the website contain?

It contains both research results and work in progress. Family studies and individual pages explain our current conclusions, the sources used and the questions still open. The people index also includes entries from WikiTree that have not yet been researched in detail.

Where should I start?

  1. Find a person by name, dates or place. Open their page for life details, relatives, source references and any research notes.
  2. Browse a family study to see how records from the same area fit together.
  3. Read an open question to see the evidence collected and the next record needed.

For an example, follow Alexander Glasgow of Lisnagaver from the records of his household to the question of his parents.

How does it relate to WikiTree?

This site brings together research for the WikiTree Glasgow Name Study. Where a person has a confirmed WikiTree profile or individual research page, their page here links to it. Some people found in records do not yet have a confirmed WikiTree match.

The family tree shows relationships recorded on WikiTree. Some of those links remain uncertain. Families displayed together are not necessarily descended from one known ancestor.

How is the research checked?

Research notes explain which conclusions are supported by records and which are probable or possible. Follow the citations to the original record, archive description, index or transcription; each has different limits.

AI tools assist with searches, transcription and drafting. Their suggestions need checking against the records. A place in the tree, a matching name or an AI suggestion does not by itself establish a family relationship.

Y-DNA research compares paternal lines alongside their documented ancestry. A DNA connection alone cannot name a missing ancestor.

What is included?

People who may still be living are omitted. The people index, family tree and DNA pages were compiled at different times, so their coverage differs.

Saved people and recently viewed pages stay in your browser. Saving a person here does not edit WikiTree or publish your bookmarks.
