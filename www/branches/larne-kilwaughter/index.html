Larne & Kilwaughter

East Antrim records preserve several Glasgow households from 1653 onward. The question at the heart of this study

Can the seventeenth-century Larne, Glenarm and Kilwaughter occurrences be connected to the later documented Kilwaughter family without filling the missing generations by name or locality alone?

The 20 December1764 Ballykeel lease proves that James Glasgow was Robert Glasgow’s son. It does not name Robert’s father or supply the missing earlier generations. The local working tree now tentatively connects Robert of Larne through a possible1618 Glasgow identity and places the Belfast shipmaster James beneath him. These selected uncertain links supply a provisional shared family, not documentary proof; the later Larne and Inishrush identity comparisons remain separate questions. The working model now places Adam of Inishrush and the reconstructed older Alexander under the older Larne ropemaker, both uncertain; it does not prove either son or James’s own ancestry. The inspected 1821 Crossle census abstract independently records Ann Glasgow, 50, with sister Jane and brother James, both 47, at No. 22 Main Street, Larne. This strengthens the possible comparison between that Ann/Jane pair and the 1832 lease renewal, while Ann Glasgow and James Glasgow remain unproved identities. It does not change any earlier parent or sibling attachment.

What this does not show

Reviewed locality evidence and specifically supported family links; uncertain exported-tree ancestry is excluded.

Reading the evidence

1821–1832 · inspected abstract, possible identities

Ann, Jane and James at No. 22 Main Street

Research rationale, 23 October 2026: test whether an independently recorded Larne household helps identify the Anne and Jane Glasgow named together in the 25 February 1832 renewal of James Glasgow’s 1779 Newtown lease. The inspected Crossle genealogical abstract, page 23, image 00142, is headed 1821 census, County Antrim, parish and town of Larne. At No. 22 Main Street it records Ann Glasgow, 50, Jane, her sister, 47, and James, her brother, 47. The sibling roles are explicit for these record subjects. The ages imply births about 1770–1771 for Ann and 1773–1774 for Jane and James; these are estimates, not recorded birth dates. Equal reported ages do not establish twins. This is an abstract of the census, not an inspection of the original census sheet; uncertain occupation and land-note readings are not used.

The 1832 lease renewal names Anne and Jane Glasgow. The 1821 Ann/Jane sibling household supplies a specific, independently recorded pair in Larne and strengthens a possible identity across the records. No inspected record equates No. 22 Main Street with the recital’s 24-foot north-side tenement or proves that the same women held it in 1832. Ann Ann Glasgow, monument-defined daughter of James James Glasgow and his wife Jane, remains a possible identification for the abstract’s Ann. Her monument-derived birth about 1767–1768 differs by roughly three years from the abstract age; that discrepancy does not decide the identity. The monument father died in 1812 and his wife Jane died in 1820, so neither can be the living 1821 brother James or sister Jane. Their documented parentage stays confined to the monument-defined Ann; no parentage transfers to the abstract household.

James James Glasgow, with an uncertain birth about 1773 and a Larne association, is a compatible comparison for the 47-year-old brother only. His adult identity, possible 1830 freehold application and proposed father James Glasgow remain separately uncertain. Shared sibling surnames support a natal-Glasgow reading but do not independently prove birth surnames or parents. The current study selects no existing-profile sibling edge, parent, identity merge or vital correction from the abstract. Earlier Larne ropemaker, estate and parent-confidence assessments retain their separate grades. The 1832 renewal and intervening title records, the exact premises, and a named parent or sibling bridge are the next discriminators.

1737–1773 · selected father replacement

James the ropemaker: Hugh preferred as father, possible 52%

Research rationale: choose the strongest existing parent hypothesis for a substantial Larne/Inishrush branch rather than leaving the recorded Hugh comparison outside the working family.

Select Hugh Hugh Glasgow as the preferred possible father of James James Glasgow, the older Belfast/Larne ropemaker, at 52% subjective confidence, replacing William William Glasgow in the local working tree. The archived chronology explicitly places a James, son of Hugh, at New Town/Larne in 1737 and identifies its reported 1762 Janet Shutter groom with that James. Hugh is an independently documented Larne merchant and elder, jointly leasing Newtown property in1736 and dealing with Hugh Shutter in the original1749 mortgage. The later James/Janet account and the distinctive Jane Shutter name in the younger William’s monument fit a recurring Glasgow–Shutter network. These combined named-parent, locality, generation and family-association clues favour Hugh over retaining a William father without a named-son record.

This is a preferred working identification, not a proved father clause for the adult ropemaker. The adult’s1737 birth may itself derive from that reconstruction; the independent1773 affidavit establishes only birth before27 October1743. The original1737 entry,1762 marriage and alleged James/Janet stone remain uninspected. Hugh Shutter’s relationship to Janet is unknown, and a business association does not identify a father-in-law. A different Hugh remains possible. No mother is selected: Ann is not established as the1737 child’s mother by the reviewed account.

That is a serious rival explanation: the reported1737 child could have died and the name been used again. It is not a recorded death of that earlier child. Withdraw the categorical earlier inference that the1737 James must have died young. Do not equate the two James profiles, invent a second wife, or infer surviving brothers with the same forename as an established fact. The older ropemaker was already an adult in1760–1761 and over thirty in1773, so he remains distinct from the younger monument/tanner comparison.

William and Hugh’s joint1736/1757 property activity makes a wider family or business relationship plausible without choosing its degree. William could be father, uncle or another associate. The specific reported Hugh-son identification and Hugh–Shutter connection now favour Hugh, but do not exclude William. The percentages express comparative research judgment, not a measured frequency or independent-source calculation.

Kilwaughter: James’s 1669–1726 continuity is probable

The preferred identification is one James Glasgow from the Kilwaughter Demesne hearth household of 1669 to the Kilwaughter overseer of probably 29 May 1701 and the later elder marked deceased in 1726. Assess that continuity as probable rather than unresolved. The hearth roll places Jo and James Glascho consecutively in the same demesne, records 5347–5348. The later congregational extracts again contain both names acting for Kilwaughter: John senior in 1700 and an unmarked John in 1700/01, whose individual identity remains open, and James as overseer in probably 1701. John’s already-probable continuity supplies a coherent parallel ageing household, rather than leaving James’s later occurrence as an isolated name match. James’s later elder, 1722 commissioner and 1727 Kilwater probate-index accounts were already assessed as probably one man. The repeated local pairing, the particular district served and an ordinary adult generation in 1700/01 together favour continuity over an otherwise unattested replacement James. The preference uses the combined local sequence; no literal same-man clause is required.

The inference is not documented identity. Assuming an adult aged at least 21 in 1669 would make James at least about 74 in 1722 and 78 in 1726; those are conditional age calculations, not recorded ages or a new birth bound. His local commission in 1722 is compatible with advanced age, but the long career is the strongest reason for caution. No age at death, occupation, holding or named relative equates the occurrences. Neither the 1726 death annotation nor the 1727 probate is newly assigned to a separate earlier James; they remain events of the later elder whose identification with the 1669 man is now probable.

Larne evidence synthesis
1700–1727

Three heads of household and a session elder

Classon Porter’s printed extracts from the Larne session record name John Glasgow, sen., as stipend bondsman for Kilwaughter and Robert Glasgow for the South Side on 17 May 1700, and James Glasgow as overseer for Kilwaughter, probably on 29 May 1701. The extracts are printed under 1699 and 1700; the weekdays written into the minutes fix the years. They come from a manuscript copy of the session record, not the original.

No Glasgow is among the twenty elders listed from Mr Ogilvie’s ordination in November 1700. James Glasgow stands on the session list that opens the 1721 book, was a commissioner when the Presbytery met at Larne in 1722 and is marked “deceased 1726”; a will of James Glasgow of Kilwater is indexed in 1727. Those four are probably one man. The maintained review now favours, at probable rather than proved strength, identification with the James of the 1669 hearth-money roll; a same-name successor remains possible.

No record states how these men were related to each other, to the 1669 householders or to the men born about 1700–1710. That John and James of 1669 were close kin is possible, on consecutive entries with the same uncommon spelling; brother is no better supported than any other degree. In the printed General Synod rolls no Glasgow represented Larne in any year from 1694 to 1720; the ruling elder James Glasgow of 1698, 1703 and 1705 sat beside the ministers of Drumaul and Duneane.

Robert of Ballykeel/Kilwaughter leased Hugh Shannon half the lower farm, about 31½ acres. Both original images were inspected. Two assignments checked — 21 September 2026

The ropewalk assignment, memorial 173104, p. 510, calls its witness James Glasgow Tanner. The separate messuage assignment, memorial 173108, opening, image 259, calls its witness James Glasgow Farmer on p. 513. Both are dated 28 March and registered 2 November 1768; both repeat the occupation in the affidavit and name John Houston as another witness. The first concerns a 31-year ropewalk lease at £1 14s 1½d yearly; the second a 60-year messuage lease at £5 2s 6d. The tanner evidence remains valid. The farmer’s identity and whether the witnesses were the same man remain unresolved; no kinship is stated.

Elizabeth’s monument and estate association — 21 September 2026

The Larne family monument transcription, p. 47, records Elizabeth, William Glasgow’s wife, dying 17 September 1832 aged 60, supporting birth about 1772. Memorial 548257, p. 522, dated 8 November 1823 and registered 17 May 1826, names Elizabeth Glasgow of Larne, widow, as administratrix of Jane Stewart late of Curran, deceased. She is a strong but unproved match to William’s widow; the deed states no relationship to Jane. Its south-side Newtown mortgage property is distinct from the north-side James–Anne–Jane title chain. James Glasgow, writing clerk, witnesses it; his identity remains unresolved. Jane’s administration and the earlier mortgage are the next tests.

A new will target: Mary’s marriage settlement, 1843

An original marriage settlement dated 10 February 1843, registered 18 February, names John McGavock of Larne, innkeeper, and Mary Glasgow of Larne, spinster. She conveys half shares of north- and south-side Newtown tenements and property north of Pound Road. The continuation makes part of the settlement conditional on £250 being paid by the trustees or executors of Hugh Glasgow, deceased. Both original images were inspected.

This is a documented estate connection with unresolved identities. It does not call Hugh Mary’s father, give the will’s date, or establish that the intended marriage occurred that day. Mary has not been identified with William’s daughter Mary-3989, and the testator has not been assigned to either earlier Hugh. Hugh’s will and the earlier leases behind Mary’s half shares could reveal the missing succession. Trace these alongside Elizabeth’s 1824 renewal and the separate Anne–Jane renewal of 1832; shared streets alone do not establish descent.

The original 1851 household return lists John McGavock of Larne Cross Street, merchant and innkeeper, as a widower married in 1824, 1827 and 1843. Its death table lists wife Mary, aged 37, dying of jaundice in autumn 1843. This is a strong candidate household, but no maiden name is supplied. William’s daughter Mary is commemorated as dying on 18 October 1844, also aged 37. The conflicting year prevents a secure identification; inspect the marriage and burial registers and trace the property before joining these records.

Provisional placement, low confidence: Hugh is a possible father of Mary, the intended bride, with the estate payment representing a provision for her marriage. This is the leading relationship hypothesis from the transaction, not its explicit wording. It provisionally places him in a later Larne generation around William-3324. Identifying him with Hugh-1499, who died in 1767, would require a trust or estate continuing about 76 years; the memorial does not establish that history. If the bride proves to be William’s daughter Mary-3989, her monument establishes William as father and Hugh would instead be a collateral relative, possibly an uncle. Hugh’s own parentage remains unproved; the comparative working ranking below identifies the preferred candidates.

Hugh-3931, an adult householder at Tamlaght O’Crilly in 1740, is a possible father of the testator, with low confidence. The generations can fit, but the 1740 list gives no children and names another Hugh. No inspected record distinguishes the proposed father from those alternatives. The settlement gives no residence for the deceased Hugh: Larne is the bride’s residence and the property’s location, so Tamlaght must not be treated as a geographic exclusion. Hugh-3931 → the testator → Mary is a testable chain of two uncertain links, not established descent.

Preferred father candidates for the later Hugh

For an explicitly uncertain placement, James Glasgow-3991 ranks first, John Glasgow-4179 second, and Hugh Glasgow-3931 remains an alternative. James and John belong to a suitable generation for a son Hugh who could father the 1843 bride. James is explicitly a son of elder Hugh in the Larne monument; John's connection to elder Hugh is already uncertain. A son called Hugh and granddaughter called Mary would be compatible with the names in that family, but naming patterns do not establish either relationship. The reported Mary sister comes from the derivative congregational chronology and remains conditional for John.

Preferred working chain: elder Hugh-1499 → documented son James-3991 → possible son Hugh, deceased by February 1843 → possible daughter Mary, McGavock's intended bride. James's Larne household and property context make this a more specific model to test; no inspected record yet names his son Hugh or matches the bride's property to his. Both new links have low confidence. John's model remains viable, but using his assumed father to support another attachment must not turn the first assumption into evidence. The explicitly provisional placement has now been applied live, as described below; no new kinship evidence was discovered merely by creating the profiles.

New profiles and uncertain links — 21 September 2026

The current working chain is James Glasgow-3991 → uncertain son Hugh Glasgow-4180 → uncertain daughter Mary Glasgow-4181. Both new profiles and both uncertain father statuses are saved and verified on WikiTree. Their biographies distinguish the documented settlement/estate connection from the two inferred relationships. No mother is inferred. Hugh's birth about 1770 is only a generation estimate; Larne is not a recorded birthplace or residence for him. His death before 14 February 1843 is supported by the settlement affidavit.

Mary's probable identification with the census wife supplies the estimated birth about 1806, uncertain death in 1843 and married name McGavock. She may still be Mary-3989, whose monument gives death 18 October 1844 aged 37 and names William-3324 as father. The latter profile now carries the monument dates and a reciprocal identity-conflict note. Separate profiles are provisional, not proof of two women. A confirmed identity would require withdrawing the new Hugh-father hypothesis and retaining William's documented fatherhood. The marriage/burial records, original stone, Hugh's will and title succession remain the discriminating evidence.

Earlier Larne checks — 21 September 2026

The 1741 Ballyboly lease confirms Robert Glasgow of Kilwaughter, farmer, as witness and his 21 April 1743 proof of execution. A 12 May 1772 deed names Wm Glasgow of Larne as a witness, without enough detail to distinguish William-1505 from William-3326. These are dated locality records, not evidence for the unproved 1653/1669 bridge.
