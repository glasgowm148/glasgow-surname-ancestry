Original Currituck title comparison — 31 October 2026Both original instruments describe the same survey: James Dauge’s white-oak corner, N75E26 chains to a cypress, southerly marked trees to another white oak, and a westerly return.

Both original instruments describe the same survey: James Dauge’s white-oak corner, N75E26 chains to a cypress, southerly marked trees to another white oak, and a westerly return. This full matching geometry supplies new verification beyond the earlier 1782 abstract’s patent date and adjoining owner.

Hilary and Jean Simmons conveyed the fifty acres to Caleb Glasgow on 1 August 1761. William Glasgow and Sarah his wife conveyed the same parcel to Solomon Cowell on 25 December 1782; the later deed was acknowledged in March 1786 and registered on 25 April 1786. These execution and registration dates are distinct. Thomas Cooper and John Glasgow witness the later deed, without a stated kinship.

The original 1761 purchase and 1782 sale identify the same 50-acre parcel by the full survey fingerprint: James Dauge’s white-oak corner, N75E26 chains to a cypress, southerly marked trees to another white oak and a westerly return. The earlier 1782 abstract gave only the patent and adjoining owner. This removes an adjacent-parcel alternative and materially strengthens Caleb-to-William property continuity. Combined with the retained Currituck family claim, the compatible 31-year generation and the reported 1781 death preceding William’s sale, father-to-son transmission is the preferred explanation at 68% subjective confidence. This is a fresh combined father-profile judgement, including the unresolved Caleb account identity, not a separate conditional 98% kinship claim.

Neither deed calls William Caleb’s son or explains the intervening transfer. The 1781 death is inherited information, not corroborated by these deeds. Ordinary purchase/gift or collateral succession remains possible; Caleb-363 and Caleb-830 may be overlapping accounts and the deed cannot distinguish them. The old abstract argument already assumed parcel continuity: the new evidence independently verifies its precise geometry, not an explicit inheritance. Estate of inheritance in fee simple is legal tenure wording, not a father clause. No mother, birthday, death date, spouse maiden surname or live WikiTree flag changes.

The 29 October 58% judgement was based on the published abstracts and is retained below as historical reasoning, superseded by this original-geometry review. The interpretation does not read generic estate-of-inheritance wording as a father clause. No mother, vital or separate identity grade is changed.
