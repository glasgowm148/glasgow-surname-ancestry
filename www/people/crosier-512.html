What the research found

These research notes were last revised on 6 October 2026.

Summary of the research

Overall conclusion

James Glasgow's 1775 will directly names his wife Mary. The 1817-1818 Wilson inheritance pleadings say Mary was a daughter of one of Archibald Wilson's six sisters. Because James's mother Rachel was another sister, the pleaded relationships make James and Mary first cousins.

Neither the will nor the equity papers gives Mary the birth surname Crozier/Crosier or identifies her mother. Preserve Mary as James's wife and as a daughter of an unidentified Wilson sister, but treat the Crosier-512 surname and exact parentage as unproved.

Source findings

James Glasgow's will, dated 17 October 1775 Names wife Mary and six children. Direct derivative will transcription for spouse and household; no birth surname or parents.
John Glasgow et al. v. Robert Glasgow, 1817-1818 Says Mary was a daughter of another of Archibald Wilson's sisters. Direct derivative litigation evidence for her maternal Wilson connection and first-cousin relationship to James; exact mother unnamed.

Other research notes

Original 1822 will: readable family clauses — 31 October 2026

The readable continuation of Mary Glasgow’s will names her son John Glasgow to manage her property and gives a remainder to Mary Glasgow, described as the widow of deceased Wilson Glasgow, and her heirs. The will is dated 5 April 1822; the recorded probate is 14 October 1822. Mary’s existing Newberry family and the probate four days after her reported gravestone death make Mary, wife of James (Crosier-512), the compatible maintained account. The named son is compatible with John Glasgow-1146; the widow is compatible with Mary McCracken-3711, widow of Wilson Glasgow-1982. These identifications are family-context inferences, not additional relationship clauses.

The opening on manuscript page 489 runs below the framing of the inspected scan; the following page begins mid-clause. This is a partial reading, not a complete transcription or complete heir list. A Robert Glasgow witnesses the will and proves it; that role is not a statement that he is a son, and no exact Robert profile is assigned. The readable text neither supplies Mary’s birth surname nor identifies her parents. It does not name Tennessee Robert Glasgow-2769 as Archibald’s son or exclude an omitted heir. No father confidence, attachment, mother, vital field or duplicate decision changes.

Sources: opening, page 489 · continuation and probate, page 490. Newberry Will Book H, FamilySearch images 248–249, inspected 31 October 2026.

John, compatible existing son account · Mary, compatible widow account · Wilson, the existing deceased-husband account. Their existing relationship assessments are not revised by this record review.
