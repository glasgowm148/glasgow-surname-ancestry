The original 1820 Smith County census, page 58, household 716, has James Glasgow with one male under 10, one male 10–15 and one male 26–44; females comprise one under 10 and one 16–25. The age-band reading follows the standard column order and is consistent with the county transcription; this manuscript leaf has no column headings. The original 1840 page 270 has a James household with males 5–9, 10–14 and 60–69, two females 15–19 and one female 40–49. Those older male bands fit the circa-1776 James account, but do not identify him uniquely. An unnamed 1820 boy could fit Wesley’s estimated 1818 birth; no 1840 male 20–29 appears in this household. Wesley could already have left home, so that absence does not exclude paternity. The 1820 page groups G surnames and is not evidence that adjacent lines were physical neighbours.

The original 1850 Smith County household 1186, lines 2–9, contains the head indexed as Neely Glassgow, 32, farmer, Virginia 29, and the six indexed children Joicy A 9, John W 8, Elizabeth D 6, Andrew G 5, Holly J 3 and James W 11 months, all recorded as Tennessee-born. This is the existing Wesley-family source, not a newly discovered household. The original head’s handwriting is compatible with Wesley/Wesly; the index’s Neely reading and the later-life identification are kept distinct. No parent is named and no relationship column appears in this schedule.

James Glasgow Senior’s original Davidson County will is now located in volume 7, printed pages 407–408, FamilySearch images 233–234. It expressly names a son James and appoints him an executor. The closing probate entry records that a son/executor James qualified in April 1820; the recording heading is June 1820. The will is dated November 1819; no exact day is adopted from this reading. This replaces the earlier original-uninspected status.
