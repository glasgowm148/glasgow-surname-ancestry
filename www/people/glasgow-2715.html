Record findings and identity limits — 17 October 2026Possible William Allen Glasgow, Royal Engineers commission, 1795

Gentleman Cadet William Allen Glasgow is announced as second lieutenant in the Corps of Royal Engineers, vice Lefebvre, promoted, dated 1 October 1795. (The Caledonian Mercury, 1795-10-15, p.4.)

The commission date is 1 October 1795, not the 15 October publication day. "Gentleman Cadet" describes his prior status; the notice gives no parents, birthplace or subsequent career. A William without a recorded middle name remains a possible identification.

This may concern Glasgow-2715. Estimated c. 1779 Crawford birth is compatible with a young cadet. Estimated age and a missing middle name do not exclude him; no engineering-service connection.

Crawford family connection — 31 October 2026

George Glasgow-638 is already described in his held and live biography as the Crawford child of William Glasgow and Janet Thomson, but neither parent profile is attached. The inspected Scotland baptism index names that exact couple for George, born 8 June 1818 and baptised 14 June at Crawford. An exact-parent search returned thirteen Crawford entries spanning 1802–1828, including Isabel in 1808 and an older George in 1810. The inspected monumental-inscription transcript identifies William Glasgow-2715, gamekeeper to the Earl of Hopetoun, his wife Janet Thomson, their daughter Isabel (died 1881 aged 73), and an older George who died in July 1816. The 1841 census transcript places gamekeeper William with Janet and Isabella at Hass, Crawford; the 1851 transcript gives widower William, 72, born Crawford, with daughter Isabella and younger children. This combination supports the gamekeeper as the 1818 child’s father, with reuse of the deceased child’s name. The proposed attachment to the later Leicester adult is assessed at 88% subjective confidence, including the unresolved childhood-to-adult identification.

The baptism and census originals and gravestone image have not been inspected; these are provider index/transcript readings. FamilySearch and Findmypast reproduce the same underlying baptism index and are not independent evidence. The later Leicester adult’s exact birthday may derive from the baptism, so it is not counted as independent confirmation. His inspected 1843 GRO marriage index locates a Leicester entry with Mary Ann Norton among the same-page spouse possibilities, but gives no father or father’s occupation. The original marriage is the decisive next test. The memorial’s older George, died 1816 aged 7, cannot be the 1818 child; its reported age differs by about a year from the 1810 baptism and remains unresolved. Some sibling index birth fields contain malformed years and the 1806 John dates conflict; none is silently corrected. William Glasgow-1533, the circa-1795 Glasgow-born weaver with a Janet Thomson wife, remains a distinct comparison rather than being excluded merely for missing fields: the gamekeeper’s 1841/1851 Crawford household, older age, trade, wife and Isabel chronology provide the positive match. No exact vital replacement, mother attachment, profile merger or upstream father is inferred.

Leadhills companion memorial: age conflict — 4 November 2026The newly inspected Leadhills derivative names William Glasgow, gamekeeper to the Earl of Hopetoun, his spouse Janet Thomson and the same family dates as the held Crawford memorial.

The newly inspected Leadhills derivative names William Glasgow, gamekeeper to the Earl of Hopetoun, his spouse Janet Thomson and the same family dates as the held Crawford memorial. It gives William’s death as 26 April 1853, aged 71; the existing Crawford rendering gives the same date but age 74. These imply approximate births around 1782 and 1779 respectively, not independently recorded birthdays. The matching occupation, spouse, death dates and children identify companion renderings of the same family memorial; they are not independent witnesses and do not resolve the age disagreement. Both readings are retained without changing vital fields.

The new text gives Janet’s death as 1 April 1845, aged 62; calls John, James and George three of her children; says John and James died in infancy; and gives George’s death as 21 July 1816, aged 7. It also commemorates Isabel Glasgow, died 27 November 1881, aged 73, without expressly calling her a daughter. Her daughter identification rests on the existing baptism and household context rather than that literal line alone. The reported surname Thomson does not independently establish maiden-name wording. No new spouse, child or mother is attached.

Leadhills Cemetery is the new provider’s cemetery label; the older source is indexed at Crawford and the inherited biography combined Crawford Old Churchyard and Leadhills Cemetery. These labels do not prove two burial places or establish every commemorated person’s actual interment. The original inscription or photograph remains uninspected and is needed to settle age and cemetery precision.

George Glasgow-638 remains the later child comparison only. His held birth on 8 June 1818 is after the memorial George’s death on 21 July 1816, so he cannot be that deceased child. The existing proposed William Glasgow-2715 father attachment remains probable at 88%, including the unresolved Crawford-child to Leicester-adult identity. No new support percentage is gained from a companion index. The original 1843 Leicester marriage father and occupation fields remain the discriminating test; the older George’s 1810 baptism versus reported age 7 in 1816 also remains unresolved.

Source: Findmypast Leadhills transcript, GBPRS/SCOT/MIS/511339, inspected 4 November 2026; original monument uninspected. Existing Crawford source reused, not recaptured.
