Original military identity test — 31 October 2026The original discharge describes Sergeant John Glasgow of the 1st Battalion Royal Scots, born in the parish and town of Glasgow, Lanark, about thirty-five years old and formerly a labourer.

The original discharge describes Sergeant John Glasgow of the 1st Battalion Royal Scots, born in the parish and town of Glasgow, Lanark, about thirty-five years old and formerly a labourer. He enlisted at eighteen; the service table begins 7 May 1800. The discharge was signed at Belfast on 10 March 1817, for hernia, and recommends Glasgow for his pension. The following sheet contains his signed receipt and the certification that the disability did not arise from misconduct. The later 1st Foot pension register records admission on 26 March 1817, residence Glasgow and death on 12 January 1848.

The regiment and March 1817 pension chronology link the two military originals. The Glasgow birthplace and pensioner status fit John Glasgow-2968, the 1841 Calton army pensioner/burgh officer at 11 Struthers Street with wife Elizabeth. His rounded census age 55 permits an early-1780s birth; the inherited 1786 is not an exact contrary birthday. Glasgow-2875 has the separately held memorial lead for a January 1848 Southern Necropolis burial and Struthers Street residence. The matching street and death month make 2875 and 2968 a particularly strong identity comparison and give a concrete route from the household to the soldier.

The originals name no father, mother, wife or children and give no Struthers Street address. Identification with the Calton household and the memorial profile remains inferred, not a completed merger. The 1848 memorial is a derivative lead without an inspected grave photograph. The 1782 indexed child attached to 2875 remains a separate unproved childhood-to-adult identification: its inspected FamilySearch entry gives only August 1782, Glasgow and no parents. No father is invented or assigned; both live profiles currently have no parents. No relationship percentage, structural attachment, birth field or death field changes, and no confidence-progress gain is counted.
