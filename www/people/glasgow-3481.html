About John

31 October 2026 source review: The detailed Findmypast OPR transcript records John’s baptism on 18 August 1751 at Kirknewton and East Calder, with father John Glasgow, a weaver, residence Bankhead, and mother Christian Meck (the literal indexed spelling). This is the existing John Glasgow-3480 event and John Glasgow-3481 / Christian Meek-3817 household. The useful additional details are the father’s trade and the precise residence. The original register has not been inspected. OPR 689/20, item 1, is the provider reference; its displayed 1642–1737 register range is inconsistent with the 1751 event and requires original/catalogue checking. No birth date is newly established. The MyHeritage citation and Findmypast companion indexes R_686207931 and R_691903896 describe this same baptism and are not independent witnesses. The existing child-to-father assessment remains 98%; John-3481’s selected uncertain William-1026 father remains 23%. No identity merger, parent attachment, maternal regrade, exact vital-date change or confidence gain follows.

Overall conclusion

Reviewed: 2026-10-16

Selected Calder/Irvine working families, 16 October 2026: John of the Calder/Meek household is placed under William-1026 through the 1702 child comparison, uncertain. George of the Irvine Margt household is placed under John-1057 and Agnes Spark through the 1687 child and 1728 couple comparison, both uncertain. Existing dates, recorded-child grades and competing identities remain. Evidence and alternatives.

Glasgow-3490 was merged into Glasgow-3481 as the leading identification of the John Glasgow named as father of Jean at Kirknewton and East Calder in 1730 with the John named with Christian Meek as father of children there in 1751 and at Leith in 1755. The former circa-1730 birth estimate for the 1751 father could not fit the 1730 fatherhood; about 1710 is a generation estimate, not a recorded birth. The 1730 index does not name Jean's mother, so Christian Meek's maternity for Jean and any earlier union remain unproved.

Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026Select William Glasgow-1026 as the uncertain working father of John Glasgow-3481, the combined Calder father account recorded in 1730 and with Christian Meek in 1751–1755.

Select William Glasgow-1026 as the uncertain working father of John Glasgow-3481, the combined Calder father account recorded in 1730 and with Christian Meek in 1751–1755. This adopts possible identity with John Glasgow-1544, the child indexed at Kirknewton in 1702 with father William. The same name and parish and a normal adult generation favour this concrete child account over an unidentified origin: if baptised near birth in 1702, he would be about 28 in 1730 and 53 at the 1755 child. Those ages are conditional examples, not ages stated in the registers. The old circa 1710 birth was inferred from assumed age at the 1730 fatherhood and cannot exclude the 1702 child. The child’s existing father identity as William-1026 is itself qualified; no record explicitly identifies the later Meek husband as his son.

John-1544 and John-3481 remain separate profile accounts of a possible single man; the selection does not establish two distinct John sons. The 1702 date remains unreconciled: the child’s field uses a before 11 November boundary while its biography cites a 29 November baptism. Neither date is newly corrected. The later account combines the 1730 father of Jean, whose mother is omitted, with the 1751 and 1755 Meek household. That earlier adult identification remains a preferred working combination, not a continuous household proved by occupation, land or witnesses. John-81, estimated born 1709, remains a specific competing identity for the 1730 father. His Guthrie household is indexed with children in West Calder in 1751 and 1753 while the Meek household appears in Kirknewton in 1751 and Leith in 1755. That concrete overlap makes a simple succession of wives less persuasive; do not combine the full Guthrie and Meek families without resolving the entries. No birth estimate alone excludes John-81. Other local John origins remain possible. Ann McCook is not selected as John-3481’s mother: the reviewed 1702 child citation names William, and the attached Ann field does not supply an independently verified maternal clause. William’s existing uncertain John-3610 father and older family alternatives retain their grades; no date or family field is changed to force the new route.

The detailed Findmypast OPR transcript records John’s baptism on 18 August 1751 at Kirknewton and East Calder, with father John Glasgow, a weaver, residence Bankhead, and mother Christian Meck (the literal indexed spelling). This is the existing John Glasgow-3480 event and John Glasgow-3481 / Christian Meek-3817 household. The useful additional details are the father’s trade and the precise residence. No birth date is newly established.

The MyHeritage citation and Findmypast companion indexes R_686207931 and R_691903896 describe this same baptism and are not independent witnesses. The existing child-to-father assessment remains 98%; John-3481’s selected uncertain William-1026 father remains 23%. No identity merger, parent attachment, maternal regrade, exact vital-date change or confidence gain follows.

The newly inspected native OPR transcript for Jean in 1730 names father John but supplies no mother, residence or occupation; its 20 November date remains in disagreement with the held FamilySearch 11 November reading. It does not bridge the preferred 1730/1751 adult identity. The 1702 John/William transcript reads 29 November, with no trade or residence; the earlier before-11-November/29-November conflict remains unresolved and no childhood is proved for the Bankhead adult. The 1717 John baptism’s father is a weaver at literal Templtand. That record remains an unselected childhood comparison for Glasgow-2847, not an assigned event for Glasgow-3481. A shared weaving trade supplies context only; Bankhead and Templtand differ and no father/adult identity bridge is established.

Bounded Christian Meek, Meck and Mook marriage searches used marriage year 1730 ±30. The inspected reciprocal 1736 Leith Meek/Meck entries name groom Robert Simson, not John Glasgow; the named fathers James Meck and James Simson, candle makers, cannot be assigned to the Meek wife. No Mook rows were returned; other returned rows were not tested. This is not exhaustive marriage coverage or evidence that no Glasgow marriage exists. The held 1748 Court of Session title “Bankhead v Glasgow and others”, CS237/B/2/25, gives no forename or address and is not identified with this weaver; an exact-reference official-web query returning no result is not archival absence.
