Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026Select the older William Glasgow-1026 as James Glasgow-3526’s uncertain working father.

Select the older William Glasgow-1026 as James Glasgow-3526’s uncertain working father. William’s Kirknewton household is documented by Bessie in 1682 and William in 1705. James is the father-name account behind John’s 1717 Kirknewton/East Calder entry, with a Helen 1714 association held in a collaborative tree. James’s 1688 birth is an unsupported profile estimate. If the rough 1660/1688 generations are close, William would have been about 28 at his birth, and the proposed son falls between William’s already recorded children. This same-area, compatible household generation is preferred provisionally, at low confidence. The combined Kirknewton/East Calder label does not establish the exact historic parish or continuous residence, and no record names William as James’s father.

John-3525’s earlier biography links its father to James Glasgow-1547, whereas the maintained local family uses James-3526. The 1717 index names James, not either modern profile ID. James-1547, associated with Andrew Glasgow-1546 and a 1702 entry, remains a surviving alternative childhood identity for the adult father. If baptised as an infant in 1702 he would be about 15 in 1717 and about 12 at Helen’s tree-held 1714 date. That makes the ordinary older-William model more straightforward, but neither a later childhood baptism nor an erroneous Helen association is excluded. No merge or categorical biological exclusion is selected. Andrew-1546 therefore remains an alternative father through that identity, not a newly attached parent. Andrew-1319 or another older Calder father remains possible. William-3974, the separate 1671 child of Andro, would imply younger fatherhood at a literal 1688 birth; the date estimates alone do not exclude him. Do not undo the split between that child and the older William-1026. The already attached Irish James-1025 may be another account of this James, a relative or an unrelated namesake. Sharing a proposed father does not prove two distinct James sons, and no Scottish-to-Tyrone identity or migration is established. Ann McCook is not selected as James’s mother, and Christian Cook’s attachment to James-1547 is not transferred. James’s wife and the two children’s mothers remain unknown. Helen’s exact 1714 date and father association are tree-held, not an inspected baptism. John’s 1717 record supplies a father name but not a birth or parents for the adult James. The existing child-parent grades, James’s estimated 1688 field, William’s selected uncertain John-3610 father and all older alternatives are preserved.

Selected uncertain family — 16 October 2026James Glasgow-3526 is selected as the uncertain working father of John Glasgow-2847 , husband of Margaret Anderson and father of the West Calder children recorded from 1745 to 1755.

James Glasgow-3526 is selected as the uncertain working father of John Glasgow-2847, husband of Margaret Anderson and father of the West Calder children recorded from 1745 to 1755. The route provisionally identifies that adult with John Glasgow-3525, indexed at neighbouring Kirknewton and East Calder on 31 March 1717 with father James. The parish setting, exact forename and surname, and feasible generation favour this concrete local childhood over an unidentified origin: if baptised near birth in 1717, John would have been about 28 in 1745 and 38 in 1755. The later family also contains a James, although a common repeated forename does not independently establish a grandfather. No register entry yet joins the child to Margaret Anderson’s husband. This is a low-confidence identity-based parent model, not a proved father clause for the adult.

James Glasgow-3526 is the maintained account for the James named in the 1717 record. Its own circa-1688 birth and selected uncertain William Glasgow-1026 father remain prior hypotheses. The child’s earlier biography instead names James Glasgow-1547. That 1702 Kirknewton childhood, son of Andrew Glasgow-1546, remains an alternative identity for the adult James. If baptised near birth in September 1702, he would have been about fourteen at the March 1717 baptism, making that route less straightforward than the older James account. An older-child baptism or an incorrect age estimate remains possible; neither is asserted as fact. The named father James is distinct from the question of which modern James account represents him. The earlier child-parent grade is not upgraded, and neither James’s ancestry receives new certainty.

No mother is selected for the West Calder John. The retained 1717 citation names James but supplies no mother in the existing account. Neither Ann McCook from the older William household nor Christian Cook from James Glasgow-1547’s childhood is transferred into this generation. John’s new proposed father does not identify the later life of his son John Glasgow-2846: that child remains separate from the Larne Rose Allan husband Glasgow-4179 unless their own identity bridge is established. Jean Glasgow-1922’s Kirk marriage and all existing child biographies remain outside the new parent inference.

The West Calder household is represented by Margaret Glasgow-2849 in 1745, John Glasgow-2846 in 1747, James Glasgow-2848 in 1750, and Jean Glasgow-1922 in 1755, with parents John Glasgow and Margaret Anderson. The 1755 citation is West Calder OPR 701,10/202, FR 142; the other retained citations are parish indexes. No original register image has been newly inspected. The present circa-1730 birth and the former biography’s before-1739 wording are unsupported estimates from a child’s event, not recorded ages or a childhood identification. The 1717 hypothesis requires an earlier birth than circa 1730 but does not contradict a measured date. Those structured fields remain unchanged; the hypothetical date must not be presented as a new birth fact. All four children, their mother, their dates and existing relationship grades are retained.

One uncertain father is newly selected through a possible child-to-adult identity. No mother, original inspection, merge, date change, live WikiTree edit or confidence upgrade is claimed. The 1717 child and later father remain separate profile accounts and may be duplicates rather than two distinct brothers. Working connectivity counts retained accounts and inherits the uncertainty in their older ancestry; the family-size gain is not identity evidence.
