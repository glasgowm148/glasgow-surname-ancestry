About Lates

Original register inspected, 31 October 2026: The original register copy of John Glasgow’s will is now inspected: Cumberland County Will Book C, printed pp.161–162, FamilySearch DGS 005534167, images 358–359. It is dated 14 March 1779 and the probate endorsement says 23 September 1779, correcting the earlier reported 3 September. It names wife Ann, daughter Lates, daughter Agnes and son Joseph; Ann is described as with child. The estate is to support wife and children for eight years before sale and distribution, with provision for the unborn child if it survives. Wife Ann and friend Thomas Cellar are appointed executors. James Neely, Joseph Glasgow and Mary Arter witness the will; the witness Joseph is distinct from the named son. This is an inspected register copy, not the separate original estate packet. The register supplies no married name or adult residence for Agnes, and therefore does not identify her as Nancy (Glascoe) Wright. Nancy’s father stays 37% Possible. The record-defined Lates and Joseph father links remain 98%; John’s own James-of-Moneymore father remains 30%. Ann’s maternity of the three already-born children is not expressly stated. No father or mother attachment, confidence percentage, identity merge or vital field is changed. Next test: estate distribution, guardianship or receipts after the eight-year provision, looking for Agnes/Nancy and a Wright husband; do not repeat the completed will-image inspection.

Assess John as their father at 98% each, strongly supported by the explicit abstracted relationship and matching will context. Identity and family

Peters Township family assessment —20 October 2026Fendrick’s checked 1944 abstract of the 1779 Peters Township will identifies John Glasgow’s wife Ann, daughters Lates and Agnes, and son Joseph.

Fendrick’s checked 1944 abstract of the 1779 Peters Township will identifies John Glasgow’s wife Ann, daughters Lates and Agnes, and son Joseph. Lates Glasgow (Glasgow-4028) and Joseph Glasgow (Glasgow-4029) are the existing record-defined daughter and son accounts, not speculative later adult identifications. Assess John as their father at 98% each, strongly supported by the explicit abstracted relationship and matching will context. The manuscript has not been inspected, so this does not certify the spelling or completeness of the abstract. Joseph the son is distinct from the adult Joseph who witnessed the will.

Assess James of Moneymore, James Glasgow II (Glasgow-1024), as John of Peters Township’s probable father at 76%. The 2024 Adams County family account specifically identifies the John who died in 1779 as brother of Robert, Arthur and Joseph. Fendrick’s abstract independently supplies a contemporary Joseph Glasgow witness in John’s Peters Township will; James Neely’s presence also fits the later Neely-associated emigrant family. Faris’s catalogue description identifies Robert’s father as James of Moneymore and reports four emigrant brothers. Together these identify a specific compatible family, rather than only another local James. Extend that probable father across the preferred fourth-brother identification, while rating John lower than the three established brothers because his place in that group depends partly on the later account and witness identification. Assess the John-to-each-established-brother relationship as probable at 83%.

The original will and probate packet remain uninspected. The checked abstract gives 1779, not the execution/probate days; existing 14 March and 3 September date limits are preserved from the earlier cited profile account, not newly verified. The will index verifies John’s locator, Cumberland Will Book C, p.161, but supplies no relatives. Ann is named as wife, not expressly mother of the children; her existing maternal attachments remain unassessed. Nancy (Glascoe) Wright is only a possible identification of Agnes: an approximately 1778 birth fits a child alive in 1779 and Agnes/Nancy can be compatible name forms, but the later Maryland/Indiana household has no inspected parent, guardianship or estate bridge to Peters Township. Her existing John father is therefore possible at 37%, not confident. The witness Joseph is not proved to be Joseph of Wythe County. The family-history article may depend on Faris and is not independent corroboration of his genealogy. The older James-1025 father of James-1024 remains 18%; no Scottish migration, Mary Harkness maternity, new mother, identity merge or live edit follows. Exact percentages express individual subjective judgments, not measured frequencies or multiplied independent probabilities.

Original 1779 will register — 31 October 2026

The original register copy of John Glasgow’s will is now inspected: Cumberland County Will Book C, printed pp.161–162, FamilySearch DGS 005534167, images 358–359. It is dated 14 March 1779 and the probate endorsement says 23 September 1779, correcting the earlier reported 3 September. It names wife Ann, daughter Lates, daughter Agnes and son Joseph; Ann is described as with child. The estate is to support wife and children for eight years before sale and distribution, with provision for the unborn child if it survives. Wife Ann and friend Thomas Cellar are appointed executors. James Neely, Joseph Glasgow and Mary Arter witness the will; the witness Joseph is distinct from the named son. This is an inspected register copy, not the separate original estate packet.

The register supplies no married name or adult residence for Agnes, and therefore does not identify her as Nancy (Glascoe) Wright. Nancy’s father stays 37% Possible. The record-defined Lates and Joseph father links remain 98%; John’s own James-of-Moneymore father remains 30%. Ann’s maternity of the three already-born children is not expressly stated. No father or mother attachment, confidence percentage, identity merge or vital field is changed. Next test: estate distribution, guardianship or receipts after the eight-year provision, looking for Agnes/Nancy and a Wright husband; do not repeat the completed will-image inspection.

[Opening, p.161](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-899B-J7VD) · [Continuation/probate, p.162](https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-899B-J761). Earlier statements that the will manuscript remained uninspected and that probate was 3 September are superseded; the separate packet remains unread.
