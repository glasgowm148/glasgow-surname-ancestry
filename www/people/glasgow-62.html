Danville deposition — 27 October 2026

The original deposition, with its attestation, records James Glasgow and Roderick R. Hudnall at the National Hotel in Danville on 8 April 1840. It belongs to Pittsylvania chancery case 1841-001, James M. Sutherlin and wife against Armistead G. Weir, written “Wier” in the deposition caption. The case’s closing year is not the deposition date.

James says that in 1836 he heard Weir say he had bought fodder from Sutherlin; he thought the quantity was two stacks. He also gives an opinion of a stack’s value. The page bears a signature reading James Glasgow. This is witness testimony about another man’s purchase, not evidence that James bought or sold the fodder. Hudnall’s separate answer follows; it must not be attributed to James.

The name and court locality fit this James, whose held profile cites an 1840 Pittsylvania census, but the manuscript supplies no age, occupation, residence, spouse or parent to establish the match. Retain Glasgow-62 as a compatible candidate. The separate Virginia-born James M. Glasgow-193 and James William Glasgow-2925 have held Ohio and Tennessee household accounts; those locations alone do not exclude a temporary Danville appearance. No duplicate person or new family connection is proposed.

A notice dated 6 April 1840 names James Glasgow and Roderick R. Hudnall and schedules their depositions for Wednesday the 8th at the National Hotel in Danville. It corroborates the date and venue of the already inspected testimony; it is not a second May appearance and adds no family identifier.
