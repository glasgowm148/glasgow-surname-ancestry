Other research notes

Danville deposition — 27 October 2026

The original deposition, with its attestation, records James Glasgow and Roderick R. Hudnall at the National Hotel in Danville on 8 April 1840. It belongs to Pittsylvania chancery case 1841-001, James M. Sutherlin and wife against Armistead G. Weir, written “Wier” in the deposition caption. The case’s closing year is not the deposition date.

James says that in 1836 he heard Weir say he had bought fodder from Sutherlin; he thought the quantity was two stacks. He also gives an opinion of a stack’s value. The page bears a signature reading James Glasgow. This is witness testimony about another man’s purchase, not evidence that James bought or sold the fodder. Hudnall’s separate answer follows; it must not be attributed to James.

The name and court locality fit this James, whose held profile cites an 1840 Pittsylvania census, but the manuscript supplies no age, occupation, residence, spouse or parent to establish the match. Retain Glasgow-62 as a compatible candidate. The separate Virginia-born James M. Glasgow-193 and James William Glasgow-2925 have held Ohio and Tennessee household accounts; those locations alone do not exclude a temporary Danville appearance. No duplicate person or new family connection is proposed.

A notice dated 6 April 1840 names James Glasgow and Roderick R. Hudnall and schedules their depositions for Wednesday the 8th at the National Hotel in Danville. It corroborates the date and venue of the already inspected testimony; it is not a second May appearance and adds no family identifier.

Across the initial and follow-up batches, 84 of the case’s 89 scans were visually reviewed. Images 46, 47, 57, 58 and 61 remain unread after rendering failures; faint or damaged writing elsewhere further limits negative conclusions. A background-only extraction from image 46 was not counted as a reading. Suspected Glasgow readings on images 78–80 were checked against the images and rejected as Haynes or Sutherlin. No complete-case absence claim is made. The 1818 original bond has since been inspected (30 October review below). Signature comparison and the separate 1815 family-identification test remain open.

Pittsylvania death and parent-claim review — 23 October 2026The inspected USGenWeb 1860 Pittsylvania death transcription records James Glasgow, cooper, age 71 years 10 months 12 days, Pittsylvania birthplace, cause “killed,” and Susan Glasgow as informant…

The inspected USGenWeb 1860 Pittsylvania death transcription records James Glasgow, cooper, age 71 years 10 months 12 days, Pittsylvania birthplace, cause “killed,” and Susan Glasgow as informant and wife. Its parent column and exact death-date cell are blank. This is a modern transcription of county returns; the original register was not inspected. The existing 28 September 1860 date comes from the previously cited death index, FHL 2048580. Combining that date with the transcribed age yields 16 November 1788; this is a calculated birthday, not independent birth evidence, so the approximate birth field is retained.

The marriage-bond compilation, printed p. 101/PDF p. 104, visually checked 23 October, gives James Glasgow/Susanna High, 21 October 1818, with Freeman High bondsman and no James parent. Printed p. 83/PDF p. 86 lists Robert Glasgow as bondsman for the 22 December 1815 Eudaley/Glasgow marriage. The original review below supersedes that role: Herod Worsham was surety, while Robert Glasgow signed a separate age-and-willingness declaration. The declaration remains a local associated lead, not an established father or proved brother/sister link. The compilation is not the original bond. The 1818 original has since been inspected, as described in the 30 October source review below; the 1815 family-identification test remains open.

A contributed Ancestry aggregation names Robert Glasgow 1760–1839 and Sarah Cooper 1763–1853 as parents, without an inspected supporting record. No named parent is selected from this claim. Robert-1530 has matching years but is a documented Glasgow weaver with Jack/Brash marriages; he is not identified by a year match. Robert-12, the Ohio immigrant with different wives, remains unconnected to this household. Robert-395 is specifically inconsistent: his son James was already dead in a 1837 newspaper account, whereas this cooper lived to 1860. William-357 and his Sarah Cooper 1756–1812 are a different Currituck couple; neither can be renamed or imported to fill this gap. The 1815 bond may help identify a local family, but does not establish the contributed Robert/Sarah couple.

Original Hendrick–Eudaly packet — 31 October 2026The original Pittsylvania bond of 1 February 1819 names Nathaniel Hendrick and William Glasgo as obligors for Nathaniel’s licence to marry Elizabeth Eudaly.

The original Pittsylvania bond of 1 February 1819 names Nathaniel Hendrick and William Glasgo as obligors for Nathaniel’s licence to marry Elizabeth Eudaly. Both names appear in the signature entries; William’s surname there is written Glasgow. The associated declaration, dated 30 January 1819 in Elizabeth Eudaly’s name, states that she has no objection to Nathaniel obtaining a marriage licence to marry her. William Glasgo is named under the witness heading. The matching endorsement reads Hendrick, marriage bond, to the Governor.

These originals confirm the previously indexed William association and add the bride’s dated declaration. They name no parent or relationship between William, Elizabeth and James Glasgow-62. The 1819 bride is a specific possible continuation of the Elizabeth Glasgow who obtained a licence with Robert Eudaley in this county in December 1815: the given name, unusual married surname and continuing Glasgow associates fit. Neither packet calls her a widow or recites the earlier marriage, so that identity remains a candidate, not a proved remarriage or evidence of Robert’s death.   14. James and William Glasgow recall Little Fall Creek neighbours and tenancy

    1851-10-01 · Exchange Hotel, Danville, Pittsylvania County, Virginia, United States · Original inspected; named deponents and retrospective neighbourhood evidence; profile identity qualified

    The original depositions taken at the Exchange Hotel in Danville on 1 October 1851 name James Glasgow and William Glasgow. James says he was a neighbour of Leonard Fitzgerald in 1821 and recalls renting part of the Little Fall Creek property around 1826 through an agent. William separately says he was Fitzgerald’s neighbour in 1821 and thinks Fitzgerald remained there in 1827, while expressly not being positive. Both signatures appear. The testimony gives neither Glasgow man’s age, occupation, spouse or parents and does not call the two men relatives.   16. Nathaniel Hendrick / Elizabeth Eudaly licence bond; William Glasgo surety and declaration witness. Associated-family research context only for James Glasgow-62.

    1819-02-01 · Pittsylvania County, Virginia, United States · Original documentary roles confirmed; cross-record identities and James kinship unresolved

    The original Pittsylvania bond of 1 February 1819 names Nathaniel Hendrick and William Glasgo as obligors for Nathaniel’s licence to marry Elizabeth Eudaly. Both names appear in the signature entries; William’s surname there is written Glasgow. The associated declaration, dated 30 January 1819 in Elizabeth Eudaly’s name, states that she has no objection to Nathaniel obtaining a marriage licence to marry her. William Glasgo is named under the witness heading. The matching endorsement reads Hendrick, marriage bond, to the Governor. These originals confirm the previously indexed William association and add the bride’s dated declaration. They name no parent or relationship between William, Elizabeth and James Glasgow-62. The 1819 bride is a specific possible continuation of the Elizabeth Glasgow who obtained a licence with Robert Eudaley in this county in December 1815: the given name, unusual married surname and continuing Glasgow associates fit. Neither packet calls her a widow or recites the earlier marriage, so that identity remains a candidate, not a proved remarriage or evidence of Robert’s death. A non-expert visual comparison of the 1819 and 1851 William signatures is compatible in broad cursive forms, but not diagnostic; the surety is not identified as the Little Fall Creek deponent or with any particular existing William profile. Existing William-3678 and William-563 remain weak unselected comparisons; their separate Wythe and Ohio accounts supply no specific Pittsylvania bridge. James’s father remains Unknown and unrated.
