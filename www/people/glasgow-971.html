Summary of the research

Overall conclusion

Current father assessment (22 October 2026): 97% (proved) for Glasgow-1348. Recorded 1792 child and Smith household; John’s own disputed childhood and Alexander’s later occupation remain separate. This scoped assessment supersedes earlier grades for this parent pair; other identity questions remain as qualified below.

The 22 October 2026 Findmypast review below records the matched events and qualified comparisons, preserving existing vital dates and relationship assessments. No new person or parent attachment is proposed.

Alexander Glasgow of Old Court, County Cork, formerly of Auchenraith, Lanarkshire, died at Old Court on 19 October 1873; the printed Irish probate calendar records his will proved at Cork on 10 December 1873 by Robert Pirrie, one of the executors, with effects under £80,000. A burial index records him at Douglas on 24 October 1873, and an indexed Greenock death notice gives his age as 81. Marriage notices of 1853 and 1855 call Jessy, wife of Edward Eyre Newenham, his only daughter and William James Glasgow his only son. His profile's birth in 1792 and 1826 marriage to Ellen Smith have not yet been checked against records.

  • New: burial. Ffolliott collection, Douglas parish (Cork & Ross), burial of Alexander Glasgow, 24 October 1873 (IRE/FFOLLIOTT/REG/145696).
  • New: estate notice naming the executor and date of death. Cork Constitution, 9 January 1874, p.1 (BL/0001648/18740109/009, OCR read): statutory notice to creditors of "Alexander Glasgow, late of Oldcourt, in the county of Cork, Esquire, deceased, who died on the 19th day of October, 1873"; claims to the solicitor for "Robert Pirrie, the executor", probate granted in the Cork District Registry. The Original Will Register entry (Cork District Will Books, May 1873–Dec 1874, images 007604247_00158–00166, IRE/ORIG/WILL/REG/15268) indexes the names associated with the grant as "Robert Shan", "Edward Eyre", "Robert Pissie" and "John Pinie" — probably Robert Shaw, Edward Eyre [Newenham], Robert Pirrie and John Pirrie, but these readings need the image; the will itself was not read.
  • New: death notices in Scotland. Greenock Advertiser, 23 October 1873 (index FN-D-2F174051-..., article BL/0000970/18731023/002) is indexed with death 19 October 1873 at Cork, aged 81 (index fields; page OCR not read); North British Daily Mail, 21 October 1873 (BL/0002683/18731021/066); Cork Constitution, 21 October 1873 (BL/0001648/18731021/002; snippet: "at his residence, Old Court, county Cork, Alexander Glasgow, Esq."). Also indexed: Death Duty Register index, 1873, IR 27/385 (BMD/D/DDUTY/1357936); Great Western Railway shareholder register, death 19 October 1873 (GBOR/GWR/00051801).
  • New: widow's death. Western Morning News, 22 December 1873, p.1 (BL/0000329/18731222/001, OCR read): "At Old Court, county of [Cork] ... widow of the late Alexander Glasgow, in her 79th year." Her forename and the day are illegible in the OCR. The age (born about 1794–95) is compatible with Ellen Smith, but the notice as read does not name her.
  • New: only son and only daughter in contemporary notices. Southern Reporter, 2 August 1855, p.2 (BL/0000876/18550802/060): on 31 July at Monkstown Church, "William James Glasgow, Esq., only son of Alexander Glasgow, Esq., of Old Court, to Dora, eldest daughter of Bernard H. Shaw, Esq., of Monkstown." Dublin Evening Mail, 10 June 1853, p.3 (BL/0000433/18530610/043): 7 June at Douglas, Edward Eyre Newenham of Maryborough to "Jessy, only daughter of Alexander Glasgow, Esq., of Old Court, county of Cork, late of Auchenraith, Lanarkshire." The 1855 notice conflicts with the profile's third child, John: by 1855 William James was the only living son, so John, if he existed, had probably died. The 1853 notice directly links the Cork man to Auchenraith.
  • New (context): Old Court gate-lodge was attacked by Fenians seeking arms in mid-March 1868 (the reports differ between Sunday night and Monday morning), "the residence of Alexander Glasgow, Esq., a magistrate" (Dublin Evening Mail, 19 March 1868, BL/0000433/18680319/042, and widely reprinted). Directories: Henry & Coughlan 1867, Douglas (IRE/IET0012/367/017); Slater 1870, Cork (IRE/IET0100/0646/054).
  • Not a record of him: Findmypast's "Irish Newspaper Marriage Notices" entry for Alexander Glasgow, Cork Examiner, 20 November 1854 (FN-M-DE3EE6D2-...) is a misindexed one-guinea subscription to the South Infirmary, not a marriage.
  • New Zealand (family, not him): Lyttelton Times, 29 November 1889 (BL/0003346/18891129/038): Francis Bernard, "fourth son of the late William James Glasgow, of Old Court, County Cork, Ireland, and Brooklands, Nelson, New Zealand", married Marisa Victorine Malfroy at Hokitika on 20 November 1889. Landed Estates Court sale notices of 1891 (e.g. Irish Times, 27 March 1891, BL/0001683/18910327/149) name Robert Pirrie as administrator of William James Glasgow, deceased, in the Newenham estate case. No New Zealand record of Alexander himself was found (Findmypast NZ index: only an 1874 birth and 1880 marriage of other Alexanders).
Source findings — 1873 probate calendar entry, 10 December 1873

The printed Irish Calendar of Wills and Administrations for 1873 (right-hand page of the pp. 246–247 opening; an image of the opening is kept in the owner's old research archive) reads: "GLASGOW Alexander. 10 December. The Will of Alexander Glasgow late of Old Court County Cork Esquire deceased who died 19 October 1873 at same place was proved at Cork by the oath of Robert Pirrie of West George-street Glasgow Esquire one of the Executors. Effects under £80,000."

Seen as an image of the printed calendar, read by on-device OCR and checked against the column layout. Grade: proved for the death date and place, the grant at Cork on 10 December 1873 and Robert Pirrie's role as one of the executors. The calendar gives £80,000 as the upper limit of the sworn effects; it does not support the captured biography's statement that Alexander left £80,000 to Robert Pirrie. The will itself has not been read, so the legatees are unknown.

Inspected source review — 31 October 2026The 1935 printed Glasgow burgess register has been visually inspected.

The 1935 printed Glasgow burgess register has been visually inspected. On 14 September 1795 John Glasgow, merchant, entered as husband of Helen, lawful daughter of James Smith, mason and burgess/guild brother (printed p.197, PDF 207). On 1 July 1817 Alexander Glasgow, merchant in Wilson’s Court, Argyle Street, entered as eldest son of John Glasgow, merchant and burgess/guild brother (p.306, PDF 316). On 3 May 1844 John Glasgow, merchant, entered as a younger son of John Glasgow, merchant and burgess/guild brother (p.450, PDF 460). The first entry strongly identifies the maintained John-1348/Helen Smith household; the sons are supported comparisons with Alexander-971 and John-1355. The sons’ entries do not name their mother, and the three admissions must not be treated as a literal statement that all three men form one family. These are inspected published register transcriptions; the underlying manuscript admission registers are unread.

Burke’s Visitation, vol.2, printed armorial section p.67 (PDF 341), has now been read in the scan. It explicitly describes Alexander of Old Court, formerly Auchinraith, as eldest son of the late John Glasgow and Helen, daughter of James Smith, and grandson of Alexander Glasgow, merchant, and Agnes Monro of Edinburgh. The family passage was already quoted in the held biography; inspecting it is not discovery of a previously unknown grandfather name. The underlying Lyon matriculation remains unread.

The FamilySearch citation X17D-67M was checked directly: John was born 13 October 1771 and christened 19 October 1771 at Barony, parents Alexander Glasgow and Mary Munro. The copied 3 December 1765 date attached to that same citation is wrong; no independent 1765 record was verified in this review. This corrects the source account, not the vital fields or later identity of either modern John profile.

The independently identified John–Helen Smith merchant household now has inspected burgess evidence, and Burke explicitly gives its Alexander grandfather with a Monro wife associated with Edinburgh. That rare paired family description is more specific than the earlier chronological comparison with Alexander-1309’s Alexander/Mary Munro household, whose 1757 marriage was recorded in Edinburgh and Barony. The corrected 1771 Barony child fits the merchant’s possible generation. This modestly strengthens the whole existing father-profile hypothesis from 38% to 45% Possible; it does not make it Probable. Burke names Agnes Monro, not the documented Mary Munro of Alexander-1309’s couple. Their identity cannot be assumed, and the 1771 child has no inspected occupation, wife or estate bridge identifying him as the merchant rather than the Colinton account John-1304. The merchant’s circa 1770 birth is an inherited estimate, not independent corroboration. Retain both modern Johns and the current father selection, all mothers, vital fields and upstream grades. No new father attachment or merge follows. The 1741 childhood identification of Alexander-1309 is not certified by this review. Exact percentages are subjective judgements, not measured probabilities.
