Source findings — UCL Legacies of British Slavery summary, copied 10 March 2025

The UCL LBS entry for Robert Glasgow late Robertson, as copied into the owner's earlier notes, gives his dates as 1775 to 27 January 1845 and reports, from an e-mail of Dr Anthony J. Cooke (9 February 2011), that he died at Pau, France, in 1845, his inventory being NRS SC70/1/69/533. It also records an Edinburgh MD (1797) and FRCS (1802), his marriage to Anne Glasgow on 2 August 1804, four children: Robert the heir, Philadelphia Jane, Anne and Charlotte Marie Cecilia, and an 1841 census household at Holly Walk, Leamington Priors, that includes Charlotte Robertson, aged 10.

Seen as: a copied catalogue summary, not inspected originals; the 1841 household is quoted from that summary. A death in January 1845 agrees with the son's succession in 1845 and with 'the late Robert Glasgow of Montgreenan' in September 1847. The named daughters Anne and Charlotte Marie Cecilia support his identification as father of the 1847 brides. Next record: NRS SC70/1/69/533.

Recorded in archive/dump/TheGlasgows.md (owner's checklist export of 10 March 2025); carried into this file on 7 October 2026.

Identity and family

Earlier conclusion — newspaper identity review, 6 October 2026
  • Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser, 1847-09-02, p.4: the late Robert Glasgow, Esq., of Montgreenan, county Ayr (father of both brides) — strong match. Unique designation 'of Montgreenan', described as 'the late' in Sept 1847. Of the Montgreenan owners: Dr Robert Glasgow-1076 died in 1827. Wikipedia says he had no offspring, but his own findings record natural children, including Anne, wife of Robertson-30564, so that is not a ground for exclusion; he remains a weaker possible alternative (corrected 7 October 2026; see research/Glasgow-1076/findings.md). Robert Robertson of Prenderguest, son-in-law, inherited in 1828 and took the name Glasgow; the LBS claim names 'Robert Glasgow Esq. (late Robertson) and Anne his wife'. His son Robert Robertson-Glasgow inherited in 1845 and lived to 1860 (thepeerage), so he cannot be 'the late' in 1847. Only Robertson-30564 survives. Sources: Wikipedia: Montgreenan; UCL LBS claim 15 Feb 1836; thepeerage p67979

Record findings and identity limits — 17 October 2026Paisley double marriage, 1847A notice published on 2 September 1847 reports two marriages at Paisley on the 25th ultimo, interpreted as 25 August.

A notice published on 2 September 1847 reports two marriages at Paisley on the 25th ultimo, interpreted as 25 August. George Sherbrooke Airey, Royal Navy lieutenant and son of the late Lieutenant-General Sir George Airey, married Anne, second daughter of the late Robert Glasgow. At the same time Rev George Drake, son of Rev John Drake, rector of Stourton, married Charlotte Maria Cecilia, youngest daughter of the late Robert Glasgow of Montgreenan, Ayr. No mother is named. (Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser, 2 September 1847, p. 4, marriages.)

The combination of the Montgreenan designation, Robert Robertson’s later Glasgow surname, death in 1845, and daughters Anne and Charlotte Marie Cecilia supports identification with this family. Charlotte’s distinctive full name and Drake married surname are especially strong. The newspaper directly states its father and spouse relationships; the profile assignments remain qualified and no mother is inferred.

Compatible family profiles are Robert Robertson, later Glasgow, Anne Glasgow, Charlotte Marie Cecilia Glasgow and Rev George Drake.

Located census transcript and daughter-in-law comparison — 31 October 2026The Holly Walk household now has a precise census locator: HO107/1135, enumeration district 10, folio 11, page 15, Leamington Priors (FreeCEN district Kenilworth).

The Holly Walk household now has a precise census locator: HO107/1135, enumeration district 10, folio 11, page 15, Leamington Priors (FreeCEN district Kenilworth). FreeCEN transcribes Mary Glasgow 19 and Wilhelmina Robertson 1, both born Scotland, with Robert Glasgow 65. It gives no relationship labels. This may be the source of the old copied WikiTree paragraph, so it is one census record, not independent family testimony.

A specific daughter-in-law alternative can now be tested. Paterson’s 1866 county history, printed pp. 513–514 (PDF 249–250), says the elder Robert’s eldest son and heir, born 22 May 1811, married Mary-Wilhelmina, daughter of John Campbell of Stonefield, on 11 July 1839; their children include Wilhelmina-Colquhoun. The checked existing bride is Campbell-38211 and groom Robertson-Glasgow-11. A married Mary beside the infant of this same family could be that daughter-in-law rather than another daughter of the household head. This is a new comparison, not an established identification.

The age problem remains real: FreeCEN gives Mary 19 in 1841, while Campbell-38211’s public profile gives 1816 and copies an 1851 age 34. The latter original is also unread. The infant’s full name and birth particulars are not established by FreeCEN. Paterson is a compiled family history, not an original marriage entry, and its adjacent Montgreenan descent narrative has known conflicts with the separately held court evidence. Do not transfer the book’s explicit John Campbell parentage to Glasgow-3021 before identifying the two Marys. Retain the selected Robert Robertson father at 45%, Jane’s father at 78%, all mother/vital fields and separate profiles; no father replacement, merge or confidence improvement is recorded.
