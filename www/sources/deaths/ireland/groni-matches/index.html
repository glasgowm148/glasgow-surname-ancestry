Coverage, survival & limitations

Irish civil births, marriages and deaths

Civil indexes and images online cover births 1864–1925; marriages 1845–1950 (non-Roman Catholic from 1845, Roman Catholic from 1864); deaths generally 1871–1975, with earlier death material being added or patchy. An index hit is not itself an inspected register image.

Reading the evidence: an index, transcript, provider search page and original image are different sources. A recorded image link does not mean its reading or identity match is confirmed. Inspect the assessment and source page before changing a family relationship. Repository guides checked 4 October 2026.

Civil-death index match review

Northern Ireland Glasgow death entries

A user-supplied transcription of GRONI death-search results records 152 distinct entries. All 147 non-Robert references have been verified in live WikiTree biographies: 72 matched to existing profiles, 22 cited as research leads on existing profiles, and 53 on newly created profiles. The five Robert death entries are handled separately.

The review cross-references 307 supplied births and 102 marriages. Fifteen newly profiled children have matching birth entries, including George Glasgow of Cookstown and Mary Glasgow of Belfast; their profiles use the indexed birth dates. The three Armagh children whose birth entries name Hewitt as their mother's maiden surname are linked to Helen (Hewitt) Glasgow, spouse of William Glasgow. This supersedes the former Unknown mother placeholder, Hewitt-9718, which was merged into Hewitt-8271.

In Belfast, Sarah born in 1889 and Sarah born in 1891 are linked to their Mitchell mother. Their births share the mother's maiden surname and district; the later Sarah was born after the first died. Adam and Samuel are linked to their Moore mother on the same birth-index evidence.

The stated death dates and ages are index details; age-derived birth years are approximate. A registration district does not establish an exact death address. Paid certificates were not consulted. When multiple death entries compete for one identity, the biography cites them without assigning both as that person's death.

William Glasgow, David Glasgow and John Glasgow each have two cited death-index leads that cannot both be their death. Their structured death fields remain open. The William and David returns rank among the most useful full records to view because an informant, occupation or precise address could separate the same-name men.

Ann Glasgow's 14 March 1877 entry is cited on Anne (Cushnehan) Glasgow. The 1890 Rosetta entry is cited on Rose Ann Glasgow as a possible identity, and the 1867 Margaret entry is cited on Margaret's profile while preserving its more specific place evidence.
