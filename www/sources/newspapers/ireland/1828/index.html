James Glasgow

No. 32 Rosemary Street, Belfast (former business premises)Birth unknown

A shop and stores were described as formerly occupied by Mr James Glasgow; the leasehold was advertised for sale in 1828.

Published 1828-04-01 · Belfast News-Letter · p. 3

What the notice says

An auction notice describes the large shop and stores at No.32 Rosemary Street, Belfast, as formerly occupied by Mr James Glasgow. The lease interest was offered for sale on 1 April 1828; the notice dated 21 March gives 34 years unexpired from 1 November next and £30 late Irish currency yearly rent.

Jas. Glasgow

BroughshaneBirth unknown

Listed for proficiency in the business of the Junior Division class at Belfast Institution College Department awards.

James Glasgow

Belfast Institution (Logic and Belles Lettres section)Birth unknown

Named for general eminence during the session in daily examinations and composition of exercises on class subjects.

Published 1828-05-09 · Belfast News-Letter · p. 3

What the notice says

At the Belfast Institution College Department awards on 2 May 1828, Jas. Glasgow of Broughshane was listed for proficiency in the business of the Junior Division class. Separately, the Logic and Belles Lettres section named James Glasgow for general eminence during the session in daily examinations and composition of exercises on class subjects. The notice does not establish whether these two entries refer to one student or two.

Evidence and identity limits

James Glasgow, Institution student / assistant and Castledawson minister, 1828–1848: The Institution mathematics/student/assistant sequence, Castledawson call and ordination, and Indian missionary family notices converge strongly on the checked career of James McClure Glasgow-1780. The current biography’s 6 October 1835 call is corrected locally to ordination; the call had been reported by 10 July. No parent or sibling relationship is upgraded.
