Published 1838-07-27 · Freeman’s Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser · p. 3

What the notice says

Under Antrim Assizes, Jane Glasgow is named as the mother of the deceased Anne Armstrong. Euphemia Armstrong is named as mother of John Armstrong, who was married to Anne. The report says Anne died on 23 June 1838 at printed Bally-cluck; the manslaughter defendant William M’Gowan was found not guilty.

Evidence and identity limits

The original report directly establishes these named relationships, the printed locality and the acquittal. It does not give ages, Jane’s spouse or residence, or Anne’s birth surname. Retain Bally-cluck as printed. The checked catalogue supports compatible but unproved Jane Glasgow comparisons McClure-6341 and Glasgow-1779; neither is selected or attached. No concrete candidate was located for Anne, John or Euphemia.

At roughly 65 in 1838, she remains an age- and region-compatible possible mother candidate. The report does not identify her as McClure-6341 or name Anne among the listed children; the unmarked family links are leads, not corroboration. Do not attach or change relationship certainty.

Why this candidate?

The public profile and local dossier identify Jane (Glasgow) Armstrong, born about 1799 in County Antrim, as daughter of John Glasgow and Jane McClure. That name, region and chronology permit consideration as Anne’s mother, but the profile reports her death in 1837, based on a Racavan burying-ground memorial transcription, before the newspaper’s 1838 report. Retain as a qualified alternative because that profile chronology/source identification is not independently resolved; do not attach or upgrade a relationship.

Place not statedBirth unknown

signed a parishioners’ request for a meeting at the Corn Exchange Rooms on 27 November 1838 to arrange collection for O’Connell’s Annuity

Published 1838-11-26 – 1838-11-27 · Freeman's Journal and Daily Commercial Advertiser · p. 1

What the notice says

A request by parishioners of St Andrew’s parish in Dublin, including St Mark’s, St Peter’s and St Anne’s, sought a meeting at the Corn Exchange Rooms at 3 p.m. on 27 November 1838 to arrange collection for O’Connell’s Annuity. J. Glasco signed the appeal.

Evidence and identity limits

This proves a signatory association with the appeal only. It does not establish occupation, age, family, residence, contribution amount or attendance. The 27 November issue is a reprint of the same planned event (“THIS DAY”), not proof of a second act. The 26 November issue says the planned meeting is tomorrow, 27 November. It repeats the same appeal, not a second act.
