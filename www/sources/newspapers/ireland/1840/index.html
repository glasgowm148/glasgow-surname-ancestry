What the notice says

Rev. Adam D. Glasgow receives a unanimous call to Berry Street Presbyterian Church, Belfast, in connection with the General Synod of Ulster.

Evidence and identity limits

The repeated name and institutional setting support the education/teaching continuity; the ministerial continuation and attribution to Adam Dickie Glasgow-1781 remain qualified. The newspaper does not spell out Dickie or identify parents. 5 March is the presentation date, not a proved ordination date. Identity review (6 October 2026): Belfast News-Letter, 17 Jan 1840: Rev. Adam D. Glasgow gets a unanimous call to Berry Street Presbyterian Church, Belfast. The NZ Presbyterian register independently names Adam Dickie Glasgow as minister of Berry Street, so the subject is Glasgow-1781 (raised to strong).

Who this may be

Adam Dickie Glasgow (Glasgow-1781)strong
[b. c. 1815; d. 1863-03-18] · minister called to Berry Street, Belfast
Why this candidate?

NZ Presbyterian register entry for Adam Dickie Glasgow (b. 1815 Clough, Co. Antrim): minister of Berry Street Presbyterian Church, Belfast; 15 years at Rajkot, India; wife Jane Thompson of Co. Down (b. 1822); brother Dr James Glasgow, missionary and professor. The notice prints the initial D and the congregation Berry Street. Name, initial, unique congregation and period all agree. No other Rev. Adam Glasgow in the catalogue fits: the other Adams (3902, 1622, 3373, 2959, 2605, 3151, 1395, 1405, 2419 etc.) are farmers, children or emigrants.

Evidence and identity limits
