What the notice says

At an entertainment for Robert L. Rogers on retiring as teacher of elocution, his class met at the Royal Temperance Hotel, Waring Street, Belfast, and during the session in Rev. Dr. Cooke’s Church. On Thursday 8 April 1841, the Rev. A. J. Glasgow spoke on elocution’s importance for candidates for the sacred ministry.

Evidence and identity limits

Rev. A. J. Glasgow, Belfast speech (1841): Prefer Adam Dickie Glasgow-1781 tentatively: his actual biography places the Presbyterian minister in Belfast before India/New Zealand. Printed A. J. versus documented Adam D. remains a real initial conflict, not a silent correction. 1780 is strongly disfavoured by Bombay arrival in February 1841 and family presence there through May, including his daughter’s 16 March birth. A letter signed James Glasgow and dated Rajkote, 14 August 1841, reports on fellow missionary Alexander Kerr. “Brother missionary” is occupational language here, not evidence of sibling relationship.

Birth estimate / source

Rev. ministerial role documented by 1836; estimated age 18+ at that role, not stated by the source and not a sourced birth date.

Occupation / trade

Published 1841-10-19 · Belfast News-Letter · p. 2

What the notice says

Under “GENERAL ASSEMBLY’S FOREIGN MISSION—SICKNESS AND DEATH OF THE REV. A. KERR,” the newspaper says a communication from Rev. Mr. Glasgow was read about fellow missionary Alexander Kerr. The letter is dated “Rajkote, 14th August, 1841” and is signed “James Glasgow.” In this context “brother missionary” means colleague/fellow worker, not a biological brother. A separate second letter dated 18 August begins below the signature; its author/signature is outside this crop and should not be attributed by adjacency.
