Published 1861-01-07 · Belfast News-Letter · p. 4

Additional excerpts from this issue — 1

What the notice says

Shocking catastrophe at Rasharkin: Robt. Glasgow, described as a neighbour at Lisnagaver, warned James M’Caughern about a dangerous gable before it fell; Glasgow narrowly escaped while leaving the doorway and raised the alarm.

Evidence and identity limits

Genuine Glasgow surname in a local incident. Robert-938 and Robert-3449 remain compatible candidates; no age, parents, occupation or link to a valuation holding is stated. A later Scottish death does not exclude Robert-938 being in Ireland in 1861. Identity review (6 October 2026): Accident report (Belfast News-Letter, 7 Jan 1861) from Rasharkin: Robert Glasgow, a neighbour at Lisnagaver, warned James M'Caughern about a dangerous gable, escaped its fall and raised the alarm. Robert is probably Glasgow-938, who lived at Lisnagaver in 1851 and 1866. James M'Caughern is unresolved. No kin are stated.

Who this may be

Robert Glasgow (Glasgow-938)probable
[b. about 1840–1841; d. 1902-11-27] · neighbour at Lisnagaver
Why this candidate?

Glasgow-938 (b. c.1840) was in his father Alexander's Lisnagaver household in the 1851 census (age 10), and his 5 Apr 1866 civil marriage calls him a bachelor farmer of Lisnagaver. Lisnagaver residence is documented on both sides of 1861. Competitors: Glasgow-3449 (b. c.1797, married labourer, died in Ballymoney Workhouse 1868) is linked to Lisnagaver only by an unproved 1834 tithe candidacy; Robert Irvine Glasgow-3897 (b. 1827) belongs to Daniel's Kilrea family. Probable, not strong: no age or father is given.

James McCaughern (McCaughern-22)possible
[b. c. 1800; d. ?] · householder whose gable fell
Why this candidate?

WikiTree has McCaughern-22 (James, farmer, father of John McCaughern of Gortereghy, 1851 Rasharkin marriage; probable 1834 Gortereghy tithe occupier) and Mc_Caughern-19 (James, 1824-1889, Ballydonnelly, Rasharkin; Ancestry-tree source only). Robert-938 later held a house at Gortereghy (1864 revision), which slightly favours McCaughern-22. Possible only. Robert-938's mother Mary McCaughan has a similar surname, but no kinship is stated.

What the notice says

Tin Tenement, Main Street, and Monrush town parks for sale at Cookstown; apply to Mr William Glasgow, Cookstown; notice dated 15 August 1861.

Evidence and identity limits

Unassigned same-name contact. No kinship or proof that William owned the entire property. Identity review (6 October 2026): Sale notice (Belfast News-Letter, 19 Aug 1861) for the Tin Tenement, Main Street and Monrush town parks, Cookstown: apply to Mr William Glasgow, Cookstown. The contact is either the retired draper Glasgow-3814 or the Drapersfield partner William; it cannot be decided, and no kin are stated.

Who this may be

William Glasgow (Glasgow-3814)possible
[b. c. 1796; d. 1883-07-04] · contact for property sale
Why this candidate?

A Cookstown William in 1861 acting for town property. Glasgow-3814 (retired merchant and draper, in Cookstown until his 1883 death at Loy) fits, but so does the Drapersfield partner William (unprofiled, or the same as 3814). Killycurragh farmers 1737/1694 are weaker. The notice does not say William owned the property.
