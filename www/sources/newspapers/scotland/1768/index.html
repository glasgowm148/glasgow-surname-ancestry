Published 1768-02-06 · The Caledonian Mercury · p. 2

What the notice says

William Glasgow is named as master of the Lady Forbes in the Sutton shipping casualty report.

Evidence and identity limits

The 6 February Caledonian report names William Glasgow master of Lady Forbes, loaded with rum for Waterford, driven ashore near Sutton, crew saved. It describes the same named master/vessel/grounding as the 23 January Freeman’s Journal report in different wording; this is a curated same-event grouping, not an exact-text reprint. Belonging to Glasgow describes the vessel, not personal residence or origin. Identity review (6 October 2026): Jan/Feb 1768: William Glasgow, master of the Lady Forbes (of Glasgow) with rum for Waterford, driven ashore near Sutton, crew saved. No WikiTree William can be singled out.

George Glasgow

Glasgow (merchant designation); lands at Kilwinning; sale at Irvineb. by c. 1750 adult estimate

described as a Glasgow merchant · advertised for roup sale lands at Kilwinning including Nether-Mains, Nethermains Dean and Nethermains Niven · sale arranged at James Shaw’s, Irvine, on the second Tuesday of July

Birth estimate / source

Merchant role establishes adult status; assuming age 18+ by 1768, age unstated.

Occupation / trade

Published 1768-06-04 – 1768-07-11 · The Caledonian Mercury · p. 3

What the notice says

A private-sale advertisement describes Nether-Mains, Nethermains Dean, Nethermains Niven and other lands at Kilwinning as belonging to George Glasgow, merchant in Glasgow, offering them by roup at James Shaw’s, Irvine, on the second Tuesday of July.

Evidence and identity limits

Thirteen June/July 1768 issues preserve a private-sale notice. Earlier copies say July next and July copies July inst.; retain the issue-specific wording without asserting another sale or completed transfer. Merks/shillings of old/new extent are historical land valuations, not stated acreages. Belonging is the advertisement’s description, not an independently inspected title. One provisional merchant/property study consolidates the exact named Nethermains subject and compatible Glasgow partnership context, retaining separate sale stages. Compatible profiles remain possible. George-3312’s recorded 1768 death does not exclude later reprints or past-tense property references; no continuity of active life, ownership, completed sale or family is proved. The Manchester inventor and officers remain separate. Identity review (6 October 2026): June-July 1768 sale of Nether-Mains and other Kilwinning lands 'belonging to George Glasgow, merchant in Glasgow'. Identified as Glasgow-3312 (probable) through the Commissariot entry 'George Glasgow, of Nethermains' and the Martin/Ashinyards descent of Nethermains; also shows Glasgow-3312's 1768 death date is doubtful.
