How to read these notices

Cards lead with the person named in the notice; profile candidates are assessed separately. Birth cutoffs labelled “by c.” are estimates, usually assuming an adult professional was at least 18; open the birth note for the basis. Event groups are broad navigation aids built from each notice’s record type. A relationship filter identifies family wording in the notice, not a proved match to a WikiTree family.

Occupation / tradePlace / address

Published 1776-06-26 – 1776-07-30 · The Caledonian Mercury · p. 3

What the notice says

William Glasgow, tenant and co-possessor with William Wilson of Cleugh-head and Clovenfordsyke, is named in a planned land-sale notice at John’s coffee-house, Edinburgh, for 31 July 1776.

Evidence and identity limits

William Glasgow, Cleugh-head and Clovenfordsyke tenancy notices, 1774–1777: William Glasgow is explicitly tenant and co-possessor with William Wilson, not a buyer, landlord or proved relative. West Calder and the named farms are land context. Planned roup dates are not completed sales. Nine original issues preserve an initial notice and its adjournment. No existing profile is identified. Glasgow-1463 is a weak name/region comparison with a broad uncertain before-1816 birth bound; no West Calder tenancy bridge. One tenant subject consolidates the two sale stages without inventing relatives.
