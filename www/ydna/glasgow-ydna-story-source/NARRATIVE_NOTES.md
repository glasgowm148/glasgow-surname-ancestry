# Narrative edition — analysis and design notes

## What changed

The opening view is a continuous explanation rather than an audit dashboard. The page first establishes the historical question, introduces the inherited-branch model using FT4811/FTA30932, and then connects the evidence in five chapters. Specialist tools remain available but no longer dictate the reading order.

The FT4811 fork is an interactive explanation. It makes the parallel role of Churnside/Brown explicit, follows Duffield/Glasgow down the separate FT32941 path, and explains the additional geographic value of Wright. The options represent branches of the reported genetic family, not exact sons or migration events.

The Robert-1749 example highlights three complementary observations within the same reported family: shared FT20271, the three-marker STR signature, and the FTE32242 child branch associated with James M. The controls illustrate existing states; they never rewrite the data.

## Historical reasoning

The explanation separates three jobs performed by three kinds of information:

1. Branch structure identifies the related paternal family.
2. Ancestor estimates supply time depth and a useful historical period.
3. Earlier sourced geography and historical networks help explain the route.

The Norman-era arrival model is retained as the project's leading reconstruction. The explanation develops its positive case: the northern sister-line background, the medieval British surname fork, and the proposed Ferrers/Vipont/Anglo-Scottish connections. It does not convert the FT4811 ancestor interval into an arrival interval or imply a numerical probability.

Earlier British arrival, Norse-era movement and separate crossings are retained as different ways to explain the same broad family history. Each comparison specifies the setting, why it fits, the evidence that would strengthen it, and the next useful geographic comparison. A missing medieval identity is not treated as a reason to stop reconstructing the wider history.

The named medieval people remain concrete research candidates. They are displayed with their time/place and the reason each helps investigate the proposed bridge, rather than joined into a fabricated pedigree. The long-term occupational theme is preserved as a secondary speculative layer; it is not required by the main migration argument.

## Quantitative and epistemic continuity

The kit records, root records, pair distances, extended comparisons, directional variant counts, match set, marker values, variant list and matrix audit are unchanged from the previous origins edition. The historical parent map, aliases, ages and proposed arrival-window values are also unchanged. A machine-readable integrity comparison accompanies the build.

The positive explanation of the Alexander–James J pairing uses the supplied GD 3, DYS714=27 and SAPP grouping as a candidate close-family model. SAPP is still processing the same STR evidence, not contributing an independent observation. Its period is used as a research horizon.

Unknown FT6135 states remain unknown. The detailed scenario tool retains recurrence/reversion and technical/pedigree possibilities where direct results would require them. The new front-facing language does not change the calls or promote FT6135 to a confirmed historical edge.

## Sources and scope

S0 is the supplied original Y-DNA page; S9 is the supplied timeline. The story cites these by chapter. S13 identifies the narrative synthesis. External method/history references from the earlier edition are explicitly retained references. No new live clade ages, private account data, genotypes, documentary records or migration probabilities are claimed.

## Visual and interaction checks

The reading layout uses larger paragraphs, a restrained light default, an available dark theme, local chapter navigation and separate specialist navigation. Diagrams have ordinary buttons with visible selected states, explanatory output, keyboard operation and ARIA relationships. Mobile inheritance controls use concise labels so all three choices remain visible.

The browser suite reports 101 passing content/interaction checks and 140 passing responsive cases across seven widths (320–1920 pixels), ten views and both themes. File/URL navigation was blocked by the environment; the HTML was injected unchanged into Chromium. See the test report for the precise scope rather than treating the counts as an evaluation of the historical interpretation.
